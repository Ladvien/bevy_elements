## Model adopted

**Geometry.** One cloud layer, a spherical shell about a planet centre at $\mathbf c = (x_{cam},\ -R,\ z_{cam})$ with $R = 6\,360\,000$ m (the radius of Bevy's `Atmosphere::earth`, whose entity sits at $-R\hat y$, `bevy-0.20-render.md` "Atmosphere"), so world $y = 0$ is the planet surface and altitudes $h$ [m] are measured above $y = 0$ (the sea-level datum; terrain heights share it). A point $\mathbf p$ has altitude $h = |\mathbf p - \mathbf c| - R$. The shell bounds are $r_b = R + h_b$ and $r_t = R + h_t$; clouds follow the planet's curvature as in Schneider 2015 (l. 1077–1105) and Högfeldt §5.3. The march range of a view ray is $[t_0, t_1]$ with $t_0 = \max(0, t_{enter})$, $t_1 = \min(t_{exit},\ 32\,000\ \mathrm m,\ t_{scene\,max})$ ($t_{scene\,max}$ is the maximum over the march block of the reconstructed scene distance, $\infty$ for sky; Nubis l. 1086). 32 km equals both the weather-map extent and Bevy's default `aerial_view_lut_max_distance` (`$R/bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/atmosphere/mod.rs:336,358`, 3.2e4). A ray reaches a 1500 m base at 32 km at an elevation of about 2.5° (bisection on the spherical shell: 2.54°; 5° → 16.95 km, 2° → 39.5 km), so a clear band remains at the horizon; it is hidden by fading the cloud contribution to nothing between 20 km and 32 km of mean cloud depth (smoothstep on both $L$ and $1 - T$) and by the aerial perspective applied to the cloud.

**Layer type, base and thickness from `Weather`.** `Weather` carries no cloud type, so a pure function `CloudLayer::from_weather(&Weather)` derives it from continuous fields (smoothstep $S(a, b, x)$):

$$ w_{str} = \max\big(S(0.5, 0.9, c),\ S(0.9, 0.98, \mathrm{RH})\big),\qquad w_{cb} = \max\big(\mathrm{clamp}(s/6, 0, 1),\ S(12, 16, u_{10})\,S(0.9, 1.0, c)\big) $$

$$ b = \mathrm{lerp}\big(0.5\,(1 - w_{str}),\ 1,\ w_{cb}\big),\qquad w_{low} = \max\big(S(0.9, 0.98, \mathrm{RH})\,(1 - S(0.1, 0.5, R)),\ S(0.1, 0.3, R)\,(1 - S(0.8, 1.5, R))\big),\qquad h_b = \mathrm{lerp}(1500, 600, w_{low}) $$

with $c$ = `cloud_cover`, $\mathrm{RH}$ = `relative_humidity`, $s$ = `storminess` (strikes/min), $u_{10}$ = `wind_speed_ms`, $R$ = `precipitation_mm_h`. $b$ is the weather-map type (0 stratus, 0.5 cumulus, 1 cumulonimbus; the plan's convention, which differs from Nubis' 0 stratus / 0.5 stratocumulus / 1 cumulus: cumulonimbus is added at 1.0 because Schneider 2015 l. 719 turns heavy precipitation into cumulonimbus). Thickness $H(b) = 800 + 1000\,\min(b/0.5, 1)$ for $b \le 0.5$ and $1800 + 6200\,(b - 0.5)/0.5$ above (800 / 1800 / 8000 m: the plan's values), top $h_t = h_b + H$. Checked in arithmetic over the ten preset targets of the plan (temperate climate, wind factor 1): Clear and PartlyCloudy $b = 0.5$, base 1500 m; Overcast, Rain, Snow $b = 0$, 1500 m; Fog and Drizzle $b = 0$, base 600 m; Thunderstorm and Blizzard $b = 1$, 1500 m, top 9500 m; DustStorm (not in the plan's type list) $b = 0.5$. Both thresholds are design values for lack of a source ([INFERENCE]); they reproduce the plan's table at all ten presets and are continuous in the blend. The map's $b$ is uniform over the map (no spatial type variation; Nubis varies it with noise).

**Noise textures (generated once by compute; recipe of Hillaire's TileableVolumeNoise, which realises Schneider 2015).** All noises tile with the texture period. Let $W_k(\mathbf x) = 1 - \mathrm{worley}(\mathbf x, k)$ be the inverted Worley noise with $k$ cells across the tile, $P(\mathbf x)\in[0,1]$ the 3-octave Perlin fBm at frequency 8 (a periodic variant of `perlin3`: `src/noise.wesl` wraps its lattice at 4096 cells, not at 8, so a `perlin3_periodic(p, period)` is needed; `worley3(p, period)` already tiles). Base texture $128^3$ `rgba8unorm` (8 MB):

- $W^{pw} = 0.625\,W_{8} + 0.25\,W_{32} + 0.125\,W_{56}$ (cells 4·{2, 8, 14}); R $= \mathrm{remap}(P, 0, 1, W^{pw}, 1)$ (Perlin dilated by Worley, "Perlin-Worley");
- G $= 0.625\,W_{8} + 0.25\,W_{16} + 0.125\,W_{32}$, B $= 0.625\,W_{16} + 0.25\,W_{32} + 0.125\,W_{64}$, A $= 0.75\,W_{32} + 0.25\,W_{64}$ (cells 4·{2, 4, 8, 16}).

Detail texture $32^3$ `rgba8unorm` (128 KB): R $= 0.625\,W_2 + 0.25\,W_4 + 0.125\,W_8$, G $= 0.625\,W_4 + 0.25\,W_8 + 0.125\,W_{16}$, B $= 0.75\,W_8 + 0.25\,W_{16}$ (cell counts 2·{1, 2, 4, 8}), A = 1. The last octaves sit at the Nyquist limit of the tile (the generator's own comment); tolerated as in the source. Sampled with trilinear filtering and `Repeat`. World tiles: base 8192 m (texel 64 m, finest Worley cell 8192/64 = 128 m, coarsest 8192/8 = 1.0 km), detail 1024 m (texel 32 m, cells 64–512 m): design values ([INFERENCE]) chosen so a fine step (25–400 m) is never below the detail texel pitch for the 25 m minimum step, and so a cumulus lobe is about 1 km; tune against the screenshots. Högfeldt's 128×32×128 shape texture (a quarter of the vertical resolution) is a memory option not adopted.

**Weather map (512² `rgba8unorm` = 1 MB, extent $E_w$ = 32 000 m, texel 62.5 m, camera-centred, origin snapped to a whole texel, `Repeat` addressing; recomputed every frame).** Channels: R coverage $r$, G precipitation $g$, B type $b$, A unused.

- *Field.* $n(x, z) \in [0, 1]$ is a 3-octave 2D Perlin fBm (wavelengths 8, 4, 2 km, octave gain 0.5; mapped from the measured $[-p_{max}, p_{max}]$ to [0, 1]) evaluated at world coordinates minus the cloud drift $\mathbf d(t) = \int 2\,u_{10}\,\hat{\mathbf w}\,dt$ (CPU `f64`, wrapped), so that clouds stay fixed in the world as the camera moves and drift with the wind. The factor 2 is the plan's; it agrees with the neutral log-law of `wind.md`: $u(1500\ \mathrm m)/u_{10} = \ln(1500/0.03)/\ln(10/0.03) = 1.86$ and at the 2300 m layer top 1.94 (arithmetic). Time is `Time<Virtual>`, the same as wind and fog, so `WorldClock::time_scale` does not accelerate clouds.
- *Threshold so that the sky fraction equals `cloud_cover`.* A noise field has no known quantile on a 32 km window containing only about 16 independent 8 km features, so a fixed remap such as $\mathrm{remap}(n, 1 - c, 1, 0, 1)$ gives an area fraction that wanders by ±0.1. The method adopted is a per-frame GPU histogram: kernel `cloud_field` writes $n$ (quantised to 8 bits) to the A channel of the weather map and accumulates a 256-bin histogram of the bytes in a storage buffer (workgroup-local `atomic<u32>` bins merged with global atomics); kernel `cloud_threshold` (one workgroup) scans the 256 bins from the top until the cumulative mass reaches $c$ and writes the interpolated threshold $\theta$ (with sub-bin linear interpolation) to a 16-byte buffer; kernel `cloud_weather` then writes
$$ r = \mathrm{clamp}\Big(0.5 + \frac{n - \theta}{\Delta},\ 0,\ 1\Big),\quad \Delta = 0.12;\qquad r \equiv 1\ \text{for}\ c \ge 0.995,\quad r \equiv 0\ \text{for}\ c \le 0.005 $$
so $r \ge 0.5 \iff n \ge \theta$ and the area fraction of texels with $r \ge 0.5$ equals $c$ to within the mass of one histogram bin (at most about 0.012 for an fBm of this type: pdf at most about 3 over 256 bins; [INFERENCE: pdf bound, to be measured]). $\theta$ lags the field by one frame (the histogram of frame $f-1$ is used for frame $f$): the map drifts at $2u_{10}$ per second, a few metres per frame. "Sky fraction" in the plan is therefore defined as the map area fraction, which a CPU histogram of the readback can assert; the visible 3D fraction is lower because the density remap erodes thin edges (see Validation 2).
- *Precipitation.* $g = \mathrm{clamp}(R/25,\ 0,\ 1)\cdot r$: the plan's precipitation darkening, restricted to where there is cloud, so $g \le R/25$ and $g = 0$ where $r = 0$ (the precipitation note samples this channel and relies on both; agreed over IRC with `ResearchPrecipLightning`).
- *Type.* $b$ from `CloudLayer::from_weather`.

**Density.** For a sample at altitude $h$ with height fraction $f = (h - h_b)/H$ ($0 \le f \le 1$ else density 0), with $r, g, b$ from the weather map at the sample's $(x, z)$:

1. Height gradient (Nubis, two combined remaps; Schneider 2015 "3 mathematical presets"): $G(f) = \mathrm{clamp}\big(\mathrm{remap}(f, a_0, a_1, 0, 1)\big)\cdot\big(1 - \mathrm{clamp}(\mathrm{remap}(f, a_2, a_3, 0, 1))\big)$ with the four numbers lerped component-wise by type: stratus $(0.00, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30)$, cumulus $(0.00, 0.1625, 0.88, 0.98)$, cumulonimbus $(0.00, 0.10, 0.90, 1.00)$. The stratus and cumulus quadruples are the Horizon values as recalled from the GPU Pro 7 listing and are NOT in the corpus copy of the slides ([INFERENCE: recalled]); the cumulonimbus quadruple is a design value (tall column, dense from 10 % to 90 % of its height). Mean of $G$ over $f$: stratus 0.200, cumulus 0.849 (computed). With wind skew $\mathbf p \mathrel{+}= f\,\hat{\mathbf w}\cdot 500$ m and the drift offset applied to the noise coordinates (Nubis ll. 523–524).
2. Base shape: $S = \mathrm{remap}\big(R_{base},\ -(1 - \mathrm{lf}),\ 1,\ 0,\ 1\big)$ with $\mathrm{lf} = 0.625\,G_{base} + 0.25\,B_{base} + 0.125\,A_{base}$ (the generator's "packed" form), then $S' = S\,G(f)$.
3. Coverage as an erosion (Nubis l. 477) with the anvil inflation of the top (Nubis l. 539): $r' = r^{\,\mathrm{remap}(f,\,0.7,\,0.8,\,1,\,\mathrm{lerp}(1, 0.5, \beta))}$, $\beta = S(0.5, 1, b)$; $\rho_0 = \mathrm{clamp}\big(\mathrm{remap}(S', 1 - r', 1, 0, 1)\big)\cdot r'$.
4. Detail erosion (only where $\rho_0 > 0$): $\mathrm{hf} = 0.625\,R_{det} + 0.25\,G_{det} + 0.125\,B_{det}$, inverted toward the cloud base ("invert the Worley at the base for wispy shapes", Schneider 2015 l. 661): $\mathrm{hf}' = \mathrm{lerp}\big(\mathrm{hf},\ 1 - \mathrm{hf},\ \mathrm{clamp}(10 f, 0, 1)\big)$; $\rho = \mathrm{clamp}\big(\mathrm{remap}(\rho_0,\ 0.2\,\mathrm{hf}',\ 1,\ 0,\ 1)\big)$. The erosion strength 0.2 is recalled from GPU Pro 7 ([INFERENCE]). The 2D curl distortion of the detail lookup (Schneider 2015, l. 601) is not adopted: the plan lists no curl texture and the effect is a detail-scale swirl; `curl3` per fine sample would cost three analytic Perlin evaluations.
5. Extinction: $\sigma_t = \sigma_{peak}(b)\,\rho$ [m⁻¹], $\sigma_s = \sigma_t$ (albedo $\rho_{alb} = 1$, Hess et al. via Hillaire §5.2, RTR4), with $\sigma_{peak}$ lerped by type: stratus 0.05 (centre of [0.04, 0.06]), cumulus 0.085 (centre of [0.05, 0.12]), cumulonimbus 0.12 (the top of the cumulus range; no cumulonimbus measurement was found, [INFERENCE]). Light-path extinction is multiplied by $1 + g$ (precipitation darkening: Schneider 2015 "absorption increases for rain clouds"; the factor 1 is a design value, [INFERENCE]).

**Ray march (spherical shell, per low-resolution texel).** $Q$ = primary step count by quality (32 / 48 / 64 / 96). Fine step $\Delta = \max\big(\mathrm{clamp}((t_1 - t_0)/Q,\ 25,\ 400)\ \mathrm{m},\ (t_1 - t_0)/(1.5\,Q)\big)$, so the count never exceeds $1.5\,Q$ and near/vertical rays get small steps (Högfeldt §5.1; Schneider 2015 64 → 128). Start offset $\Delta\cdot u$ with $u$ from interleaved gradient noise shifted per frame (Deviation 5). Two-phase march (Schneider 2015 ll. 1113–1149, Nubis ll. 1043–1055): coarse steps of $2\Delta$ sampling only the base shape (one 3D tap + the weather map) until $S'\,r' > 0$; step back one coarse step; fine steps ($\Delta$, base + detail) until 10 consecutive fine samples return $\rho = 0$ (Nubis' count), then coarse again. Terminate when $T_{view} < 0.01$ (Högfeldt §6.2.1 and Schneider's alpha → 1) or at $t_1$. Samples whose weather-map $r = 0$ and $b$ uniform are skipped without a base-noise tap.

**Lighting at a fine sample** (all in cd/m², illuminances in lux):

- *Sun colour at the layer.* $\mathbf E_{sun}(\mathbf p) = E_{toa}\ \mathbf T_{lut}(r_p, \mu_s)\ v(\mu_s, r_p)$ with $E_{toa} = 130\,000$ lx (`light_consts::lux::RAW_SUNLIGHT`, the value the Bevy sun uses; `src/celestial/mod.rs:129`), $r_p = |\mathbf p - \mathbf c|$, $\mu_s = \hat{\mathbf u}_p\cdot\hat{\mathbf l}_s$ ($\hat{\mathbf u}_p$ the local vertical), $\mathbf T_{lut}$ the transmittance from the point to the top of the atmosphere read from Bevy's transmittance LUT (see GPU mapping; reachable, so the plan's analytic fallback is not used), and $v$ the planet-shadow visibility: 0 if $\mu_s < -\sqrt{1 - (R/r_p)^2}$ (`ray_intersects_ground`, `bruneton_functions.wesl:140-142`), blended over $\pm 0.0044$ rad (half the sun's diameter, design value) so the terminator is not a hard edge. Because $r_p$ is the *sample's* radius, the layer is lit by the sun after the ground has gone dark: the horizon dip $\arccos(R/(R + h))$ is 0.79° at 600 m, 1.24° at 1.5 km, 1.85° at 3.3 km and 3.13° at 9.5 km (computed), so a cumulonimbus top keeps direct sun until the sun is 3.1° below the horizon (the Bevy sun light itself switches off at $-2°$, `SUN_OFF_DEG`, `celestial/mod.rs:114`; the cloud pass reads the sun from its own uniform, never from `DirectionalLight::illuminance`, which is cloud-dimmed and zero below the horizon). Moon: the same with $E_{moon}$ = `CelestialFrame.moon_lux` and the moon direction; the pass lights with one directional body at a time (the sun while its altitude is above $-4°$ [the dip of a cumulonimbus top is $-3.1°$], else the moon), switched by a smooth weight over 1° so no frame has two light-march sets. Zenith transmittance of the Earth medium at the layer, computed here from Hillaire's coefficients as $\exp(-\sigma_R H_R e^{-h/H_R} - \sigma_M H_M e^{-h/H_M} - \sigma_O\cdot15\,000)$ with $\sigma_M = 4.44\times10^{-6}$: 1.5 km (0.9515, 0.8872, 0.8007), 2.0 km (0.9542, 0.8925, 0.8118), 3.3 km (0.9600, 0.9045, 0.8379); the flat-atmosphere oracle $T(\mu)\approx T_{zenith}^{1/\mu}$ gives (0.751, 0.502, 0.278) at $\mu = \sin 10°$ and (0.905, 0.787, 0.641) at 30° for 1.5 km (within about 5 % of the LUT for $\mu \ge 0.2$ [INFERENCE: flat-slab error not measured]).
- *Phase.* Dual-lobe Henyey–Greenstein, $p_{hg}(\theta, g) = \dfrac{1 - g^2}{4\pi\,(1 + g^2 - 2g\cos\theta)^{3/2}}$ [sr⁻¹], $p(\theta) = w\,p_{hg}(\theta, g_f) + (1 - w)\,p_{hg}(\theta, g_b)$ with $g_f = 0.8$, $g_b = -0.5$, $w = 0.7$ on the forward lobe (the plan says "g₁ = 0.8, g₂ = −0.5, mix 0.7"; Hillaire's Eq. 18 is $\mathrm{lerp}(p(g_0), p(g_1), w)$, so with $g_0 = -0.5$, $g_1 = 0.8$ the same $w = 0.7$ weights the forward lobe; the reverse reading would give a backscatter-dominated cloud, $p(0) = 1.087$ instead of 2.512, and no silver lining: Deviation 6). Values (computed): $p(0°) = 2.5120$, $p(90°) = 0.0224$, $p(180°) = 0.1467$ sr⁻¹; $\int p\,d\Omega = 1$ (1.00008 by quadrature).
- *Multiple scattering (Wrenninge via Hillaire §5.8, RTR4 Eq. 14.18).* Three octaves $n = 0, 1, 2$ with $a = b = c = 0.5$ (the plan's values; $a \le b$ holds, so energy is conserved): scattering weight $a^n$, light-path extinction $b^n$, phase $p_n(\theta)$ = the dual lobe with both $g$ scaled by $c^n$ (the usual implementation of "$p(\theta c^n)$" for an HG lobe [INFERENCE: not stated by the sources read]). Phase values: $p_1(0) = 0.2281$, $p_2(0) = 0.1210$, $p_1(180°) = 0.0701$, $p_2(180°) = 0.0660$. View-ray extinction keeps the medium's own $\sigma_t$ for every octave; only the light-path transmittance and the scattering amount change per octave (so the six light taps are shared by the three octaves, three `exp` calls).
- *Light-path optical depth $\tau_l$.* 6 samples toward the sun in a cone (Schneider 2015 ll. 1175–1195; Hillaire 4 and Högfeldt 4 at growing spacing): five segments of length $\ell_i = 30\cdot 2^i$ m, $i = 0..4$ (30, 60, 120, 240, 480 m; cumulative 30, 90, 210, 450, 930 m; midpoints 15, 60, 150, 330, 690 m) and a sixth "far" sample at 1800 m with length 1740 m (spans 930–2670 m, Schneider's far sample "to capture shadows cast by distant clouds"). Sample positions are displaced perpendicular to the light by $\mathrm{tan}\alpha = 0.1$ times their distance in a per-pixel per-frame random direction (cone, [INFERENCE: cone angle]). Light samples use the base shape, weather map and height gradient but not the detail noise (Schneider: cheap version for light; $-2$ taps per sample). $\tau_l = (1 + g)\sum_i \sigma_{peak}\,\rho_i\,\ell_i$; no extrapolation beyond 2.67 km (a known under-estimate of self-shadowing for a low sun in a thick layer: path length in a 1.8 km cumulus at 10° is 10 km).
- *Powder (Schneider 2015).* $P = 1 - e^{-2\tau_l}$ applied to octave 0 only, weighted by view: $P_{eff} = \mathrm{lerp}(1,\ P,\ \mathrm{clamp}(0.5 - 0.5\cos\theta, 0, 1))$ with $\cos\theta = \hat{\mathbf v}\cdot\hat{\mathbf l}$ ($\hat{\mathbf v}$ the ray direction, $\hat{\mathbf l}$ toward the light): 0 weight looking at the sun, 1 looking away (Schneider: "increasing as we look away from the sun"). $P \le 1$, so it can only lower radiance and the energy bound below still holds. Patry 2021 and Hillaire 2016 obtain the dark edges without it; the plan lists it, the octave sum fills crevices, so it is applied to octave 0 only ([INFERENCE]).
- *Ambient.* Isotropic sky radiance $L_{sky} = E_d/\pi$ over the upper hemisphere scatters, for an isotropic phase $1/(4\pi)$, a source term $S_{amb} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}\cdot 2\pi L_{sky} = \dfrac{E_d}{2\pi}$ [cd/m² per unit of in-scattered fraction], weighted by the Hillaire/Schneider gradient $w_a(f) = a_b + (1 - a_b) f$ with $a_b = 0.2$ (§5.5.1 "[a, 1] gradient, a% from ground bounce"; the value is [INFERENCE]), neutral white (Hillaire: artists desaturate the sky-blue ambient). $E_d$ is the diffuse horizontal illuminance: $E_d = f_d\cdot E_{scene}$ where $E_{scene}$ is the metered illuminance of `celestial::scene_illuminance` before cloud dimming and $f_d = \mathrm{lerp}(1,\ 0.15,\ S(-2°, 10°, h_\odot))$ (the plan's "0.15 × sun illuminance" sky share at high sun, all of it diffuse in twilight; [INFERENCE: blend range]). Under a thick layer the same $E_d$ lights the cloud; the ground under it is dimmed by the shadow-map factor below. **Amended (Deviation 30): the ambient takes the colour of the diffuse light at the sample**, the chromaticity of $E_{sky} + E_\odot\,\hat l_y\,(1 - e^{-\tau_l})$ (the clear sky's irradiance from Bevy's environment map plus the share of the light's beam the cloud above has scattered), keeping the luminance $S_{amb}$ above.
- *Lightning flash.* Uniform $\mathrm{flash} = (\mathbf x_f, I_f)$ with $I_f$ in candela, as agreed with the lightning note: $\mathbf x_f$ is the top of the channel, $(x_{strike}, h_b, z_{strike})$ with $h_b$ = `CloudLayer::base_m` (the cloud-side end of the bolt, not the 300 m scene `PointLight`, which lights the ground only; a source 300 m up would light the deck from 1.2 km below), and $I_f$ = 7.96×10⁶ cd (1e8 lm / 4π) × the frame-integrated envelope $e(t)$ written by the lightning plugin (peak only for a first stroke at a frame start, 0.83 of it averaged over a 1/60 s frame, restrikes 0.1–0.25 of it). Added source $S_{flash}(\mathbf p) = \dfrac{I_f}{4\pi}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\max(d^2, 50^2)}\,e^{-\tfrac12\sigma_t(\mathbf p)\min(d, 150)}$, $d = |\mathbf p - \mathbf x_f|$ (isotropic, with a cheap local transmittance). At $d = 500$ m the peak flash illuminates a point with $I_f/d^2 = 31.8$ lx; at 300 m 88 lx (computed). Under the crate's exposure this is invisible at noon and obvious at dusk or night (scene illuminance below about 1000 lx): the `storm` example's flash frame is checked at 21:30.
- *Total.* Per fine sample, per colour channel,
$$ S = \sum_{n=0}^{2} a^n\,\mathbf E_{sun}\,e^{-b^n\tau_l}\,p_n(\theta)\,\big[P_{eff}\ \text{if}\ n = 0\big]\ +\ S_{amb}\,w_a(f)\ +\ S_{flash},\qquad T_s = e^{-\sigma_t\Delta},\qquad \mathbf L \mathrel{+}= T_{view}\,S\,(1 - T_s),\quad T_{view} \mathrel{*}= T_s $$
the energy-conserving integration of Hillaire Eq. 17 with $\sigma_s/\sigma_t = 1$ (so $\int_0^\Delta \sigma_s e^{-\sigma_t x}S\,dx = S(1 - T_s)$). Mean front depth for the aerial perspective: $D = \sum T_{view,k}\,t_k / \sum T_{view,k}$ over samples with $\rho > 0$ (Hillaire Eq. 21).

**Aerial perspective and composite (Hillaire §5.9.1; Nubis l. 1114).** After the march, at $D$: $\mathbf L_{ap}(D)$ is read from Bevy's aerial-view LUT (log-space RGB, `exp(sample.rgb) * saturate(t / t_slice)`, `functions.wesl:147-164`; the 3D LUT is indexed by screen UV and slice $D/t_{max} - 0.5/n_{slices}$) and $\mathbf T_{ap}(D) = \min\big(\mathbf T_{lut}(r, \mu)/\mathbf T_{lut}(r_D, \mu_D), 1\big)$ from the transmittance LUT (`sample_transmittance_lut_segment`, `bruneton_functions.wesl:146-160`; the aerial LUT of Bevy 0.20 stores no transmittance, `aerial_view_lut.wesl:62`). With the distance fade $\phi = S(20\,\mathrm{km}, 32\,\mathrm{km}, D)$ and $T_c$ the march transmittance:
$$ \mathbf L_{out} = \big(1 - \phi\big)\Big(\mathbf L_{cloud}\,\mathbf T_{ap}(D) + \mathbf L_{ap}(D)\,(1 - T_c)\Big),\qquad T_{out} = 1 - (1 - \phi)(1 - T_c) $$
(the sky behind the cloud already carries the in-scatter to infinity, weighted by $T_c$ in the blend below; adding $\mathbf L_{ap}(D)(1 - T_c)$ restores the in-scatter in front of the cloud to full weight). The luminance is multiplied by the camera `view.exposure` (`ViewUniform.exposure`, `$R/bevy_render-0.20.0/src/view/mod.rs:740`) before it is stored, exactly as Bevy's sky pass does (`render_sky.wesl:71-72`), so every history texel is a display-referred value of modest range (day: radiance 3×10⁵ cd/m² × exposure 8.0×10⁻⁵ = 25; night: about 20 cd/m² × 22.4) and half-float suffices; when the exposure changes between frames the history is rescaled by $e_t/e_{t-1}$. The composite is a fullscreen pass over the HDR main target with `LoadOp::Load` and blend `rgb = src.rgb + dst.rgb·src.a`, `alpha: (Zero, One)`: $\mathbf C = \mathbf C_{scene}\,T_{out} + \mathbf L_{out}$. Cloud extinction is achromatic, so a scalar $T$ and plain blending suffice (Bevy's sky uses dual-source blending for chromatic atmospheric transmittance). The depth mask: a pixel whose reconstructed scene distance is below the cloud-entry distance $t_{enter}$ of its ray gets $(\mathbf L, T) = (0, 1)$ exactly; sky pixels ($\mathrm{depth} = 0$, infinite reverse-Z) and pixels with scene distance beyond $t_{enter}$ get the cloud. Geometry that is inside the layer is therefore treated as lying behind it (limitation; it affects hills when the Fog preset puts the base at 600 m, where the near field is dominated by the ground fog of `fog.md`).

**Temporal reprojection (Schneider 2015 ll. 1287–1310; RTR4 §14.4.2; Hillaire §5.5.3; Högfeldt §6.1).** Block size $k = 1/\text{scale}$: 2 (High, Ultra) or 4 (Low, Medium). The march writes a $\lceil W/k\rceil \times \lceil H/k\rceil$ texture; frame $f$ owns slot $s(f) = \mathrm{bayer}_k(f \bmod k^2)$ within each $k\times k$ block (a 2×2 Bayer order for $k = 2$, a 4×4 Bayer order for $k = 4$), and the ray of low-resolution texel $(i, j)$ passes through the full-resolution pixel $(k\,i + s_x, k\,j + s_y)$ so that after $k^2$ frames every pixel has been marched once (a 4-frame cycle at the plan's half-resolution settings, exactly the plan's "4-frame accumulation"; 16 frames at the quarter-resolution settings, Schneider's own 4×4 / 16-frame scheme; Deviation 8). A full-resolution `cloud_resolve` kernel then writes the new history $H_f$ ($\mathrm{rgba16float}$: premultiplied exposed radiance and $T$): a pixel in slot $s(f)$ takes its freshly marched value; every other pixel takes the previous history reprojected with the previous frame's clip matrix, $\mathbf p' = M_{prev}\,(\mathbf c_{cam} + \hat{\mathbf v}\,t_{enter}(\hat{\mathbf v}))$ ($t_{enter}$ is analytic from the shell, so no stored depth; translation parallax is included, rotation dominates), bilinearly sampled, clamped to the min/max of the current frame's 3×3 low-resolution neighbourhood in the same colour space (AABB clamp, so a disoccluded or changed region cannot ghost), and replaced by the fresh value where $\mathbf p'$ leaves the screen (Högfeldt: α = 1 off-screen). Previous clip matrices come from Bevy's `PreviousViewData` (`$R/bevy_core_pipeline-0.20.0/src/prepass/mod.rs:103-110`; populated every frame for every `Camera3d` view by `prepare_previous_view_uniforms`, `$R/bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/prepass/mod.rs:724-767`, no motion-vector prepass required). Camera cuts and `Weather` preset changes need no special case: a changed pixel is overwritten within $k^2$ frames; the history is dropped (all pixels fresh) when the render target is resized. Hillaire's moving-average blend is not used: with one fresh sample per pixel per cycle the static image is exact to the step-jitter noise (Validation 9).

**Cloud shadow map and the sun's illuminance.** `cloud_shadow` writes a 1024² `rgba16float` map covering an 8000 m square centred on the camera XZ (origin snapped to 7.8125 m texels) for the plane $y = 0$ (Hillaire §5.9: flat-planet projection, transmittance in a 2D texture projected onto the world). Channels: R = $T_{dir}$, G = $\tau_{dir}$ (clamped to 60), B = $g$ (precipitation, so wetness and rain shelter can use it), A = 0. For texel centre $\mathbf q = (x, 0, z)$ and the unit direction $\hat{\mathbf l}$ toward the light with its elevation floored at $\arcsin 0.1 = 5.74°$ (a lower sun would put the layer beyond the 32 km weather map; the sun is then dim and reddened anyway): 8 samples at altitudes $h_i = h_b + (i + u)/8\cdot H_{max}$ ($u$ the IGN jitter), positions $\mathbf q + \hat{\mathbf l}\,h_i/\hat l_y$, length $ds = H_{max}/(8\hat l_y)$, density from the weather map and the base shape only (no detail), and
$$ T_{dir}(\mathbf q) = \exp\Big(-(1 + g)\sum_{i=0}^{7}\sigma_{peak}\,\rho^{base}_i\,ds\Big). $$
Consumers (terrain, water, weathered materials, M3–M4) sample it by world position $\mathbf P$ with the parallel-ray shift: $\mathrm{uv} = \big(\mathbf P_{xz} - \hat{\mathbf l}_{xz}\,P_y/\hat l_y - \mathrm{origin}\big)/\mathrm{extent}$, `Clamp` addressing, outside the map $T = 1$. `CloudShadows { map, origin, extent }` is the plan's resource; this note adds `light_direction: Vec3` (the unit direction the map was generated for: the sun by day, the moon below $-4°$ sun altitude; Deviation 4), and `reference_height` is fixed at 0 so it needs no field. The same kernel also writes the camera value to a 16-byte buffer: the mean of $T_{dir}$ over the 9×9 texels (about 70 m) around the camera texel, read back at the `ElementsReadback` tick (4 Hz) into `CloudShadows::camera_transmittance`, smoothed with a 1 s real-time exponential, initialised from the M1 formula ($\bar T_0 = 1 - c^{3.4}$, so the first frames reproduce M1) before the first readback.
$$ E_{sun} = E_{raw}\,\big(0.25 + 0.75\,\bar T\big),\qquad \bar T = \mathtt{camera\_transmittance} $$
replaces the M1 factor `cloud_transmission(cover) = 1 − 0.75·cover^3.4` in `src/celestial/mod.rs:235-245` (sun and moon light), in `scene_illuminance(.., clouds)` (`celestial/mod.rs:269`, used by `auto_exposure` at `:329` and by `src/fog/mod.rs:271`), with $0.25 + 0.75\bar T = 1 - 0.75(1 - \bar T)$: the same Kasten & Czeplak overcast floor, the obscuration $c^{3.4}$ now measured instead of assumed. The floor 0.25 is the diffuse light that still reaches the ground through a thick cloud (the plan's "illuminance × transmittance" would drive an overcast scene to black: Bevy's atmosphere cannot see the clouds, so its sky ambient is unchanged); at $\bar T = 0$ the result equals M1's cover = 1. The star pass multiplies by $(1 - \mathrm{cloud\_cover})$ (`celestial/stars.rs:226,340,354`, `stars.wesl:79`); stars are drawn in the transparent pass (an `AlphaMode::Add` material) before the cloud composite, which attenuates them by $T_{out}$ already, so that factor is set to 1 in M2 (it would be applied twice).

**Update (Deviation 24).** The factor $0.25 + 0.75\bar T$ above is now the *total*, not what the lights carry: the sun's and moon's `DirectionalLight` carry $\bar T$ (`CloudLight::direct`) and the camera's `AmbientLight` the remaining $0.25(1 - \bar T)$ (`CloudLight::diffuse`), with `scene_illuminance` and the exposure on the total.

**Aerial perspective, fog and ordering.** The cloud composite runs after the stars, moon and water (all in the main transparent pass), before Bevy's `VolumetricFog` (`.after(MainPass).before(EarlyPostProcess)`) so the ground fog attenuates the clouds behind it, and before precipitation (`celestial.md`, plan 2.2).

## GPU mapping

**Bevy 0.20 facts verified for this domain** (`$R` = `~/.cargo/registry/src/index.crates.io-1949cf8c6b5b557f`):

- *Where the passes go.* `Core3dSystems { Prepass, MainPass, EarlyPostProcess, PostProcess }`, chained weakly (`$R/bevy_core_pipeline-0.20.0/src/schedule.rs:50-55,69`). `Core3dPlugin` registers `(main_opaque_pass_3d, main_transparent_pass_3d).chain().in_set(MainPass)`, `tonemapping.in_set(PostProcess)` and `upscaling.after(PostProcess)` (`core_3d/mod.rs:162-166`). Bevy's atmosphere builds its LUTs `.after(Prepass).before(MainPass)` and draws the sky `.after(main_opaque_pass_3d).before(main_transparent_pass_3d)` (`$R/bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/atmosphere/mod.rs:192-199`); `volumetric_fog` is `.after(MainPass).before(EarlyPostProcess)` (`volumetric_fog/mod.rs:100-103`); `bloom.before(tonemapping).in_set(PostProcess)` (`$R/bevy_post_process-0.20.0/src/bloom/mod.rs:79`); the generic `FullscreenMaterial` default is `PostProcess` before `tonemapping` (`$R/bevy_core_pipeline-0.20.0/src/fullscreen_material.rs:95-99`). **Composite:** `cloud_composite.in_set(Core3dSystems::MainPass).after(main_transparent_pass_3d)`: after the sky, stars, moon and transparent water, before volumetric fog, before `tonemapping` (so it blends in scene-linear HDR). It writes the main target directly with `LoadOp::Load` (`ViewTarget::main_texture_view()`, `bevy-0.20-render.md` "ViewTarget ping-pong": no `post_process_write`, the shader never reads the scene colour). **March and resolve:** also in `Core3d`, `.after(Core3dSystems::Prepass).before(cloud_composite)` (they need this frame's depth prepass and the atmosphere LUTs, both ready after `Prepass`), as `ViewQuery` systems using `ctx.command_encoder().begin_compute_pass(..)` (`bevy-0.20-render.md` "Procedural pass" last bullet). **Weather, shadow, histogram, noise kernels** are view independent: `RenderGraph` schedule `.before(camera_driver)` like `src/fog/gpu.rs:46-52,100-139`, with the camera position in the extracted `CloudGpu` resource.
- *Transmittance LUT is reachable.* `AtmosphereTextures { transmittance_lut, multiscattering_lut, sky_view_lut, aerial_view_lut: CachedTexture }` is a `Component` on the render-world view entity (`$R/bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/atmosphere/resources.rs:389-394`), created each frame by `prepare_atmosphere_textures` (`:397-465`); the transmittance LUT is `Rgba16Float`, `STORAGE_BINDING | TEXTURE_BINDING`, 2D, size `AtmosphereSettings::transmittance_lut_size` = 256×128 by default (`:404-417`, `mod.rs:349`); the aerial LUT is `Rgba16Float` 3D 32³, log-space RGB, distances linear to 32 km (`:446-458`, `functions.wesl:147-164`). `AtmosphereSampler` is a `Resource` deref'ing to a linear sampler (`resources.rs:231-232`); `GpuAtmosphereSettings` is `pub` (`mod.rs:367`). Bevy itself binds these from a second consumer, the environment-map probe, with its own bind group and layout (`environment.rs:49-53,123-135,160-170`), which is the precedent for a custom node: query `&AtmosphereTextures` on the view and bind `.transmittance_lut.default_view` / `.aerial_view_lut.default_view`. Not public: `AtmosphereBindGroups` (`resources.rs:596`), so the cloud kernels declare their own layout. The WESL functions of Bevy (`sample_transmittance_lut`, `sample_aerial_view_lut`) are declared against Bevy's group-0 bindings 0–13 (`bindings.wesl`), so importing them would force that group layout; the cloud shaders instead re-derive the two mappings (transmittance $(d, \rho)$ mapping above; the aerial slice formula quoted above) in their own binding layout. The LUT is a pure function of the medium and the camera's atmosphere settings, so it is valid as soon as `prepare_atmosphere_textures` has run; after a quality or `AtmosphereSettings` change the `CachedTexture` is re-created, so bind groups are rebuilt every frame in `RenderSystems::PrepareBindGroups` (cheap), ordered `.after(prepare_atmosphere_textures)` for the history textures.
- *Depth.* `DepthPrepass` (inserted by the `ElementsCamera` observer) makes `ViewPrepassTextures { depth: Option<DepthStencilAttachment>, .. }` (`$R/bevy_core_pipeline-0.20.0/src/prepass/mod.rs:126`), with `depth_only_view() -> Option<&TextureView>` at `:147-151`: bindable as `texture_depth_2d` in a compute shader (`textureLoad`), a separate texture with `TEXTURE_BINDING`, no camera setting needed (`bevy-0.20-render.md` "Depth access"). - *History textures.* Two full-resolution `rgba16float` `TEXTURE_BINDING | STORAGE_BINDING` textures per view, ping-ponged with `FrameCount` from the `TextureCache` exactly like Bevy's TAA (`$R/bevy_anti_alias-0.20.0/src/taa/mod.rs:391-425`; Halton jitter from `FrameCount` at `:354-378`). - *Sun illuminance.* The sun is a `DirectionalLight` spawned in `src/celestial/mod.rs:124-154` with `illuminance: lux::RAW_SUNLIGHT`; each frame `update_sky` writes `light.illuminance` for the `Sun` and `Moon` entities (`celestial/mod.rs:235-246`: `RAW_SUNLIGHT * clouds` or 0). - *Diagnostics.* `ctx.diagnostic_recorder()` and `pass_span(&mut pass, name)` as in `src/fog/gpu.rs:116-139`; names `elements_clouds_noise`, `_weather`, `_shadow`, `_march`, `_resolve`, `_composite` (`render/<name>/elapsed_gpu`; Vulkan and DX12 only, `bevy-0.20-render.md` "Diagnostics").

## Validation targets

Targets come in three kinds: CPU oracle tests (a CPU mirror of the density, phase and integration code, `#[test]`, no GPU), GPU tests (`#[ignore = "needs a GPU"]`, readback of the stated texture), and visual checks. Every tolerance is derived or stated as a design target.

1. **Cloud type function (CPU).** `CloudLayer::from_weather` over the ten preset targets (plan table, temperate climate): $b$ = 0.5, 0.5, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0.5 for Clear, PartlyCloudy, Overcast, Fog, Drizzle, Rain, Thunderstorm, Snow, Blizzard, DustStorm (±0.001); $h_b$ = 1500 except Fog and Drizzle 600 (±1 m); $h_t$ = 3300, 3300, 2300, 1400, 1400, 2300, 9500, 2300, 9500, 3300; $g = \min(R/25, 1)$ = 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.02, 0.16, 1.0, 0.06, 0.16, 0. (These are the values the function was checked against in arithmetic above.) Continuity: $|b(w_1) - b(w_2)| \le 0.05$ for 20 evenly spaced blends between any two presets.
2. **Sky fraction equals `cloud_cover` (GPU readback, CPU histogram).** Weather map copied to a buffer; fraction of texels with $r \ge 0.5$ (byte ≥ 128): for $c \in \{0.05, 0.4, 0.6, 0.95\}$ within ±0.02 of $c$ (one histogram bin carries at most about 0.012, plus byte rounding of $r$); $c = 0$ → exactly 0; $c = 1$ → exactly 1; strictly increasing in $c$ over 20 values, over three camera positions 10 km apart and at drift offsets 0, 7 and 31 km. The CPU mirror of the field kernel (f64 transliteration in `src/gpu/noise_mirror.rs`) must give the same fraction to ±0.005 with exact quantile thresholding. The *visible* sky fraction (3D, fraction of 4096 sky directions with $T_{march} < 0.5$) is asserted only as a band: $\in [0.5\,c,\ 1.0\,c + 0.05]$ for $c \in \{0.05, 0.4, 0.95\}$ with the cumulus type ([INFERENCE: the erosion of thin edges lowers it; to be measured, then tightened]).
3. **Noise textures (GPU readback of the two 3D textures).** All texels within [0, 255]; periodic: for each axis the mean absolute difference between texel 0 and texel $N-1$ is within 1.5× the mean absolute difference between interior neighbours (no seam); values match the CPU mirror within ±3/255 per texel on a 4096-texel sample; channel means of A (base) and B (detail) are recorded in the test as measured constants (the note does not predict them).
4. **Phase function (CPU).** $p_n(\theta)$ integrates to 1 within 1e-3 for $n = 0, 1, 2$ (computed: 1.00008, 1.0000004, 1.00000005); $p_0(0°) = 2.5120$, $p_0(90°) = 0.0224$, $p_0(180°) = 0.1467$, $p_1(0°) = 0.2281$, $p_2(0°) = 0.1210$, $p_1(180°) = 0.0701$, $p_2(180°) = 0.0660$ sr⁻¹ (±0.1 %).
5. **Analytic slab (CPU mirror of the integrator, then the GPU kernel with a debug "uniform slab" density).** A homogeneous slab of thickness $H$ and extinction $\sigma$, view ray normal to the slab toward the sun ($\theta = 0$), sun overhead, ambient and powder off, atmosphere transmittance 1. Octave 0: $L/E = p_0(0)\,\sigma H e^{-\sigma H}$; octave $n \ge 1$: $a^n p_n(0)\,e^{-b^n\sigma H}\big(1 - e^{-\sigma H(1 - b^n)}\big)/(1 - b^n)$. At $\sigma H = 1$ (the maximum of $\sigma H e^{-\sigma H}$): 0.9241 (single scattering) + 0.0544 + 0.0166 = **0.9951** for $N = 3$ octaves. Two checks with different tolerances, because the 6-sample light path is a rectangle rule over segments of 30, 60, 120, 240, 480 m sampled at their midpoints and is not exact in a slab (for $H = 900$ m and a sample with 300 m of slab above it, the segments whose midpoints lie inside the slab cover 210 m, an optical-depth error of 0.1 at $\sigma H = 1$; the worst case is about 0.17, i.e. up to about 15 % in $e^{-\tau_l}$ for single samples, [INFERENCE: arithmetic on the segment table, not simulated]): (a) the GPU kernel against the CPU mirror of the same algorithm (same segments, same jitter): 1 % relative (float error only); (b) the CPU mirror against the closed forms above: 10 % relative at $Q = 64$ and 12 % at $Q = 32$ (view-ray integration is exact for the homogeneous slab by Hillaire Eq. 17; the tolerance is the light-path rectangle rule). Oblique sun, single scattering: $L/E = p(\theta)\,(e^{-\sigma H} - e^{-\sigma H/\mu_s})/(1/\mu_s - 1)$; at $\sigma H = 1$, $\mu_s = 0.5$, $\theta = 0$: 0.5841 (same two tolerances). The test also confirms the energy rule $a \le b$: with $a = 0.5$, $b = 0.3$ the multiple-scattering sum is smaller than with $a = b = 0.5$, and a construction with $a > b$ is rejected.
6. **Energy bound (CPU mirror and GPU).** With ambient and flash off, for every pixel of a stress scene (cover 1, cumulonimbus, full sun, camera inside the layer and above it, view toward and away from the sun), luminance of the cloud radiance before exposure $\le E_{sun}(\mathbf p)\cdot\sum_n a^n\max_\theta p_n(\theta) = E_{sun}\cdot 2.656$ (computed: $2.512 + 0.5\cdot0.2281 + 0.25\cdot0.1210$) with $E_{sun} \le 130\,000$ lx, i.e. at most $3.45\times10^5$ cd/m²; with ambient, $+\,E_d/(2\pi)$. White furnace: sun off, ambient $E_d = 2\pi$ lx, a stratus slab of $\tau \ge 20$ looked at from above: $L = S_{amb} w_a(f_{top}) = 1.0\cdot w_a$ within 3 %; from below at the base $L = a_b\cdot 1.0$ within 3 % ($a_b = 0.2$).
7. **Transmittance of a column (CPU and GPU).** Homogeneous slab, vertical ray, no scattering terms: $T = e^{-\sigma d}$ within 1 % at $Q \ge 32$ for columns that do not reach the early exit: $\sigma = 0.002\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, $d = 1000$ m → 0.1353; $\sigma = 0.0004$, $d = 1000$ → 0.6703. A 1 km stratus column at the peak coefficient: $\sigma_{peak} = 0.05$, $d = 1000$ → analytic $T = 1.9\times10^{-22}$; the march stops at $T < 0.01$ (early exit), so the assertion is finiteness and $T \in [0, 0.01]$ with no NaN and no negative values, and the composite then shows the sun-side radiance of an opaque cloud. Model column of the default stratus layer (CPU mirror of the density, vertical, $c = 1$): vertical optical depth $\tau_{col} = \sigma_{peak}\int\rho\,dh$; the prior estimate is $0.05 \times 800\ \mathrm{m} \times 0.200 \times \bar\rho$ with $\bar\rho \approx 0.3$–$0.4$ → 2.4–3.2, i.e. $T_{dir} \approx 0.04$–$0.09$ (arithmetic with an assumed $\bar\rho$ [INFERENCE]); the test asserts $\tau_{col} \in [2, 8]$ averaged over 4096 columns with $c = 1$, and the recorded measured value replaces this band. If it falls below 3 before M2 ships, raise the stratus $\sigma_{peak}$ toward 0.06 (Hess upper bound) before touching anything else.
8. **Sun colour at the layer (CPU oracle vs GPU readback).** The LUT lookup in the cloud kernel equals the analytic Earth-medium optical depth computed in f64 from Hillaire's coefficients to 2 % per channel for 1.5 km at $\mu_s$ = 1: (0.9515, 0.8872, 0.8007), 3.3 km: (0.9600, 0.9045, 0.8379), and $\mu_s = 0.1736$ within 5 % of the spherical-ray integral (the flat $T^{1/\mu}$ oracle (0.751, 0.502, 0.278) is only an upper-level sanity bound). Planet shadow: at 1.5 km, $v > 0$ with the sun at $-1.0°$ and $v = 0$ at $-1.5°$ (dip 1.244°), at 9.5 km $v > 0$ at $-3.0°$ and 0 at $-3.3°$ (dip 3.13°).
9. **Temporal scheme (GPU, fixed camera and fixed time).** (a) Static camera, frozen weather and drift: after $k^2$ frames the full-resolution history equals a reference rendered with every pixel marched fresh (a debug mode with $k = 1$ and the same jitter sequence) to within 1e-3 relative on pixels not touched by the AABB clamp; rms difference to a 512-step reference ≤ 4 % of the mean cloud luminance at $Q = 64$ ([INFERENCE: step-jitter noise level, to be measured]). (b) Camera yaw of 1° per frame for 60 frames: mean absolute difference between the reprojected history and a fresh full-resolution march at the final pose ≤ 3 % of the mean luminance over pixels that stayed on screen; fraction of pixels whose history was clamped ≤ 10 % ([INFERENCE]). (c) After a forced Clear → Thunderstorm change, residual error against the converged image ≤ 1 % after $k^2$ frames (16 at Low and Medium, 4 at High and Ultra).
10. **Composite and depth mask (GPU).** Pixels whose scene distance is below $t_{enter}$ come out bit-identical to the scene colour (we add $(0, 0, 0)$ and multiply by $T = 1$); sky pixels with $T = 1$ and $\mathbf L = 0$ are unchanged *when $\bar T = 1$* (with cloud overhead the composite multiplies sky pixels by the sky gain of Deviation 25: the test compares only the rows the wall covers, 41–58 of 64, and counts the sky rows 0–5 that the cloud changes); a pixel with scene geometry at 500 m under a 1500 m base is unchanged; blend equation `src.rgb + dst.rgb·src.a` verified against a CPU reference on a 16×16 test image. The reprojection of a pixel whose history is outside the screen takes the fresh value (history ignored).
11. **Cloud shadow map (GPU readback and CPU mirror).** (a) Clear (c = 0.05, cumulus): fraction of texels with $T_{dir} < 0.5$ in [0.01, 0.12]; mean light factor $0.25 + 0.75\bar T$ over 64 random camera positions $\in [0.93, 1.0]$. (b) Overcast (c = 0.95, stratus): at least 90 % of texels with $T_{dir} \le 0.2$; $0.25 + 0.75\bar T \in [0.25, 0.37]$ (the upper bound is the M1 factor `cloud_transmission(0.95)` = 0.370, so M2 is never brighter than M1 under overcast). (c) Rain/Thunderstorm (c = 1): $0.25 + 0.75\bar T \in [0.25, 0.30]$ (M1: 0.25). (d) Mean $T_{dir}$ over the map strictly decreases with cover for $c = 0.05, 0.4, 0.95$, by at least 0.1 each step. (e) Ratio of the sun's beam (`DirectionalLight::illuminance`) Overcast / Clear ≤ 0.05 (0.002 measured; before Deviation 24 the light carried the whole factor, ≤ 0.4, 0.252), the beam equals the camera transmittance of the readback within 5e-3, and the camera's ambient light, as horizontal illuminance $\pi L \cdot 0.452$, is 0.2–0.25 of `RAW_SUNLIGHT` under Overcast at noon in June (sun 68°; 0.210 measured) and under 5 % of that in Clear (0.0000). **What great looks like (ambient, iteration 13).** Clouds belong to the light they are in. At noon a thick deck and the bases of cumulus stay the neutral grey of diffused sunlight, never blue; only thin edges take the sky's blue. At golden hour the sun-facing rims glow orange and the bodies warm with the reddened light scattered into them. After sunset the clouds in the Earth's shadow turn violet-grey under the purple light; away from the sun in nautical twilight they take the mauve of the glow that lights them, never a neutral grey cardboard. At night they keep the night sky's hue. Brightness is not this change's to judge: a cloud's luminance is the model's $S_{amb}$, unchanged. (Arithmetic for the nautical case: a thick base lit by the whole hemisphere shows $w_a S_{amb} = 0.2..1 \times \bar L/2$, while the anti-solar sky at −8° is a small fraction of the hemisphere mean $\bar L$, so clouds lit by the western glow may read lighter than the eastern sky; the iteration-12 backlog item on view-dependent exposure owns that contrast.) ## Deviations from this plan

1. **The sun light factor is $0.25 + 0.75\,\bar T$, not $\bar T$. Superseded by Deviation 24: the factor is now the total, the beam alone is $\bar T$.** The plan multiplies the sun `DirectionalLight` illuminance by the transmittance at the camera. Bevy's atmosphere does not see clouds, so its sky ambient stays at the clear-sky value; a direct-only factor would make a shadowed ground nearly black next to a bright blue ambient and would regress the M1 overcast level (`cloud_transmission(1) = 0.25`, Kasten & Czeplak 1980, `celestial/mod.rs:161-165`). The adopted form keeps that floor and measures the obscuration instead of assuming it. Source: Kasten & Czeplak 1980 via the M1 code and `celestial.md`. (What was true: the floor kept the ground off black, but as 25 % of the sun's beam it left crisp shadows under full overcast, which is the defect Deviation 24 removes.)
2. **Stars lose their $(1 - \mathrm{cloud\_cover})$ factor in M2.** The plan (1.1) says "until then by `Weather.cloud_cover`", i.e. a temporary M1 stand-in; the cloud composite attenuates the stars by the march transmittance (they are drawn in the transparent pass before it), so keeping the factor would apply it twice.
3. **Coverage threshold by per-frame GPU histogram, not a fixed remap.** The plan says the red channel is thresholded so the sky fraction equals `cloud_cover` but gives no method; a fixed remap of a 32 km window of 8 km noise cannot hit the fraction (±0.1, arithmetic above). Method: 256-bin histogram and a one-workgroup scan on the GPU, one frame of lag. Source: this note's analysis; the plan's requirement is unchanged.
4. **`CloudShadows` gains `light_direction` and `camera_transmittance`.** The plan lists `{ map, origin, extent }`. A consumer must know which light direction the map was generated for (the moon at night) and the lighting needs the readback camera value; the generation plane is fixed at $y = 0$ so no height field is added. Hillaire 2016 §5.9: the shadow map is projected assuming a flat planet around the camera.
5. **Jitter is interleaved gradient noise, not a blue-noise texture.** The plan says "blue-noise jitter". A blue-noise texture would be a binary asset (the crate ships none besides the moon map and `stars.bin`) or a void-and-cluster generator, and the corpus has no source for either; interleaved gradient noise $\mathrm{fract}(52.9829189\cdot\mathrm{fract}(0.06711056 x + 0.00583715 y))$ (Jimenez 2014) is analytic and gives every 3×3 pixel block a flat histogram, which suits temporal accumulation (corpus `10.48550_arxiv.2310.15364` §2). Bevy 0.20 ships it with a per-frame offset (`xy + 5.588238·(frame mod 64)`) as `bevy_pbr::utils::interleaved_gradient_noise(pixel, frame)` (`bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/render/utils.wesl:45-48`); the cloud march imports that function, so no copy and no constant to keep in sync. Högfeldt used a Van der Corput sequence, Hillaire a random offset: any of them hides banding.
6. **Dual-lobe mix 0.7 weights the forward lobe.** Clarification of an ambiguous default, not a change of value: $p = 0.7\,p_{hg}(0.8) + 0.3\,p_{hg}(-0.5)$ (Hillaire Eq. 18 with $g_0 = -0.5$, $g_1 = 0.8$, $w = 0.7$). The alternative reading gives $p(0°) = 1.087$ and $p(180°) = 0.336$ and removes the silver lining.
7. **The cloud pass applies the atmosphere to the clouds (aerial perspective) and fades them into haze at 20–32 km.** The plan names a composite with a depth test only. Without the atmosphere the clouds sit unattenuated over a hazed horizon (Hillaire 2016 Fig. 41, §5.9.1; Schneider 2015 "atmosphere occludes clouds over depth"). The result is baked before the history, so no depth channel is stored.
8. **The temporal window is $k^2$ frames: 4 at the half-resolution settings, 16 at the quarter-resolution ones.** The plan says "4-frame accumulation (Schneider)" with quality scales 1/4 and 1/2; Schneider's scheme refreshes one pixel of each $k \times k$ block per frame, so a 1/4-scale march needs 16 frames (Schneider 2015 ll. 1287–1298 uses exactly 4×4 / 16). Interpreted as per-axis scales.
9. **Shadow samples use the weather map and the base noise (one extra tap per step), not the weather map alone.** The plan says "marched along the sun direction through the weather map in 8 steps"; without the base shape the ground shadows would be featureless discs that do not line up with the rendered cloud edges. Cost 0.05 ms (computed). Source: Hillaire §5.9 (the shadow map is a bake of the same cloud model).
10. **The cloud layer's type, base and precipitation darkening are derived from `Weather` by a continuous function (`CloudLayer::from_weather`).** The plan assigns types and bases per preset name, but `Weather` is the per-field blend (no preset id). The function reproduces the plan's table at all ten presets (Validation 1) and is designed here ([INFERENCE], no external source).
11. **Extinction fades per sample from 16 km to 32 km, in addition to the $\phi$ fade of Deviation 7.** With only the mean-depth fade, clouds between 20 and 32 km ended in hard walls at the march limit (boxy pale slabs with vertical edges in the Partly Cloudy screenshot, measured by clipping the march at 15 km, which removed them). $\sigma_t \mathrel{*}= 1 - S(16\,\mathrm{km}, 32\,\mathrm{km}, t)$ at every fine sample. Source: measurement (screenshots).
12. **The coverage field is periodic in the weather-map extent, the soft edge is 0.18, and the map's A channel is a dome factor.** (a) The field's lattice wraps every 32 km (4 cells of the 8 km octave, 8 and 16 for the next two), so the map has no seam where its `Repeat` addressing wraps at ±16 km from the camera and its histogram is exactly independent of the camera position and the drift: measured area fractions are within 0.0003 of the cover at three camera positions and at drifts of 0, 7 and 31 km (`tests/gpu_clouds.rs`, `gpu_tests.rs`). The kernel `cloud_field` therefore only fills the histogram (it does not write an A channel). (b) The field is normalised by its measured maximum $p_{max}$ = 0.385 (`FIELD_MAX`; the 3-octave periodic noise on its $z$ slice peaks at 0.374 over $10^5$ points and 0.383 on a dense grid; the first guess 0.55 used only two thirds of [0, 1]). The soft edge $\Delta$ is 0.18 on that scale (0.12 on the first). (c) With coverage alone the clouds were extruded prisms (flat tops, vertical walls). The A channel, previously unused, holds $a = \mathrm{clamp}((n - \theta)/0.37, 0, 1)$ and the height gradient is evaluated at $f / s$ with $s = 1 + \min(4\,\mathrm{kind}, 1)\,(0.3 + 0.7\sqrt a - 1)$, so cumulus and cumulonimbus blobs are full height in their core and 30 % of it at their edge; stratus is unchanged. Source: measurement (screenshots; the area-fraction test is unaffected because $r \ge 0.5 \iff n \ge \theta$).
13. **The owner pixel joins the history as an exponential moving average (weight 0.35 of the fresh sample), it does not replace it.** The note's "exact to the step-jitter noise" left visible per-pixel grain. The fresh value is clamped history mixed toward the sample; the first frames of a history and off-screen reprojections still take the fresh value. Consequence: Validation 9(a) cannot hold as stated (history equals a fresh reference after $k^2$ frames); it is replaced by a steadiness measure (below). Source: measurement (screenshots).
14. **Jitter is a per-pixel, per-frame white-noise hash (`noise::hash31`), not interleaved gradient noise; the first fine sample after the coarse phase starts at the jittered position.** IGN's diagonal lattice produced a regular hatching in the screenshots, and fine marching that started unjittered at the shell entry produced concentric step rings. This supersedes Deviation 5's choice of function. Source: measurement (screenshots).
15. **The light-path cone uses fixed golden-angle azimuths (2.39996 rad · $j$) per sample, not a per-pixel random direction.** A random direction per pixel and frame made the lit cloud grainy (a 2×2 halftone); the fixed kernel (Schneider's cone vectors are fixed too) makes the lighting a smooth function of position. Source: measurement (screenshots).
16. **The march range is not clipped by the scene depth.** The note takes $t_1$ as the maximum scene distance of the block. The march stops at the shell exit or 32 km; a block is skipped only when every pixel in it shows a surface nearer than that pixel's cloud entry, and the composite masks per pixel by the same test. Clipping would store scene-dependent history that goes wrong when geometry moves.
17. **The Bevy medium's real scale heights are used as the oracle of Validation 8.** `ScatteringMedium::earth` defines its falloffs over the fraction of the atmosphere's thickness (100 km for `Atmosphere::earth`), with scales 8/60 and 1.2/60, i.e. effective scale heights of 13.3 km (Rayleigh) and 2 km (Mie), and an ozone tent of 25 ± 15 km (`bevy_light-0.20.0/src/atmosphere.rs:197-229, 338-404`, `functions.wesl:175-180`). The sky and the cloud light LUT are this medium, not Hillaire's 8 km / 1.2 km. Transmittance at 1.5 km for the zenith sun: kernel (0.926, 0.833, 0.690), integral of Bevy's medium (0.921, 0.824, 0.672), integral of the note's coefficients (0.951, 0.887, 0.801). 24. **The light under cloud is split: the beam $\bar T$ stays on the sun's and moon's `DirectionalLight` and the diffuse remainder $0.25(1 - \bar T)$ moves to the camera's `AmbientLight` (supersedes Deviation 1; the total, and so the exposure, is unchanged).** Defect: under Thunderstorm ($\bar T = 0$) the `DirectionalLight` was still 25 % of the sun and shadow-casting, so boxes cast crisp shadows. Physically, under thick cloud the beam is gone and what is left is light the cloud scatters: Kasten & Czeplak's global fit reaches 0.25 of the clear value at full cover (source and its limits in `celestial.md` Deviation 18, and above under Sources), and the model's own column optical depth is 6.04 (Validation 7), so the beam is $e^{-6} \approx 0.0025$. Adopted: `celestial::CloudLight { direct = T̄, diffuse = 0.25 (1 − T̄) }`, one function (`cloud_light`, `from_beam`) read by `update_sky` (sun and moon `illuminance = E · direct`), the new `update_ambient` (`AmbientLight::brightness = Σ E_b F(h_b') · diffuse / π / 0.452` cd/m², colour from the sun's dusk tint and the moon's tint at luminance 1, 0 when neither body's light is on), `scene_illuminance` and `auto_exposure` (total), `update_distance_fog` (total), `update_precipitation` (its `e_sun`, `e_moon` are the lights' own illuminance, i.e. the beam; `e_sky = total − beam · sin h` is then the sky and the diffuse light, as it was designed to be) and the cloud pass (which lights the clouds from the unshadowed sun above them with its own `RAW_SUNLIGHT · weight`, never from the light's illuminance, and meters its ambient source on `CloudLight::CLEAR`). The factor $0.452$ is Bevy's shading of ambient light (`ambient_light()` multiplies `diffuse_color` by `F_AB(1.0, NdotV).x`, which at roughness 1 is $0.468 - 1.04 \cdot 0.015 = 0.452$ for every `NdotV`, `pbr_ambient.wesl:18`, `pbr_lighting.wesl:527-544`); without it a ground that should show the same luminance as before showed 0.45 of it (measured on the first render). 30. **The cloud ambient takes the colour of the diffuse light it stands for: the sky's, plus the sunlight the cloud has scattered; its luminance is unchanged (supersedes "neutral white" in Model *Ambient*; iteration 13).** Defect, captured in `weather_tour` PartlyCloudy on 2026-04-20 (`/tmp/i13/before/`): in civil twilight (sun −4.2°) the overhead base is a neutral (57, 57, 59) against a violet-to-peach sky; in nautical twilight (sun −8.1°, away from the sun) the clouds are a light neutral grey (76, 77, 77) against a navy sky (49, 52, 68); at golden hour (sun +1.8°) the base is a neutral (64, 62, 60) under a blue-grey sky. The ambient source was `vec3(ambient)`, a scalar, so whatever lit the sky the shadowed cloud was grey. Hillaire 2016 §5.5.1 samples the cloud ambient from Frostbite's global light probe of the sky, i.e. coloured, "the first non directional term" (the hemisphere mean), and gives artists a desaturation knob because, without multiple scattering, the clouds came out "slightly blue"; his conclusions list the single colour without direction as the first thing to improve. Schneider & Vos 2015 (`s2015-advances-ppt-944-mb-pdf-5-mb-2`, ll. 1221–1237): "ambient color contribution increases with height", taken from the sky so that the colours follow the time of day with no baking. Adopted (`march.wesl` `sky_irradiance`, `ambient_light`; `CloudGpu::sky_probe`): the march reads Bevy's own sky, the cube `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight` renders for the camera (`GeneratedEnvironmentMapLight::environment_map`; `sample_sky_view_lut` per texel, absolute radiance before exposure, `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/atmosphere/environment.wesl`), averaged over 16 directions spread evenly over the upper hemisphere (a spherical Fibonacci set). The cube is lit by the sun's `DirectionalLight`, which carries `sun_cloud_factor` of the clear sun, so the mean is divided by that factor (`celestial::sky_clear_gain`, `CloudParams::air.y`): the sky above a layer is not dimmed by it. The night sky's neutral background radiance that the camera shows behind the atmosphere (`celestial::NIGHT_SKY_LUMINANCE`, the `SkyBackground` clear colour, same units; `CloudParams::atmo.w`) is added, and the sum times π is the sky's irradiance $E_{sky}$. At each sample the ambient's colour is the chromaticity of $E_{sky} + E_\odot\,\hat l_y\,(1 - e^{-\tau_l})$: the sky's irradiance plus the share of the light's beam (through a horizontal plane) the cloud above the sample has scattered out of it, which a cloud of albedo ~1 keeps as diffuse light. Its luminance stays $S_{amb}$, so the exposure-calibrated brightness of every cloud is unchanged and only the hue moves. A thin edge takes the sky's colour; the inside of a thick deck by day takes the sun's, with the sky's share added; once the clouds' light is the moon (below −4°) or off, the sky's colour carries the ambient. The far veil uses the same law with its own $\tau_l$. A first version took the sky's colour alone and was rejected by Validation 20 and the horizon capture: under `Overcast` at noon the deck turned periwinkle above a white horizon band (far-row tint 0.249 against the veil's 0.126), because deep in a thick deck the sun term is extinguished and only the ambient is left. That is the physical reason behind Hillaire's "slightly blue" and his artists' desaturation: the diffused sunlight term makes the knob unnecessary. Without the night background the tint at night came from a moonlit atmosphere negligible next to the airglow floor, and a crescent-moon night drew royal-blue clouds (10, 11, 19) in a brown-black sky (11, 9, 7). Without an environment map on the camera (a game removed `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight`) the march binds a 1×1 white cube.
