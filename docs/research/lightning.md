## Model adopted

**What is simulated.** Four things, each with its own clock: a seeded strike process (CPU, deterministic, `Time<Virtual>`); a bolt shape (CPU, deterministic from a 64-bit seed); a flash of light (render: an emissive ribbon mesh, a `PointLight`, the cloud `LightningFlash`, the precipitation brightening); and thunder (a delayed message on `Time<Real>`). The plan's "game time" in the strike-count test is `Time<Virtual>` at `WorldClock::time_scale` 1 (the headless test runs at scale 1, `tests/walk.rs`); at other time scales strikes follow wall-clock seconds, because a 0.2 s bolt decay, a 343 m/s thunder delay and a rate in strikes per minute share one clock; it is a choice the plan left open [INFERENCE].

**Strike process.** Rate $\lambda(t) = \text{storminess}(t)/60$ s⁻¹, with `Weather::storminess` = 0 for every preset except Thunderstorm, 6.0 (`weather.rs:65`), eased in and out over the preset transition (20–60 game minutes) by the weather blend. Time-rescaling construction (the inhomogeneous Poisson process): strike $i$ occurs when the cumulative hazard $\Lambda(t) = \int_0^t \lambda\,dt'$ reaches $\sum_{j \le i} E_j$ with $E_j = -\ln u_j$ unit exponentials from the hash; inside a frame $\Lambda$ rises linearly at the frame's rate, the strike's time is interpolated within the frame (so a strike is never late by up to a frame and a frame can hold several). Deterministic streams (SplitMix64, `hash64(seed, index) = mix64(seed ^ index·0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15)`, `weather.rs:316-318`): with `DOM = 0x6C69_6768_746E_696E` ("lightnin"), $u(s, i, k) = \big((\text{hash64}(s \oplus DOM, 8i + k) \gg 11) + 0.5\big)/2^{53}$ ($f64$, never 0 or 1), $s$ = `Climate::seed`; $k$ = 0: interval, 1: radius, 2: bearing, 3: the bolt seed (the 64-bit hash itself), 4: restrike count, 5–7: spare. The domain constant keeps the stream independent of the hourly weather scheduler, which uses the same hash on hour indices (without it strike $i$ and hour $i$ would share a hash value). Position: radius $r = 8000\sqrt{u_1}$ m, bearing $\phi = 2\pi u_2$, offset from the followed camera's XZ at the strike time (uniform over the 8 km disc), ground height from the terrain (M3; 0 before), `at.y` = ground height. A scripted `StrikeLightning { at }` message takes its bolt seed from `hash64(seed ^ DOM, (1 << 63) | n)`, $n$ counting scripted strikes. The process runs whether or not a camera exists (the visuals need one; with no `ElementsCamera` the position is the last followed position, default the origin). Reference results from a Python prototype of exactly this specification (the prototype asserts the repo's own `hash64` test vectors): `Climate::seed` 0 / 1 / 7 / 42 / 1234, constant storminess 6, 600 s: 60 / 62 / 45 / 64 / 51 strikes; seed 42's first four strikes are at 11.951, 15.450, 19.188, 38.584 s, with first-strike offsets from the camera $(-1174.5, 7352.9)$ m (r = 7446.1 m, bearing 1.73 rad) and bolt seed `0x2d573357dae43c17`. The first three strikes of seed 42 are at radii 7446.1, 3800.3 and 5663.6 m. Over 2 000 seeds the count in 600 s has mean 59.72 and sd 7.80 against the Poisson 60 and 7.746, and 99.25 % of seeds fall in [40, 80] against the theoretical 99.18 % (computed). The blessed golden `the_lightning_schedule_is_frozen` after implementation must reproduce the table or the specification above changed.

**Thunder.** One `Thunder { position, distance_m, loudness }` per flash, $d$ = distance from the followed camera at the strike to the strike point (3-D), `loudness` $= 1/(1 + d/1000)$ (plan; 0.5 at 1 km, 0.111 at 8 km), emitted when `Time<Real>` has advanced $d/c$ since the `LightningStrike`, $c$ = 343 m/s (plan). The first frame whose real time is at least $t_{strike} + d/c$ emits it, so the arrival lag is in $[0, \Delta t_{frame})$. For a uniform disc the mean distance is $2R/3$ = 5 333 m and the delay is 15.6 s on average, 23.3 s at the 8 km edge, 0 to 2.9 s inside 1 km (1.6 % of strikes). $c$ depends on air temperature, $c = 331.3\sqrt{1 + T/273.15}$ m/s (textbook relation, [INFERENCE: no source read]): 325 / 331 / 337 / 343 / 349 m/s at −10 / 0 / 10 / 20 / 30 °C, i.e. the delay at 8 km is 24.6 / 24.2 / 23.7 / 23.3 / 22.9 s. A thunderstorm's `Weather::temperature_c` is the cooled value (preset Δ = −5 °C), typically 5–15 °C, so 343 m/s is up to 2 % fast and 8 km thunder arrives up to 0.5 s early; kept as the plan's constant because no source was read for the temperature form.

**Bolt shape** (pure CPU function `bolt::generate(seed, ground, top_y) -> Bolt`, no ECS). Reed & Wyvill's model with the plan's numbers, generated from the ground up so that the strike point is exact and the cloud end carries the horizontal wander. Main channel: from the attachment point $\mathbf g$ upward along the seed axis $\hat{\mathbf a} = +y$ until the channel reaches $y = $ `CloudLayer::base_m`; each segment has length $\ell \sim U(5, 15)$ m (mean 10 m) and direction $\hat{\mathbf a}$ rotated by a polar angle $\theta = |N(16°, 8°)|$ at a uniform azimuth about $\hat{\mathbf a}$, **relative to the seed axis, not to the previous segment** (Reed & Wyvill: this keeps the channel linear; a rotation relative to the previous segment would random-walk). $E|\theta| = 16.13°$ (the fold at 0 moves mass 2.3 % of the time; $E\max(\theta,0) = 16.06°$, rms 17.9°, computed). Properties of this model (computed, 100–200 bolts, 1 500 m channel): 158.0 main segments (the mean vertical rise per segment is $10\,E\cos\theta = 9.52$ m), path length over vertical rise 1.051 (theory $1/E\cos\theta$ = 1.0505), mean horizontal wander of the cloud end 37.8 m (rms 42.4 m), mean angle between successive segments 22.6° (sd 11.2°; this is the quantity Uman's "about 16°" refers to, and the seed-relative model gives 22.6°, not 16°: Reed & Wyvill's own text says they simulate the 16° as an angle to the seed, which is what is done here). Branches: each main-channel segment spawns a branch with probability $p_0 = 0.15$ (plan); a branch starts at the segment's end, its axis is the parent segment's direction rotated by a polar angle $U(0.18, 0.75)$ rad (10.3°–43.0°, Reed Fig. 4) at a uniform azimuth, it has $n_b \sim U\{10, \dots, 20\}$ segments (Reed Fig. 4) built with the same rule about its own axis; branches of branches (twigs) spawn from branch segments with $p_1 = 0.025$ (Reed Fig. 4), have the same length rule, and carry no further branches (depth ≤ 2). Width: main channel $w_0$, a branch $w = c\,w_{parent}$ with $c \sim U(0.45, 0.6)$ (Reed Fig. 4). Branch glow factor $l = c_2\,l_{parent}$, same law. Expected sizes (computed over 200 bolts): 23.7 main-channel branches (0.15 × 158), 349 branch segments, 130 twig segments, total 637 segments (sd 100). A ribbon pair is 4 triangles per segment: about 2 500 triangles on average.

*Why the plan's "0.6× the length of the parent" is not used.* With $p = 0.15$ per segment and a child 0.6 times the parent's length at every level the process is supercritical: a branch of $n$ segments has $0.15\,n$ children, more than one when $n > 6.7$. Expected segments for a 150-segment trunk: 2 175 with one level of branches, 18 578 with two, 98 294 with three, 1.56 × 10⁶ unlimited (computed). The only 0.6 in Reed & Wyvill is the upper bound of the **width** ratio of a branch (`UNIFORM{0.45, 0.6}`), so the plan's 0.6 is read as that ratio [INFERENCE: the plan text says length]. Reed's own settings are subcritical (branch probability 0.025 × 15 segments = 0.375 offspring per branch, expected total segments for a 150-segment trunk 240 with unlimited depth, computed), which is what makes a depth limit unnecessary for them and necessary here.

**Strokes and the envelope.** First stroke at $t_0 = 0$: main channel plus branches. $k \sim U\{1, 2, 3\}$ restrikes (plan) at $t_j = t_{j-1} + \Delta_j$, $\Delta_j \sim U(40, 80)$ ms (plan; mean 60 ms, geometric mean 58.9 ms against Rakov's 60 ms), main channel only (Reed; Kim & Lin: dart leaders "do not exhibit as much branching"; Poelman: 8.3 % of flashes contain a forked stroke), each along the same polyline as the first stroke (Kim & Lin: dart leaders follow the previous channel). Amplitudes: $a_0 = 1$, $a_j \sim U(0.1, 0.25)$ for $j \ge 1$ (first-stroke peak currents are 2–3× larger, Rakov; optical power is proportional to current squared (Quick et al. snippet) so a ratio 1/9–1/4; "almost a factor of 10 less" (Arizona lecture snippet)). Each stroke decays exponentially, $e_j(t) = a_j\,e^{-(t - t_j)/\tau}$ for $t \ge t_j$ with $\tau = 0.2\,\text{s}/\ln 100$ = 43.4 ms, i.e. the plan's "decays over 0.2 s" read as down to 1 % at 0.2 s [INFERENCE for the 1 %]; $\tau$ is close to the mean continuing-current duration of 44.5 ms (Poelman Table 1), the glow the eye and a 60 Hz camera integrate after a return stroke. Between strokes 60 ms apart the envelope falls to $e^{-60/43.4}$ = 25 %, so the restrikes read as flicker. The envelope is sampled **frame-integrated** so a stroke that falls between frames is never skipped: for a frame $[t_a, t_b]$, $\bar e = \frac{\tau}{\Delta t}\sum_j a_j\,\big(e^{-(\max(t_a, t_j) - t_j)/\tau} - e^{-(t_b - t_j)/\tau}\big)\,[t_b > t_j]$; $\int \bar e\,dt = \sum_j a_j\,\tau$ over the whole flash (energy conserved to 1e-9), and a first stroke starting at the frame's start averages 0.83 of its peak in a 1/60 s frame, 0.46 if it starts mid-frame, 0.010 if it starts at the frame's end (computed; the rest lands in the next frame). Flash length: last stroke at 0–180 ms (mean 120 ms) plus 200 ms: 200–380 ms, mean 320 ms, against 264 ms for all flashes and 371 ms for multiple-stroke flashes (Poelman). Multiplicity 2–4 (mean 3.0) against 3.67 observed, 3–5 typical (Rakov) and 32 % single-stroke flashes (Poelman) that the plan has none of; kept (visual budget).

**Luminance and units.** The plan's bolt luminance is 50 000 cd/m². Bevy treats an **unlit** material's output as display-referred: `pbr.wesl:83-87` writes `base_color` as the colour (no exposure), and emissive is multiplied by exposure only with `emissive_exposure_weight` (default 0, `pbr_material.rs:100,867`); lit light and the `PointLight` are multiplied by `view.exposure` (`pbr_functions.wesl:850`). So 50 000 written into an unlit colour is a pixel value of 50 000, not 50 000 cd/m². Scene luminance $L$ must be multiplied by the camera's exposure $m = 2^{-EV_{100}}/1.2$ (`camera.rs:275-277`) on the CPU each frame. Values from the crate's own exposure model (`celestial/mod.rs:352-390` test values): clear noon EV 13.336 ($m$ = 8.06e-5), sunrise 6.478 (9.35e-3), civil twilight 0.857 (0.46), full moon −2.235 (3.92), moonless −4.747 (22.4). $50\,000 \times m$ = 4.0 / 467 / 2.3 × 10⁴ / 1.96 × 10⁵ / 1.12 × 10⁶: saturated white (above 1) in every case, and at night it exceeds the largest finite `Rgba16Float` value (65 504, the HDR main-target format, `docs/research/bevy-0.20-render.md`), which would store +∞ [INFERENCE: not run] and, through tonemapping, can turn into NaN or black pixels. Several additive ribbons overlap (crossed ribbon pair, branches, restrikes), so the written value is clamped: $v = \min(L \cdot m,\ 4096)$ per layer (12 stops above white, 16 overlaps below the half-float limit); the clamp is active for $EV_{100} < 3.347$. The plan's number 50 000 cd/m² is kept as $L$. It is orders below the physical value: from 2.4 MW/m peak optical power per unit length (Quick et al., VNIR) and a 6.5 cm channel the radiance of a Lambertian cylinder is $P'/(\pi^2 d)$ = 3.7 × 10⁶ W m⁻² sr⁻¹, times an assumed luminous efficacy of 30–150 lm/W [INFERENCE: not measured for this band] = 1.1–5.6 × 10⁸ cd/m²; rendered as a ribbon of width $w$ with the same luminous intensity per unit length it is $L\,d/w$ = 3.7–36 × 10⁶ cd/m² for $w$ = 1–2 m. The difference does not show on screen: both saturate, and the look is set by the glow profile and the clamp, not by the number. The unit semantics and the clamp are Deviation 4. Fog is kept on (`fog_enabled = true`): a bolt 5 km away in a 3 km-visibility rain is attenuated by $e^{-1.3\times10^{-3}\cdot 5000}$ = 0.0015, and since the 4096 clamp is applied before the fog the bolt still exceeds white ($4096\,T \ge 1$) out to 6.4 km in that rain (computed; [INFERENCE] that this is the right look).

**Ribbon geometry and shading.** A crossed pair of axial ribbons (RTR4 §13.6: axial billboards for line-like objects; two ribbons at 90° keep a visible width from every direction without per-frame work) built once per strike from the polyline. The core half-width of a segment is $w = \max\big(w_{phys},\ \tfrac12\,k_{px}\,d_i\,\delta\big)$ with $\delta = 2\tan(\text{fov}_y/2)/\text{height}$ the pixel angle (1.07 mrad on a 1080p, 60° view), $d_i$ the distance from the followed camera at the strike to the segment, $k_{px} = 2$ pixels of core, and $w_{phys}$ = 3.25 cm (half the 6.5 cm mean channel diameter, arXiv:2104.14454 snippet; Kim & Lin: "several centimeters"); the quad (the ribbon) has half-width $W = 9.6\,w$, Reed & Wyvill's glow-to-core width ratio ($3.84/0.4$). The cross-section brightness is their core plus glow, $I(r) = m\,e^{-(r/w)^{n}} + g\,l\,e^{-(r/W)^2}$ with their sample $n$ = 10; it is baked as a 64 × 1 `rgba8unorm` ramp over $r \in [0, W]$ used as `base_color_texture` on a `StandardMaterial { unlit: true, fog_enabled: true, alpha_mode: AlphaMode::Add, double_sided: true, cull_mode: None, .. }` (RTR4 §5.5: additive blending for glow-like effects), so no custom shader is needed; `base_color` carries $v \times$ tint each frame (display-referred, clamp above). Tint linear RGB (0.85, 0.92, 1.0) [INFERENCE: blue-white, no source read for the spectrum]. Branch segments scale $w$ and $l$ by the width ratio $c$ per level (Reed & Wyvill); restrikes ($a_j$) draw the main channel only, so a branch's brightness is the first stroke's only.

**The flash light.** At each stroke the strike spawns one `PointLight { intensity: Φ·e(t), range: 5000, shadow_maps_enabled: false, .. }` at 300 m above the strike (plan), $\Phi$ = 1 × 10⁸ lm, updated every frame with the frame-integrated envelope, despawned when $e < 10^{-3}$. Bevy converts lumens to candela as $\Phi/4\pi$ = 7.96 × 10⁶ cd (`light.rs:574`) and attenuates by $1/d^2$ times a smooth window that reaches zero at `range` (`pbr_lighting.wesl:128-136`). Illuminance on a surface facing the light at horizontal distance $x$ from the strike (light 300 m up, slant $\sqrt{x^2 + 300^2}$, window included; computed): 44.2 lx at 300 m, 23.4 at 500 m, 7.27 at 1 km (window 0.996), 1.84 at 2 km (0.947), 0.659 at 3 km (0.753), 0 at 5 km; directly beneath 88.4 lx. Against the exposure model: a mid-grey surface ($\rho$ = 0.18) 1 km away gets $0.18 \times 7.27/\pi$ = 0.42 cd/m², which at moonless-night exposure (22.4) is 9.3 (nine times white) and at full-moon exposure (3.92) is 1.6, but at clear noon (8.06e-5) is 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ and under a thick-cloud day (illuminance a quarter of clear, EV about 11.3 [INFERENCE]) about 10⁻⁴ against a lit scene near 0.5: the flash lights the night and is invisible by day, which is physically right. Is 10⁸ lm the right power? From 20 kJ radiated in the VNIR per return stroke (isotropic, Wemhoner et al. snippet) and 30–150 lm/W: 0.6–3 × 10⁶ lm·s per stroke, i.e. averaged over one 1/60 s frame 3.6 × 10⁷–1.8 × 10⁸ lm, and over the whole 0.2 s decay 3–15 × 10⁶ lm. The plan's 10⁸ lm is a first stroke averaged over one frame (1.2 × 10⁸ at 100 lm/W); kept (Deviation 8 records the conditions). Shadows are off: a shadowed point light is six shadow-map passes per flash; Tatarchuk's reason for the lightning lightmaps is exactly that cost. Bevy supports up to 10 directional lights (`bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/render/light.rs:229-237`, per `docs/research/bevy-0.20-render.md`), so the sun and moon leave room for a third, shadowed, directional "flash" light if uniformly aligned flash shadows are wanted (Tatarchuk's requirement); not adopted, plan default.

**Clouds and precipitation.** Every frame the domain writes `LightningFlash { position, intensity_cd }` (0 when no flash is active): `position` is the top of the channel, $(x_s, \text{base}_m, z_s)$ with `CloudLayer::base_m` from the clouds note, not the 300 m point, because the light that reaches the cloud deck comes from the in-cloud end of the channel; `intensity_cd` = $\Phi\,e(t)/4\pi$. On a cloud base 1 km above the channel's lower end it adds $E = 7.96\times10^6/10^6$ = 8 lx, a hundred or more times the moonlit cloud-base illuminance and invisible by day. The precipitation pass reads the same resource (`precipitation.md`).

## GPU mapping

Mostly CPU; there is no compute pass. **Modules** (`src/lightning/`, following the climate split so the strike logic runs on `MinimalPlugins`: `ensure_core` / `ensure_gpu_core`, `src/plugin.rs:185-196`): `strikes.rs` (CPU core: the rate integrator, the hash streams, `StrikeLightning`, `LightningStrike { position, seed }`, `Thunder { position, distance_m, loudness }`, the delayed-thunder queue; needs only `Weather`, `ElementsView`, `Climate`, `Time`; `add_message` for the three messages), `bolt.rs` (pure functions: `generate`, `Bolt { segments, levels }`, the stroke schedule and the frame-integrated envelope, no Bevy types beyond `Vec3`), `visual.rs` (needs `Assets<Mesh>`, `Assets<StandardMaterial>`, `Assets<Image>`: builds the ribbon pair and the 64 × 1 ramp, spawns `Mesh3d` + `MeshMaterial3d` + `PointLight`, updates `base_color` and `intensity` each frame, writes `LightningFlash`, despawns; added through `ensure_gpu_core`). The strike systems run in `Update` in `ElementsSystems::Simulation`, ordered after `ElementsSystems::Weather` so they read the blended `Weather`, and the visual update `.after(ElementsSystems::Celestial)` so the exposure is current (`celestial/mod.rs:321-345` writes `Exposure::ev100`; the bolt reads `Exposure::exposure()` of the followed camera, `camera.rs:275-277`).

**Render-side objects.** Ribbon: one `Mesh` per strike, `PrimitiveTopology::TriangleList`, attributes position, uv (the ramp coordinate across the width), $\approx$ 5 100 vertices and 2 500 triangles on average (p99 ≈ 3 800), `RenderAssetUsages::RENDER_WORLD` after upload; material `StandardMaterial` fields `unlit` (`pbr_material.rs:677`), `fog_enabled` (`:680`), `alpha_mode: AlphaMode::Add` (`alpha.rs:54`), `base_color_texture` (`:58`): additive blend materials are drawn in `Transparent3d`, i.e. inside `main_transparent_pass_3d` (`core_3d/main_transparent_pass_3d_node.rs:19-44`), before the particle pass and before Bevy's volumetric fog; `base_color` is HDR (`LinearRgba` above 1 is allowed). Light: one `PointLight` per active flash, `range` 5000 (a cut-off: `point_light.rs:59`, smooth window `pbr_lighting.wesl:128-136`), `shadow_maps_enabled` false (`point_light.rs:71`). Cluster cost: a point light with a 5 km range is assigned to every cluster in view while it lives (≤ 0.4 s). `LightningFlash` is an `ExtractResource`d uniform for the clouds pass and the precipitation pass, no new binding in this domain. The ramp image is `TextureFormat::Rgba8Unorm`, sampled with a clamp-to-edge linear sampler.

**Budgets and limits.** CPU: bolt generation is about 640 segments of 3–4 random draws, microseconds; a mesh upload per strike, at most a few per second even at storminess 6 (0.1 strikes/s, so nothing to batch). GPU: 2 500 triangles of additive blending at up to a few hundred pixels of width, negligible against the plan's budgets (the plan gives lightning no budget). WebGPU: no compute, no storage textures, no storage buffers; one material texture, one sampler: inside every limit. `max_inter_stage_shader_variables` and the 8 storage-buffer limit are not touched.

**Bevy facts relied on** (all verified in the vendored source): `Exposure::exposure()` is `exp2(−ev100)/1.2` (`camera.rs:275-277`); unlit output bypasses exposure (`pbr.wesl:83-87`); emissive bypasses it unless weighted (`pbr_functions.wesl:832`, `pbr_material.rs:100,867`); `PointLight::intensity` is lumens, converted to candela by $1/4\pi$ (`point_light.rs:54`, `light.rs:574`); `range` is a cut-off with a smooth window (`point_light.rs:59`, `pbr_lighting.wesl:128-136`); `AlphaMode::Add` exists (`alpha.rs:54`); the HDR main texture is `Rgba16Float` (`docs/research/bevy-0.20-render.md` "ViewTarget", `view/mod.rs:994`).

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Branches: 0.6× length replaced by Reed & Wyvill's branch model with a depth limit.** Plan: branch probability 0.15 per segment, "branches are 0.6× the length of their parent". Adopted: probability 0.15 on the main channel and 0.025 on branches (Reed & Wyvill Fig. 4), 10–20 segments per branch, polar angle to the parent $U(0.18, 0.75)$ rad, depth ≤ 2, and 0.6 read as the upper bound of Reed & Wyvill's *width* ratio $U(0.45, 0.6)$. Reason: with 0.15 per segment and children 0.6× the parent in length the recursion is supercritical: 2 175 / 18 578 / 98 294 expected segments at depth 1 / 2 / 3 for a 150-segment trunk and 1.56 × 10⁶ unlimited (computed), against 637 for the adopted model; the plan's 0.6 appears in the source only as a width ratio (Reed & Wyvill Fig. 4).
2. **Deviation direction is relative to the seed axis and the bolt is generated ground-up.** Plan: "angle deviation N(16°, 8°)", main channel from the cloud base to the ground point. Adopted: each segment's direction is the seed axis rotated by $\lvert N(16°, 8°)\rvert$ at a uniform azimuth (Reed & Wyvill: relative to the seed so the channel stays linear), generated from the attachment point upward so the strike point is exact and the horizontal wander (rms 42 m at 1.5 km) sits in the cloud. The 16° is therefore an angle to the axis; the mean angle between successive segments is 22.6° (computed). Not a number change; recorded because a rotation relative to the previous segment, the other natural reading, random-walks off the target.
3. **Strike clock.** Plan: "storminess / 60 per second", test "600 s of game time". Adopted: `Time<Virtual>` seconds (equal to game seconds at `WorldClock::time_scale` 1). See Model; not a number change.
4. **Bolt luminance is display-referred and clamped.** Plan: "unlit HDR material, intensity 50 000 cd/m²". Adopted: the physical luminance 50 000 cd/m² is multiplied by the camera exposure on the CPU each frame and clamped, $v = \min(L \cdot 2^{-EV_{100}}/1.2,\ 4096)$, written into the unlit material's `base_color` (Bevy's unlit output is not exposure-scaled, `pbr.wesl:83-87`; unclamped, 50 000 × the night exposure of 22.4 = 1.1 × 10⁶ overflows `Rgba16Float`, 65 504). The luminance itself stays 50 000 cd/m² although the physical value is 10⁷–10⁸ cd/m²: both saturate, and the plan's value is the one the glow profile and clamp are tuned around.
5. **Restrike amplitudes added.** Not in the plan (which gives one decay and restrike times): $a_j \sim U(0.1, 0.25)$ of the first stroke. Sources: Rakov (first-stroke currents 2–3× subsequent), Quick et al. snippet (optical power ∝ current²), Arizona lecture snippet (≈ 10×). Without it every restrike would be as bright as the first, which no source supports.
6. **Decay constant.** "Decays over 0.2 s" is taken as $e^{-0.2/\tau}$ = 1 %, $\tau$ = 43.4 ms. Close to the mean continuing-current duration 44.5 ms (Poelman Table 1). A reading, not a sourced change.
7. **Cloud flash at the channel top.** The plan sets the cloud `flash` uniform without a position; adopted: the channel top at `CloudLayer::base_m`, with the intensity of the 1 × 10⁸ lm light (7.96 × 10⁶ cd). The 300 m point is under the 600–1 500 m cloud base and would light the deck from the wrong end.
8. **Flash power kept, with its conditions.** $\Phi$ = 1 × 10⁸ lm is a first return stroke averaged over one 1/60 s frame (20 kJ × 100 lm/W × 60 s⁻¹ = 1.2 × 10⁸); the same stroke averaged over the 0.2 s decay is 10⁷ lm. Kept. The envelope's frame-integrated sampling (Model) is what keeps the frame-averaged value right at any frame rate; the peak would be about $a\,\tau/\Delta t$ lower than the stroke's instantaneous peak in a 1/60 s frame (0.83 at best).
9. **PointLight shadows off; temperature-corrected speed of sound not adopted.** The plan does not say; shadows off for cost (Tatarchuk §3.3.4). The plan's constant 343 m/s stays: the temperature relation is textbook and no source was read, so no deviation (the numbers are in Model for whoever wants it).
