# Trails: footprints, ruts and tracks in snow, sand and mud

Everything a game does to this crate's world has been one way: the environment informs the game (`Environment`, `WeatheredMaterial`, `PrecipitationBlocker` shelter), but nothing a character, an animal or a vehicle does leaves a mark. Trails close that loop: an entity with an `Imprint` presses into the ground under it (snow, dry or wet sand, soil, mud, each with its own depth and its own rim), and the weather erases what it pressed (new snow fills the trench, the wind smooths it, a melt takes it away, wet sand and mud slump). The terrain's simulation grid is 4 m at High, two orders of magnitude too coarse for a boot, so trails live in their own centimetre field that follows the camera and is drawn per pixel.

## Model adopted

**State.** A square window of $N^2$ texels of size $c$ centred (to a whole texel) on the followed camera, toroidally addressed by *world* texel: world texel $(i, j)$ lives at $(i \bmod N, j \bmod N)$, so the window slides without copying and a texel keeps its world position. Each texel holds the surface's offset $h$ from the undisturbed ground (m, negative in a trench, positive on a rim) and the coarse snow depth $s_{ref}$ it last saw. A texel that enters the window is reset to $h = 0$ (Rise of the Tomb Raider's sliding window); the drawn trail fades to zero over the outer eighth of the window so the reset is never seen.

**Material per texel** from the maps the crate already keeps, sampled bilinearly at the texel: snow where the cover's snow depth exceeds 1 cm; else sand where the terrain's sediment exceeds 0.5 m (the terrain material's sand rule); else soil, moving from dry soil to mud with the wetness map; no imprint on rock (slope above 40°).

**Imprint.** An `Imprint` is an ellipse of half extents $(a, b)$ on its entity's local XZ, carrying a load $F$ (N) that presses straight down; the entity's translation is the sole. It is in contact while the sole is no more than 0.1 m above the undisturbed surface (terrain + snow) under its centre. In contact it carves the ellipse swept from where it was last carved to where it is now (so a wheel at 20 m/s leaves a continuous rut at 60 fps). The pressure is $p = F/(\pi a b)$, the plate width $b_p = 2\min(a, b)$, and the target depth $z$ follows the material:

- *Snow:* $z = \min\big(z_{B}(p, b_p),\ h_s(1 - \rho_s/\rho_f)\big)$ — Bekker's deep-snow sinkage with Wong's snow (US) constants, capped by the CRREL shallow-snow compaction limit for the local snow depth $h_s$ and density $\rho_s$ (the cover's SWE over its depth), $\rho_f$ = 500 kg/m³ (CRREL's category for ground pressures under 210 kPa).
- *Sand:* $z_B$ with Wong's dry sand.
- *Soil to mud:* $\log z = \mathrm{lerp}(\log z_{B,15\%},\ \log z_{B,32\%},\ w)$, Wong's sandy loam at 15 % and 32 % moisture, by the wetness $w$.
- Every $z$ is capped at $L_0$ = 0.30 m (Alvarado's loose layer), which also bounds the small-$n$ fits outside their measured range.

The carve is plastic and cumulative (Alvarado): $h \leftarrow \min(h, -z)$ over the ellipse; stepping into an old print deepens it only if the new target is deeper. The rim: a share $2\nu$ of the volume carved this step (Alvarado Eq. 12; ν = 0.05 snow, 0.5 sand, 0.35 soil and mud) rises on a ring of width $r = \max(2c, 0.3\min(a, b))$ outside the ellipse with a linear profile, by the carve at the nearest point inside the edge times the ellipse's area-to-perimeter ratio (local conservation per unit length of edge).

**Relaxation.** Every step the surface slumps where its slope exceeds the material's angle (Sumner's erosion in conservative flux form: between each pair of 4-neighbours a share σ = 0.2 of the height excess over $c\tan\theta$ moves down): snow θ = 90° (it holds a vertical wall: Sumner, CRREL), dry sand 34° (the crate's repose angle, `dust.md`), wet sand 90° (pendular cohesion, Su et al.), saturated sand (puddled) 10°, soil 90°, mud 63° (Sumner's mud θ_out = 1.1 rad).

**Erasure by the weather.** (1) *New snow:* the cover map is the authority on how much snow arrived; when the coarse snow depth under a texel grows by Δ, a trench is filled by Δ ($h \leftarrow \min(h + Δ, \max(h, 0))$) and rims are left (the new snow lies on them as on the flat). (2) *Melt and settling:* a trench in snow is never deeper than the snow ($h \ge -h_s$) and a rim not higher than half of it. (3) *Wind:* the saltation flux smooths the micro-relief as diffusion of $h$ with diffusivity equal to the volumetric flux, the snow drift's $k_d/\rho_w\,(u - u_t)^3 \cdot \rho_w/\rho_s$ or the sand's $k\,u^2(u - u_t)$ — about 3×10⁻⁵ m²/s for snow at 10 m/s, which fills a 30 cm print in about five minutes, and nothing below the threshold. (4) *Rain:* wet sand and mud slump at their angles above.

**Rendering.** The terrain material and `GroundExtension` read the published field by world XZ and draw it per pixel, never as geometry (the 4 m vertex grid cannot hold it): parallax occlusion mapping of the height field from $+h_{max}$ down to $-h_{max}$ along the view ray (Tatarchuk's linear search with a piecewise-linear intersection, 8 to 32 samples by the view angle, so a rim also hides the trench behind it), the hit point's world position used for the lighting, the normal from the field's central differences, a soft self-shadow toward the sun from Tatarchuk's blocker-to-receiver ratio applied to the direct share of the light, and an occlusion term for the ambient from how far the trench floor lies below its surroundings. In snow, the floor of a print is compacted snow (smoother; the sky's blue in its shade as Barré-Brisebois' transition tint); where a print reaches the bottom of the snow the ground under it shows (The Last of Us Part II).

## GPU mapping

`TrailsPlugin` (module `trails`, with the terrain and the cover maps). Storage buffers: `state` (vec2 per texel: $h$, $s_{ref}$) in two ping-pong copies, `stamp` (two `atomic<u32>` per texel: the deepest carve's f32 bits, and the rim added this step in fixed point of 1 µm) and an `imprints` buffer of at most 64 entries (the nearest to the camera, as `PrecipitationBlocker`s are), each with its swept segment, ellipse, yaw, pressure-derived constants and contact sole height. Two kernels a frame: `trail_stamp` (one 16 × 16 workgroup per imprint over its swept bounding box, at most 64 × 64 texels; contact test, material, target depth, `atomicMax` on the carve, `atomicAdd` on the rim), then `trail_step` (every texel: reset on entry, apply the stamp and clear it, snow refill and clamps, wind diffusion, slump, write the next `state` and publish $h$ and its gradient to a write-only `rgba16float` texture the materials sample). The texture is toroidal by world texel, sampled with a repeating sampler, and the shader finds the window from the view position, so the materials' uniforms do not change as the camera moves. Within WebGPU's limits: three storage buffers and one storage texture per kernel stage; the materials gain one sampled texture and one sampler.

## Validation targets

1. **Sinkage (CPU, `trails::model`).** A 77.5 kg walker on one foot (ellipse 0.05 × 0.13 m, 37.2 kPa): dry sand 3.39 cm, dry soil 0.48 cm, mud 30 cm (the $L_0$ cap; Bekker 39 cm), 30 cm of fresh snow at 100 kg/m³ 24.0 cm (the CRREL cap; Bekker 31.2 cm), the same snow settled to 300 kg/m³ 12.0 cm (±1 %). The log-interpolated soil depth is monotone in wetness.
2. **An imprint presses its depth (GPU).** On flat snow, one still imprint for a few frames: the field's minimum under it equals the CPU model's depth (±3 %), and the rim volume is $2\nu$ of the carved volume (±25 %: the ring is discrete).
3. **A fast imprint leaves a continuous rut (GPU).** At 20 m/s the carve along its path has no texel shallower than half the target depth.
4. **Snow refills (GPU).** Under snowfall that raises the coarse snow by Δ, a trench shallows by Δ (±10 %) and the flat around it is unchanged.
5. **The window keeps a trail in place (GPU).** A trail stays at its world position (±1 texel) as the camera moves 10 m, and is gone after the camera has moved a window away and back.
6. **Sand slumps to its angle (GPU).** A deep print in dry sand ends with no slope steeper than tan 34° + 10 %.
7. **Visual.** At 2–20 m, at noon and with a low sun: footprints and ruts read as depressions with crisp walls in snow and rounded rims in sand; shadowed inside at a low sun; no swimming as the camera moves; no seam where the window ends; trench floors in thin snow show the ground. Over a game hour of snowfall the trail fades away.
8. **Performance.** High (1024²): the two kernels ≤ 0.15 ms, the parallax ≤ 0.2 ms at 1920 × 1080 with half the frame ground inside the window.

## What great looks like

A line of boot prints in fresh powder, each a crisp-walled pocket with a soft lip and a blue-grey shadow on its sun side, deep enough to read from 20 m. A car's two ruts pressed through thin snow to the dark wet ground beneath, with a low ridge of pushed snow between and beside them. Beach prints in dry sand with rounded slumped walls and a raised rim, and in wet sand sharp and shallow. A muddy track after rain: deep, glossy, the rims standing. Then the weather taking it back: new snow softening and filling the prints until only shallow dimples remain, wind smearing the sand prints into the dune. And no trace of the machinery: no edge where the window ends, no popping as the camera moves, no aliasing at grazing angles.
