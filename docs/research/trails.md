# Trails: footprints, ruts and tracks in snow, sand and mud

Everything a game does to this crate's world has been one way: the environment informs the game (`Environment`, `WeatheredMaterial`, `PrecipitationBlocker` shelter), but nothing a character, an animal or a vehicle does leaves a mark. Trails close that loop: an entity with an `Imprint` presses into the ground under it (snow, dry or wet sand, soil, mud, each with its own depth and its own rim), and the weather erases what it pressed (new snow fills the trench, the wind smooths it, a melt takes it away, wet sand and mud slump). The terrain's simulation grid is 4 m at High, two orders of magnitude too coarse for a boot, so trails live in their own centimetre field that follows the camera and is drawn per pixel.

## Model adopted

**State.** A square window of $N^2$ texels of size $c$ centred (to a whole texel) on the followed camera, toroidally addressed by *world* texel: world texel $(i, j)$ lives at $(i \bmod N, j \bmod N)$, so the window slides without copying and a texel keeps its world position. Each texel holds the surface's offset $h$ from the undisturbed ground (m, negative in a trench, positive on a rim) and the coarse snow depth $s_{ref}$ it last saw. A texel that enters the window is reset to $h = 0$ (Rise of the Tomb Raider's sliding window); the drawn trail fades to zero over the outer eighth of the window so the reset is never seen.

**Material per texel** from the maps the crate already keeps, sampled bilinearly at the texel: snow where the cover's snow depth exceeds 1 cm; else sand where the terrain's sediment exceeds 0.5 m (the terrain material's sand rule); else soil, moving from dry soil to mud with the wetness map; no imprint on rock (slope above 40°).

**Imprint.** An `Imprint` is an ellipse of half extents $(a, b)$ on its entity's local XZ, carrying a load $F$ (N) that presses straight down; the entity's translation is the sole. It is in contact while the sole is no more than 0.1 m above the undisturbed surface (terrain + snow) under its centre. In contact it carves the ellipse swept from where it was last carved to where it is now (so a wheel at 20 m/s leaves a continuous rut at 60 fps). The pressure is $p = F/(\pi a b)$, the plate width $b_p = 2\min(a, b)$, and the target depth $z$ follows the material:

- *Snow:* $z = \min\big(z_{B}(p, b_p),\ h_s(1 - \rho_s/\rho_f)\big)$ — Bekker's deep-snow sinkage with Wong's snow (US) constants, capped by the CRREL shallow-snow compaction limit for the local snow depth $h_s$ and density $\rho_s$ (the cover's SWE over its depth), $\rho_f$ = 500 kg/m³ (CRREL's category for ground pressures under 210 kPa).
- *Sand:* $z_B$ with Wong's dry sand.
- *Soil to mud:* $\log z = \mathrm{lerp}(\log z_{B,15\%},\ \log z_{B,32\%},\ w)$, Wong's sandy loam at 15 % and 32 % moisture, by the wetness $w$.
- Every $z$ is capped at $L_0$ = 0.30 m (Alvarado's loose layer), which also bounds the small-$n$ fits outside their measured range.

The carve is plastic and cumulative (Alvarado): $h \leftarrow \min(h, -z)$ over the ellipse; stepping into an old print deepens it only if the new target is deeper. The rim: a share $2\nu$ of the volume carved this step (Alvarado Eq. 12; ν = 0.05 snow, 0.5 sand, 0.35 soil and mud) rises on a ring of width $r = \max(2c, 0.3\min(a, b))$ outside the ellipse with a linear profile, by the carve at the nearest point inside the edge times the ellipse's area-to-perimeter ratio (local conservation per unit length of edge).

**Relaxation.** Every step the surface slumps where its slope exceeds the material's angle (Sumner's erosion in conservative flux form: between each pair of 4-neighbours a share σ = 0.2 of the height excess over $c\tan\theta$ moves down): snow θ = 90° (it holds a vertical wall: Sumner, CRREL), dry sand 34° (the crate's repose angle, `dust.md`), wet sand 90° (pendular cohesion, Su et al.), saturated sand (puddled) 10°, soil 90°, mud 63° (Sumner's mud θ_out = 1.1 rad).

**Erasure by the weather.** (1) *New snow:* the cover map is the authority on how much snow arrived; when the coarse snow depth under a texel grows by Δ, a trench is filled by Δ ($h \leftarrow \min(h + Δ, \max(h, 0))$) and rims are left (the new snow lies on them as on the flat). (2) *Melt and settling:* a trench in snow is never deeper than the snow ($h \ge -h_s$) and a rim not higher than half of it. (3) *Wind:* the saltation flux smooths the micro-relief as diffusion of $h$ with diffusivity equal to the volumetric flux, the snow drift's $k_d/\rho_w\,(u - u_t)^3 \cdot \rho_w/\rho_s$ or the sand's $k\,u^2(u - u_t)$ — about 3×10⁻⁵ m²/s for snow at 10 m/s, which fills a 30 cm print in about five minutes, and nothing below the threshold. (4) *Rain:* wet sand and mud slump at their angles above.

**Rendering.** The terrain material and `GroundExtension` read the published field by world XZ and draw it per pixel, never as geometry (the 4 m vertex grid cannot hold it): parallax occlusion mapping of the height field along the view ray (Tatarchuk's linear search with one sample per texel of ground, then a bisection on a cubic B-spline of the field; Deviation 3), the hit point's world position used for the lighting, the normal from the B-spline height (Deviation 4), a soft self-shadow toward the sun or moon applied to the direct share of the light, and an occlusion term for the ambient from how far the trench floor lies below its surroundings. In snow, the floor of a print is compacted snow (smoother, bluer: Deviation 8); where a print reaches the bottom of the snow the ground under it shows (The Last of Us Part II); a trench through grass shows the soil, and water stands on the floors of ruts in saturated ground (Deviation 6).

## GPU mapping

`TrailsPlugin` (module `trails`, with the terrain and the cover maps). Storage buffers: `state` (vec2 per texel: $h$, $s_{ref}$) in two ping-pong copies, `stamp` (two `atomic<u32>` per texel: the deepest carve and the highest rim proposed this frame, as f32 bits), `blocks` (the largest |h| per 8 × 8 block) and an `imprints` buffer of at most 64 entries (the nearest to the camera, as `PrecipitationBlocker`s are), each with its swept segment, ellipse, yaw, pressure, plate width, effective carve per edge length and contact sole height. Four kernels a frame in one pass `elements_trails`: `trail_stamp` (one 16 × 16 workgroup grid per imprint over its swept bounding box, at most 128 × 128 texels; contact test, material, target depth, `atomicMax` on the carve and the rim), `trail_merge` (every texel: reset on entry, apply the stamp's targets and clear them, snow refill and clamps; `a` → `b`), `trail_blocks` (block maxima) and `trail_relax` (slump and wind diffusion `b` → `a`, and publish $h$, the dilated block occupancy and the Sobel gradient to a write-only `rgba16float` texture). The render world remembers the window origin of the last frame it ran (pipelined rendering hides it from the main world). The texture is toroidal by world texel, sampled with a repeating sampler, and the shader finds the window from the view position, so the materials' uniforms do not change as the camera moves. Within WebGPU's limits: seven storage buffers and one storage texture per kernel; the materials gain one sampled texture and one sampler.

## Validation targets

1. **Sinkage (CPU, `trails::model`).** A 77.5 kg walker on one foot (ellipse 0.05 × 0.13 m, 37.2 kPa): dry sand 3.39 cm, dry soil 0.48 cm, mud 30 cm (the $L_0$ cap; Bekker 39 cm), 30 cm of fresh snow at 100 kg/m³ 24.0 cm (the CRREL cap; Bekker 31.2 cm), the same snow settled to 300 kg/m³ 12.0 cm (±1 %). The log-interpolated soil depth is monotone in wetness. (`a_walker_sinks_as_the_note_computes`, `soil_sinks_deeper_the_wetter_it_is`.)
2. **An imprint presses its depth (GPU, `an_imprint_presses_its_depth_and_raises_its_rim`).** On flat snow, one still imprint for 20 frames: the field's minimum under it equals the CPU model's depth (±3 %; measured 23.65 cm for 24.0, the texel centres of a 10 cm sole), and the rim volume is $2\nu$ of the carved volume (0.075–0.125; measured 0.094).
3. **A fast imprint leaves a continuous rut (GPU, `a_fast_imprint_leaves_an_unbroken_rut`).** At 20 m/s the carve along its path has no texel shallower than half the target depth (measured: the shallowest is the full 24.0 cm).
4. **Snow refills (GPU, `new_snow_fills_a_trench`).** When the cover's snow rises by 5 cm, a trench shallows by 5 cm (±0.5 cm; measured 23.65 → 18.64 cm) and the flat around it is unchanged (0.0000 m).
5. **The window keeps a trail in place (GPU, `the_window_keeps_a_trail_in_place`).** A trail stays at its world position as the camera moves 10 m (measured identical, −23.65 cm), and is gone after the camera has moved a window away and back (0.0000 m).
6. **Sand slumps to its angle (visual, Deviation 9).** Dry-sand ruts end as rounded, slumped V's, not slots.
7. **Visual.** At 2–20 m, at noon and with a low sun: footprints and ruts read as depressions with crisp walls in snow and rounded rims in sand; shadowed inside at a low sun; no swimming as the camera moves; no seam where the window ends; trench floors in thin snow show the ground. Over a game hour of snowfall the trail fades away. Reviewed over 18 rounds of captures (`trails` example: snow at 10.5 h and 15.6 h, sand at noon and 18.3 h, mud after rain, and under the `Snow` preset at ×120). Snow and mud read as specified; dry-sand prints at a 62° noon sun are faint (3.4 cm dishes slumped to 34° shade only 0.88 → 0.46 on their far walls, as real ones do) and read clearly at a low sun. Snowfall softens the trails at the cover map's rate (1.5 mm/h of water, about 1.5 cm of snow an hour), so a 24 cm trench needs most of a day, not an hour, to fill: the plan's hour assumed a heavier fall.
8. **Performance.** High (1024²): the kernels ≤ 0.15 ms, the parallax ≤ 0.2 ms at 1920 × 1080. Measured (`bench_elements`, High, hills, `PartlyCloudy`, 1080p, RTX 3090 at 1.5–1.9 GHz, minimum over the run, two runs each): `elements_trails` 0.119 ms with `ELEMENTS_IMPRINTS=16` (0.187 ms before the merge and relax kernels skipped the ground lookups of untouched texels); `main_opaque_pass_3d` 0.054–0.058 ms with `ELEMENTS_TRAILS=off` and 0.057–0.058 ms with the 16 wheels' tracks round the camera, a difference within the run-to-run spread. [INFERENCE: a frame filled with trails costs more; the march is bounded at 96 + 32 samples a pixel.]

## What great looks like

A line of boot prints in fresh powder, each a crisp-walled pocket with a soft lip and a blue-grey shadow on its sun side, deep enough to read from 20 m. A car's two ruts pressed through thin snow to the dark wet ground beneath, with a low ridge of pushed snow between and beside them. Beach prints in dry sand with rounded slumped walls and a raised rim, and in wet sand sharp and shallow. A muddy track after rain: deep, glossy, the rims standing. Then the weather taking it back: new snow softening and filling the prints until only shallow dimples remain, wind smearing the sand prints into the dune. And no trace of the machinery: no edge where the window ends, no popping as the camera moves, no aliasing at grazing angles.

## Deviations from this plan

Each found in the visual review (`/tmp/trails/r*/`, crops at 2–3×) or a GPU test, and fixed in the shipped model:

1. **The rim is a plastic target, not an accumulation, and spreads over a ring as wide as the carve is deep.** As specified, every stamp added `2ν × (what the carve added this step)` at the ring. A still sole's inner wall never reaches its full depth (the soft wall), so "what it added" never fell to zero and 30 frames of contact stacked 30 rims into fins that cast long shadows (`r2/walk_zoom2.png`). Adopted: the stamp proposes the rim a single carve from flat ground would raise and the merge raises the surface to it (`atomicMax`), as the carve lowers it; a rim never raises a cell already below the flat (a moving imprint's ring lies over what it carved a frame earlier, which otherwise refilled every rut, `r3/car_zoom3.png`). The proposal is `2ν · z · (A/P)_eff · (π/2R) sin(π d/R) / (1 + κ d)` at `d` outside the edge, over a ring `R = max(ring, z)`, capped at 0.1 m: a narrow ring with its crest at the wall made a crease the field aliased into ticks along a diagonal rut (`r11/ticks.png`); the half sine has no crest at the wall, and `1/(1 + κd)` (κ the ellipse's curvature at a cap, zero along a sweep's sides) keeps the volume as the ring widens. Pressing twice in the same place changes nothing, and the measured rim is 9.9 % of the carved volume for `2ν` = 10 % (Validation 2).
2. **The carve's wall is 2.5 texels wide, at most half the ellipse's narrower half extent; the effective carve per edge length is `A/P · (1 − e + 0.3 e²)`.** A 1.5-texel wall made a bilinear staircase along curved ruts (`r1/car_zoom.png`); a 2.5-texel wall on a 10 cm sole left it no floor (Validation 2 read 22.2 cm for 24). `1 − e + 0.3 e²` is the mean of the profile over the ellipse (`∫₀¹ (1 − smoothstep(1 − e, 1, r)) 2r dr`), which keeps the rim's share right for the softened carve.
3. **The parallax samples one texel of ground per step (8–96), from +0.1 m above the plane (rims) to −0.35 m below it, skips empty ground, and refines on a cubic B-spline of the field.** Tatarchuk's 8–32 samples by angle over ±0.35 m took 14 cm steps at grazing views against 5 cm texels: crossed prints and stepped shadows (`r1/walk_zoom.png`). The relax pass publishes, per texel, the largest |h| over its 8 × 8 block and the eight around it (an extra 8 × 8-block kernel, `trail_blocks`); a fragment whose ray start, middle and end see none skips the march. After the linear search, the bracket is aligned to the B-spline reconstruction (Sigg & Hadwiger 2005, *Fast third-order texture filtering*, GPU Gems 2 ch. 20: four bilinear taps) and bisected four times on it; the hit's height and normal come from the B-spline too (the normal from its central differences a texel apart). The bilinear field kinked every texel along a rut at a shallow angle to the grid (`r6/rut_wall.png`); the B-spline does not (`r7/rut_wall.png`).
4. **The normal is the B-spline height's central difference at the hit, not a stored per-texel gradient.** Central differences, then a Sobel gradient, both published per texel, fluted the glossy walls of wet mud ruts running diagonally to the grid with a stripe per texel (`r8/mud_zoom.png`, `r9/mud_zoom.png`). The published `.zw` (Sobel) remains for custom shaders.
5. **The self-shadow's penumbra is `smoothstep(0, 1, Δh / (0.01 + 0.25 u))`, sampled one texel of ground apart toward the light at a spacing the same for every pixel (at most 32 samples over a 1.5 m reach).** The ratio `Δh / (0.005 + 0.1 u)` over 12 samples switched on and off at the field's texel-scale kinks: dark blocks and light bands across sand ruts (`r6/sand_cmp.png`). Spacing the samples by each pixel's own reach moved all of them wherever the count changed by one, and broke the shadow's edge into regular dark ticks along a rut's far wall (`r14/ticks.png`; gone with the shadow off, `r14/ticks_ns.png`, and with the fixed spacing, `r15/ticks.png`).
6. **A trench through grass or soil shows the soil, and water stands only on a rut's floor.** The layer blend kept grass on a trench floor; and the "ruts in saturated ground hold water" term set a flat, mirror-smooth water normal on the walls too, so mud ruts read as white ribbons (`r7/sheet.png`). Water is now weighted by `1 − smoothstep(0.3, 0.8, |∇h|)`. [INFERENCE: both thresholds.]
7. **An imprint's first frame carves nothing, and a jump of more than 1 m in a frame starts a new path.** A newly spawned imprint's `GlobalTransform` is the origin until propagation runs after the simulation, so it carved at the origin and swept a trench from there to its real place (the first GPU test measured 168 carved texels for a 10 × 26 cm sole).
8. **Compacted snow on a trench floor is `(0.86, 0.91, 0.97)` × the snow's albedo and 0.75 × its roughness** ([INFERENCE]; Barré-Brisebois's sky-tinted transition, and the bluer, glazed look of pressed snow).
9. **Validation 6 (sand slumps to its angle) is visual, not a GPU test.** A test needs sediment, which the crate generates only through the procedural presets (the flat terrain has none); the `sand` captures (`r7/sheet.png`) show the dry-sand ruts slumped to shallow, rounded V's.
10. **Slumping moves material toward all eight neighbours** (the diagonals against `√2 · tan θ` over their longer run, at half the share), not Sumner's four. With four, mud rut walls (67° against mud's 63°) slumped in grid-aligned steps on a rut diagonal to the grid.

No source's number is changed: Wong's constants, CRREL's 500 kg/m³, Alvarado's `L0` and Poisson ratios, Sumner's slump angles and the crate's 34° are used as published.

## Addendum: a press threshold (#39)

**Sources.** None new. The Bekker–Wong sinkage and CRREL snow cap above are unchanged; the addendum only adds a floor on the depth the kernel will press.

**Model.** `TrailSettings::min_depth_m` (m, default 0). In `trail_stamp`, an imprint whose target depth `want = sinkage(g, load, width)` on the texel's own ground is below the threshold writes neither carve nor rim for that texel. Snow ground (`is_snow`, depth above `SNOW_GROUND_M`, 1 cm) is exempt, so fresh snow always presses (the issue's edge case: the crate's own line). The test is per texel on the ground the kernel already resolves, so a footprint straddling a wet patch and dry soil is cut at the ground's own boundary, not by a lookup the game made earlier. `0` is exactly the old behaviour (`want < 0` is never true).

**Parameters.** `min_depth_m` 0 (off). The dry-soil step presses 0.48 cm, 0.5 wetness about 4 cm, dry sand 3.4 cm, so 0.01 m stops dry soil and keeps sand, wet soil and snow (Validation 1 above).

**GPU mapping.** One new `vec4<f32>` (`press`, x: the threshold) at the end of `TrailParams`; one early `return` in `trail_stamp` after `want`. No new buffer, binding or pass.

**Validation targets.** CPU: the gate `presses(ground, min_depth, load, width)` is false for dry soil at 0.01 m and true for wet soil, sand and any snow, and true for every ground at 0. GPU: on game ground at wetness 0 with `min_depth_m` 0.01, the trail field's largest |h| after a 760 N foot is 0; at wetness 0.5 at least 0.01 m; at 0 the field is unchanged.

**Not done (deliberate).** `Environment::sinkage_at` (the issue's second shape) and `paths_resist_prints`: the issue asks for either shape and lists the route rule as related; the threshold alone removes the duplicated physics for the reported case. The route rule is left on the backlog.
