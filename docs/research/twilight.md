# Twilight and the night sky behind the atmosphere

Scope: what the sky looks like from the moment the sun's disc touches the horizon until astronomical night, and the black background the atmosphere is drawn over. Three defects found in iteration 12 by capture and by measuring Bevy's sky on a linear target (`/tmp/i12`):

1. **The night sky was brown at 40 DN.** Bevy's default `ClearColor` is sRGB (43, 44, 47), linear (0.024, 0.025, 0.028). It is written to the HDR target before the sky and is never multiplied by the exposure, and Bevy's atmosphere draws over it with its transmittance, so every pixel of sky carried a constant linear (0.022, 0.0205, 0.0186): the grey seen through the atmosphere's zenith transmittance (0.93, 0.84, 0.66), a warm brown. Measured at the zenith on a linear `Rgba16Float` target with all lights off (sun −20°, moon below the horizon, four dates): exactly that value every time. By day it is 0.1 % of the sky; at night, under an exposure ~2²⁵ higher, it is the whole sky.
2. **Twilight did not exist.** The sun's `DirectionalLight` was switched off below −2° (`SUN_OFF_DEG`, carried from M1 without a source), and Bevy's atmosphere is lit only by directional lights. From −2° to −18° (about 75 minutes of every summer evening at latitude 45°) the sky had no light at all; what showed was item 1.
3. **The low sun blew the frame out.** The exposure meters horizontal illuminance (`celestial.md` Deviations 11 and 15), which falls ~7 stops from noon to sunset while the sky toward the sun falls far less; at a sun of 1.8° the sky reads 252 DN and the ground is overexposed. With the twilight sky lit (item 2) the same metering turns the whole frame white toward the sun from +12° to −6°.

## What great looks like

- **Sunset and the golden hour:** the sky toward the sun reads as a bright warm gradient, yellow-orange at the horizon to pale blue overhead; only the immediate surroundings of the disc clip. The ground is darker than the sky and warmly lit; objects between the camera and the sun read as silhouettes with their sky-lit sides still visible. Nothing in the frame is a flat white field.
- **Civil twilight (0° to −6°):** toward the sun an orange-to-yellow band low on the horizon under a pale blue-violet sky (Haber's purple light near −3°); away from the sun the blue Earth's shadow rising with the pink Belt of Venus above it; the zenith blue throughout (Hulburt). The ground is dim and lit by the sky only: no hard shadows.
- **Nautical twilight (−6° to −12°):** the afterglow shrinks to a narrow orange-red band on the sun's side; the rest of the sky deep blue, darkening to navy; the brightest stars appear.
- **Night:** behind the atmosphere only the night sky's faint glow; a moonless sky is near-black with stars and the horizon's silhouettes still readable against it; a moonlit sky is a dim blue. Nothing brown.
- **Continuity:** a time-lapse from +10° to −18° changes smoothly: no step when the sun's disc sets, no step when its light goes off.
- **Reference techniques:** Hillaire's LUT model lit by a sun below the horizon (Bevy's atmosphere already does this when it is given the light); exposure that meters illuminance and caps on the brightest sky (Patry 2021).

## Model adopted

1. **Behind the atmosphere is the night sky's own glow, not Bevy's grey.** `dress_camera` takes over the clear colour of an `ElementsCamera` whose clear colour is still `ClearColorConfig::Default` (marker `SkyBackground`); a camera that set its own keeps it. Each frame `sky_background` clears it to `L_night · exposure` with `L_night = E_floor / π = 1.59×10⁻⁴ cd/m²`, the radiance of a uniform sky that gives the metered illuminance floor `E_floor = 0.0005 lx` (`celestial.md` §Auto exposure). The clear colour is written before the sky and never exposed, so it is given in display units, the same treatment `DistanceFog::color` needs (`AGENTS.md`); Bevy's atmosphere draws over it with its transmittance, as airglow and zodiacal light are seen through the air. By day it is ~10⁻⁸ of the sky; on a moonless night it reads ~11 DN (sRGB), so silhouettes stay readable against the sky instead of vanishing into black. [INFERENCE: Krisciunas & Schaefer 1991's dark-sky zenith brightness, 79 nL ≈ 2.5×10⁻⁴ cd/m², recalled, not re-read, is of the same order.]
2. **The sun lights the atmosphere through twilight.** The sun's `DirectionalLight::illuminance` is `E_sun · g(h) · sun_cloud_factor` (Model 3) with `h` the sun's true altitude and

   $$ g(h) = \begin{cases} 1 & h \ge 0 \\ e^{k h} & -6° \le h < 0 \\ e^{-6k + k_{tail}(h+6)} & -18° < h < -6° \\ 0 & h \le -18° \end{cases} \qquad k = 0.44,\ k_{tail} = 0.983\ \text{per degree} $$

   Bevy already removes the direct light from every surface once the disc is below the horizon (Sources), so `g` only changes the sky, the aerial perspective and the atmosphere's environment light. Shadow maps are off below −0.5° (the disc has set), which also saves the sun's cascades all night. `g` is a calibration of Bevy's renderer, not physics: lit at full strength below the horizon, Bevy's zenith falls only ~13× from sunset to −5.5° (Hulburt: ~220×) and keeps a floor of ~4.1 cd/m² per full sun, constant within 3 % from −9.3° to −18.4°, from its multiple-scattering LUT. `k` was fitted on the measured zenith relative to the sun's −0.49° row: a first fit of 0.56 against an extrapolated sunset value fell 2.8× too fast by −5.5°; with 0.44 the zenith follows Hulburt's 0.427 decades per degree at 0.94×, 1.01× and 0.65× at −2.2°, −3.9° and −5.5° (Validation 1). Below the knee at −6° the zenith Bevy draws is that floor, which only falls if the light does: there `g` continues at `k_tail = ln 10 · 0.427 = 0.983` per degree (Hulburt's slope, which his observations keep beyond 6.5° though primary scattering no longer explains them). With the single slope 0.44 the dusk time-lapse lifted the floor into an olive sky from −13° and then snapped to black at −18°; with the tail the sun's share at −18° is 4.1 · g = 2×10⁻⁶ cd/m², 1.5 % of the night background, and the fade is monotonic.
3. **Once its disc has set, the sun lights only the sky, and under cloud it carries the dome's total.** By day the sun's `DirectionalLight` carries the cloud beam `T̄` at the camera and the cloud composite restores the sky dome with `sky_gain = total / T̄` (`celestial.md` Deviation 18). Below the horizon that rule broke in the dusk time-lapse: the cloud shadow map, cast along a light below the horizon, reads `T̄ ≈ 0`, so `sky_gain` reached its 250× bound and multiplied everything behind the sky pixels (the night background, the stars) into an olive glow that snapped back to black when the light went off at −18°. The sun's cloud factor is now `sun_cloud_factor = total + (T̄ − total) · v` with `v = sun_disc_visible(h)` (Bevy's visible-disc rule over the disc's 0.27° radius): the beam while the disc is up, the dome's total once it has set, so `sky_gain` is exactly 1 below the horizon and the twilight sky under cloud is dimmed as the day dome is. Consumers that light without Bevy's transmittance take the sun only while its disc is up: the precipitation draw (`draw.wesl:198, 206, 250`) multiplies the sun's illuminance by `v`, so rain is not lit from below the horizon. The cloud march has its own light choice (`pick_light`) and is unchanged; the cloud-scattered ambient (`cloud_ambient`) counts the sun while its light is on, so under cloud the twilight now lights the ground.
4. **The exposure is capped by the sky toward the sun.** The auto exposure is `max(EV_illuminance, EV_glow)` with

   $$ EV_{glow} = \log_2\frac{L_{glow}(h)\,\beta(c)}{1.2\,D},\qquad D = 3.2 $$

   `L_glow(h)` the cloud-free luminance of the sky 5° above the horizon toward the sun, measured on Bevy's atmosphere with the scaled sun (table under Parameters, log-linear between rows), `β(c) = 1 − c^{3.4}` the cover's mean beam (`beam_transmittance`, Kasten & Czeplak), and `D` the display value (1 = display peak before tonemapping) the glow may reach. `D = 3.2` is the glow's display value at a sun of 27° under the illuminance metering (14 320 cd/m² × 2.21×10⁻⁴), so the afternoon is metered exactly as before and only the low sun is capped (Patry's threshold rule). The glow is light scattered along tens of kilometres of air toward the sun, so it is dimmed by the cover's mean beam, not by the cloud shadow over the camera: a low sun's slant ray almost always finds a cloud, and the first prototype, dimmed by `T̄`, left the 1.8° frame white.

   Two consequences, measured and kept. The cap does not depend on where the camera looks: a camera facing away from a low sun is exposed for the glow behind it, up to ~2.5 stops darker than the illuminance alone (`docs/dawn.png`, sun 3°, reads as a dim blue sky over a dark, warmly lit ground); a view-dependent meter (the brightest sky in the frame, Patry's screen highlights) is the follow-up. And the illuminance target uses the cloud shadow at the camera while the glow uses the cover's mean beam, so a camera standing in a cloud's shadow under a sunlit sky no longer opens up past it (`weather_tour` PartlyCloudy at noon 0.4 stop darker, the sky a richer blue; Overcast and Clear at noon unchanged within 0.002 MAE).

## GPU mapping

No new pass, buffer or binding. The sun's light already exists and Bevy's atmosphere already loops over both lights every frame, so keeping it on through twilight costs nothing in the sky passes; switching its shadow maps off below −0.5° removes its cascades during twilight and night. CPU: one exponential, one 17-row table walk and one clear-colour write per camera per frame.

## Validation targets

1. **Zenith fall (GPU, linear target, Clear, no cloud):** the zenith luminance divided by its value at the sun's −0.49° row follows Hulburt's 10^{−0.427·Δh} within a factor 2 at −2.2°, −3.9° and −5.5° (`tests/gpu_twilight.rs`).
2. **Zenith colour:** blue leads red and green from −2° to −6° (blue to blue-purple; Hulburt, Haber's purple light).
3. **The night background:** with both lights off (sun below −18°, moon down) the zenith reads under 10⁻⁵ linear at Bevy's default exposure, against the old 0.02; a camera spawned with its own clear colour keeps it.
4. **Noon and afternoon untouched:** the glow target does not exceed the illuminance target by more than 0.1 EV at a sun of 27° and above; it exceeds it from 8° down to −4° and by more than a stop at −0.5°; under overcast it never binds (`celestial` unit test).
5. **Glow ceiling:** under the automatic exposure the sun-side glow's display value is ≤ 3.2 + 10 % at suns of 2.2°, −0.5° and −2.2° (`tests/gpu_twilight.rs`).
6. **No surface lit from below, no amplified night:** the sun's visible-disc factor is 0 below −0.27° and 1 above +0.27°, and the precipitation draw multiplies its sun term by it; below the horizon the sun's cloud factor is the dome's total and `sky_gain` is 1 under any cloud (`celestial` unit tests).
7. **Continuity:** `g` falls monotonically from 1 at the horizon, and at −17.99° the sun's share of Bevy's sky floor is under 2 % of the night background (`celestial` unit test); the `day_night` time-lapse from 19:24 to 22:36 on 2026-06-15 (no moon) fades monotonically, with no step at the disc's setting or at −18°.
8. **Visual (capture, judged per `AGENTS.md`):** `weather_tour` Clear toward the sun (heading 290°, pitch 12°) and away (110°) at 17.0, 18.0, 18.6, 19.0, 19.3, 19.6, 19.9, 20.3 and 21.2 h on 2026-04-20; PartlyCloudy, Overcast and Fog at 19.6 h; `terrain_tour` hills and mountains at 21.6 h; `day_night` at 04:18; `weather_tour` Clear at 23 h. Each against `What great looks like`: no white field toward the sun, a blue zenith in twilight, the Belt of Venus away from the sun, no brown night sky, silhouettes against a moonless sky.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **The sun's light stays on to −18°, scaled by `g(h)` (supersedes `SUN_OFF_DEG = −2°` from M1, which had no source).** Hulburt 1953 and Haber 2005 for the twilight sky existing at all; `k = 0.44/°` is fitted on Bevy's renderer to Hulburt's measured zenith, and `k_tail = 0.983/°` below −6° is Hulburt's own slope (Model 2). Below the horizon the light's cloud factor is the dome's total instead of the beam (Model 3; amends `celestial.md` Deviation 18).
2. **An `ElementsCamera` that kept Bevy's default clear colour is cleared to the night sky's background radiance at its exposure.** Jensen 2001 for what lies behind the air; the measurement in item 1 of the scope for why Bevy's grey cannot stay.
3. **The auto exposure is capped by the sun-side glow (amends `celestial.md` Deviations 11 and 15).** Patry 2021: luminance used only when the highlight exceeds a threshold. `celestial.md` Validation 25's numbers are the illuminance target `ev100_for`, which is unchanged; what the camera receives below a sun of ~25° is now the larger of that and `EV_glow`.
