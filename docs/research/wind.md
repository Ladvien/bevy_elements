## Model adopted

**Log-law forcing profile** (neutral surface layer; WMO reference exposure):

$$ u(z) = u_{10}\,\frac{\ln\!\big(\max(z_{agl}, z_0)/z_0\big)}{\ln(10/z_0)},\qquad z_0 = 0.03\ \mathrm{m},\ u_{10}\ [\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}],\ z_{agl}\ [\mathrm m] $$

At $z_{agl} = 10$ m the numerator and denominator are identical, so $u(10\,\mathrm m) = u_{10}$ exactly. Other values (computed): $u(1\,\mathrm m)/u_{10} = 0.604$, $u(2) = 0.723$, $u(5) = 0.881$, $u(20) = 1.119$, $u(50) = 1.277$. $\ln(10/0.03) = 5.8091$. The profile depends only on height, so it has zero divergence on flat ground ($\partial u/\partial x = 0$).

**One simulation step** (Stam 1999 structure; no viscosity/diffusion step: air viscosity is negligible at 4 m cells):

1. *Forcing:* $\mathbf u \leftarrow \mathbf u + (1 - e^{-r\,\Delta t})\,(\mathbf u_{target} - \mathbf u)$ with relaxation rate $r = 0.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $\mathbf u_{target} = \hat{\mathbf d}\,u(z) + \mathbf u_{curl} + \mathbf u_{gust}$ ($\hat{\mathbf d}$ = prevailing wind direction, unit XZ vector, blows toward).
2. *Curl noise:* $\mathbf u_{curl} = 0.35\,g\,u_{10}\ \nabla\times\boldsymbol\psi(\mathbf x/L, t)$, $g$ = `gustiness` ∈ [0, 1], $\boldsymbol\psi$ a vector of three Perlin-noise components (Bridson 2007 §2.2); the curl is taken with central differences of displacement 1e-3 of the noise cell (Bridson used 1e-4 of the domain in single precision; a larger step is chosen for f32 stability on the GPU, [INFERENCE]), normalised so its rms is 1 (the curl of unit-amplitude noise has speeds $O(1/L)$). The potential is multiplied by $A(z_{agl})$ rising smoothly from 0 at the ground to 1 above 4 m (Bridson §2.3–2.4: modulate the potential so the result stays divergence-free; tangential ramp near solids).
3. *Semi-Lagrangian advection:* $\mathbf u^{n+1}(\mathbf x) = \mathbf u^n(\mathbf x - \Delta t\,\mathbf u^n(\mathbf x))$ with trilinear interpolation (Stam 1999 method of characteristics; unconditionally stable).
4. *Solid mask* $m(\mathbf x) \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 where the cell centre is below terrain height; velocity in solid cells is 0 and the Poisson stencil treats them as Neumann walls. In M1 the ground is the plane $y = 0$.
5. *Divergence* (central differences, cell size $h = 4$ m): $\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u = \frac{u_{x+} - u_{x-} + v_{y+} - v_{y-} + w_{z+} - w_{z-}}{2h}$.
6. *Jacobi pressure solve* of $\nabla^2 p = \nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u$ (Stam Eq. 4, Neumann boundary): $p^{k+1}_{ijk} = \tfrac16\big(\sum_{6\text{ nbrs}} p^k - h^2\,\mathrm{div}_{ijk}\big)$, neighbours in solid cells replaced by the centre value; 10/20/30/40 iterations by quality; pressure warm-started from the previous frame.
7. *Projection:* $\mathbf u \leftarrow \mathbf u - \nabla p$ (central differences, same $h$).
8. *Publish:* $(\mathbf u_x, \mathbf u_y, \mathbf u_z, I)$ with $I$ = local turbulence intensity $= |\mathbf u_{curl}|/\max(|\mathbf u|, 0.1)$ is written to the `rgba16float` 3D texture.

**Gusts and gust factor.** Gust events are a Poisson process of rate $0.1\,g\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$. A gust is a travelling band that adds a peak speed $0.5\,g\,u_{10}$ along $\hat{\mathbf d}$ inside a 30 m wide band moving at $1.2\,u_{10}$. Over open land the peak 3-s gust relative to the 10-min mean is the *gust factor* $G = 1 + g_p I_u$ with turbulence intensity $I_u \approx 0.17$ at 10 m for $z_0 = 0.03$ m (Eurocode form, $= 1/\ln(10/z_0)$) and peak factor $g_p \approx 3.5$–4. The plan's maximum combined fluctuation is $u_{curl}$ rms-scale $0.35\,g$ plus gust-band peak $0.5\,g$, i.e. an effective gust factor $G = 1 + 0.5\,g$ from the band alone ($G$ = 1.10 at $g$ = 0.2, 1.15 at 0.3, 1.25 at 0.5, 1.45 at 0.9), rising to $1 + 0.85\,g$ when a band coincides with the curl peak (1.77 at $g$ = 0.9). WMO/TD-1555 Table 1.1 gives 1.38–1.66 for 3-s gusts in 600 s over open land in tropical-cyclone conditions; the plan's range 1.10–1.45 (band alone) is below that, which is intended: TC turbulence exceeds fair-weather turbulence, and only Thunderstorm/Blizzard (g = 0.9) reaches the lower edge of the table. `WindState.gust_factor` is defined here as $G = 1 + 0.5\,g$ [INFERENCE: the plan names the field but not its formula]. The curl-noise amplitude $0.35\,g\,u_{10}$ corresponds to $I_u = 0.35\,g$: 0.175 at $g = 0.5$, matching the Eurocode $I_u = 0.172$ at 10 m for $z_0 = 0.03$ m, and 0.32 at $g = 0.9$ (inside WMO Table 4.1's 0.25 in-land strong-wind value plus margin).

**Toroidal domain.** Cell $(i,j,k)$ stores world cell $(\lfloor x/h\rfloor,\lfloor y/h\rfloor,\lfloor z/h\rfloor)$ with $i = \lfloor x/h\rfloor \bmod N_x$ etc. The domain re-centres only in whole cells; newly exposed slabs are initialised to the forcing profile. Because addressing is toroidal, the published texture is sampled in world space as $uvw = \mathrm{world}/\mathrm{extent}$ with `ImageAddressMode::Repeat`, so no origin subtraction is needed; `WindFieldParams` still carries `origin` and `extent` so a consumer can fall back to the prevailing wind outside $[\mathrm{origin}, \mathrm{origin} + \mathrm{extent})$. This mapping is not spelled out in the plan; it is the only layout that avoids a per-frame un-wrapping copy.

**Expected Jacobi convergence.** For the 7-point Laplacian with Neumann boundaries on an $N_x\times N_y\times N_z$ grid the Jacobi iteration matrix has eigenvalues $\lambda_{pqr} = \tfrac13(\cos\tfrac{\pi p}{N_x} + \cos\tfrac{\pi q}{N_y} + \cos\tfrac{\pi r}{N_z})$. After $n$ iterations the residual of mode $pqr$ is multiplied by $\lambda_{pqr}^n$. Computed for the plan's quality settings:

| grid | iterations | smoothest-mode factor $\lambda^n$ | rms factor over all modes (cold start, white divergence spectrum) | rms factor over modes with $\lvert\lambda\rvert<0.9$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32×8×32 (Low) | 10 | 0.984 | 0.088 | 0.042 |
| 48×12×48 (Medium) | 20 | 0.986 | 0.053 | 0.010 |
| 64×16×64 (High) | 30 | 0.988 | 0.039 | 0.0028 |
| 96×24×96 (Ultra) | 40 | 0.993 | 0.031 | 0.00083 |

So 30 Jacobi iterations on 64×16×64 reduce a *white* residual to ≈ 3.9 % rms (residual of the smoothest mode, $p=1$ along one horizontal axis, only to 98.8 %; the checkerboard mode has $\lambda = -1$ and is not damped at all), which is why the pressure is warm-started and why the forcing is built to be nearly divergence-free (log profile: exactly 0; curl noise: 0 up to finite-difference error; only advection interpolation and the gust band inject divergence). A smooth, near-divergence-free field converges across frames because the solve resumes from the previous pressure. The `gpu_wind` divergence bound (< 1e-2 s⁻¹) is expected to hold when the pre-projection mean $|\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u|$ is below ≈ 0.25 s⁻¹ ($1\times10^{-2}/0.039$); this is [INFERENCE] and the test decides it.

**Curl-noise spectrum (Kim et al. 2008; refines step 2).** The plan's single curl-noise scale gives eddies of one size only. Kim et al. build turbulence as $\mathbf y(\mathbf x) = \sum_{i=i_{min}}^{i_{max}} \mathbf w(2^i\mathbf x)\,2^{-\frac56 (i - i_{min})}$, where $\mathbf w$ is the curl of a band-limited noise taken in noise-tile coordinates (so every band has the same, $O(1)$, amplitude); their per-band energy law $e_t(2k) = e_t(k)\,2^{-5/3}$ is Kolmogorov's $-5/3$ spectrum, and $2^{-5/6} = 0.5612$ is the per-octave amplitude (Perlin's heuristic is $\tfrac12$). Adopted for `u_curl`: two octaves, $L_0 = 32$ m and $L_1 = 16$ m (8 and 4 cells), each octave's curl normalised to unit rms *before* weighting, then weights $(1,\ 0.5612)/\sqrt{1 + 0.5612^2} = (0.8721,\ 0.4895)$ so that the total rms is still 1 and the plan's $0.35\,g\,u_{10}$ amplitude is unchanged. A third octave (8 m) is not used: it would be 2 cells per wavelength, the grid's Nyquist, which trilinear advection damps, and Perlin noise is only weakly band-pass (Lagae §6.2) so frequency clamping (§5.2.1) must be conservative; the 4-cells-per-wavelength floor is a design choice [INFERENCE]. The 3-octave weights would be $(0.8409, 0.4719, 0.2649)$ for reference. Kim's energy-weighted injection and advected eddy coordinates are not adopted (Sources). Cost: 2 octaves × 6 partial derivatives × 2 central-difference samples = 24 Perlin evaluations per cell per frame; 65 536 cells at High is 1.6 M evaluations, small beside the Jacobi passes.

**Boundary conditions (Harris §38.3.3 "Boundary Conditions", §38.2.4).** Harris solves a closed box with ghost cells: pressure Neumann (ghost = value just inside, scale +1) and no-slip velocity (ghost = −1 × value just inside, so the *average* of the two cells is zero at the wall). The toroidal addressing above is storage only; the logical domain is a box that scrolls, so a stencil at a logical edge must not read the wrapped cell from the opposite face (the plan and this note's step list are silent on this). Edge rules, mapped from Harris: (a) pressure is Neumann at every logical edge and at solids (ghost = centre value; this is the plan's step 6 for solids); (b) at the ground (M1: the plane $y = 0$; M3: the terrain mask) the *normal* velocity component uses Harris's mirror (−1) so $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf n = 0$ at the face — the plan's "solid-cell velocity = 0" enforces it only on average — and the tangential component is left free (free-slip), because full no-slip would drive the first cell toward 0 while the log-law forcing target there is $u(2\,\mathrm m) = 0.72\,u_{10}$ [INFERENCE]; (c) the lateral edges are open: Harris's −1 no-slip would make them walls and block the mean wind, so their ghost velocity is a copy of the cell just inside (zero gradient) and the forcing relaxation (step 1) supplies the inflow [INFERENCE]; (d) the top is a lid (zero normal velocity, Neumann pressure) [INFERENCE]. A pure-Neumann Poisson problem is solvable only when net flux in equals net flux out; uniform mean wind satisfies this, and the Jacobi iteration tolerates the small mismatch from gusts because the pressure level is free.

**Jacobi counts against Harris.** Harris runs "usually 20 to 50" iterations (§38.3.3) from a zero initial pressure each step. The plan's 20 / 30 / 40 (Medium / High / Ultra) sit inside that range; Low (10) is below it and relies on the warm start (the forcing changes the field by only $1 - e^{-r\Delta t}$ per frame). Harris reports no divergence residual, so the `gpu_wind` divergence bound has no corpus source and rests on the eigenvalue analysis above. Numbers unchanged.

**Operator consistency (derived, [INFERENCE]; Harris Table 38-1 uses the same pair).** The plan's divergence and gradient are central differences ($2h$ stencil) while the Jacobi solve inverts the compact 7-point Laplacian. The composite $\nabla\!\cdot\nabla_c$ is the *wide* Laplacian $(p_{i+2} - 2p_i + p_{i-2})/(4h^2)$, so even with an exactly solved Poisson equation the central-difference divergence is not removed. For a plane wave of wavenumber $k$: compact eigenvalue $-2(1-\cos kh)/h^2$, wide eigenvalue $-\sin^2(kh)/h^2$, so the divergence left after projection is $\sin^2(kh/2)$ of the divergence before it. For wavelength $\lambda$ in cells: 64 cells 0.0024, 32 cells 0.0096, 16 cells 0.038, 8 cells (32 m, the curl-noise $L_0$) 0.146, 4 cells (16 m, $L_1$) 0.50, 2 cells 1.0 (the checkerboard mode, invisible to central differences). Harris accepts this; the plan keeps it. Consequence for tests: residual divergence must be judged on smooth fields (λ ≥ 16 cells) or measured with the same central operator; the curl forcing's own divergence is already ≈ 0, so the factor matters only for divergence created by advection, which is smooth. A staggered (MAC) grid (Harris §38.5.3, Fedkiw et al.) would remove the mismatch but changes the storage layout and the publish step; not adopted.

**Advection and vorticity (Harris Listing 38-1, §38.5.1).** Harris backtraces with a hand-written bilinear fetch because floating-point textures were not filterable, and clamps back-traces to the edge (`CLAMP_TO_EDGE`, with the boundary conditions updating the edge texels so that this "operates correctly"); this crate needs the same hand-written fetch for a different reason (state lives in storage buffers, which have no hardware filtering): 8 taps per cell in 3D, with back-traces clamped to the logical domain edges (not wrapped, per the boundary rule above). Semi-Lagrangian interpolation damps vorticity; Harris restores it with $\mathbf f_{vc} = \varepsilon(\Psi\times\omega)\,\delta x$. Not adopted: the 0.5 s⁻¹ relaxation to a curl-noise target re-injects small-scale vorticity every frame. Adopt it only if the bench shows the published turbulence-intensity channel decaying toward zero under advection; it would add a curl-and-force kernel.


**Implementation (measured, 2026-10-13).** Curl noise is computed from *analytic* Perlin derivatives (`perlin3_d` in `src/noise.wesl`, Quílez's closed form for quintic gradient noise), not central differences: with a 10⁻³-cell step, f32 coordinates beyond ~10³ lattice units leave an ulp larger than the step, so finite-difference curls degrade with distance from the world origin. The f64 transliteration matches finite differences to 1.3×10⁻⁹ and its mixed partials agree to 10⁻¹⁰ (so the curl is exactly divergence-free in exact arithmetic). The noise lattice is periodic with 4096 cells, so the drifting offset wraps seamlessly at 4096 coarse cells (131 km at L = 32 m). Each potential and octave uses an integer lattice offset (`seed_offset`), i.e. a separate hash seed (Lagae §6.2). Measured unit-rms constant of `curl3`: rms 1.4104 over 10⁵ points → `CURL_NORMALISE = 0.709`, asserted by `the_kernel_normalisation_constants_match_the_noise` against the CPU mirror `src/gpu/noise_mirror.rs`. GPU result (`tests/gpu_wind.rs`, RTX 3090, High, u₁₀ = 10 m/s, gustiness 0.3, 120 frames): mean |u| at 10 m 9.95 m/s, mean central-difference |∇·u| 8.2×10⁻⁴ s⁻¹.

## GPU mapping

Domain: camera-centred, whole-cell scrolling, toroidal addressing (above). One compute pass per kernel per frame, workgroup `@workgroup_size(4, 4, 4)` (64 invocations; limit 256). Dispatch counts: High 64×16×64 cells → 16×4×16 = 1024 workgroups per kernel; Ultra 96×24×96 → 24×6×24 = 3456.

State buffers: two ping-pong `Handle<ShaderBuffer>` of `array<vec4<f32>>` (velocity xyz, w = turbulence), `read`/`read_write` via `var<storage, ...>`; one `array<f32>` divergence; two `array<f32>` pressure (ping-pong); one `array<u32>` solid mask. Sizes at High: vec4 buffer 65 536 × 16 B = 1 MiB; at Ultra 221 184 × 16 B = 3.4 MiB; f32 buffers 0.25 / 0.84 MiB. All created with `RenderAssetUsages::RENDER_WORLD`, usage `STORAGE | COPY_SRC | COPY_DST` (the `ShaderBuffer` default).

Per-kernel binding count (storage buffers / storage textures / uniforms), all ≤ 8 buffers and ≤ 4 storage textures per stage (WebGPU defaults, `docs/research/bevy-0.20-render.md` limits table: 8 and 4):

| kernel | storage buffers | storage textures | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| forcing (+ curl noise + gust bands) | 2 (vel in, vel out) | 0 | uniform: params; gust bands (≤ 16) in the params uniform |
| advect | 2 (vel in, vel out) + 1 (mask) = 3 | 0 | |
| solid mask | 2 (terrain height in, mask out); 1 in M1 (flat ground: writes mask only) | 0 | M3 binds the terrain height buffer |
| divergence | 3 (vel in, mask in, div out) | 0 | |
| Jacobi (×n) | 4 (p in, p out, div in, mask in) | 0 | ping-pong swaps bind groups per iteration |
| project | 3 (vel in/out, pressure in) + 1 (mask) = 4 | 0 | |
| publish | 1 (vel in) | 1 (`rgba16float` write-only 3D) | |

**As implemented:** all wind kernels share one bind-group layout (uniform, velocity in/out, solid mask, divergence, pressure in/out, the published texture) so four pre-built bind groups cover every ping-pong combination in one compute pass: 6 storage buffers and 1 storage texture per stage, inside WebGPU's defaults (8 and 4) but above the `downlevel` 4 storage buffers, which this crate does not support. The per-kernel counts below are what each kernel touches. Maximum: 4 storage buffers in one stage, 1 storage texture in one stage. No `read_write` storage texture is used (the publish texture is write-only; consumers sample it as a filterable 3D texture). If a device reports the `downlevel` limit of 4 storage buffers the Jacobi and project kernels still fit exactly (4); the plan's splitting rule is not needed.

Readback: the plan's `EnvironmentSnapshot` copies the velocity buffer (High: 1 MiB at 4 Hz); Ultra's 3.4 MiB is also below the 1024² downsample threshold. Frame cost at High is the plan's wind + fog compute ≤ 0.3 ms (Verification M1); 30 Jacobi dispatches of 65 536 cells are small, but each is a separate dispatch with a barrier, so the bench must measure it.

**Noise functions in `noise.wesl` (Lagae et al. 2010) as used by wind.** Perlin (lattice gradient) noise is the right primitive for the per-frame forcing kernel: it is the fastest of the surveyed functions (§7: sparse-convolution, wavelet and better-gradient noise are slower), needs no stored tile, and Bridson builds curl noise directly on it. Known artefacts and what to do about them: (1) it is only weakly band-pass and its amplitude distribution is not Gaussian (§6.2), so the unit-rms normalisation in step 2 must be *measured* per octave (sample 10⁵ points once, store the constant), not assumed from a Gaussian; (2) the hash correlation that produces axis-aligned streaks (§6.2; Kensler et al. "better gradient noise", §3.1.2) matters most for a curl, which differentiates the potential: use an independent hash per potential component rather than only a large spatial offset of one noise (Bridson's trick), plus Kensler's separate gradient table if the hash is cheap [INFERENCE: Lagae reports the artefact, not this remedy]. That the noise is zero at every lattice point affects the potential's values, not its derivatives, so the curl needs no special handling. Worley (cellular, §3.4.2) is not band-pass and is not used by wind or fog; it belongs to the cloud shaping noise in M2.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Jacobi iterations at Medium/High lowered to 10/12 (from 20/30); Low and Ultra unchanged (10, 40).** The plan's performance contingency applies: at High the wind pass measured 0.50 ms GPU at full clock (RTX 3090, 1800 MHz, 36 dispatches) against the plan's 0.3 ms wind + fog budget, and each Jacobi dispatch costs ≈ 14 µs whatever the grid. The listed first remedy is fewer iterations; the warm start makes them nearly free in accuracy: on the High grid, `tests/gpu_wind.rs` measured mean |∇·u| 8.2×10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ with 30 iterations and 8.4×10⁻⁴ with 10 (flat ground, u₁₀ = 10 m/s). The mask kernel was also folded into forcing and the fresh-cell pressure reset into divergence (34 → 17 dispatches at High). Terrain (M3) adds solid boundaries and must re-check the divergence bound before keeping these counts.
2. **Open window edges are Dirichlet for the pressure (Neumann only on solid walls and the lid), and the window follows the ground (M3, `terrain.md` Deviations 14 and 15).** The re-check Deviation 1 asked for: over `hills(42)` with Neumann pressure on every edge the mean |∇·u| 12 m above the ground was 2.8×10⁻² s⁻¹ at 12 and at 30 iterations alike, because a terrain that blocks more of the window on one side than the other leaves the open edges' net flux unbalanced (no solution of the pure-Neumann Poisson problem); with Dirichlet edges 5.3×10⁻⁴ (4 iterations), 1.3×10⁻⁴ (12), 1.3×10⁻⁴ (30), the counts applied one after the other to one running field (300 frames each); fresh runs per count gave 3.2×10⁻⁴, 2.3×10⁻⁴ and 1.3×10⁻⁴. The iteration counts of Deviation 1 stand. Flat ground, `tests/gpu_wind.rs`: 9.6×10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ at 12 iterations (8.4×10⁻⁴ before).

Otherwise no plan number changed.
