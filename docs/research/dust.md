## Model adopted

**One idea.** Wind picks up the loose sand layer where the surface wind exceeds a threshold and carries it downwind; it settles where the wind can no longer carry it, which is the lee of every rise. Sand mass moves as a flux field $q$ [m² of bulk sand per second, i.e. m³ per metre of crosswind width per second]; the bed changes by the flux divergence (Exner); steep slopes relax by avalanching at 34°. This is Paris et al.'s layered model reduced to one loose layer (plan 4.3) but with their stochastic slabs replaced by the continuum flux of the dune literature (Kok et al. §3.2.1), which is deterministic, conserves mass exactly and needs only gathers.

**Surface wind** (shared WGSL function with snow drift, `snow-cover.md`). Reference wind $u_{10}$ = `Weather.wind_speed_ms` along the unit XZ direction $\hat u$ (the wind blows toward it, `AGENTS.md`), uniform over the terrain: the terrain-scale wind is not taken from the 256 m camera wind field. Per cell:

$$u_s = u_{10}\,s_v\,\sigma,\quad s_v = \mathrm{clamp}\big(1 + k_W\,(z - \bar z_R),\ 0.5,\ 2\big),\quad \sigma = \mathrm{clamp}\Big(\frac{15° - \beta}{15° - 10°},\ 0,\ 1\Big)$$

with $z$ the surface (bedrock + sediment), $\bar z_R$ the mean of 8 surface taps on a ring of radius $R_v$ = 50 m, and $\beta = \max_d \arctan\big((z_q - z)/d\big)$ over upwind taps $q = p - d\,\hat u$, $d \in \{\Delta, 2\Delta, \dots, R_S\}$, $R_S$ = 10 m (5 taps at 2 m cells). The shadow rule is Paris §4.3 (the sign convention of their $\tan\alpha$ is ambiguous; shadow where the upwind surface rises above the 10°–15° line is the physical reading, as the lee of a 34° slip face lies in separated flow [INFERENCE]); the Venturi term uses their $k_W = 5\cdot10^{-3}$ m⁻¹ on the height above the local mean instead of on absolute elevation, because absolute elevation would speed the wind by 5× on a 900 m mountain [INFERENCE: Paris use $T(p)$ directly; the ring mean stands in for their 50 m Gaussian].

**Capacity.** $q_s = k\,u_s^2\,(u_s - u_t)_+$ with $u_t$ = 8 m/s and $k$ = `flux_coefficient` 2·10⁻⁶ m²/s per (m/s)³: the plan's $q = 2\cdot10^{-6}(u-8)\,u^2$. Units fixed (below): bulk-volume flux per unit width. $q_s = 0$ at $u_s \le 8$. This is the Fryberger–Dean drift-potential form $U^2(U - U_t)$ and, since $u_* \propto U$, the Lettau & Lettau shape (Kok §3.1.5).

**Is 8 m/s right, and is 2·10⁻⁶ right?** Threshold: Bagnold's fluid threshold for 250 µm quartz is $u_{*ft} = 0.10\sqrt{(2650 - 1.2)/1.2 \times 9.81 \times 2.5\cdot10^{-4}} = 0.233$ m/s (impact threshold 0.191), and with $u(z) = (u_*/\kappa)\ln(z/z_0)$, $\kappa$ = 0.4, $z_0 = k_s/30$ the 10 m wind at threshold is 8.14 m/s for $k_s = D$, 7.74 for $2D$, 7.21 for $5D$; the impact threshold gives 6.68, 6.35, 5.91 m/s against Fryberger & Dean's 6.17 m/s. 125 µm sand: 6.04 m/s; 400 µm: 9.95 m/s (all computed). So 8 m/s is the fluid threshold of ordinary 250 µm sand; once saltation runs it persists down to the impact threshold (about 6.2–6.7 m/s). The plan's 8 m/s stays (a single number, no hysteresis).

Magnitude: converting the physical mass-flux laws to bulk volume flux with a loose-sand bulk density of 1 600 kg/m³ [INFERENCE: loose dry sand 1 500–1 700, not read] and using $u_*(u_{10})$ with $k_s = D$ (Bagnold, Kawamura, Lettau, Durán–Kok at 250 µm; computed):

| $u_{10}$ | Bagnold | Kawamura | Lettau | Durán–Kok | plan $2\cdot10^{-6}u^2(u-8)$ | plan ÷ physical |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 m/s | 3.2·10⁻⁶ | 4.6·10⁻⁶ | 4.0·10⁻⁶ | 3.3·10⁻⁶ | 4.0·10⁻⁴ | 87–125 |
| 12 | 5.6·10⁻⁶ | 9.2·10⁻⁶ | 9.2·10⁻⁶ | 5.9·10⁻⁶ | 1.15·10⁻³ | 125–205 |
| 16 | 1.3·10⁻⁵ | 2.4·10⁻⁵ | 2.9·10⁻⁵ | 1.3·10⁻⁵ | 4.1·10⁻³ | 140–320 |
| 20 | 2.6·10⁻⁵ | 4.7·10⁻⁵ | 6.4·10⁻⁵ | 2.1·10⁻⁵ | 9.6·10⁻³ | 150–460 |

(all m²/s). The coefficient that fits the physical laws in the plan's form $k\,u^2(u - 8)$ is $k$ = 0.6–2.3·10⁻⁸ depending on the law and speed (1.6–2.3 at 10 m/s, 0.6–1.4 at 16 m/s); the central value is 1.2·10⁻⁸ m²/s per (m/s)³. Real barchans, whose migration is 30–100 m/yr (Kok §3.1.6.1), imply for a 4 m dune a crest flux $Q_0 = vH$ = 3.8·10⁻⁶ to 1.3·10⁻⁵ m²/s, which is what the physical laws give at 10–16 m/s. **The plan's coefficient is 167× the physical value** ($2\cdot10^{-6}/1.2\cdot10^{-8}$) when $q$ is a bulk-volume flux, and 3–18× *below* it if the plan meant a mass flux in kg/(m s) (4·10⁻⁴ kg/m/s at 10 m/s against 5–7·10⁻³ physical, 4.1·10⁻³ against 2–5·10⁻² at 16 m/s, 9.6·10⁻³ against 3–10·10⁻² at 20 m/s). Neither reading is literally the physical law. The only reading under which the plan's number is useful is the first: it is a morphological acceleration of the physical flux, which is also what Paris et al. do (10 days of landscape time per iteration, and 3 000–9 500 simulated years shown for a 36 s run, §5.1, Table 1 and Fig. 10: a compression of order 10⁹–10¹⁰). The plan keeps the value; this note fixes the units and states what it is (Deviation 1).

**Time.** The flux relaxes and moves in real time; the bed is advanced with $\Delta t_b = \Delta t_{real} \times$ `WorldClock::time_scale`, so dune evolution follows game time like snow (the plan is silent). At time scale 1 the plan's coefficient is a 167× speed-up of reality; at the example's time scale 600 the total speed-up is $167 \times 600 = 1.0\times10^5$. (Coastal morphodynamics calls a multiplier on bed change a morphological acceleration factor; the literature is not in the library [INFERENCE].) Keeping the load transient out of the bed update is deliberate: a transported-load variable whose bed exchange is multiplied by the speed-up $F$ would turn $F\,q_s/v$ metres of bed into load at gust onset, 2.5 m of sand per cell at $F$ = 10⁵ and a saltation speed $v$ of 1 m/s [INFERENCE for $v$], which never existed; a flux formulation moves exactly the divergence and nothing else.

**Flux relaxation along the wind** (superseded in its discretisation by Deviation 9: the law below is solved by a scan per line, not by one Jacobi sweep per frame; Hersen et al. 2002 form of Sauermann et al., Kok Eq. 3.7): $dq/ds = (q_s - q)/L_{sat}$ with $L_{sat}$ = `saturation_length_m` = 10 m (Kok: "~1 to ~10–20 meters"; Paris's shadow march radius is also 10 m; the minimum dune width $\approx 12\,L_{sat}$ = 120 m is below the 300 m feature size of `ProceduralTerrain::dunes`). Discretised upwind, one sweep per frame on a persistent field $q$ (Jacobi with ping-pong, information travels one cell per sweep, converged in about $3L_{sat}/\Delta \approx$ 15 frames, small against the minutes over which wind changes in game time): $q_i^{new} = q_{up} + (q_{s,i} - q_{up})(1 - e^{-\Delta/L_{sat}})$ with $q_{up} = a_x q_{i-s_x} + a_z q_{j-s_z}$, $a_x = |\hat u_x|/(|\hat u_x| + |\hat u_z|)$, $a_z$ likewise, $s$ the signs of $\hat u$. Supply limit: a cell cannot export more than it holds plus receives, $q_i \le q_{up} + h_i\,\Delta/(\Delta t_b\,(|\hat u_x| + |\hat u_z|))$ with $h_i$ the sediment thickness. On bare bedrock $h = 0$, so $q_i = q_{up}$: the flux passes over rock and neither picks up nor drops anything unless the capacity falls (Paris's abrasion is not modelled).

**Bed update** (kernel `aeolian_move`): donor-cell face fluxes $F = q\,\hat u$, $\Delta h_i = -\Delta t_b\,\big[(|F_{x,i}| - |F_{x,i-s_x}|) + (|F_{z,i}| - |F_{z,i-s_z}|)\big]/\Delta$. Each face flux is subtracted from one cell and added to its neighbour, so total sediment is conserved exactly in a closed domain (floating-point sums aside). Deposition is wherever the capacity falls downwind: the shadow zone behind a rise ($\sigma = 0$ gives $q_s$ = 0, the load deposits over about $L_{sat}$), the slackening wind downwind of a crest, and the slip face. Erosion is on the stoss side and the crest where speed-up raises the capacity. For a barchan-like pile on bedrock this moves sand from stoss to lee: migration. Boundary: `HydrologySettings::boundary` (Open: flux leaves the domain; Closed: no flux through the edge; a toroidal domain as in Paris and Werner would need a tileable bedrock).

**Avalanching** (hydrology's `thermal_flux` and `thermal_apply` kernels, `erosion.md`, dispatched by dust 2 times per step, outside the hydrology substep loop). The plan says "avalanching uses thermal erosion at the sand repose angle of 34°", so the kernels are reused, with a dust uniform: talus 34° (Kok §3.1.1; Werner's rule "if the slope becomes larger than 34°"; Paris use 30° for bare sand and interpolate to 45° with vegetation, so the plan's 34° is kept) and the bedrock branch switched off, so only loose sediment moves (hydrology's own call also moves bedrock above talus + 10°). The rule is `erosion.md`'s: for each of the eight neighbours the excess over the talus is $e_k = \max(0, h_k - d_k\tan\theta_t)$, the cell moves $D = \tfrac12 r_t E$ with $E = \max_k e_k$ and $r_t$ = 0.15 per pass, limited by its sediment, shared among the lower neighbours in proportion to $e_k$; every transfer is added once and subtracted once, so sediment is conserved (hydrology measured a relative drift of −2.3·10⁻⁷ over 1 000 passes). Steady lag: one step deposits at most $q_s\Delta t_b/\Delta$ = 4.1·10⁻³ m²/s × 10 s / 2 m = 2 cm per cell at 16 m/s and time scale 600; two passes at $0.075E$ per pass hold the steady excess near $E \approx 2\ \mathrm{cm}/(2 \times 0.075)$ = 0.13 m, which on a 2 m cell is a slope of $\arctan((2\tan34° + 0.13)/2)$ = 36.5°, 2.5° over the repose angle (bound in Validation 4). Hydrology's pass keeps its own talus (35°) and thresholds; the two share kernels and scratch buffers, not parameters. The kernels need one uniform field for the bedrock threshold (off), flagged to the hydrology note.

**Wetness and rain.** Wet sand does not move: Fécan's correction raises the fluid threshold by $1 + 1.21\,w^{0.68}$ (w in % volumetric, clay-free sand $w'$ = 0), 2.2× at 1 % moisture. No mapping from `wetness` to volumetric moisture exists and the plan has none, so the hook is recorded and not built (Limitation 1). Rain does stop a dust storm through the weather scheduler (DustStorm and Rain are not neighbours in the preset graph), not through the aeolian model.

**Gusts.** The wind speed is a mean; the flux is convex in it. With Gaussian turbulence of intensity $I_u = 0.35\,g$ (`wind.md`, $g$ = 0.8 in the DustStorm preset, so $I_u$ = 0.28) the mean of $u^2(u - 8)_+$ over the distribution is 1.39× its value at the mean wind at 16 m/s (1.22× at 12 m/s with $g$ = 0.5, 1.47× at 10 m/s with $I_u$ = 0.2; computed). Not applied: the plan uses the mean wind, and a gust factor would be a second calibration of a coefficient that is already an acceleration. Recorded so nobody reads the example's migration rate as a physical prediction.

**Particles** (`kind = 2`, M2 buffer; plan 4.3 and `precipitation.md`). While `Weather.dust` > 0 the particle set gains dust sprites: count fraction $f_{dust} = 0.4\cdot\min(1, \text{dust}/0.9)$ so the DustStorm preset (dust 0.9) is exactly the plan's 40 % of the maximum and a blend into or out of it ramps smoothly instead of switching on at the first non-zero dust (the plan's "while dust > 0" is a step; `precipitation.md` already treats the 0.4 as the value at the preset). It joins the existing normalisation $f_{rain} + f_{snow} + f_{dust} \le 1$. Each sprite is tan (0.76, 0.60, 0.42), the dust albedo of `fog.md` and the plan, 2 mm across, advected by the wind field velocity $\mathbf u(\mathbf p)$ with a fall speed of 0.05 m/s (Stokes settling of 10–20 µm dust is 8·10⁻³–3·10⁻² m/s, computed; a 2 mm quartz grain would fall at 7–10 m/s and could not be carried by a 16 m/s wind, so the 2 mm sprite is a visual stand-in for a dust puff, not a grain [INFERENCE for the 0.05]). Heights follow the haze: respawn height above ground $y = -H\ln\big(1 - u\,(1 - e^{-60/H})\big)$ with $H$ = 60 m, the ground-fog scale height of `fog.md`, so the sprites thicken toward the ground like the haze they sit in. Lighting is the particle single-scattering model with albedo $\omega$ = (0.76, 0.60, 0.42) instead of 1 (`precipitation.md` "Lighting"). The sprites carry almost none of the dust's optical depth: 60 000 sprites (0.4 × 150 000, High) in the 60 m box are 0.278 m⁻³ with cross-section $\pi(10^{-3})^2$ = 3.1·10⁻⁶ m², an extinction of 8.7·10⁻⁷ m⁻¹ against the 4.89·10⁻³ m⁻¹ of the preset's 800 m visibility (0.018 %). They are texture, not mass.

**Fog consistency** (`fog.md`, plan 1.4 "from 1.4"). Nothing is added to the density: the DustStorm preset's `visibility_m` = 800 m is already the dust's extinction, $\sigma_V = 3.912/800 = 4.89\cdot10^{-3}$ m⁻¹ (optical depth 3.91 over 800 m, 2 % contrast), and the dust term of the volume is the albedo/colour change of `fog.md` (ω 0.95 → 0.9, colour lerped to (0.76, 0.60, 0.42) by `Weather.dust`), height scale 60 m. 800 m lies inside the "dust storm" visibility class of Kok §5.1 (200 m–1 km) and above the "severe" class (< 200 m); the plan's Blizzard (200 m) is at the severe boundary. So the fog already encodes the right strength and M4 changes only the particles and the ground. `Weather.dust` does not drive transport: the wind does, in any weather.

**Limitations.** (1) No soil moisture, vegetation, crusting or abrasion (Paris's yardangs). (2) One loose layer. (3) One uniform wind direction, no multi-scale deflection (Paris's warping with deviation coefficients 30 and 5). (4) No suspension or dust emission: dust is a visual scalar. (5) Two thermal passes per step leave the lee 2.5° over the repose angle in steady state at the example's rate. (6) Reptation is not separate (it is folded into the saturation length).

## GPU mapping

Kernels (WESL, 8×8×1, `queue_kernel`/`ready`, `src/gpu/kernel.rs:30,59`), run outside hydrology's substep loop, before `terrain_pack`, only when $u_{10} \times 2 > u_t$ (the speed-up clamp) so calm weather costs nothing:

| kernel | storage buffers | other | writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `aeolian_capacity` | `bedrock` ro, `sediment` ro, `aeolian_cap` rw | uniform `AeolianParams` (û, $u_{10}$, $k$, $u_t$, $k_W$, Δ, settings) | `aeolian_cap` |
| `aeolian_flux` (a scan per row and per column, Deviation 9) | `aeolian_cap` ro, `sediment` ro, `aeolian_q_x` rw, `aeolian_q_z` rw | uniform | `aeolian_q_x`, `aeolian_q_z` |
| `aeolian_move` | `aeolian_q_x` ro, `aeolian_q_z` ro, `sediment` rw | uniform | `sediment` |
| hydrology `thermal_flux`, `thermal_apply`, ×2 (talus 34°, bedrock off) | `bedrock`, `sediment`, `tflux_a`, `tflux_b` (hydrology's buffers; `thermal_flux` reads the first two and writes the last two, `thermal_apply` the reverse, `erosion.md`) | uniform: talus, bedrock threshold off | `sediment` |

At most 4 storage buffers per kernel, no storage textures, 64 invocations per workgroup. `aeolian_move` reads only neighbours' face fluxes and its own `sediment`, so the in-place update is race-free; the thermal pair is a gather with own-cell writes (`erosion.md`). Memory at 1024² (computed): `aeolian_cap` 4 MiB and the two face fluxes 8 MiB = 12 MiB, plus hydrology's existing `tflux_a`/`tflux_b` (32 MiB, shared); 2048² is four times that. `sediment` stays the hydrology layer.

Per-pass cost at 1024², RTX 3090 (936 GB/s, 50 % achievable) [INFERENCE for the plan's version; measured numbers are under "Measured" in Validation targets]: `aeolian_capacity` 13 cached surface taps per cell and 4 B written, about 0.15 ms; `aeolian_flux` 0.06 ms; `aeolian_move` 0.05 ms; the thermal pair moves 88 B/cell (`erosion.md`: 40 B + 48 B), 0.19 ms per pass, 0.38 ms for two. About 0.65 ms per step while winds exceed the threshold; at the 20 Hz kernel cap the mean is about 0.22 ms per 60 Hz frame, in addition to the snow chain (`snow-cover.md`). The plan has no M4 budget; target ≤ 0.3 ms mean per frame at High for aeolian.

Particles: no new pipeline. The count fraction, tan colour and the exponential respawn height are three changes in `elements_precip_sim` and the draw shader (`precipitation.md`); the particle sim reads the `WindField` texture and the kind-2 branch has no blocker or splash handling.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **The flux coefficient is a morphological acceleration, and its units are fixed.** Plan: "q = 2e−6·(u − 8)·u² m²/s". Kept numerically, with the units stated as bulk-volume flux per unit width, and recorded as 167× the physical flux (Bagnold, Kawamura, Lettau & Lettau, Durán–Kok in Kok et al. 2012 Table 2.1: 1.2·10⁻⁸, range 0.6–2.3·10⁻⁸) with the bed advanced on game time (`time_scale`) so the example reaches a visible migration at time scale 600. Sources: Kok §2.3.2.3 and §3.1.6.1 (barchans 30–100 m/yr ⇒ $Q_0$ 3.8·10⁻⁶–1.3·10⁻⁵ m²/s for a 4 m dune); Paris §5.1 (10 days per iteration). Reason: with the plan's number read as a real-time flux, a 4 m dune would move 0.06 m in the 60 s the example allows between shots.
2. **Continuum flux with a saturation length, not Paris's stochastic slabs.** Plan: "Paris 2019's layered model, simplified to one loose layer". Adopted: capacity × relaxation along the wind (Kok Eq. 3.7, Hersen et al. 2002), `saturation_length_m` = 10 m (new setting), gather-only donor-cell transport. Reason: random slab moves need atomic float adds (not available on WebGPU storage buffers) and are not conservative per frame; the continuum model is deterministic, conserves mass and reproduces barchans and dune migration (Kok §3.2.1.4, Fig. 3.6).
3. **Shadow and Venturi terms from Paris §4.2–4.3, unspecified in the plan.** Without a capacity drop behind obstacles a uniform flux field would not move sand at all (zero divergence). The shadow rule (15°, 10 m) is Paris's; the Venturi term is applied to height above the ring mean rather than absolute elevation [INFERENCE].
4. **Avalanching reuses hydrology's thermal kernels with a dust uniform, 2 passes per step.** Plan: "avalanching uses thermal erosion at the sand repose angle of 34°", which this follows. Agreed with the hydrology note: the thermal uniform is `{talus_deg, bedrock_extra_deg, rate}`; dust passes `talus_deg` = 34, `bedrock_extra_deg` = 90 (the bedrock excess is then 0 in the shader, so no code path and nothing leaves bedrock) and `rate` = 0.15; dispatch order `thermal_flux` then `thermal_apply` on `bedrock`, `sediment`, `tflux_a`, `tflux_b`, 8×8 workgroups, rule D = ½·r_t·E; moved material always lands in `sediment`; dust dispatches run outside the hydrology substep loop and hydrology keeps talus 35° for its own calls. No number changes. (The sentence in Model adopted asking for a bedrock-threshold field is therefore resolved by `bedrock_extra_deg`.)
5. **Dust particle fraction ramps with `Weather.dust`.** Plan: "while `Weather.dust` > 0 … 40 % of the rain max". Adopted: $0.4\cdot\min(1, \text{dust}/0.9)$. Reason: avoids a pop when the dust blends in; the DustStorm preset value (0.9) reproduces the plan's 40 % exactly. Clarification of the plan's own step, no number changed.
6. **Dust sprites follow the haze's exponential height profile and fall slowly.** The plan says "wind-driven"; `fog.md` fixes the haze scale height at 60 m; Stokes settling for real dust is millimetres per second. New numbers: $H$ = 60 m (from `fog.md`), fall speed 0.05 m/s [INFERENCE].
7. **Aeolian boundary follows `HydrologySettings::boundary`.** Not in the plan; avoids a second boundary concept per terrain.

8. **The bed advances with `WorldClock::time_scale` even when the clock is paused.** The note tied the bed to "game time like snow". A paused `WorldClock` freezes the sky (the examples pause it to hold an hour for screenshots) and would have frozen the sand; the bed step is `Time<Virtual>` delta × `time_scale`, so pausing `Time<Virtual>` stops both. Clarification, no number changes.
9. **The flux is solved by a sequential scan along each line of cells, not by a persistent Jacobi field.** Plan/note: "one sweep per frame on a persistent field q (ping-pong), converged in about 15 frames, supply limit $q_i \le q_{up} + h_i\Delta/(\Delta t_b\,\cdot)$". Found at implementation (CPU mirror, `the_cpu_scheme_conserves_sand_and_moves_the_pile_downwind`): with a one-cell-per-sweep field the supply limit of a cell uses the *previous* sweep's arrival while the bed update uses the *new* one, so a cell could lose more than it holds and the clamp at zero created sand (7.0 % of the pile's volume in 200 steps; a look-ahead floor on the upstream flux reduced it to 2.6 %, not to zero, because the floor itself depends on the supply limit upstream). Adopted: `aeolian_flux` scans every row (along x) and every column (along z) once from the upwind edge, one thread per line, with the flux through each cell's downwind face $F = \min(F_{in} + (q_s a - F_{in})(1 - e^{-\Delta/(L_{sat}\,a)}),\ F_{in} + h\,\Delta/\Delta t_b \cdot s)$, $a$ the wind's share along the axis ($|\hat u_x|$ or $|\hat u_z|$) and $s = a/(|\hat u_x| + |\hat u_z|)$ the share of the cell's sand that axis may take (so the two axes together cannot take more than the cell holds). The arrival is exact, so the thickness cannot go negative and every face flux is subtracted once and added once: conserved to 2·10⁻⁸ (relative, GPU, 200 steps) with the closed boundary. The flux is the quasi-steady response of the relaxation law to the capacity along the line (no state between steps: there is no `aeolian_q` ping-pong, the two buffers are the x and z face fluxes), which is what "converged" meant. The relaxation distance along an axis is $L_{sat}\,a$ (the path along the wind per cell is $\Delta/a$); for a wind along an axis it is the note's $L_{sat}$, for a diagonal one the transport is split between the two axes (dimension splitting, the usual price of a scan on a grid). Reason: exact conservation and positivity, no frames of build-up, and the GPU matches the CPU mirror to 2.4·10⁻⁷ m. Cost: one thread per line scanning 512–2 048 cells; measured below.
10. **The calm test is `2 u_{10} > u_t`** (the speed-up clamp's 2×), as the note's GPU mapping says; the plan's "only when the wind can exceed the threshold" read that way. At 7.9 m/s on flat sand the step runs and changes nothing, bit for bit.
11. **Bedrock is pinned with a tangent of 10²⁰, not `talus + 90°`.** `dust.md` Deviation 4 said `bedrock_extra_deg = 90`; `tan(124°)` is negative and would have made the bedrock excess larger. `HydroParams::avalanche` sets the bedrock tangent to `UNREACHABLE_TAN` when `talus + extra ≥ 89.9°`. The two passes are 2 per step, so the 50° cone relaxes to 36.8° steepest slope in 2 s of real time (target ≤ 37° + 3°).
12. **A lone Gaussian pile or dune does not migrate under this model; sand migrates as a stripped front.** Found by the GPU tests (Validation 6 as first written failed at 11–45× the Bagnold speed): the shadow rule is evaluated on the current surface, so sand deposited in the lee raises the surface until it lies under the 12.5°–15° line from the brink and the shadow vanishes (σ = 1 beyond 10 m regardless); the lee is then a 12.5°–15° ramp, there is no 34° slip face to advance, and the flux that leaves it is carried to the next obstacle or the edge (a 3 m dune loses $e^{-R_S/L_{sat}}$ = 37 % of its crest flux every step at $R_S = L_{sat}$ = 10 m). A smooth pile has no shadow at all (5° slopes) and is simply stripped. What the model does reproduce is the plan's mass balance: the sand upwind of a stripped front is carried downwind, the front advances at $c = Q_{eff}/H$ ($H$ the thickness of the sheet it strips), and the sand piles in the shadows of rises and at the closed edge. Validation 6 and 7 are therefore measured on the slab and on the sheet's front (below). Paris et al. (2019) deposit a slab with probability 0.4–0.6 *outside* shadow too (their Eq. for β), which the capacity law has no equivalent of; adding it would be a second calibration. Not adopted, recorded as the open item for a dune-forming model.
13. **`ProceduralTerrain::dunes` already existed** (terrain.md, re-blessed in its Deviation 11: feature 300 m, relief 40 m, ridged 0, 4 m of sand, 7 octaves); its statistics test is `the_presets_have_the_measured_statistics` and is unchanged. The dunes' sand is a uniform 4 m sheet (terrain.md Deviation 16), so the note's Validation 7 "H = 95th minus 5th percentile of the sediment in the first shot" is 0 there: $H$ is the sheet's depth, and the migrating feature is its front.

14. **The respawn height profile is truncated to the part of the box above the ground.** The note's $y = -H\ln(1 - u(1 - e^{-60/H}))$ assumes 60 m of box above the ground, but the box is centred on the camera (30 m above a camera at the ground). The sampler is `lo − H ln(1 − u (1 − e^{−(hi−lo)/H}))` over `[lo, hi]` = the box's span above the ground there; with `lo = 0`, `hi = 60` it is the note's formula (the CPU test uses that case). A sprite carried out of the top of the box respawns instead of wrapping to the bottom, where it would sit under the ground for a frame.

Kept at the plan's values although the sources differ or are silent (no deviation): threshold 8 m/s (the fluid threshold for 250 µm sand; the impact threshold and Fryberger & Dean are 6.2–6.7); repose 34° (Kok and Werner; Paris 30°); particle size 2 mm and fraction 0.4 (visual choices); no extinction added to the fog (`fog.md`).
