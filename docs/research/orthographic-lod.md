# Terrain level of detail under an orthographic camera, and the vertices on the height samples

Issue #36 (Sanctuary, an isometric town on the crate's owned `Terrain` under a fixed orthographic camera 457 m (1500 ft) back from its focus, pitched 35.26°, showing 9–29 m of ground top to bottom). Its figures stand on its own height function, the heights it bakes into the crate's height map, and the owner's playtest saw their feet sink into the drawn ground ("Is it meant to be mud?"). The CDLOD patches are selected by distance from the camera's eye and morphed by `view.lod_view_world_position`, the eye too; under an orthographic camera the eye is 457 m from everything on screen, so the town is drawn in level-1 patches partly morphed to level 2, and level 0 (range `r_0 = 2 S_0`, 183 m on Sanctuary's 2.86 m cells) is never selected anywhere on screen.

## Sources

- **Issue #36** (`https://github.com/Ladvien/bevy_elements/issues/36`, read 2026-10-25): the requirement, the proposal (select and morph by the focus under an orthographic camera; the centre a uniform in `TerrainMaterialParams` instead of `view.lod_view_world_position`; a perspective camera unchanged), the edge cases (the pool capacity for a view wider than `r_0`, no pop as the focus walks, the prepass and shadow views on the same centre, `Environment::terrain_height` answering the bilinear) and the four acceptance criteria quoted under Validation targets. Its table (Sanctuary's map, 512 cells over 1463 m, sampled every 0.5 m over the town): drawn minus the game's height, p90 0.17 m for "the map's bilinear (what level 0 draws)", 0.29 m for level 1 unmorphed, 0.65 m for level 2.
- **Strugar 2009**, *Continuous distance-dependent level of detail for rendering heightmaps* (JGT 14(4), corpus `10.1080_2151237x.2009.10129287`): "every texel in the source heightmap data is represented with one vertex in the regular grid" is the full-detail case the LOD approximates (l. 56); an unpredictable terrain LOD "causes rendering errors such as unwanted intersection or floating of objects placed on the terrain" (l. 70); for a shadow map "the actual LOD selection should still be based on the main camera settings … to avoid rendering artifacts that would be caused by difference in the terrain mesh resulting from two different LOD functions" (l. 219); the distance that drives the morph may use an approximate vertex position "as long as the approximating function is consistent over the whole dataset domain" (l. 261). The crack-free bound of the ranges is `terrain.md` Deviation 12.
- **Asirvatham & Hoppe 2005**, *Terrain rendering using GPU-based geometry clipmaps*, GPU Gems 2 ch. 2 (corpus `10.1145_1015706.1015799_gpugcm`): nested grids "centered about the viewer", whose "aim is to maintain triangles that are uniformly sized in screen space" (l. 16); the transition blend is computed "based on the (x, y) location relative to the continuous viewer position" (l. 163). The centre of a terrain LOD is what the view's detail must be greatest around, a free choice of the scheme.
- **Luebke et al. 2003**, *Level of Detail for 3D Graphics*, §3.3 "Screen-Space Error" (corpus `book_level_of_detail_3d_graphics_luebke`, ll. 1898-1932), Eq. 3.1: an object-space error `ε` at distance `d` is `p = ε x / (2 d tan(θ/2))` pixels for a view `x` pixels across with field of view `θ`. The factor `2 d tan(θ/2)` is the width the view spans at that distance; an orthographic view spans its fixed size `H` at every distance, so there `p = ε x / H` everywhere: the screen-space error of a level does not fall with distance.
- **Lindstrom et al. 1996**, *Real-time, continuous level of detail rendering of height fields* (SIGGRAPH, corpus `10.1145_237170.237217`): the screen-space-error-driven terrain LOD with `λ` "the number of pixels per world coordinate unit in the screen x-y coordinate system" (l. 234); the vertex error is the vertical deviation of the coarser triangle from the finer ones, `δ_B = |2B − (A + C)|` (l. 103).
- **Akenine-Möller et al. 2018**, *Real-Time Rendering* 4th ed., §4.7.1 (corpus `book_real_time_rendering_4th_akenine`, l. 3540): under an orthographic projection "objects maintain the same size regardless of distance to the camera".
- **Epic Games**, *Landscape Technical Guide* (`https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/landscape-technical-guide-in-unreal-engine`, read 2026-10-25): a landscape is a grid of vertices, one per heightmap sample; a landscape of `A × B` components of 63 quads is `(63A + 1) × (63B + 1)` vertices, the shared edge row duplicated in each component.
- **Unity**, *Terrain Settings reference* (`https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/terrain-OtherSettings.html`) and `TerrainData.heightmapResolution` (`https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/script-reference/unityengine/terraindata/heightmapresolution`, read 2026-10-25): the heightmap resolution is a power of two plus one (513 = 512 + 1), the samples being the mesh's vertices.
- This crate: `docs/research/view-anchor.md` (the `ElementsFocus`, `ElementsView { position, eye }`, the orthographic test), `docs/research/terrain.md` ("CDLOD selection", Deviation 12), `src/grid.wesl` and `TerrainGrid::sample` (cell `(i, j)`'s sample sits at `origin + (i + ½, j + ½) c`; the bilinear clamps at the edge cells), `src/terrain/mod.rs` (`patch_transform`: a patch's corner at `ix · S`, the grid's corner), `src/terrain/render.wesl` and `src/hydrology/water.wesl` (`terrain_vertex_position`, `water_vertex_position`), `src/rocks/model.rs` (`lattice_height`: the scattered rocks seat on the patch mesh's own triangles), Bevy 0.20 `bevy_render/src/view/mod.rs:1244-1276` (`lod_view_world_position` is a camera view's own translation; a directional shadow view's is its camera's, through the `RenderShadowLodOrigin` resource `PbrPlugin` inserts; a point or spot light's is the resolved `ShadowLodOrigin`). Under a perspective camera every view of the terrain therefore already morphs about the same eye; under an orthographic one about the same far-off eye.

## Model adopted

1. **The centre.** Under a perspective followed camera the patches are selected about the eye and morphed about `view.lod_view_world_position`, as before (Strugar's 3D distance from the observer: constant triangle density on screen). Under an orthographic followed camera a level's screen-space error is the same at every distance (Luebke Eq. 3.1 with `2 d tan(θ/2)` replaced by the view's fixed height), so distance from the eye selects nothing useful; the detail must be greatest where the game's attention is, as a clipmap centres on its viewer (Asirvatham & Hoppe). The centre there is the camera's `ElementsFocus` (`ElementsView::position`); without a focus it is where the camera's forward ray first meets the terrain (marched over the height readback at half a cell between the terrain's lowest and highest heights, refined by bisection); a view whose ray meets no terrain, or before the first readback, keeps the eye.
2. **One centre for the selection and every view's morph.** `update_patches` selects about the centre, and both owned terrain materials carry it (`TerrainMaterialParams::lod_centre`, `WaterParams::lod_centre`: xyz the centre in the metre frame, w 1 when the vertex stage morphs about it, 0 about `view.lod_view_world_position`), so the main pass, the depth prepass and every shadow view of the terrain displace the same vertices (Strugar l. 219). The scattered rocks, seated on the surface the patches draw, morph their seat about the same centre.
3. **The vertices sit on the height samples.** Before this note, a patch's grid started at the grid's corner, half a cell off the samples: a level-0 vertex was the bilinear at a cell corner, the mean of four samples, and level 0 drew a triangulation of 2 × 2 means, not the map (measured below: p90 0.15 m off the bilinear on the hills at 4 m cells, against 0.34 m for level 1). Unreal and Unity put one vertex on every height sample; Strugar's full-detail case is "every texel … one vertex". The patches are now placed half a cell in from the grid's corner (`patch_transform`), so level `k`'s vertices sit on every `2^k`-th cell centre, level 0's on every one, and level 0 draws the samples themselves. A vertex past the last centre is clamped onto it (the row of quads beyond it collapses); the drawn terrain spans the cell centres, half a cell inside the grid's extent on every side, where the bilinear was the edge cell's flat clamp. The node boxes the selection measures move with the vertices, so every vertex of a node lies in its box and the crack-free bound is unchanged.
4. **What level 0 draws.** At a vertex the drawn height is the sample, the bilinear exactly. Inside a quad the two triangles (split along the `(1, 0)–(0, 1)` diagonal, `patch_mesh`) and the bilinear agree on the edges and differ most at the centre, by a quarter of the quad's twist: for samples `h00, h10, h01, h11` the bilinear there is `(h00 + h10 + h01 + h11)/4` and the triangles' `(h10 + h01)/2`, a difference of `(h00 + h11 − h10 − h01)/4`. That bound is the least any triangle mesh on the samples can reach; `Environment::terrain_height` keeps answering the bilinear.
5. **The ranges are unchanged.** `LodConfig::derive` is a function of the node sizes and heights alone and its bound `m_k ≥ r_{k−1} + diag_k` holds about any centre. With the centre on the ground the ground near it is within `r_0` (`r_0 = 64` cells: 183 m on Sanctuary's cells, 256 m at `High` on 4 m cells), so a view up to `r_0` across is level 0 everywhere. The pool's capacity is the same as for a perspective eye at that point (the capacity test already samples eyes 2 m above the ground).
6. **No opt-in.** The old orthographic selection drew no level 0 on screen and a coarser level the nearer the camera stood back; nothing depends on it. A perspective camera's selection about its eye and its morph are unchanged; only the half-cell shift of point 3 moves its node boxes, so a patch on a range's boundary can change level, as it would for an eye half a cell away.

## Parameters

| Name | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Centre under perspective | the eye (`ElementsView::eye`), the morph `view.lod_view_world_position` | unchanged; Strugar |
| Centre under orthographic | `ElementsView::position` when the camera has a focus, else the forward ray's first hit on the readback, else the eye | issue #36 |
| Ray march | half a cell, between the terrain's min and max heights (the bounds tree's root), 16 bisections | the readback's own resolution |
| Vertex lattice offset | `c/2` in x and z, every level | Unreal, Unity, Strugar l. 56 |
| `lod_centre.w` | 1 orthographic, 0 perspective | — |

## GPU mapping

- `TerrainMaterialParams` and `WaterParams` gain `lod_centre: Vec4`, read only by the vertex stage: `let centre = select(view.lod_view_world_position / units, lod_centre.xyz, lod_centre.w > 0.5)`. A moving focus rewrites the uniform every frame (one re-prepared bind group per material, as the rain's ripple phases already do); a perspective camera writes it once.
- The patch's translation is its corner plus `c/2` (`patch_transform`); the vertex stage clamps a vertex to the last sample, `min(xz, origin + (size − ½) c)`, one `min` per vertex. The fragment stage is untouched.
- CPU: `TerrainRuntime::lod_centre` (`Some` under an orthographic followed camera), set in `ElementsSystems::Simulation` before the materials are written and read by `update_patches` in `PostUpdate`, so the selection and the uniform agree within the frame; `node_box` and the `vertex_position` mirror take the offset and the clamp; the rocks' `PatchLevels` take the vertex lattice's origin and the centre.

## What great looks like

An isometric orthographic view of the hills, 18 m of ground on screen: posts standing on `Environment::terrain_height` every 2 m all stand on the drawn ground, their 10 cm red feet showing whole, none swallowed by a swelling of the mesh and none on stilts, on the slopes and across the crests as on the flats. Walking the focus at a walking pace, the ground under the posts does not breathe or pop and the feet stay put; the dunes and the mountains' scree the same. A wide orthographic view (400 m) shows level 0 around the focus and coarser levels morphing smoothly away from it, no crack (no red under the terrain). Every perspective view looks exactly as before but for sub-pixel silhouette shifts.

## Validation targets

1. **Issue acceptance 1 (CPU, `lod.rs`).** An orthographic `ElementsCamera` 457 m back from its `ElementsFocus` at 35.26°: every patch within 30 m of the focus is level 0; its vertices there are unmorphed and equal the height function at the samples to 1e-3 m; and between the vertices the drawn surface (the patch mesh's triangles) is within `|twist|/4` + 1e-4 m of the bilinear.
2. **Issue acceptance 2.** Under a perspective camera the selection is about the eye, focus or not, and the vertex stage morphs about `view.lod_view_world_position` (`lod_centre.w = 0`); the drawn surface and the selection for the same eye are those of a centre at the eye.
3. **Issue acceptance 3.** Walking the focus 100 m in 1 m steps (orthographic centre), no vertex of a swapped patch jumps more than the morph of one step (the existing no-pop bound), and no edge cracks (the existing crack test, run about ground-level centres).
4. **Issue acceptance 4.** The main pass, the depth prepass and the shadow views read one uniform centre (by construction: one vertex function, one uniform); on the GPU an orthographic view 400 m high, its morph band on screen, renders the terrain with no hole to the clear colour (a prepass deeper than the main pass would leave the main pass's fragments failing the depth test).
5. **Captures.** `terrain_tour` `ELEMENTS_ORTHO=18 ELEMENTS_PINS=1`: the red feet whole at 1x and in 4× crops on the hills, mountains, dunes and plains, at noon, dawn toward and away from the sun, and under snow; at 9 and 29 m views; a 240-frame walk (hills, mountains) with no pop; a 400 m view over the red underlay with no crack; the perspective set (eye level, down, 12 m, aerial, dawn, dusk, plains, dunes, scree, mountain foot) unchanged to a level of 255 but at silhouettes.
6. **Performance.** The terrain's passes unchanged under a perspective camera (`bench_elements`, 4K meadow, opaque over the early prepass); under an orthographic camera with a walking focus the frame's CPU time within noise of a still one.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Level 0 did not draw the map's bilinear**, as the issue's first table row assumes: its vertices sat on the cell corners. The vertices moved onto the samples (Model 3), which also moves the node boxes by half a cell, so for the same eye a perspective selection can differ at a range's boundary. Acceptance 2 is therefore tested as "a perspective camera selects and morphs about its eye", not as a selection identical to the old one.
2. **Acceptance 1's 1e-3 m holds at the vertices.** Between them the triangles depart from the bilinear by up to a quarter of the twist (Model 4; p90 0.047 m on the hills at 4 m cells), the least a mesh on the samples can reach.
3. **No `TerrainLodCentre` opt-in**: under an orthographic camera the focus is the centre (Model 6).
4. **The mountains keep a residual.** On the `mountains` preset at 4 m cells, a quarter of a quad's twist exceeds a 10 cm foot often: in the isometric capture 22 % of the posts' feet stay hidden (45 % before), and the CPU estimate at that site is 14 % of the posts more than 8 cm under the drawn ground and 19 % on slopes above 45°, where an 8 cm foot also buries its own uphill side. The hills (0-3 %), dunes (0 %) and plains (0 %) are clean. A figure on rugged rock would need a query that answers the drawn triangles rather than the bilinear, which the issue does not ask for; it is a backlog item.
5. **The patch-count test moved its camera half a cell.** `a_flat_terrain_selects_the_measured_patch_counts` stood its camera on the grid's centre, symmetric for the old node boxes; the boxes now start half a cell in, so the camera that sees the same symmetric selection stands half a cell off the centre, and the counts are unchanged.

## Addendum — the drawn-surface query (issue #40)

Deviation 4 named a query that answers the drawn triangles as a backlog item; Sanctuary measured the cost on a 512² town at 9.375 ft cells (p99 0.004 ft, max 0.703 ft, 0.15 % of points over 0.15 ft, at creases where buildings stand).

**Model.** Level 0's quad `(i, j)` has its vertices on samples `i, i+1` × `j, j+1` and its two triangles from `patch_mesh`'s index order `[i, i+N+1, i+1]`, `[i+1, i+N+1, i+N+2]`, i.e. split along `(1, 0)–(0, 1)`. With `w` the fractional position in the sample lattice (the same `g = (p − origin)/cell − ½`, taps clamped as in `TerrainGrid::sample`): if `w.x + w.y ≤ 1` the surface is `h00 + w.x (h10 − h00) + w.y (h01 − h00)`, else `h11 + (1 − w.x)(h01 − h11) + (1 − w.y)(h10 − h11)`. It is continuous across the diagonal (both give the plane through the shared edge) and across quads (each edge is linear in its two samples), equals `sample` at samples, and differs from it by `|t| · w.x · w.y` in the lower triangle (the bilinear is the plane plus `t · w.x · w.y`, `t = h00 + h11 − h10 − h01`) and by the mirrored `|t| (1 − w.x)(1 − w.y)` in the upper one, at most `|t|/4` at the diagonal's midpoint. No new source is needed: the formula is the mesh's own index order (`src/terrain/mod.rs` `patch_mesh`) and Model 4 above.

**Parameters.** None. **GPU mapping.** None: a CPU query over the readback `TerrainSnapshot`, like `terrain_height`. Levels above 0 draw coarser surfaces; this answers level 0, as `sample` does.

**Validation targets.** (1) Against `vertex_position`'s level-0 triangles on the hills to 1e-4 m at random points; (2) equal to `sample` at every cell centre and within `|t|/4` of it elsewhere; (3) continuous across quad and patch boundaries; (4) at the clamped edge equal to `sample`.

**Deviation.** `Environment::terrain_height` stays the bilinear (the query a flora or fog consumer wants; its documentation now says so); the new `Environment::terrain_drawn_height` answers the triangles.
