## Model adopted

**Why CDLOD (and not the alternatives).** The plan's choice stands. (1) One 33 × 33 mesh for every patch is Andersson §5.4's design and CDLOD's §2.3; it needs no stitching (§2.4) and no neighbour bookkeeping, while Andersson's 33 × 33 + nine index permutations (§5.4.1) needs an adjacency pass and nine index buffers, i.e. nine bins in Bevy's batching. (2) CDLOD's LOD function is the 3D distance to the vertex, so shadows (§2.2.2) and a camera high above the terrain behave (below). (3) Deliot's concurrent binary tree needs three compute kernels per frame, compute-written indirect dispatch and draw arguments (a `MeshMaterial3d` entity does not take those from the stock mesh pipeline, so a custom render phase would be needed [INFERENCE: not traced in the 0.20 source]), a heap of 2^depth bits per camera (128 MB at depth 28) and, in its shipped form, mesh shaders (`_release-content/migration-guides/mesh_shaders.md` exists for 0.20; mesh shaders are not part of the WebGPU baseline [INFERENCE]), so it conflicts with the wasm requirement; its attraction (1.5 cm triangles at 2 × 2 km) is irrelevant for a heightfield that is 2 m per cell. (4) Geometry clipmaps (Strugar §1) lose to CDLOD on tall terrain for the reason Strugar gives (the LOD ignores the observer's height).

**Placement.** `Terrain` is centred on its `Transform`: the grid origin is `o = T.xz − extent/2` and a stored height `h` is the world height `T.y + h`; rotation and scale of the transform are not supported (grid-aligned storage) and a non-identity one logs `warn!` once [INFERENCE: the plan is silent on anchoring; this makes the quick-start camera at the origin stand over the middle of the 2048 m default]. Cell size `c = extent.x / resolution.x` (2 m at High). `resolution` is clamped per axis to the quality maximum (256 / 512 / 1024 / 2048, plan) and rounded down to a power of two; cells must be square, so if `extent.x/res.x ≠ extent.y/res.y` the constructor logs `warn!` once, takes the larger of the two as `c` (the coarser axis binds, so neither axis exceeds the quality maximum) and re-derives both axes as `res = round(extent / c / 32) · 32` [INFERENCE]. `Terrain` spawns one child entity pool (below), so the terrain's own `Visibility` hides everything and despawning the terrain despawns the pool.

**Layer buffers and the render pack.** The simulation layers are the contract in Sources. Because Bevy leaves only three fragment-stage storage buffers free at the WebGPU default limit of 8 (below), the materials never bind the sim layers; they bind packed render buffers written by two small kernels:

- `terrain_cells: array<vec4<f32>>`, one per cell: `x` surface height `bedrock + sediment` (m), `y` sediment depth (m), `z` water depth above the surface (m), `w = bitcast<f32>(pack2x16float(∂h/∂x, ∂h/∂z))`, the dimensionless slope of the surface by central differences over ±1 cell (`(h(i+1) − h(i−1)) / (2c)`, edge cells one-sided). Written by `terrain_pack` after the last simulation substep of a frame in which hydrology or erosion ran (and once after generation). Bound by `TerrainExtension`, `WaterExtension` (water-rendering.md) and `GroundExtension`, and by the wind solid-mask and precipitation kernels.
- `flow_cells: array<vec4<f32>>`: `x, y` depth-averaged velocity `(u, v)` m/s (the hydrology velocity layer), `z` turbidity `suspended / max(water, 1e-3)` (a dimensionless volume fraction), `w` spare. Written by `flow_pack`. Bound only by `WaterExtension`.
- M4's `cover_cells` (snow depth, wetness, snow water equivalent) is a *second* pack with its own writer and cadence (agreed with `ResearchM4`); the terrain and ground fragment shaders bind exactly `terrain_cells` + `cover_cells`. `TerrainExtension` binds `cover_cells` with `visibility(vertex, fragment)` because plan 4.1 displaces the surface by the snow depth, and that displacement is part of the shared vertex function, so it is repeated in `prepass_vertex_shader` (Bevy's default prepass shader would draw the undisplaced surface into the depth prepass and the shadow maps); `GroundExtension` does not displace and binds it fragment-only.

The gradient is stored so the fragment shader gets a smooth normal from four bilinear loads instead of the 16 loads of a per-pixel cross filter (Andersson Listing 1); a bilinear blend of central-difference gradients is smooth across cell edges, which the gradient of the bilinear *height* is not (it is piecewise constant per cell and shows the grid). `pack2x16float` keeps ~3 decimal digits of slope, enough (a 1/1000 slope error is 0.06°).

Memory (84 B per cell: bedrock 4 + sediment 4 + water 4 + flux 16 + velocity 8 + suspended 4 + `suspended_next` 4 + `source_map` 8 + `terrain_cells` 16 + `flow_cells` 16): 256² = 5.25 MiB, 512² = 21 MiB, 1024² = 84 MiB, 2048² = 336 MiB. The largest single binding is `terrain_cells` / `flux` at 2048²: 64 MiB against `max_storage_buffer_binding_size` 128 MiB and `max_buffer_size` 256 MiB (`$R/wgpu-types-30.0.1/src/limits.rs:376,378`, `Limits::defaults`). 4096² would not fit; the plan's maximum 2048² does.

**Procedural generator** (`terrain_generate`, once at spawn; the GPU result is not bit-reproducible across vendors, which the plan already accepts; the CPU mirror in tests is statistical). With `p = (x, z)/F` (`F = feature_size_m`, the base lattice cell), lacunarity λ = 2 and gain `g = 0.5` (Musgrave `ω = λ^{−H}` with H = 1; the crate's `fbm3` uses the same gain, `src/noise.wesl:142`), octave `k` samples `n_k(p) = perlin3(R_k p λ^k + v_k, z_k)` with a 137.5° rotation `R_k` (golden angle, `2.39996·k` rad) and a seeded integer offset `v_k` (Galin's `n(R_i p + v_i)` against grid artefacts) and `z_k = 0.5 + k` (never an integer: Lagae §6.2). The seed enters as an integer lattice offset `(seed·2654435761 + k·1031) mod 4096` on each axis. Two sums:

$$ f(p) = \frac{\sum_{k<K} g^k\, n_k(p)}{\sum_{k<K} g^k}, \qquad r(p) = \frac{\sum_{k<K} g^k\, s_k(p)}{\sum_{k<K} g^k},\quad s_k = w_k\,(1-|n_k(p)|)^2,\ w_0 = 1,\ w_{k} = \operatorname{clamp}(2\,s_{k-1},0,1) $$

`f` is Musgrave's noise-synthesis fBm; `r` is the ridged multifractal (Galin: `r = 1 − |n|`; the multifractal feedback `w_k` makes high frequencies follow the previous octave's value, the recurrence quoted in Sources; the offset 1 / gain 2 / squaring are Musgrave's published defaults from Ebert et al. ch. 12, [INFERENCE: from memory]). The unit-range terrain value and the height:

$$ u_f = \operatorname{clamp}\!\big(\tfrac12 + \tfrac{f}{2\cdot 0.37},0,1\big),\quad u_r = \operatorname{clamp}\!\big(\tfrac{r - 0.12}{0.76},0,1\big),\quad h = h_{base} + R\,\big[(1-q)\,u_f + q\,u_r\big] $$

with `R = relief_m`, `q = ridged`. The constants 0.37, 0.12 and 0.76 map `f` ∈ ±0.37 and `r` ∈ [0.12, 0.88] — about the 0.5 to 99.5 percentiles of each component, so roughly 1 % of the cells clip — onto [0, 1], measured here on 4 × 1024² samples (2048 m, cell 2 m, 8 octaves) with a numpy port of 2D gradient noise: `f` percentiles −0.43 / 0.36 (rms 0.176), `r` percentiles 0.12 / 0.88 (mean 0.52). `R` is therefore the range of a single component; blending two components with `q > 0` compresses the range of the sum (measured 0.5–99.5 percentile spread of `h − h_base`: hills 96.7 m of `R` = 120, mountains 676 m of 900, dunes 40 m of 40, plains 14 m of 15). The constants must be re-measured on the crate's own noise at implementation and frozen by a golden (Validation 1). The octave count is `K = min(octaves, ⌊log₂(F / 2c)⌋ + 1)` so the finest wavelength `F/2^{K−1}` is not below `2c` (Musgrave: omit frequencies the grid cannot hold); default `octaves = 8`, which is 8 for hills, mountains, plains and 7 for dunes at the 2 m cell. The sediment layer starts at `initial_sand_m · (1 − smoothstep(25°, 34°, slope))` (sand only where it can lie at its repose angle, `snow-cover.md`/plan 4.3 use 34°) and `bedrock = h − sediment` [INFERENCE for the slope ramp; the plan has only the scalar]. `Terrain::flat(h)`: `bedrock = h`, `sediment = 0`.

Reading them against Musgrave's crossover scale (relief ≈ feature size means ≈ 45° slopes): `R/F` = 0.20 / 0.60 / 0.13 / 0.019, and the median slopes rise with it. The plan's `mountains` default starts with 70 % of the ground above 40° and a median of 50°, steeper than natural mountains (cliff-forming, eroded to talus 35° by `erosion.md`'s thermal pass within minutes of simulated time); it is kept as the plan's default (a change needs a source) and flagged here: a user wanting gentler ground lowers `relief_m` or raises `feature_size_m`. Lowering the gain only helps weakly (mountains, fBm only: median 48.5° at g = 0.5, 41.8° at 0.45, 37.4° at 0.4, 34.8° at 0.35), because the slope is set by `R/F` and the first octaves. `base_height_m` defaults to 0 in all presets (the plan lists none; y = 0 is the sea-level datum of the atmosphere, `clouds.md`).

**Heightmap import.** `TerrainSource::Heightmap { image, height_range_m }`: `h = lerp(range.start, range.end, v)` where `v` is the red channel in 0–1. The resample kernel (`terrain_heightmap`) writes `bedrock` at `resolution`: for each destination cell it box-averages `n = min(⌈src/dst⌉, 4)` taps per axis (bilinear taps when the source is smaller), all with `textureLoad` and hand-written weights (no sampler: `R32Float` is not filterable and a compute kernel needs no sampler). Formats and 0.20 loader facts (verified): `ImageLoaderSettings::default()` has `is_srgb: true` and `expand_grayscale: true` (`$R/bevy_image-0.20.0/src/image_loader.rs:179-180`; fields documented at `:148` and `:160-164`), so a grayscale PNG loads as an RGBA image and an 8-bit one as `Rgba8UnormSrgb`; heights must be read linearly, so the crate accepts `R8Unorm`, `Rgba8Unorm`, `R16Unorm`, `Rgba16Unorm`, `R16Uint`, `R32Float`, `Rgba16Float`, `Rgba32Float`, and logs `error!` naming the asset path (plan: "the terrain stays unspawned") for any `*Srgb` format with the remedy `ImageLoaderSettings { is_srgb: false, expand_grayscale: false, .. }`. The 16-bit normalised formats need the wgpu feature `TEXTURE_FORMAT_16BIT_NORM`, "a native only feature" (`wgpu-types-30.0.1/src/features.rs:633-649`); Bevy enables every adapter feature under its default `Functionality` priority (`renderer/mod.rs:319-326`), so native works and a WebGPU browser that lacks it fails the texture creation: the kernel therefore also takes `R16Uint` (a `texture_2d<u32>`, `v = f32(raw)/65535`), which `ImageLoaderSettings::texture_format` can force (its doc names exactly this use, `image_loader.rs:134-139`; whether the PNG decoder honours it for a 16-bit file was not run, [INFERENCE]). An 8-bit map has 256 height steps (3.5 m over 900 m): the note recommends 16 bit and `info!`s when it sees an 8-bit format with `range` > 100 m. Float kernels use a `texture_2d<f32>` binding with `Float { filterable: false }`, uint kernels `Uint`: two shader-def variants of one entry.

**CDLOD selection (CPU, once per frame, in `PostUpdate` before `TransformSystems::Propagate`, `$R/bevy_transform-0.20.0/src/plugins.rs:13-15`).** Leaf patch = 32 × 32 cells, `S_0 = 32c` (64 m at High; the plan's `extent/(resolution/32)`). Levels `k = 0..K−1` with node size `S_k = S_0·2^k` and `K = ⌈log₂(max(extent)/S_0)⌉ + 1` (6 at High, 7 at Ultra, 5 at Medium, 4 at Low; a non-power-of-two extent makes a small forest of roots). LOD range `r_k = r_0 · 2^k` (Strugar §2.1, each range twice the previous) with the **first range `r_0 = 2·S_0`** (128 / 64 / 256 / 512 m at High / Ultra / Medium / Low). The recursion is Strugar's `LODSelect` exactly: a node whose AABB does not intersect the sphere of radius `r_k` about the camera is skipped (returns false, the parent covers it as a partial selection); a node at level 0, or at level k whose AABB does not intersect the sphere `r_{k−1}`, is selected whole; otherwise its four children recurse. The distance is the 3D distance from the camera to the node's AABB (the padded min/max of the cells under it), so a camera at 300 m sees no level 0 or 1 below it (Strugar Figure 4). No frustum test in the CPU selection: Bevy culls each view against the patch's `Aabb` (below), and that is what makes shadow cascades work, because the LOD is a function of the main camera only (Strugar §2.2.2) while the culling is per view. The camera used is the lowest-`order` `ElementsCamera` (plan Shared architecture); with none, the selection idles and keeps the last pool.

Why `r_0 = 2 S_0` and not the plan's 64 m: a morph transition is correct only between neighbouring nodes that differ by at most one level (Strugar §2.6: a node supports a transition "between two LOD layers"). A node at level k+2 has every point at least `r_{k+1} = 2 r_k` from the camera; a level-k neighbour reaches at most `r_k + diag(S_k, height)` from it, and the shared edge lies in both, so a two-level gap needs `2 r_k ≤ r_k + √(2 S_k² + H²)`, i.e. `r_k ≲ √3 S_k` for a patch whose height range is as large as its width: with `r_0 = S_0` this is possible. A CPU simulation of the selection (this note; 2048 m extent, leaf 64, 150 random camera positions and heights 5–600 m, AABB heights 0–200 m, adjacency tested on shared edges) found **734 adjacent pairs two levels apart with `r_0 = S_0`, 400 with `r_0 = 1.5 S_0`, and 0 (maximum difference 1) with `r_0 = 2 S_0`**. A two-level gap is a crack (the edge vertices of the finer node morph onto its own coarser grid, not onto the coarser neighbour's), so the plan's 64 m is wrong at High (`S_0 = 64`: `r_0 = S_0`), Medium and Low; at Ultra (`S_0 = 32`) `2 S_0 = 64` is the plan's number. It is also better looking: the triangle edge in pixels at the outer end of range `r_k` is `(c·2^k) · P / r_k` with `P = H_px / (2 tan(fov/2))` ≈ 935 px at 1080p and 60° vertical: `r_0 = S_0` would give 29 px edges, `2 S_0` gives 14.6 px at every level (7.3 px at the inner end), the 8–16 px the paper's distribution aims at.

Morph: `m_k = 0.7·r_k` (plan: the last 30 %; Strugar's range is 15–30 %). `morphK(d) = clamp((d − m_k) / (r_k − m_k), 0, 1)`, `d` the distance from `view.lod_view_world_position` (below) to the vertex's world position at its *unmorphed* grid position and its sampled height; the top level has `morphK = 0` (nothing coarser exists). The constraint that every node's vertices are unmorphed at the inner edge of its range is `m_k ≥ r_{k−1}`, i.e. a morph fraction ≤ 1 − r_{k−1}/r_k = 50 % for doubling ranges (derived; 30 % satisfies it). The converted CDLOD text lost the paper's `morphK` formula; the clamp-linear form is the standard one [INFERENCE for the exact expression, Strugar §2.2.1 for the area].

Min/max quadtree: a `terrain_minmax` kernel reduces each 32 × 32-cell leaf of `terrain_cells` to `vec4(surface_min, surface_max, water_surface_max, water_depth_max)` (16 B per leaf: 16 KiB at High, 64 KiB at Ultra) and the 4 Hz readback copies only that; CPU builds the upper levels by max/min. Patch `Aabb` in local space: `Aabb::from_min_max(Vec3(0, h_min − 2, 0), Vec3(1, h_max + 2, 1))` (plan: padded by 2 m) with the entity transform `translation = (o.x, T.y, o.z)` relative to the terrain, `scale = (S_k, 1, S_k)`, so world `y = T.y + local y`. Before the first readback a patch has the generator's analytic bound `[h_base − 2, h_base + R + 2]`. Erosion and deposition can change heights by far less than 2 m between 250 ms readbacks (`erosion.md` rates); a larger change only shortens the AABB, never the displaced vertex, so the failure is a patch culled early at the frustum edge, not a crack.

Pool: `capacity = 192` entities per pool, each `{ Mesh3d(shared 33² patch), MeshMaterial3d<TerrainMaterial>, Transform, MeshTag::new(level), Aabb, NoAutoAabb, Visibility }`; `MeshTag` is constructed with `MeshTag::new(lod)` in 0.20 (the struct gained a debug-only private `type_id`, so `render.md` §MeshTag, which shows a plain `value` struct, is stale: `$R/bevy_mesh-0.20.0/src/components.rs:205-229`, migration guide `mesh_tag_new.md`). Slots are keyed by `(level, ix, iz)` so an unchanged patch keeps its entity and writes nothing; unused slots get `Visibility::Hidden`. The capacity is the measured worst case plus margin: selection counts in the same simulation (2048 m extent, camera heights 2–300 m, AABB heights 0–200 m, 300 random positions): High ≤ 94 patches (≤ 124 over fixed test cameras), Ultra ≤ 121 (160), Medium ≤ 70 (88), Low ≤ 43 (52); flat terrain, camera 30 m above the centre: High 52 `{L0: 16, L1: 12, L2: 12, L3: 12}`, Ultra 64, Medium 40, Low 28.

**Vertex stage.** The patch mesh is a unit grid `x, z ∈ [0, 1]` in 32 × 32 quads (1089 vertices, 2048 triangles, `u16` indices, no normals or UVs needed; built with `Mesh::new`, whose default `compress_attributes` is empty, `$R/bevy_mesh-0.20.0/src/mesh.rs:290`; the shader still calls Bevy's `decompress_vertex` for safety, `forward_io.wesl:59-90`). In WESL (`terrain.wesl`, one function shared by the forward and the prepass vertex entry points so the two displace bit-identically, which the depth-equal test of the main pass requires):

```wesl
fn terrain_vertex_position(local: vec3<f32>, instance_index: u32) -> vec4<f32> {
    let m = mesh_functions::get_world_from_local(instance_index);      // translation = patch origin (x, terrain y, z), scale = (S,1,S)
    let lod = mesh_functions::get_tag(instance_index);                  // MeshTag::new(level)
    let gi = round(local.xz * 32.0);                                    // grid index 0..32
    let step = params.cell * f32(1u << lod);                            // vertex spacing S/32 = c·2^k
    var wxz = vec2(m[3].x, m[3].z) + gi * step;                         // unmorphed world xz
    let h0 = terrain_height(wxz);                                       // bilinear of terrain_cells.x via grid_taps
    let d = distance(view.lod_view_world_position, vec3(wxz.x, m[3].y + h0, wxz.y));
    let k = morph_k(d, lod);                                            // 0 at the top level
    let odd = gi - 2.0 * floor(gi * 0.5);                               // (i mod 2, j mod 2)
    wxz = wxz - odd * step * k;                                         // Strugar morphVertex
    return vec4(wxz.x, m[3].y + terrain_height(wxz), wxz.y, 1.0);
}
```

`view.lod_view_world_position` is the distance origin: for a camera view it is the camera, and for the cascaded shadow views of a directional light "the position of that camera" (`$R/bevy_render-0.20.0/src/view/mod.rs:1244-1280`, `view.wesl:88-93`), which is exactly Strugar's rule that the shadow LOD follows the main camera and needs no per-frame material write; `view.world_position` would be the light's in a shadow view. The vertex is placed in world space (the shader reads the origin and the terrain's world y from the instance matrix's translation and the size from its scale, and writes the final xz and y itself), so a rotated or non-uniformly scaled terrain `Transform` is unsupported (see Placement). Prepass: `prepass_vertex_shader()` is overridden with the same function, because the default `ShaderRef::Default` prepass vertex shader (`$R/bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/prepass/prepass.wesl:70-140`) would draw the flat grid into the depth prepass and into every shadow map (`extended_material.rs:72`, render.md §MaterialExtension). In the prepass output `world_normal` is built from the gradient in `terrain_cells.w` only under `NORMAL_PREPASS_OR_DEFERRED_PREPASS` (`prepass.wesl:105`).

**Fragment stage (`TerrainExtension`, forward and prepass/deferred in one file).** Following `pbr.wesl:1-40` the file is gated with `@if(PREPASS_PIPELINE)`; `fragment_shader()` and `deferred_fragment_shader()` both point at it so a deferred camera also gets the layer blend (else `E::deferred_fragment_shader() = Default` would shade a deferred terrain with the base material only, `extended_material.rs:84-90`); the prepass fragment shader stays Bevy's (nothing is discarded). Steps:

1. *Cell fetch*: four loads of `terrain_cells` around the world XZ (`grid_taps`, `src/grid.wesl:42-55`) give surface height, sediment depth, water depth and the gradient `(gx, gz)`; normal `n = normalize(−gx, 1, −gz)` — Andersson's cross filter written with the stored central difference (`n = (h_L − h_R, 2c, h_T − h_B)/2c` with `T` the row above, −Z, `B` the row below, +Z, the hydrology contract's orientation).
2. *Slope and weights.* Slope angle `α = acos(n.y)` plus a detail perturbation `3°·n_d` with `n_d = perlin3(world·0.05) ∈ [−1,1]` (Andersson §5.2.3: add detail to the computed mask so the transition is not "dull and unnaturally smooth"). With `smoothstep(a, b, x)`:
   `w_rock = smoothstep(37°, 43°, α)` (plan: rock above 40°; the ±3° ramp is [INFERENCE]);
   `w_sand = smoothstep(0.3, 0.7, sediment_m) · (1 − smoothstep(26°, 30°, α)) · (1 − w_rock)` (plan: sand where sediment > 0.5 m and slope < 30°);
   `w_soil = (1 − w_rock)(1 − w_sand) · smoothstep(28°, 34°, α)`;
   `w_grass = (1 − w_rock)(1 − w_sand)(1 − smoothstep(28°, 34°, α))`.
   The plan names four colours but gives no rule for soil; the 28°–34° band is the slope where loose cover sits at its repose angle (talus 35°, plan 3.2) and grass cannot hold [INFERENCE]. The four weights sum to 1 by construction (Validation 7).
3. *Colour.* The plan's four colours are read as sRGB-encoded (`Color::srgb`): linear rock (0.1005, 0.0890, 0.0783), soil (0.0732, 0.0397, 0.0196), sand (0.5382, 0.3931, 0.1960), grass (0.0397, 0.0835, 0.0134); read as linear they would be rock 0.35, grass 0.32 and sand 0.76, too bright for natural ground, whereas the sRGB reading gives dry-sand 0.54/0.39/0.20 and dark soil 0.07 [INFERENCE: interpretation, no albedo table in the corpus]. Roughness 0.9 (rock, sand, grass) and 0.95 (soil), metallic 0, reflectance default [INFERENCE: matte natural ground]. Detail: albedo ×(1 + 0.25·`fbm3(world·0.8, 3)`) faded out between 40 and 120 m, and a normal perturbation from the analytic derivative of the same noise (`perlin3_d`, `src/noise.wesl:44-87`) of amplitude 0.04, faded out by 60 m. The plan's "triplanar procedural detail noise" is a 3D noise sampled at the world position: a procedural 3D function has no projection axis, so there is nothing to blend across (triplanar blending is a texture-projection device) — the result is what triplanar would approximate, with no seams and one evaluation instead of three.
4. *Lighting.* `pbr_input_from_standard_material` supplies the base PBR input, the layer colours replace `base_color`, `n` replaces the world normal, `apply_pbr_lighting` shades, and the cloud shadow scales the result: `ρ = (0.25 + 0.75·T_px) / (0.25 + 0.75·T_cam)`, clamped to [0, 4], `T_px` the red channel of `CloudShadows.map` at `uv = (P.xz − l.xz·P.y/l.y − origin)/extent` (`clouds.md` Consumers; `Clamp` addressing; `T = 1` outside; a missing map is Bevy's white fallback image, so `T = 1`) and `T_cam = CloudShadows.camera_transmittance`, the factor the sun's illuminance already carries (`src/clouds/mod.rs:136-139`). The ratio keeps the average terrain brightness equal to what the dimmed `DirectionalLight` produces and gives the pixel-scale contrast; it also scales the sky ambient, which is the approximation (the exact route, scaling only the sun term, needs a copy of Bevy's directional light loop) [INFERENCE]. Terrain under water (`terrain_cells.z` > 0) is additionally multiplied by the *sun leg* of the absorption, `exp(−σ·d/μ_s')`, from the shared `bevy_elements::water::bed_attenuation` (`water-rendering.md`); the *view leg* is applied by the water's absorb pass, so it is not applied here (it would be counted twice).
5. *Wetness, puddles, snow* are M4 and read `cover_cells`.

**`GroundExtension` (`TerrainRender::External`).** The consumer's mesh keeps its own geometry and vertex shader; `GroundExtension` is `#[uniform(100)] params: GroundParams`, `#[storage(101, read_only)] terrain: Handle<ShaderBuffer>` (the same `terrain_cells` handle), `#[storage(102, read_only)] cover: Handle<ShaderBuffer>` (M4), `#[texture(103)] #[sampler(104)] cloud_shadow: Option<Handle<Image>>` — non-bindless (the extension does not implement `#[bindless]`, and `combined_bind_group.rs:17-24` enables bindless only if *both* base and extension are bindless, so no binding array interferes with storage buffers). `GroundParams { origin: vec2, cell: f32, y0: f32, size: uvec2, shadow: vec4 (origin.xy, extent, T_cam), light: vec4 }`. It samples by world XZ: water-covered darkening and puddles from the water depth (M3; the wetness map and snow arrive with `cover_cells` in M4, so the plan's M3 `external_ground` "wet shading under rain" is the water-depth-driven wet darkening `1 − 0.5·smoothstep(0, 0.02, d)`, the plan's puddle ramp of `wetness.md` [INFERENCE: the plan lists the visual in M3 but the wetness model in M4]) and cloud shadows. It does not displace and does not blend layers. In External mode the crate still spawns the *water* pool (floods are crate geometry; the consumer owns only the ground), with the selection running for water only [INFERENCE: the plan says "simulates only" and 3.3 says the water pool shares the CDLOD selection; both hold if only the water patches are spawned].

**Readback and wiring (plan 3.4).**
- `Environment::terrain_height(Vec2) -> Option<f32>`: the snapshot holds `TerrainSnapshot { origin, cell, size, y0, surface: Arc<[f32]> }` copied from a `terrain_height_rb` buffer (`array<f32>`, surface only, extracted by `terrain_pack`'s sibling `terrain_readback` kernel; above 1024² a `downsample` 2× box kernel first, per the plan's rule). It bilinearly samples with the same `grid_taps` rule as the shader (cell centres at +0.5) and returns `y0 + h`, `None` outside the extent or before the first readback. With `ErosionSettings.enabled == false` and no bake the surface cannot change, so it is read once after generation and then only when `ErodeTerrain` or a sediment edit marks it dirty [INFERENCE: saves 4–16 MiB per tick]. The `Arc<[f32]>` is what `FloraGround::new(closure)` captures in `ElementsFloraPlugin` (plan 3.4).
- Wind (`wind.md` Model step 4 and GPU table): the `solid mask` kernel gains `terrain_cells` and a `GridParams` uniform. A wind cell with centre `(x, y, z)` is solid when `y < y0 + H(x, z)`, with `H` the maximum of the bilinear surface heights at the cell's four quarter-points `(x ± 1, z ± 1)` m (the wind cell is 4 m, the terrain cell 2 m; the conservative maximum makes slopes solid rather than leaky); `z_agl` in the forcing profile is `y − y0 − H_bilinear(x, z)`. The wind domain's vertical origin follows the ground: `y_origin = 4·⌊(h_cam − 8)/4⌋` with `h_cam` the snapshot surface under the camera (camera y − 8 before the first readback or without terrain), moved in whole cells (toroidal scrolling, `wind.md`) [INFERENCE: the plan says the mask binds the terrain, not how the 64 m tall domain follows a 900 m mountain]. This adds one storage buffer to the wind layout: 7 of 8.
- Precipitation (`precipitation.md` step 4, `ground mode: plane or terrain`): the particle kernel adds `terrain_cells` (read-only, 4 storage buffers of 8) and collides with `y0 + x + z` — the surface *plus the water depth*, so rain splashes on a flood [INFERENCE].
- Hydrology and erosion read and write `bedrock`/`sediment`; `terrain_pack` is the only reader that publishes to materials.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `terrain_generate` | once at spawn | 0 / 2 (`bedrock`, `sediment`) | uniform `GenParams` (seed, `F`, `R`, `q`, octaves, constants) | 8×8, 16 384 groups | ≈ 8 M `perlin3` + 8 M ridged taps: ≈ 0.3 ms once [INFERENCE] |
| `terrain_heightmap` | once at spawn | 0 / 1 (`bedrock`) + `sediment` clear = 2 | `texture_2d<f32>` or `<u32>`, uniform | 8×8 | ≈ 0.05 ms once |
| `terrain_pack` | per frame hydrology/erosion ran, and once | 3 / 1 (`bedrock`, `sediment`, `water` → `terrain_cells`) | uniform `GridParams` | 8×8 | ≈ 0.03–0.06 ms: 1 M cells, 5 neighbour loads of two layers cached in L2, 16 B write [INFERENCE] |

Every kernel stays under 8 storage buffers and 0–1 storage textures (no storage texture is used here at all), well inside WebGPU's 8 and 4. The plan's budget "hydrology + erosion at 1024² ≤ 0.6 ms at 8 substeps" does not include `terrain_pack` + `flow_pack` (≈ 0.1 ms together, once per frame regardless of substeps): the bench must report them as their own spans.

Render side (verified in 0.20.0):
- **Materials.** `TerrainMaterial = ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, TerrainExtension>`; `MaterialPlugin::<TerrainMaterial>::default()` is added by the crate; the extension implements `vertex_shader()`, `fragment_shader()`, `prepass_vertex_shader()`, `deferred_fragment_shader()` (`extended_material.rs:34-90`; defaults are `Default`) and uses bindings from 100 (render.md §MaterialExtension). Bindings: `#[uniform(100)] params: TerrainParams`, `#[storage(101, read_only)] cells: Handle<ShaderBuffer>` (default visibility vertex + fragment), `#[storage(102, read_only, visibility(vertex, fragment))] cover: Handle<ShaderBuffer>` (M4; vertex because of the snow displacement), `#[texture(103)] #[sampler(104)] cloud_shadow: Option<Handle<Image>>`. Handle<ShaderBuffer> in `#[storage]` is the documented form (`$R/bevy-0.20.0/examples/shader/storage_buffer.rs:95-98`, `bind_group.rs:134-135`). The extension is not bindless, so the combined bind group is non-bindless (`combined_bind_group.rs:17-24`, `:66-69`).
- **Storage-buffer budget at the WebGPU default limit 8.** Bevy's fragment stage, with an Atmosphere camera: `mesh` (`mesh_bindings.rs:46-49`, `VERTEX_FRAGMENT`), the three clustered-light buffers (bindings 8, 9, 10, default `FRAGMENT` visibility, `mesh_view_bindings.rs:296-330`, storage when the device allows ≥ 3, `cluster/mod.rs:39`), `atmosphere` (binding 33, `:457-464`) = **5** (the metadata buffer is `VERTEX` only, `mesh_bindings.rs:261-273`; agreed with `ResearchM4`, who caught that this note's first count of 6 was wrong). Binding-array material groups add `clustered_decals` (group 1) only where binding arrays work (native). So `TerrainExtension` fragment: 2 (`cells`, `cover`) → 7 of 8; the vertex stage: Bevy `mesh`, `metadata`, `visibility_ranges` (binding 14, `VERTEX`, `mesh_view_bindings.rs:346-356`) = 3 plus `cells` and `cover` = 5 of 8. A native device reports the adapter's limits (default priority `Functionality`, `renderer/mod.rs:319-326`), so the budget matters for wasm and for a platform that requests the default limits; the crate designs to 8.
- **Prepass and shadows.** Terrain casts shadows: `enable_prepass()` and `enable_shadows()` stay true; the shadow and prepass pipelines use the overridden vertex shader. Directional shadow passes receive a terrain patch per cascade (default `CascadeShadowConfig` has 4 cascades, `bevy_light-0.20.0/src/cascade.rs:144-152`); a sun and a moon with shadows is 8 cascade phases plus the main prepass plus the main opaque pass.
- **Batching: the pool is one draw per phase.** `Opaque3dBatchSetKey { pipeline, draw_function, material_bind_group_index, slabs, lightmap_slab }` and `Opaque3dBinKey { asset_id }` (`bevy_core_pipeline-0.20.0/src/core_3d/mod.rs:197-238`) put every entity with the same mesh asset and material in one bin; with `GpuPreprocessingMode::Culling` (the default, `gpu_preprocessing.rs:150-166`) the bin is `MultidrawableMesh` (`render_phase/mod.rs:2300-2309`) and is drawn as one instanced (indirect) draw whatever the number of patches. Per frame that is ≈ 1 main-pass draw + 1 prepass draw + 8 shadow draws (sun and moon, 4 cascades each) ≈ 10 draw calls for the ground, independent of the 52–160 patches; only per-entity CPU work scales (extraction touches only entities whose `Changed<Transform/MeshTag/Aabb/Visibility>` fired, `render/mesh.rs:1960-1975`; `check_visibility_cpu_culling` iterates all pool entities).
- **Culling.** `Aabb` is honoured by CPU culling and by GPU culling: `MeshCullingData::new(aabb)` copies the entity's `Aabb` (`render/mesh.rs:1652-1666`, `:2202`), and `Changed<Aabb>` re-extracts the instance (`:1965`), so a readback that rewrites a patch's bounds is picked up. `calculate_bounds` would overwrite a hand-set `Aabb` the first time `Mesh3d` is `Changed` (`bevy_camera-0.20.0/src/visibility/mod.rs:568-592`: the update branch has `Or<(AssetChanged<Mesh3d>, Changed<Mesh3d>)>`), so every pool entity carries `NoAutoAabb` (`:545-551`) — without it the displaced terrain is culled with the flat grid's AABB (y = 0) and mountains vanish from above. `MeshAabb::compute_aabb` is now `get_aabb` (migration guide `mesh_aabb_trait_rename.md`; render.md already uses neither). Directional-light visibility reads the same `Aabb` (`bevy_light-0.20.0/src/lib.rs:359, 539`).
- **`MeshTag` in WESL**: `mesh_functions::get_tag(instance_index)` (`mesh_functions.wesl:155`) returns `u32`; in the prepass the same import works (`prepass.wesl` imports `mesh_functions`).
- **Alpha.** Terrain is `AlphaMode::Opaque`; with `OpaqueRendererMethod::Auto` it renders forward unless the app sets `DefaultOpaqueRendererMethod::deferred()`; both shaders are provided.
- **CPU per frame**: the selection visits ≲ 6 levels × ≲ 60 nodes (< 50 µs) and the pool update writes only changed slots.

GPU cost of the draw (no plan budget exists for it; recorded as an estimate for the bench to confirm, [INFERENCE]): vertex stage ≈ 135 k vertices × 4 loads at the worst case, < 0.05 ms per phase; the 8 shadow phases re-submit the patches in their cascade frusta, ≈ 0.1–0.2 ms in total; the fragment stage is the cost: 4 loads, a 3-octave noise, the layer blend and Bevy's PBR with two directional lights and 4 cascades, ≈ 0.5–0.9 ms for a full-screen terrain at 1080p on the RTX 3090. `bench_elements` should print the `main_opaque_pass_3d` span with and without terrain (`RenderDiagnosticsPlugin`, render.md §Diagnostics).

1. **Generator statistics (CPU mirror in `src/gpu/noise_mirror.rs` style, `#[test]`):** on 1024² cells at 2 m with seed 42: `f` 0.5/99.5 percentiles within ±0.03 of −0.43 / 0.36 and `r` within ±0.03 of 0.12 / 0.88 (this note's numpy port; the crate's hashed Perlin should land within the tolerance, a larger miss re-blesses the constants 0.37, 0.12, 0.76 and the table below, once). Per preset the 0.5/99.5 percentile spread of `h − h_base` is within ±10 % of the measured 96.7 m (hills), 676 m (mountains), 40 m (dunes), 14 m (plains) — not of `R`, because the mix compresses the range (Model). Slope p50 / p95 within ±15 % of the table (hills 17.4° / 32.9°, mountains 50.3° / 69.5°, dunes 12.9° / 25.4°, plains 2.1° / 4.2°) and share above 40° within ±3 points (hills 0.6 %, mountains 70 %, dunes 0, plains 0). Source: computed here.
2. **`the_terrain_is_frozen`** golden: FNV-1a over `bytemuck::cast_slice` of the CPU-mirror heights quantised to 1 mm for `hills(42)` at 256² — frozen on the CPU mirror, not the GPU (GPU results are not bit-deterministic, plan).
3. **Selection (pure CPU function `select_patches`):** (a) *tiling*: for 1000 random cameras the selected patches cover `extent²` exactly once (sum of areas = 4 194 304 m² at 2048 m, no overlap; verified on this note's simulation for every camera tried); (b) *range invariant*: a level-k patch's AABB distance is ≥ `r_{k−1}` (k > 0) and ≤ `r_k` (top level exempt); (c) *neighbour rule*: adjacent patches differ by at most one level (734 violations at `r_0 = S_0` in the simulation above, **0 required** at `2 S_0`); (d) counts for flat terrain (AABB y = 0) with the camera 30 m above the extent centre at 2048 m, `r_0 = 2 S_0`: High 52 patches with per-level `{L0: 16, L1: 12, L2: 12, L3: 12}`, Ultra 64 `{16, 12, 12, 12, 12}`, Medium 40 `{16, 12, 12}`, Low 28 `{16, 12}` (this note's simulation; exact for the same selection rule); (e) camera 300 m above the centre: High 28 `{L2: 16, L3: 12}` (no level 0 or 1), Ultra 28 `{L3: 16, L4: 12}`; camera 1500 m above: 4 patches, all level 4 at High; (f) pool capacity 192 is never exceeded for 10⁴ random cameras with AABB heights 0–200 m (measured max 160 at Ultra over fixed cameras, 121 over 300 random).
4. **Morph continuity (CPU model of `terrain_vertex_position`):** for any two adjacent selected patches the shared-edge vertices have identical world XZ and identical heights bit for bit (same function of the same position); a patch with `morphK = 1` everywhere has its odd vertices on its even vertices (error < 1e-4 m), and its vertex set equals the level k+1 grid at the same positions; `morphK(d)` is 0 for `d ≤ 0.7 r_k` and 1 for `d ≥ r_k`, linear between (≤ 1e-6).
5. **Normals:** on a plane `h = s·x` (s = 0.5) the stored gradient gives `n` with tilt `atan(0.5) = 26.565°` (±0.1°, f16 pack error 0.06° per 1/1000); on `h = 0` the normal is `(0, 1, 0)`; edge cells use one-sided differences and stay within 1° of the interior value on the plane.
6. **Heightmap import (GPU, `#[ignore]`):** a 256² `R16Uint` ramp whose raw value is `x · 257` (so `v = x/255` after the `/65535` normalisation) with `height_range_m` 0–255 resampled to 128² gives `bedrock(i) = 2·i + 0.5` (the box average of source columns `2i` and `2i + 1`; the ramp is linear along x) within 0.01 m, and every row identical; an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` image makes the terrain `error!` and not spawn; `Terrain::flat(12.5)` reads back `12.5` everywhere and `Environment::terrain_height(Vec2::ZERO) == Some(12.5)` after the first readback, `None` outside the extent and before it.
7. **Layer blend (CPU mirror of the WESL weights):** the four weights sum to 1 (±1e-6) everywhere; at α = 45° `w_rock ≥ 0.99`; at α = 20° with 1 m sediment `w_sand ≥ 0.99`; at α = 10°, sediment 0 `w_grass = 1`; at α = 31°, sediment 0 `w_soil` ∈ (0.3, 0.7).
8. **AABB:** after a readback every patch's `Aabb` contains all 1089 displaced vertices of that patch (CPU model, with 2 m pad); every pool entity has `NoAutoAabb`; a patch's `Aabb` is unchanged by `Mesh3d` being re-inserted.
9. **Binding budget (unit test on the layouts):** `TerrainExtension`, `GroundExtension` and `WaterExtension` each declare ≤ 2 fragment-stage storage buffers; with Bevy's 5 that is ≤ 7 ≤ 8. The wind solid-mask layout is 7 of 8, the precipitation sim layout is 4 of 8.
10. **Wiring:** a flat terrain at 10 m gives wind-field `z_agl` 4 m at world y = 14 and a solid cell below y = 10 (bit-exact on the CPU model), the wind domain `y_origin` is `4·⌊(h − 8)/4⌋`, and a precipitation particle at y = 9.9 over it respawns (the same on a 1 m deep flood: collision at 11 m).
11. **Visual (plan M3):** `quickstart` with `hills(42)`: the camera at the centre sees rock on steep faces, grass elsewhere and no cracks between levels in a wireframe (`Msaa::Off`, screenshot `ELEMENTS_SHOT`); `erosion_bake` shows gullies and sediment fans on `mountains` (the sand layer appears where `sediment > 0.5 m`); `external_ground` shows the consumer's plane darkening where water stands.
12. **Cost (informational, GPU):** `terrain_pack` + `flow_pack` ≤ 0.1 ms at 1024² on an unloaded card at full clock (report the minimum over the diagnostic history, AGENTS.md trap on P-states); terrain drawing prints its own span.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **First LOD range `r_0 = 2·S_0` (128 m at High, 256 at Medium, 512 at Low, 64 at Ultra), not 64 m at every quality.** Derivation and simulation above (734 two-level neighbour pairs at `r_0 = S_0`, 0 at `2 S_0`); source: Strugar §2.6 ("a single quadtree node can only support transition between two LOD layers") plus the CPU simulation of the selection in this note. At Ultra the plan's number is unchanged. It also brings the on-screen edge length from 29 px to 14.6 px at 1080p.
2. **Materials bind two packed render buffers (`terrain_cells`, `flow_cells`) instead of the sim layers.** Plan 3.1 says the material "displaces from the height buffers". Only three fragment-stage storage buffers are free at the WebGPU default limit (Bevy uses 5 of 8); binding bedrock, sediment, water and velocity separately would need 4. One packed `vec4` also lets the fragment shader read a precomputed normal (4 loads instead of 16). Source: the Bevy files cited under GPU mapping; Andersson §5.2.2 for the per-pixel cross-filter normal this replaces.
3. **"Triplanar procedural detail noise" is a 3D noise at the world position.** No change in appearance (3D noise has no projection); one evaluation instead of three. Derivation.
4. **The plan's palette is read as sRGB-encoded colours** (`Color::srgb`), giving physically plausible linear albedos (rock 0.10, sand 0.54/0.39/0.20, grass 0.04/0.08/0.01). [INFERENCE]
5. **Slope and sediment thresholds get smooth ramps (37°–43°, 26°–30°, 0.3–0.7 m) and soil gets a 28°–34° band;** the plan states hard thresholds and names soil without a rule. Andersson §5.2.3 for ramped masks with detail.
6. **Water patches spawn for `TerrainRender::External`** (plan 3.1: "the crate simulates only"). [INFERENCE: floods must be visible; the consumer owns the ground only.]
7. **`Terrain` is anchored at its centre**, `Transform.translation.y` shifts all heights, rotation and scale are ignored with a warning (plan silent). `base_height_m` defaults to 0.
8. **Octave count is clamped by Nyquist** (`K = min(octaves, ⌊log₂(F/2c)⌋ + 1)`, default `octaves = 8`); the plan gives the field but no default. Musgrave §3.2.
9. **Pool capacity 192 entities per pool** (plan: "a pool of patch entities"). Measured (above).
10. **Heightmap formats:** `*Srgb` is rejected with a remedy; `R16Unorm` needs a native-only feature, `R16Uint` is accepted for wasm. Sources: `image_loader.rs:176`, `features.rs:633-649`.
11. **The generator's unit-map constants are `F_HALF = 0.33`, `R_LOW = 0.21`, `R_SPAN = 0.71` (not 0.37, 0.12, 0.76), and the preset table is re-blessed.** The note anticipated this (Model, "must be re-measured on the crate's own noise"): the crate's hashed 3D Perlin sliced at `z = 0.5 + k` has an rms of 0.136 for the fBm sum where the numpy 2D port had 0.176. Measured over 64 seeds × 128² points spaced 0.23 base lattice units apart (octaves 8, the stationary distribution: one 2048 m tile of a 600 m feature holds only 3.4 base cells, so a single seed's percentiles scatter by ±0.08; seed 42 gives `f` −0.347 / 0.256): `f` mean 0.0005, 0.5 / 99.5 percentiles ∓0.332 / 0.332 (1 % at ∓0.305 / 0.305), `r` 0.5 / 99.5 percentiles 0.212 / 0.917 (median 0.670, 1 % at 0.248 / 0.904). The constants are the rounded half range 0.33 and the ridged range 0.21–0.92. The unit test `the_noise_sums_fill_their_unit_ranges` pins them (±0.03) on 32 seeds × 96². The preset table is replaced by the crate's own seed-42 values at 2 m cells (CPU mirror, `the_presets_have_the_measured_statistics`; the numpy values in parentheses): hills spread 91.8 m (96.7), slope p50 / p95 12.4° / 25.5° (17.4° / 32.9°), > 40° 0.02 % (0.58 %); mountains 549.5 m (676), 41.1° / 61.9° (50.3° / 69.5°), 52.6 % (70.2 %); dunes 39.9 m (40), 9.1° / 18.6° (12.9° / 25.4°), 0 %; plains 13.7 m (14), 1.4° / 2.9° (2.1° / 4.2°), 0 %. The slopes are 25–30 % gentler than the note's table, because the crate's noise has smaller gradients at equal amplitude. Plan numbers (`relief`, `feature`, `ridged`) are unchanged, so `mountains` still starts steep (52 % of the ground above 40°) but less than the note feared; a user wanting more raises `relief_m`. `the_terrain_is_frozen` is blessed on this generator (FNV-1a `0x6f04892c64bcb5eb`, hills(42), 256², 1 mm quantisation).
12. **CDLOD: the ranges are derived from the morph-continuity bound, selection keeps whole patches, and Deviation 1's "`r_0 = 2·S_0` is enough" is incomplete.** What was found: with `m_k = 0.7·r_k` and plain doubling ranges, a level-`k` node that is about to subdivide (its box is within `r_{k−1}`) still has vertices beyond `0.7 r_k`, because the box is `√(2 S_k² + H²)` wide, so its far vertices are partly morphed when the finer children appear: a visible pop, and between the two a vertex of the coarser patch that has moved off the finer neighbour's edge: a crack. The CPU test found it at once (an edge vertex at z = 12.52 on one side, 16.00 on the other, camera 1 km away). The condition for a crack-free, pop-free transition is `m_k ≥ r_{k−1} + diag_k` with `diag_k` the diagonal of the largest level-`k` box including its height range, and `r_k ≥ m_k / 0.85` (Strugar §2.2.1: the morph covers at least the last 15 %). `LodConfig::derive` computes `r_0 = 2 S_0`, `r_k = max(2 r_{k−1}, (r_{k−1} + diag_k) / 0.85)`, `m_k = max(r_{k−1} + diag_k, 0.7 r_k)` from the readback's per-level height ranges (the tallest node of each level, padded 2 m), flat before the first readback, and the material uniform carries one `(m_k, r_k)` per level (16 levels). With these ranges two-level neighbours are impossible (a level-`j+2` node has `d ≥ r_{j+1} > r_j + diag_{j+1}` while its level-`j` neighbour's parent is within `r_j`), the 1000-camera test finds a maximum adjacent difference of 1, and the shared-edge vertices of adjacent patches are bit-identical. Strugar's partial quadrants (children outside `r_k` drawn as part of the parent) need a sub-mesh draw; a whole child patch beyond `r_k` is fully morphed, which is the same geometry, so the pool draws whole patches only (the counts below are of whole patches). Flat-terrain counts, camera 30 m up at the centre of 2048 m: High 124 `{L0: 16, L1: 60, L2: 48}`, Ultra 160 `{16, 44, 52, 48}`, Medium 76 `{16, 60}`, Low 28 `{16, 12}`; 300 m up at High 76 `{L1: 16, L2: 60}`; 1500 m up at High 16 patches, all level 3 (the note's 52 / 64 / 40 / 28 / 28 / 4 were for the plain-doubling ranges and the partial-quadrant rule).
13. **Pool capacity 256 (supersedes 9).** Worst case over 1500 random cameras (2–300 m up) per quality: Ultra flat 187, High 133 (flat, hills and mountains, generated heights), Medium 85, Low 43. A selection longer than the pool logs `warn!` once and drops the tail.
14. **The wind window's vertical origin is `4·⌊max(h_ground − 8, y_cam − 48) / 4⌋`** (the note had `4·⌊(h_cam − 8)/4⌋`). The second term keeps the camera in the 64 m window once it flies more than 56 m above the ground (the note's rule would leave a camera 300 m over a hill outside the field, reading the prevailing wind). `h_ground` is the snapshot surface under the camera, `y_cam − 8` before the first readback, and 0 (the plane) without a terrain. Without a terrain the old rule (camera − 16 m) is replaced by the same formula (floor `max(−8, y_cam − 48)`).
15. **Wind pressure: Dirichlet `p = 0` across the open window edges, Neumann only on walls and the lid** (wind.md had Neumann on every edge). Measured: over `hills(42)` the mean |div| 12 m above the ground was 2.8×10⁻² s⁻¹ with Neumann edges at 12 and at 30 Jacobi iterations alike (so not a convergence problem): a terrain that blocks more of the window on one side than the other leaves the net flux through the open edges unbalanced, and a pure-Neumann Poisson problem has no solution. With Dirichlet edges: 5.3×10⁻⁴ (4 iterations), 1.3×10⁻⁴ (12), 1.3×10⁻⁴ (30), the counts applied one after the other to one running field (300 frames each); fresh runs per count gave 3.2×10⁻⁴, 2.3×10⁻⁴ and 1.3×10⁻⁴; plains 1.2×10⁻⁴, flat 7×10⁻⁵ (cells 12 m above the ground, whole stencil in the air, 1600 cells; `tests/gpu_wind.rs` flat reference 9.6×10⁻⁴, was 8.4×10⁻⁴). Wind.md Deviation 1's iteration counts (High 12) stand: 12 is as good as 30 over hills.
16. **All layers of the hydrology contract are allocated by the terrain**, including the thermal-flux pair and the water-render pack (the note had hydrology creating them); their GPU buffers are zero-initialised without a CPU copy, and the assets stay in the main world (an uninitialised buffer holds only its size) so a caller can replace a layer's contents (the flood test writes `water`) and set `TerrainGpu::dirty`. `bedrock` is the generated surface minus `initial_sand_m`, so the surface of `dunes` is the generated field and the sand lies on lowered bedrock (plan: "sediment 4 m" without saying where the surface goes).
17. **The pack, minmax and readback kernels run together** (one pass, `elements_terrain`) in the first frame and in every frame in which something set `TerrainGpu::dirty`, instead of the readback pair running at the 4 Hz tick; the version counter then decides when the main world copies the height field and the leaf bounds back (so a terrain nobody changes is read once). `terrain_minmax` reduces each leaf with a one-cell halo: a vertex on a leaf's edge is a bilinear blend with the neighbouring leaf's first cell, and without the halo the padded box of the first mountain patch tested was exceeded by 1.25 m. The 2× downsample above 1024² is folded into `terrain_readback` (a `downsample` factor in the uniform), not a separate kernel.
18. **`GroundExtension` is forward-only and the M3 `external_ground` example shows cloud shadows**; the water darkening is in the shader (`1 − 0.5·smoothstep(0, 0.02, depth)`) but is not exercised yet: without hydrology nothing writes water, so neither the example nor a test shows it before M3.2. The terrain and ground materials do not bind `cover_cells` yet (binding 102 is reserved for M4, vertex + fragment for the owned terrain, fragment-only for the ground); the unit test counts one storage buffer each.
19. **The layer masks get wider ramps and Overlay detail instead of a ±3° slope perturbation (supersedes the ramps of Deviation 5 and the perturbation of Model 2).** Rock `smoothstep(35°, 45°, α)`, soil band `smoothstep(26°, 36°, α)`, sand `smoothstep(0.3, 0.7, sediment)` (centres unchanged: 40°, 31°, 0.5 m), each passed through Andersson's Overlay blend (`2md` below 0.5, `1 − 2(1−m)(1−d)` above; Listing 2 of §5.2.3, at full opacity) with the detail `d = clamp(0.5 + 0.75·fbm3(p/48 m, 4 octaves), 0, 1)`. Overlay keeps 0 and 1 and is the identity at `d = 0.5`, so the detail only breaks up the transitions and never moves where a layer is fully on or off (Andersson: "it does not affect areas where the base procedural mask is 0.0 or 1.0 so the base shape of the mask is kept"). Defect, `terrain_tour` with `mountains(42)` from 1.8 km up: the 6° ramps under a 20 m, ±3° perturbation drew rock, soil and grass as blobs with crisp iso-slope outlines; after the change they meet in irregular, mottled transitions (`/tmp/look2_top.png` against `/tmp/look_mtn_top.png`). Validation 7 now also checks that the weights sum to 1 for any detail, that rock at 50° and grass at 10° stay 1 under any detail, and that the detail spans 0 → 1 inside a transition (rock at 40°). Source: Andersson 2007, §5.2.3 and Listing 2 (corpus `s2007-advances-course-notes-1-6-mb`). The scale of the detail (48 m, 4 octaves) is [INFERENCE]. Most of the mountains' blobs were thermal erosion collapsing the generated faces live (erosion.md Deviation 15), not the blend.
20. **The repack request is an extracted epoch, not a shared flag (supersedes `TerrainGpu::dirty` in Deviations 16 and 17).** The main world set `dirty: Arc<AtomicBool>` and the render world cleared it after the pass; under pipelined rendering the main world's frame N+1 runs while the render world executes frame N, so a set landing before frame N's clear was wiped and that frame's change was never packed or read back. Every writer of the layers (hydrology, cover, dust, voxel ground, a test that rewrites a layer) now bumps `TerrainGpu::changes: u64` through `ResMut`; the resource is extracted by value every frame (`ensure_terrain` writes it each frame), and the render world runs the pass while `changes` is ahead of `packed` (an `Arc<AtomicU64>` it sets to the extracted `changes` after the dispatch). A later bump lives in a later extracted copy, so it can never be cleared by an earlier render frame. [INFERENCE: found by code review, not by a reproduced lost repack.]
