## Model adopted

**Scope and the one idea.** Three representations carry precipitation, each at the scale it is visible: (1) a GPU particle set in a 60 m camera-centred box for individually resolvable streaks, flakes and ground splashes (≲ 30 m); (2) an extinction term added to the ground-fog density texture for the shafts and haze in the 120 m-high near field (`fog.md` volume, 240 × 120 × 240 m); (3) the weather's `visibility_m`, which the distance fog already applies to everything farther. Langer et al. 2004 found that raising the particle count alone "does not look like dense snow" and that a sparse particle set plus a texture of the unresolved flakes does; term (2) is that texture, built volumetrically. Garg & Nayar 2007 give the same split: only drops closer than $R\,z_m$ are individually visible (below).

**Rain microphysics used.** Marshall–Palmer (Uijlenhoet 2001 Eqs. 9–11, Garg 2007 §3): $N(D) = 8000\,e^{-\Lambda D}$ m⁻³ mm⁻¹ with $\Lambda = 4.1\,R^{-0.21}$ mm⁻¹, $R$ the liquid-equivalent rate in mm/h; $\Lambda$ = 4.742 / 4.100 / 3.064 / 2.528 / 2.186 / 2.086 at $R$ = 0.5 / 1 / 4 / 10 / 20 / 25 (computed). Number density $N_T = 8000/\Lambda$ = 3 165 m⁻³ at 10 mm/h (computed). Terminal velocity: $v(D) = 3.778\,D^{0.67}$ m/s (Atlas & Ulbrich 1977 fit to Gunn & Kinzer 1949, valid 0.5–5 mm; it overshoots at the top, 11.1 m/s at 5 mm, so it is capped at 9.0 m/s, the plan's value and the Gunn–Kinzer plateau [INFERENCE for the 9.0 plateau: Garg's $200\sqrt a$ reaches 10 m/s at $D = 5$ mm, Atlas et al. 1973 as recalled gives 9.1; neither primary was read]). Check against the plan's 9 m/s: $v = 3.78$ / 6.01 / 7.89 / 9.0 m/s at $D$ = 1 / 2 / 3 / ≥ 4 mm, so 9 m/s is the speed of the largest drops only. Weighting by the water each size carries ($D^3 N$, i.e. a particle represents a fixed mass of water) the mean fall speed is 3.62 / 4.39 / 4.98 / 5.47 / 5.63 / 6.63 m/s at $R$ = 1 / 4 / 10 / 20 / 25 / 100 (computed numerically, capped $v$), and the share of rain water in drops over 4 mm is 0.01 % / 0.19 % / 0.95 % / 2.5 % / 3.4 % / 13 %. Consistency of the pair (MP + Atlas–Ulbrich) with the rate itself: $6\pi\times10^{-4}\int D^3 v N\,dD$ = 1.16 / 11.1 / 27.2 mm/h for $R$ = 1 / 10 / 25 (computed; +11 % at 10 mm/h, the known Marshall–Palmer bias of the filter-paper fit).

**Equal-water particles.** Each rain particle represents the same amount of water, so its diameter is drawn from the water-weighted size distribution $D^3 e^{-\Lambda D}$, a gamma distribution of shape 4 and rate $\Lambda$: $D = -\ln(u_1 u_2 u_3 u_4)/\Lambda$ (sum of four exponentials, exact, four hashed uniforms per respawn). Mean $4/\Lambda$ = 0.98 / 1.31 / 1.58 / 1.92 mm at $R$ = 1 / 4 / 10 / 25. The particle's fall speed is $v_i = \min(3.778\,D_i^{0.67}, 9.0)$, its streak width is $D_i$, and averaged over particles the fall speed is the water-weighted mean above (the quantity that makes the rate come out right). The particle set therefore renders the rain that carries the mass, which is also what a viewer sees as the bright, fast, thick streaks (Garg 2007: visibility of a drop $\propto$ size²).

**Active counts and the shared buffer.** `max` = quality maximum (20 k / 60 k / 150 k / 300 k). Fractions from the plan: $f_{rain} = \min(1, R_{liq}/20)$ with $R_{liq} = \text{precipitation\_mm\_h}\,(1 - s_f)$, $f_{snow} = \min(1, S/3)$ with $S = \text{precipitation\_mm\_h}\,s_f$, $s_f$ = `Weather::snow_fraction()`. The plan's formulas can sum above 1 (mixed phase: $R = 10$ mm/h, $s_f = 0.5$ gives $0.25 + 1.0$); the buffer holds `max` particles, so when $f_{rain} + f_{snow} > 1$ both are divided by their sum (0.2 and 0.8 in the example). Dust (M4) uses the same buffer: $f_{dust} = 0.4$ while $dust > 0$, joined to the same normalisation. Particle $i$ is rain if $i < n_r$, snow if $n_r \le i < n_r + n_s$, dust next, with $n_\bullet = \operatorname{round}(\text{max}\,f_\bullet)$; every other particle is inactive. Positions of active particles are uniform in the box at all times, so a particle that changes kind when a count moves keeps its position and only re-derives velocity and size. The draw is `draw(0..6, 0..(n_r + n_s + n_d))`. `Particle` is 32 bytes `{pos: vec3<f32>, kind: u32, vel: vec3<f32>, life: f32}`; `kind` packs the kind in bits 0–1 (0 rain, 1 snow, 2 dust, 3 inactive, so a readback can count active particles) and a respawn counter in bits 2–31, which seeds the per-respawn hash for diameter, flutter phase and flutter frequency, so nothing else needs storing; `life` is the age in seconds (flutter phase and splash timing). 150 k particles are 4.8 MB, 300 k are 9.6 MB.

**Motion** (one thread per particle, `elements_precip_sim`). Box: cube of edge 60 m centred on the followed camera (half extent 30 m). Per step: (1) activate: an inactive particle that becomes active is seeded uniform in the box by hash; a particle that becomes inactive is parked with `kind = 3`; (2) velocity: rain $\mathbf v = \mathbf u(\mathbf p) + (0, -v_i, 0)$, snow $\mathbf v = \mathbf u(\mathbf p) + (0, -1, 0) + \mathbf a\,0.3\sin(2\pi f t + \varphi)$ with $\mathbf u$ the `WindField` velocity (trilinear `textureSampleLevel`, `elements_wind` from `wind.wesl:25-37`, which falls back to the prevailing wind outside the field), $\mathbf a$ a hashed horizontal unit vector, $f \in [0.5, 1.5]$ Hz and $\varphi$ hashed (plan numbers; the flutter's displacement amplitude is $0.3/(2\pi f)$ = 9.5 / 4.8 / 3.2 cm at 0.5 / 1 / 1.5 Hz, computed); (3) integrate $\mathbf p \mathrel{+}= \mathbf v\,\Delta t$; (4) collide: below the ground height (plane $y = 0$ until M3, the terrain height buffer from M3 on) or inside any uploaded blocker, set `life = 0` and respawn at the top of the box with a new hashed xz; a rain particle that hit within 20 m horizontally of the camera appends a splash (below); (5) wrap toroidally: $\mathbf p \leftarrow \mathbf c + ((\mathbf p - \mathbf c + 30) \bmod 60) - 30$ per axis with $\mathbf c$ the camera position (plain world-space wrap relative to the moving box, so a moving camera keeps the density). With the camera at height $h_c$ above flat ground the particles live in the part of the box above ground, $H_{eff} = \min(60, h_c + 30)$ m, so the number density rises as the camera nears the ground: 75 000 particles at 10 mm/h are 0.66 m⁻³ at $h_c = 1.7$ m ($H_{eff} = 31.7$ m), against 3 165 m⁻³ physical (a factor 4 800: the particles are sprites for the resolvable drops, not a census; see "What the particles cannot carry").

**Wind and inertia.** The plan adds the wind velocity directly. A drop's horizontal velocity actually lags the wind by about $v_t/g$ ≈ 0.5 s (rain at 5 m/s) and 0.1 s (snow at 1 m/s) [INFERENCE: textbook relaxation time, no source read]; the wind field itself relaxes at 0.5 s⁻¹ (`wind.md`), so the lag would only smooth gusts. Not adopted (plan default); `Particle.vel` already holds the state if it is wanted later.

**Blockers.** `PrecipitationBlocker { half_extents }` + `GlobalTransform` = an oriented box. Each frame the nearest 256 to the camera are uploaded (`select_nth_unstable_by` on squared distance, ties by `Entity`), each as `{ rot: 3 × vec4 (rows of the inverse rotation), centre: vec3, half: vec3 }`, 80 B, 20 KiB in a read-only storage buffer (not a uniform array: 20 KiB exceeds the 16 KiB downlevel uniform limit, `limits.rs:563`). In the kernel $\mathbf q = R^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf p - \mathbf c)$, inside if $|\mathbf q| \le \mathbf h$ component-wise; blockers are tiled through workgroup memory (64 per tile) so each is read once per workgroup. A particle that arrives inside a blocker in one step is respawned in the same invocation, so no particle is inside any uploaded blocker at a frame boundary (the `gpu_precipitation` assertion). A respawn that lands inside a blocker (a roof above the box top) is respawned again next step. M4 reuses the same upload for snow and wetness shelter.

**Splashes.** One storage buffer `{ counter: atomic<u32>, pad×3, ring: array<vec4<f32>, 4096> }` (64 KiB + 16 B). On a rain impact inside the splash radius: `i = atomicAdd(&counter, 1u)`, `ring[i & 4095] = vec4(hit, birth_time)` with the hit point lifted 0.02 m. The counter is monotonic (wraps after 4.3×10⁹ impacts: 92 h at 13 k/s). Rate: $\dot n = N_r\,\bar v/H_{eff}\,\cdot (\pi 20^2/60^2)$: at High, 25 mm/h ($N_r$ = 150 000, $\bar v$ = 5.63 m/s, $H_{eff}$ = 31.7 m) 9.3 k/s, i.e. 2.3 k live at the ring's 0.25 s lifetime (57 % of 4 096); at 10 mm/h 4.1 k/s and 1.0 k live. Without the 20 m radius the 25 mm/h rate is 26.6 k/s and 6.6 k rings would be live, overflowing the ring (63 % of the intended lifetime); with the plan's 9 m/s and no radius 42.6 k/s. The radius is the design response; a 0.15 m ring is 7 px across at 20 m on a 1080p, 60° view ($0.15\cdot935/20$), and 4.7 px at 30 m (computed), so splashes further out are below what reads as a ring. Tatarchuk's budget is 5–8 000 splash particles per frame, the same order. The ring is drawn as 4 096 instances; an entry older than 0.25 s or never written (birth 0) collapses to a degenerate quad in the vertex shader.

**Rendering geometry** (`draw(0..6, 0..N)`, no vertex buffers). The vertex shader reads `particles[instance_index]` and builds the quad from `vertex_index`. For rain: head $\mathbf p$, tail $\mathbf p - \mathbf v_{rel}\,T$ with $T = 1/60$ s (Garg 2006: streak database exposure) and $\mathbf v_{rel} = \mathbf v - \mathbf v_{cam}$, the camera velocity of the followed view (Wang & Wade Eq. 1, without their damping $C_f$ because each streak is its own quad); both ends are projected and the quad is the screen-space capsule between them, half-width $\max(\tfrac12 D_i\,f/z,\ 0.5\ \text{px})$ with $f$ = focal length in pixels. The streak's image length is $|\mathbf v_{rel,\perp}|\,T f/z$ pixels: with $v = 5$ m/s, 1080p, 60° ($f = 935$ px) it is 39.0 / 15.6 / 7.8 / 2.6 px at $z$ = 2 / 5 / 10 / 30 m (computed), and the physical streak is 0.15 m long at 9 m/s, 0.083 m at 4.98 m/s. Snow uses the same path with a soft radial profile: diameter 4–8 mm (plan; consistent with $D_0 \in [1, 10]$ mm in Rasmussen Fig. 8) hashed per respawn, length $|\mathbf v_{rel}|T$ (1.7 cm at 1 m/s, so a flake is a slightly elongated disc), no motion blur beyond that. All geometry is camera-facing in screen space, so no tangent frame is needed (RTR4 §13.6 axial/world-oriented billboards).

**Coverage-preserving alpha** (Garg 2007 Eqs. 21–22). A drop of radius $a$ closer than $z_m = 2 f a$ covers more than a pixel and its per-pixel intensity change is independent of $z$; beyond $z_m$ it falls as $1/z$. Rendering the streak with the minimum half-width of 0.5 px and scaling alpha by the true coverage $\min(1, w_{world}f/z)$ reproduces that law: $\alpha = \alpha_0\,\min(1, D_i f / z)$ with $\alpha_0 = 0.25$ (plan). $z_m$ = 0.94 / 1.50 / 2.81 m for $D$ = 1.0 / 1.6 / 3.0 mm at 1080p, 60° (computed). Garg's visible region $z < R z_m$ with the streak at 1/20 of its near value at 30 m ($z_m / z = 1.5/30$) says the 30 m box half extent covers what contributes visibly. The physical time-coverage of a pixel by a streak is $\tau/T = D/(vT)$ = 0.019 at $D$ = 1.6 mm, $v$ = 5 m/s (Garg Eq. 21), so the plan's $\alpha_0 = 0.25$ is 13× the physical value. Tatarchuk's "milk trick" ("bias the computed raindrop colour and opacity toward the white spectrum … creates a perception of stronger rainfall") is the sourced reason to keep it; it is an art gain, not a measurement.

**Lighting.** A drop is a lens whose radiance is the refracted environment (Garg 2007 Eq. 5: $L = R\,L_e(\hat r) + S\,L_e(\hat s) + P\,L_e(\hat p)$, "drop radiance mainly due to refraction"), water does not absorb visibly, so the single-scattering form with albedo $\omega = 1$ is used (the same shape as `fog.md`'s volume, Hillaire 2015 slide 10): $L = \omega\,\big(E_{amb}/\pi + \sum_{i \in \{sun,\,moon\}} E_i\,p_{HG}(\cos\theta_i; g)\big)$ with $p_{HG}$ the Henyey–Greenstein phase function (integrates to 1), $E_{sun}$ and $E_{moon}$ the same direct illuminances the two `DirectionalLight`s carry (cloud-dimmed, `celestial/mod.rs:240,245`) and $E_{amb}$ the sky share of `scene_illuminance` (`celestial/mod.rs:269`) used by the fog colour, so fog and precipitation agree (`fog.md` Deviation 3). $g = 0.8$ [INFERENCE: forward-biased as the clouds' 0.8; no source read for raindrops]. Snow: two-sided diffuse flake, $L = \rho_s\,(E_{amb} + \sum_i E_i\,|\hat v\cdot\hat l_i|)/\pi$ with $\rho_s = 0.85$ (flora's snow albedo is $(0.80, 0.83, 0.88)$ in the plan) [INFERENCE for the shading form; Langer composites snow as constant white]. Lightning: if the `LightningFlash` resource (see `lightning.md`) is nonzero add $E_{fl} = I_{cd}/|\mathbf p - \mathbf x_{fl}|^2$ to $E_{amb}$ for that particle, and make the drop more transparent: Tatarchuk §3.6.2 biases droplet transparency by $1 - \tfrac12\,b$ with $b$ the lightning brightness, "to maintain the physical illusion of water", so $\alpha \mathrel{*}= 1 - \tfrac12\,b$ with $b = E_{fl}/(E_{fl} + E_{amb}) \in [0, 1]$ (the mapping from her artist brightness to $b$ is this note's). The result is multiplied by `view.exposure` (`view.wesl:80`): the main target is post-exposure, lit surfaces are multiplied by exposure inside Bevy's PBR shader, and unlit or custom output is not (`pbr.wesl:83-87`).

**Fades.** Soft particles over 0.3 m against the prepass depth (RTR4 §13.6.2): $\alpha \mathrel{*}= \operatorname{saturate}\big((z_{scene} - z_{part})/0.3\big)$ with $z = -near/depth$ for the perspective view (`view_transformations.wesl:169-171`, reverse-Z infinite far) from `textureLoad` on the `texture_depth_2d` view of the prepass. A fade near the camera, $\operatorname{saturate}((z - 0.15)/0.35)$, so a streak crossing the near plane does not pop (RTR4: "make billboards become more transparent as they get closer"). Atmosphere: the particles are drawn after Bevy's distance fog has already been applied to scene surfaces, so a particle at distance $d$ is multiplied by $e^{-\sigma_V d}$ ($\sigma_V = 3.912/V$, the same fog extinction) and not in-scattered (≤ 30 m, $\sigma_V d$ ≤ 0.4 at 300 m visibility, 0.04 at 3 km). Rain splashes lie on a surface: their soft fade distance is 0.05 m and the hit point is lifted 0.02 m, otherwise the 0.3 m fade erases them (Deviation 5).

**Pass placement.** The particle and splash pipelines draw in a render pass loading the main HDR texture (`LoadOp::Load` on `view_target.main_texture_view()`) in `Core3d`, `.in_set(Core3dSystems::EarlyPostProcess)`, `.after(cloud composite)`. `EarlyPostProcess` is after Bevy's `volumetric_fog`, which is `.after(MainPass).before(EarlyPostProcess)` and cannot be named (private module); drawing in `MainPass` instead would put the particles under the fog pass, which integrates fog to the *scene* depth, so a streak at 5 m in front of a wall at 100 m would be dimmed by the fog over 100 m. Blend: `PREMULTIPLIED_ALPHA_BLENDING` (output $(\mathbf L\alpha, \alpha)$), so overlapping low-alpha streaks are nearly order-independent and no sort is needed. Msaa is off on an `ElementsCamera`; a view whose `Msaa` is not `Off` skips the pass with a one-time `warn!` (the prepass depth would be multisampled). TAA, if a user adds it, also lives in `EarlyPostProcess` and is not ordered against this pass (not verified).

**Far field.** Extinction by precipitation (m⁻¹):

$$ \sigma_{rain}(R_{liq}) = a\,R_{liq}^{\,b},\ a = 2.12\times10^{-4},\ b = 0.68;\qquad \sigma_{snow}(S) = k_s\,S,\ k_s = \frac{3.912\ \text{m}^{-1}\cdot 1\ \text{mm/h}}{Vis_1},\ Vis_1 = 795.6\ \text{m} $$

$a, b$ are Ulbrich & Atlas (1985) through Kanazawa & Uchida 2025 (Q ≈ 1.16; Nedvidek 1986 has the same $a$ with $b = 0.63$; the geometric-optics upper bound is $3.64\times10^{-4}\,R^{0.63}$, derived in their Appendix A and re-derived here). $Vis_1 = 1.3\,C_3 V_t / S$ from Rasmussen Eq. 13 with dry-aggregate $C_3 = 0.017$ g cm⁻², $V_t = 100$ cm s⁻¹ and $S = 0.1$ cm/3600 s: 795.6 m, so $k_s = 4.917\times10^{-3}$ m⁻¹ per mm/h of water equivalent, linear in $S$ because $V \propto 1/S$ in that model. **One visibility authority.** The weather's `visibility_m` is already the total extinction the author wants (the presets include the precipitation: Rain 4 mm/h has 3 000 m, Thunderstorm 25 mm/h 1 500 m, Blizzard 200 m), and `fog.md` builds the distance fog and the ground volume's base term from it. Adding $\sigma_{rain}$ or $\sigma_{snow}$ on top would count it twice. The precipitation term therefore redistributes the same extinction into structured shafts:

$$ \sigma_p = \min\!\big(\sigma_{rain}(R_{liq}) + \sigma_{snow}(S),\ \sigma_V - \sigma_{floor}\big),\qquad \sigma_{haze} = \sigma_V - \sigma_p,\qquad \sigma_{floor} = \frac{3.912}{40\,000} = 9.78\times10^{-5} $$

with $\sigma_V = 3.912/V$; the ground-fog density becomes $\sigma(\mathbf p) = \sigma_{haze}\,e^{-h/H}\,(0.6 + 0.4 f) + \sigma_p\,s(\mathbf p)$ (the first term is `density.wesl:35` with $\sigma_V$ replaced by $\sigma_{haze}$; the second is new). Per preset (computed; $s_f$ from the preset's temperature offset, forced Snow and Blizzard are all snow): Drizzle $\sigma_V$ 7.8e-4, $\sigma_p$ 1.3e-4 (17 %), haze 6.5e-4; Rain 1.30e-3, 5.4e-4 (42 %), 7.6e-4; Thunderstorm 2.61e-3, 1.89e-3 (73 %), 7.2e-4; Snow 2.61e-3, 2.51e-3 (96 %, capped: dry-snow theory alone would be 7.4e-3, V = 530 m, against the preset's 1 500 m), haze at the floor; Blizzard 1.96e-2, 1.95e-2 (99.5 %, capped), haze at the floor. The Snow preset's visibility is therefore more generous than dry-snow theory (inside the observed scatter 0.65–1.4 km at 1.5 mm/h) and is kept; this is a finding about the preset, not changed here.

**Shaft modulation.** $s(\mathbf p) = \operatorname{clamp}\!\big(g(\mathbf x_{ref})/g_{nom},\,0,\,1\big)\cdot(0.4 + 1.2\,f_s)$ with $g$ the clouds' weather-map precipitation channel sampled bilinearly (map origin and extent from `CloudWeatherMap`), $g_{nom} = \operatorname{clamp}(\text{precipitation\_mm\_h}/25, 0, 1)$ (so $g/g_{nom}\le 1$ and is the local coverage), $f_s \in [0, 1]$ a 2-octave fBm of horizontal wavelength 120 m and 60 m (same `fbm3`, normalised like `fog.md`, advected by $\mathbf w\,t$), and $0.4 + 1.2 f_s \in [0.4, 1.6]$ with mean 1.0 for $E[f_s] = 0.5$ (computed), so the mean shaft extinction under full coverage is $\sigma_p$. Rain falls slanted: a drop at height $y$ left the cloud upwind, so the map and the noise are sampled at $\mathbf x_{ref} = \mathbf p_{xz} + \dfrac{\mathbf w_{xz}}{v_f}\,(p_y - y_{ref})$ with $v_f$ the mean water-weighted fall speed (1 m/s for snow) and $y_{ref}$ the volume's centre; at 7 m/s wind and $v_f$ = 4.4 m/s the shafts lean 58° from vertical. There is no height profile: rain extends from the cloud base (≥ 600 m) to the ground, so inside the 120 m volume the term is uniform in $y$. The 120 m and 60 m wavelengths are 64 and 32 texels at the 1.875 m horizontal texel of the 240 m volume, above the 4-texel floor of `fog.md`. Langer's three octaves of speed and $1/\omega$ amplitudes are the inspiration for the shaft spectrum, not a transcription (their texture is 2D image space).

**Volume optical constants.** One `FogVolume` carries one `absorption`/`scattering`. With the share of precipitation $\varpi = \sigma_p/(0.8\,\sigma_{haze} + \sigma_p)$ (0.8 is the mean haze modulation) the volume albedo becomes $\omega = (1-\varpi)\,\omega_{haze} + \varpi\cdot 1.0$ with $\omega_{haze}$ from `fog/mod.rs:228-229` (0.95 → 0.9 with dust), `absorption = 1 − ω`, `scattering = ω`; water and ice do not absorb in the visible (Kanazawa & Uchida §2.2, Rasmussen §3). The phase function stays Bevy's `scattering_asymmetry` (0.5, shared with the haze; raindrops are more forward than that, and a volume cannot carry two).

**What the particles cannot carry.** With 75 000 particles (10 mm/h, High) in the lower 31.7 m of the box the particle number density is 0.657 m⁻³. Their true cross-section is $L_s D \approx 0.083 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = 1.3\times10^{-4}$ m², so the extinction of the sprites is $8.7\times10^{-5}$ m⁻¹ (2.2×10⁻⁵ at $\alpha_0 = 0.25$) against the physical $1.02\times10^{-3}$ m⁻¹: the sprites carry 8.6 % (2.1 %) of the rain's extinction, and the fraction of pixels showing a streak is $n L_s f\,r_{max}^2/2 \approx 2.6\,\%$ per frame at 10 mm/h (5 % at saturation, computed). That is why the volume term exists. The sprites are not meant to sum to the physical optical depth.

## GPU mapping

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Far-field extinction coefficients and no double counting.** Plan: rain 0.0015 m⁻¹ per mm/h, snow 0.006 m⁻¹ per mm/h w.e., added to the fog density. Adopted: $\sigma_{rain} = 2.12\times10^{-4}\,R^{0.68}$ (Ulbrich & Atlas 1985, via Kanazawa & Uchida 2025; geometric optics $3.64\times10^{-4}R^{0.63}$ is the upper bound), $\sigma_{snow} = 4.917\times10^{-3}\,S$ (Rasmussen et al. 1999 Eq. 13, dry aggregates); and the precipitation term is taken out of the visibility's own extinction, $\sigma_{haze} = \sigma_V - \sigma_p$ with $\sigma_p \le \sigma_V - \sigma_{floor}$, so `Weather.visibility_m` stays the single authority. Reason: the plan's rain value is linear in $R$ and gives 261 m visibility at 10 mm/h and 104 m at 25 mm/h against 2.5–4.3 km and 1.4–2.4 km in the literature (table in Model, 20× too opaque at 25 mm/h); the plan's snow value is 22 % above the dry-aggregate theory, 2× the median of the nine published visibility–snowfall curves, and inconsistent with its own Blizzard preset (4 mm/h × 0.006 = 0.024 gives 163 m, the preset says 200 m); adding either on top of the visibility the presets already carry would count it twice.
2. **Rain drop sizes and fall speed per particle.** Plan: fall speed 9 m/s, width 1 mm. Adopted: each particle draws $D_i$ from the water-weighted Marshall–Palmer distribution (gamma 4, rate $4.1R^{-0.21}$ mm⁻¹), falls at $\min(3.778\,D_i^{0.67}, 9.0)$ m/s and is drawn $D_i$ wide; the plan's 9 m/s survives as the cap. The mean is 4.98 m/s at 10 mm/h (3.62–6.63 over 1–100 mm/h). Sources: Uijlenhoet 2001 (Marshall–Palmer; Atlas & Ulbrich 1977 fit to Gunn & Kinzer 1949), Garg 2007 (Gunn–Kinzer $200\sqrt a$). Reason: 9 m/s is reached only by drops over 4 mm, which carry 0.95 % of the water at 10 mm/h; a uniform 9 m/s also raises the splash rate 1.8× (22.5 k/s vs 12.5 k/s over a 60 m column at High), over the 4 096-entry ring.
3. **Splash spawn radius 20 m.** Not in the plan; added so the 4 096-entry ring is not overrun at High (26.6 k/s without it, 9.3 k/s with it at 25 mm/h; ring capacity at 0.25 s is 16.4 k/s). A ring of 0.15 m is 4.7 px at 30 m (arithmetic).
4. **Mixed-phase normalisation.** The plan's counts can sum above `max` (10 mm/h at $s_f$ = 0.5: 1.25); both are scaled by $1/(f_{rain} + f_{snow})$ when over 1. Clarification of a gap, no sourced number changed.
5. **Soft-particle fade 0.05 m for splashes.** The plan fades every particle over 0.3 m against the depth prepass; a ring lying on the ground is within 0.3 m of the surface everywhere and would vanish. Splashes use 0.05 m and a 0.02 m lift (RTR4 §13.6.2 principle: fade where the billboard intersects geometry).
6. **Pass placement.** The plan says "draws in the post-process stage after the cloud composite". Adopted: `Core3dSystems::EarlyPostProcess`, `.after(cloud composite)`, because that is the only position guaranteed after Bevy's un-nameable `volumetric_fog` (`volumetric_fog/mod.rs:100-103`, `lib.rs:55`) and before tonemapping; drawing in `MainPass` would let the fog pass dim near streaks by the fog to the scene depth.
7. **Streak direction includes camera velocity.** The plan says "stretched along the screen-space velocity"; here that velocity is $\mathbf v - \mathbf v_{cam}$ (Wang & Wade 2004 Eq. 1). Not a number change, recorded because it adds `ElementsView.velocity`.
8. **Two numeric corrections to this note's validation targets (no model change).** Validation 5 said the rain-rate integral is "within 12 %" above $R$ at 1, 10 and 25 mm/h; the computed values it lists, 1.16 / 11.08 / 27.2, are +16 % / +11 % / +9 %, so the bound is 17 % (a test would have failed at 1 mm/h). Validation 4 said the share of drops over 4 mm at 1 mm/h is 0.0001; the exact gamma tail is 6.65e-5 (0.0001 is one figure of it; the other three values match the tail to their digits).
9. **Streak opacity gain $\alpha_0$ = 1.5, clamped to 0.9 (note and plan: 0.25).** Measured with screenshots (Rain 4 mm/h and Thunderstorm 25 mm/h by day, Rain at night): at $\alpha_0$ = 0.25 with the coverage law of Model ("Coverage-preserving alpha") a 1.5 mm drop is 0.033 opaque at 10 m and 0.011 at 30 m, so the 30 000 streaks of a 4 mm/h shower are invisible against sky (nothing recognisable as rain in 1280 × 720 frames). At 1.5 (opacity $\min(1.5\,\min(1, D f/z), 0.9)$: 0.2 at 10 m, 0.07 at 30 m, 0.9 inside 1 m) the streaks read as rain at the same density; the form of the law (constant inside $z_m$, $1/z$ beyond) is unchanged. Reason for the gain: each sprite stands for ~4 800 physical drops (0.66 m⁻³ against 3 165 m⁻³ at 10 mm/h), so a single drop's physical contrast ($\tau/T$ = 0.019, Garg & Nayar 2007 Eq. 21) is not the right opacity for it; this is the "milk trick" of Tatarchuk §3.6.1, here set by eye.
10. **`ElementsCamera` really sets `Msaa::Off`.** The note and the plan assume it does; it did not: `Camera` requires a default `Msaa::Sample4` (`bevy_render/src/camera.rs:66`), so `dress_camera`'s `insert_if_new(Msaa::Off)` was a no-op and every `ElementsCamera` ran 4× MSAA (the precipitation pass logged its skip warning on the first run). `src/plugin.rs` now inserts `Msaa::Off` unconditionally; a game that wants MSAA inserts `Msaa` after spawning the camera, and the pass skips such a view with a one-time `warn!` naming the `Msaa` value (as the note says). MSAA support (a multisampled depth binding and a resolve) is not implemented.
11. **The draw pass fades at the sides of the box.** Not in the note: a particle's opacity is multiplied by $1 - \operatorname{smoothstep}(h - 8, h, \max_i |p_i - c_i|)$ with $h$ = 30 m, so the density does not end in a visible wall where the toroidal wrap re-seeds the box (screenshot of Blizzard and Thunderstorm: no visible edge).
12. **Splash ring profile.** The note leaves the ring's shape open ("expanding 0.15 m rings over 0.25 s"). Shipped: a Gaussian ring of width 0.1 R whose radius grows from 0.35 R to R and whose opacity falls as $(1 - t/0.25)^2$ (peak 0.6); a ring that starts as a filled disc (radius 0.05 R, width 0.14 R) showed as a white dot at the impact in the first screenshots.
13. **Fog uniform layout.** `precip.w` carries $g_{nom} = \operatorname{clamp}(R/25, 0, 1)$ (the CPU computes it once) instead of the weather map's texel count, which the kernel does not need (`textureSampleLevel` takes normalised coordinates). The weather map's extent is `precip.z` and its origin `slant.zw`; the neutral map is a 1 × 1 `rgba8unorm` image with green 1 at extent 1 m, origin 0, through one function (`precipitation_map` in `src/fog/mod.rs`) the clouds' `CloudWeatherMap` replaces.
14. **Dust is not part of this milestone.** Dust belongs to M4 (plan 4.3); the count function, kernel and draw shader handle rain (kind 0) and snow (kind 1) only, and kind 2 is reserved. The note's `f_dust = 0.4` joins the same normalisation when M4 adds it.
15. **`PrecipitationState` is public, and the draw pass has its own view marker.** The tests read the particle and splash buffers through `PrecipitationState` (`ReadbackOnce::buffer`), so the handles, the capacity and the active counts are a public resource; the buffer layout is in its rustdoc. The draw system's view filter is a crate-private `PrecipitationView` component added to every `ElementsCamera` (the note's `ExtractedElementsView`), so every such camera draws the particles around the one the simulation follows. The lightning flash term of "Lighting" is implemented in `draw.wesl` (`DrawParams::flash`) and fed from `LightningFlash` (the channel top at the cloud base and its intensity in cd) by `update_precipitation`. At the merge with clouds, the particles' sky light takes the same cloud factor as the scene (`sky_dimming`: the cloud shadow map's transmittance at the camera, `0.25 + 0.75·T̄`) instead of the M1 `cloud_transmission(cover)`, and the fog kernel samples the clouds' `CloudWeatherMap`, ordered after the clouds move its origin; with `CloudPlugin` disabled both fall back to the M1 factor and the neutral 1 × 1 map.
16. **The note's Bevy claims were checked against the vendored 0.20.0 source and hold:** `Core3dSystems` order and `chain_weak` (`schedule.rs:50-69`), Bevy's `volumetric_fog` is `.after(MainPass).before(EarlyPostProcess)` (`volumetric_fog/mod.rs:100-103`) and its module is private (`lib.rs:55`), `main_opaque_pass_3d, main_transparent_pass_3d` chain in `MainPass` and `tonemapping` is in `PostProcess` (`core_3d/mod.rs:162-166`), the downlevel uniform limit is 16 KiB and `VERTEX_STORAGE` is a downlevel flag (`wgpu-types-30.0.1/src/limits.rs:510, :1138`), lit surfaces are multiplied by `view.exposure` inside `apply_pbr_lighting` and unlit output is not (`pbr_functions.wesl:832-901`, `pbr.wesl`). One correction of fact: `ReadbackOnce` is removed in `First` the frame after it is spawned (`gpu_readback.rs:145-147`), so it must be spawned from a system in `Update`; reading a buffer back between two `app.update()` calls silently never completes.

Kept at the plan's values although the literature differs (art or unsourced numbers, no deviation): snow flutter 0.3 m/s at 0.5–1.5 Hz (no source found, [INFERENCE]); snow speed 1 m/s and diameter 4–8 mm (consistent with Rasmussen); box 60 m; particle maxima; count formulas; blockers ≤ 256; splash ring 4 096, 0.15 m, 0.25 s; soft fade 0.3 m for streaks and flakes.
