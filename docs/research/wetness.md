## Model adopted

**One idea.** Two per-cell scalars describe how wet the ground looks. *Wetness* $w \in [0,1]$ is the damp film a porous surface holds; *puddle* $p$ is free water, taken from the hydrology depth. They obey the same water: one wetness unit is a 2 mm film, and puddles begin exactly when the film is full.

**Wetness ODE** (kernel `wetness_update`, game time, per cell):

$$\frac{dw}{dt} = k_w\,P\,(1 - f_s)\,s_{rain}\,(1 - c_{snow}) - k_d\,\frac{E(u, T_{loc}, RH)}{E_0}\,(1 + \sigma_\odot),\qquad w \in [0, 1]$$

with $P$ = `Weather.precipitation_mm_h`, $f_s$ the per-cell snow fraction (`snow-cover.md`), $s_{rain}$ the rain shelter mask (0 under a blocker, below), $c_{snow}$ the snow cover (rain on a pack does not wet the ground), $k_w$ = `wet_per_mm` = 0.5, $k_d$ = `dry_rate_h` = 0.05 h⁻¹, $E_0$ = 0.1 mm/h and $E$ the shared function `evaporation_mm_h` (wind $u$ = `Weather.wind_speed_ms`, $T_{loc}$ the lapse-rate temperature, RH = `Weather.relative_humidity`), $\sigma_\odot = \max(\sin h_\odot, 0)$ the sun factor (`sun.altitude_deg`, as `weathering.rs:100`). Since $E/E_0 = (1+u/5)(1 + \max(T,0)/20)(1 - RH)$, this is the plan's equation term for term and `SurfaceWeathering::step` (`weathering.rs:78-84`) is the same ODE at one point. The drying term does not depend on $w$, so a wet surface dries at a constant rate until it hits 0.

**Why the three numbers belong together.** Rain at $R$ mm/h adds $k_w R$ per hour, so $1/k_w$ = 2 mm of rain fills the film. Evaporation removes $E$ mm/h of water, i.e. $E/2$ wetness units per hour; with $E_0$ = 0.1 mm/h that is $0.1/2 = 0.05$ h⁻¹ = $k_d$, exactly the plan's `dry_rate_h`. Wetness 1 is therefore a 2 mm film whose evaporation is the hydrology evaporation, and `puddle_min_m` = 0.002 m is that film: water deeper than the film holds is free water. Rain at 4 mm/h (the Rain preset) reaches wetness 1 at 0.5 h, the same instant 2 mm of free water has accumulated in a hydrology cell with no infiltration; this identity is asserted (Validation 3). The sun term $(1 + \sigma_\odot)$ is the only place the two evaporations differ: hydrology applies none (kept at the plan formula), wetness doubles drying at noon. FAO-56's radiation term supports a rise but the size depends on $R_n$: at 20 °C, RH 0.5, 3 m/s the plan gives 0.16 mm/h against FAO-56 hourly 0.106 (R_n 0, shade) / 0.211 (0.5 MJ m⁻² h⁻¹) / 0.317 (1.0), ratios 1.51 / 0.76 / 0.51, and against Penman 1948 0.330 mm/h a ratio 0.485 [all INFERENCE-based constants, see Sources]. The magnitude is right for a game and the plan's range is not narrowed. Drying times follow from the rate: table in Validation 1.

**Shelter** (kernel `shelter_mask`, run when the blocker set or the rain/snow direction changes, not every step). A ground point $\mathbf p$ is sheltered from precipitation if the ray from $\mathbf p$ toward where the precipitation came from hits any `PrecipitationBlocker` box (the 256 nearest, `precipitation.md` "Blockers"). Rain falls with velocity $(\mathbf w_{xz}, -\bar v_f)$, so the ray is $\mathbf p + s\,(-\mathbf w_{xz}, \bar v_f)$ with $\bar v_f$ the water-weighted mean fall speed $\bar v(R)$ (3.62 / 4.39 / 4.98 / 5.63 m/s at 1 / 4 / 10 / 25 mm/h, `precipitation.md`), snow with $\bar v_f$ = 1 m/s. The test is the slab method against the box in its local frame, the same as the particle kernel. Lagarde's rule — "a depth map taken from the rain direction can be used as a shadow map" — is the same idea; the plan's "0 under blockers" is the vertical special case (zero wind). Result `shelter: array<vec2<f32>>` (x rain, y snow, 1 exposed). Consequence of the slant: with wind $w$ along +x, a roof (half extents 5, 0.5, 5 at height 5 m, underside 4.5 m, top 5.5 m) shelters the ground band $x \in [-5 + 4.5\,w/\bar v_f,\ 5 + 5.5\,w/\bar v_f]$ (x from the roof centre, $|z| \le 5$): at 7 m/s and 4 mm/h ($\bar v_f$ = 4.39, ratio 1.594) that is x from 2.17 to 13.77 m, so the strip under the upwind half of the roof is wet because rain blows in under the eave, and the dry patch is displaced downwind; snow at 1 m/s and the same wind (ratio 7) is sheltered from 26.5 to 43.5 m. This is what the M2 particles do (they collide with the box and nothing else), so ground, particles and shading agree. The plan's "none under the blocker roof" in the `weathered_town` check therefore holds only for calm air (or for materials whose `exposure` is set by the caller); the example must run with wind near 0 (Deviation 1).

**Puddle mask.** $p = \mathrm{smoothstep}(d_{min}, d_{full}, d_w)$ with $d_w$ = `terrain_cells.z` (hydrology water depth, m), $(d_{min}, d_{full})$ = (0.002, 0.02) m. $p$ = 0, 0.156, 0.5, 1 at $d_w$ = 2, 6.5, 11, 20 mm. Tatarchuk's puddle depth mask and Lagarde's `AccumulatedWater` weight play the same role. With rain and no infiltration (plan 3.2) a flat cell collects 4 mm/h and shows the first puddle after 0.5 h, a 10 mm one after 2.5 h; a puddle of $d$ drains by evaporation at $E$ (0.16 mm/h at 20 °C, RH 0.5, 3 m/s): 12 mm in 75 h, so Lagarde's "puddles should not dry as quickly as the other area" holds by construction (and a hydrology note should confirm that no infiltration is intended; flagged).

**Shading, shared by all three extensions** (WGSL library `bevy_elements::surface_cover`, `load_shader_library!`, `bevy_shader-0.20.0/src/lib.rs:28`; flora cannot import it and keeps its own copy). After `pbr_input_from_standard_material` and before `apply_pbr_lighting` (`pbr.wesl:59-67`), with $w_e = \max(w, p)$:

1. **Porosity.** The extension has no porosity map, so use Lagarde's gloss-based estimate, mapping his $1 - \mathrm{Gloss}$ to Bevy's `perceptual_roughness` $r$: $\phi = \mathrm{saturate}((r - 0.5)/0.4)\,(1 - \text{metallic})$ [INFERENCE for the mapping: his gloss is Blinn–Phong based; the formula and the 0.5–0.9 span are his]. A rough wall or soil ($r \ge 0.9$) has $\phi$ = 1; plastic or glazed tile ($r \le 0.5$) 0.
2. **Albedo.** $\rho' = \rho\,(1 - 0.5\,w_e\,\phi)$ (plan). Evidence: a Lambertian surface under a water film follows $\rho_w = (1 - r_e)(1 - r_i)\rho/(1 - r_i\rho)$ with $r_e$ = 0.0659 (external diffuse reflectance of $n$ = 1.33, cosine-weighted Fresnel, computed) and $r_i = 1 - (1 - r_e)/n^2$ = 0.4720, which gives $\rho_w/\rho$ = 0.505, 0.518, 0.545, 0.575, 0.646, 0.792 for $\rho$ = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8 [derived here from the mechanism Jensen §2.1 describes; Lekner & Dorsvall itself not read]. The plan's 0.5 matches dark materials and is too dark for bright ones; Lagarde's empirical factor is 0.2–0.3 at porosity 1 (3b's 0.2, 2b's 0.3); his refractive curve gives 0.68 at $\rho$ = 0.12; flora's wood is 0.55. The plan's 0.5 lies between the physical film and Lagarde's empirical darkening and agrees with flora, so it is kept.
3. **Roughness.** $r' = \mathrm{mix}(r, \min(r, 0.1), w_e)$ (plan "roughness lerp to 0.1", never rougher than before). Lagarde's gloss ×2.5 (cap 1) is stronger; flora's bark goes only to 0.5; 0.1 is the plan value. Specular strength is not changed for the film: Lagarde judged water's F0 0.02 against a dielectric 0.04 "not visible enough in game".
4. **Puddle.** Where $p > 0$: $r'' = \mathrm{mix}(r', 0.02, p)$, reflectance so that $F_0 = 0.02$ (water, Lagarde): Bevy's $F_0 = 0.16\,\text{reflectance}^2$ (`pbr_functions.wesl:279`) gives reflectance $\sqrt{0.02/0.16}$ = 0.354 (the default 0.5 is 4 %), blended by $p$; the shading normal $N \leftarrow \mathrm{normalize}(\mathrm{mix}(N, \hat y + \mathbf r, p))$, with $\hat y$ the world up (standing water is level, not terrain-parallel; Lagarde sets the normal to (0,0,1) in tangent space) and $\mathbf r$ the ripple normal below. Water in a puddle shows the colour of the ground beneath (Tatarchuk slides L446), which is the already darkened $\rho'$ with $w_e$ = 1.
5. **Ripples.** While it rains, with intensity $f = \min(1, R_{liq}/20)$ (the same fraction as the M2 particle count): four layers à la Lagarde, layer $k$ with weight $\mathrm{saturate}((f - 0.25k)\cdot4)$; each layer's phase $\mathrm{frac}((t\,\mathrm{TimeMul}_k + \mathrm{TimeAdd}_k)\cdot1.6)$ is computed on the CPU in f64 and passed in `ElementsGlobals` (Lagarde's multipliers are not integer, so a wrapped f32 `globals.time`, which wraps hourly, `globals.wesl:4`, would jump); the ring profile is $\mathrm{DropFactor}\cdot\mathrm{ring}\cdot\sin(\mathrm{clamp}(9\,\mathrm{TimeFrac}, 0, 3)\,\pi)$ with normal strength 0.35. Rings are generated procedurally from a hashed 0.4 m world grid (one ring per cell per period 0.625 s per layer) instead of Lagarde's tiled normal texture, to avoid a texture binding and mip fading ("mipmaps make the ripples vanish"): the grid size and ring radius 0.2 m are this note's [INFERENCE]. Visible only where $p > 0$ and, like Lagarde's, only under a bright light.
6. **Rain streaks on vertical faces** (`WeatheredMaterial` only, while raining). Lagarde warns that water running down walls is "the exception, not the standard"; the plan asks for streaks, so they are subtle: with $m_v = 1 - n_y^2$ (vertical mask) and a streak noise $s = \mathrm{smoothstep}(0.55, 1, n(8x', 0.4y + 0.5\,t_s))$ where $x'$ is the horizontal world coordinate along the face, local wetness is raised to $w_e = \max(w_e, 0.6\,f\,s\,m_v)$. $t_s$ is a CPU-computed phase (period 8 s). The numbers 8, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6 are this note's [INFERENCE]; no source gives them.
7. **Snow** wins over wetness: with cover $c$ from `snow-cover.md`, apply wetness first, then mix toward `SNOW_ALBEDO` and roughness 0.9 by $c$ (flora order, `common.wesl:419-425`, "before the snow, which covers it").

**`WeatheredMaterial` and `ElementsGlobals`.** `WeatheredMaterial = ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, WeatheredExtension>`; `WeatheredExtension { exposure: f32 }` at `#[uniform(100)]`, 1 exposed, 0 sheltered; every weather effect is multiplied by it. The extension also binds the shared `ElementsGlobals` buffer, a `Handle<ShaderBuffer>` of four `vec4<f32>` (64 B) at `#[storage(101, read_only, visibility(fragment))]` (the flora precedent, `bevy_flora` `src/shading/mod.rs:505-509`, a shared storage buffer at binding 102): [0] = (wetness, snow cover, rain intensity $f$, spare), [1] = (cloud-shadow origin x, z, extent, enabled), [2] = ripple phases (4), [3] = (wind x, wind z, streak phase, spare). Wetness and snow cover come from `SurfaceWeathering`, i.e. the map average within 50 m of the camera once M4 is live (plan 4.1), the CPU point model before. The cloud shadow map is a separate `#[texture]`/`#[sampler]` pair, not a storage buffer. The fragment shader follows `pbr.wesl:59-67`: build `pbr_input`, apply `surface_cover`, then `apply_pbr_lighting` and `main_pass_post_lighting_processing`; the extension supplies a deferred fragment too (the base shader would drop its edits, `extended_material.rs:294-299`) and needs no prepass override because it changes neither alpha nor geometry (`extended_material.rs:76-80`); puddle and ripple normals are not in the prepass normal texture, so SSAO and SSR read the unrippled normal (a small error; override `prepass_fragment_shader` only if it shows).

**Where wetness comes from for terrain.** `cover_cells.y` is the sim wetness. `GroundExtension` and `TerrainExtension` read it with `terrain_cells.z` for the puddle; no `ElementsGlobals` is needed for wetness there, only for ripple phases and rain intensity.

**Limitations.** (1) Wetness has no soil moisture memory deeper than the film. (2) Melt does not wet the ground (the plan does not say it should; a melt term $k_w M_{mm}$ would be one line). (3) Blood wetness belongs to `bevy_carnage` and is not touched. (4) The wetness drying term has no cloud transmittance although the cloud shadow map is available; `weathering.rs` has none either. (5) No porosity map; per-layer values on terrain come from roughness.

| `wetness_update` | `wetness` rw, `terrain_cells` ro (z for $T_{loc}$), `snow` ro (cover), `shelter` ro | uniform `WetnessParams` (Δt, P, wind, T, RH, sun, Γ, settings) | `wetness` |
| `shelter_mask` (only when blockers or precipitation direction change) | `blockers` ro (≤ 20 KiB), `terrain_cells` ro, `shelter` rw | uniform (rain and snow fall vectors) | `shelter` |

At most 4 storage buffers per kernel, no storage textures. Memory at 1024² (computed): `wetness` 4 MiB, `shelter` 8 MiB, plus the snow buffers. Blockers are tiled through workgroup memory 64 at a time exactly as the particle kernel does (`precipitation.md`).

Per-pass cost, 1024², RTX 3090 [INFERENCE, to be recorded by `bench_elements` as `elements_wetness_*`]: `wetness_update` reads 8 B (wetness) + 16 (terrain) + 16 (snow) + 8 (shelter) and writes 4 B: 52 B/cell → 0.12 ms at 50 % of 936 GB/s, ≤ 0.04 ms mean per frame at the 20 Hz cap; `shelter_mask` is cells × blockers slab tests: 256 blockers is $1.05\times10^6 \times 256 \approx 2.7\times10^8$ tests ≈ 1.1 ms once (at about 30 flops each, 35 TFLOP/s peak with a 4× inefficiency allowance), 16 blockers 0.07 ms, and only when the set changes (a roof that moves every frame costs that every frame). `cover_pack` 0.08 ms (`snow-cover.md`).

Materials. The fragment stage has 3 free storage-buffer slots at limit 8 with an atmosphere camera (Bevy uses 5: `mesh`, three clustered-light buffers, atmosphere; `snow-cover.md` "GPU mapping" has the file:line trail, agreed by message with the terrain note). `TerrainExtension`/`GroundExtension` bind `terrain_cells`, `cover_cells` and `ElementsGlobals` = 3, no spare; `WeatheredExtension` binds `ElementsGlobals` only. A uniform `ElementsGlobals` would save the slot but a `#[uniform]` is per material: changing it every frame re-prepares every material's bind group; the shared storage handle (flora's choice) changes in place. The terrain note prefers a uniform; the cost is on record here and the decision is left to implementation (Deviation 6). On a device with fewer than 8 storage buffers per stage (limit 4, wasm downlevel) the fragment stage has none to spare: weathered materials need the storage binding and terrain extensions are not available there. Visibility of `ElementsGlobals` is fragment only; `cover_cells` is vertex+fragment (snow displacement), `terrain_cells` vertex+fragment.

## Validation targets

1. **Plan wetness check, time computed (`wetness` headless and GPU).** Rain preset for 1 game hour at 4 mm/h adds $0.5 \times 4 = 2.0$ wetness per hour, clamped: wetness is 1.000 at the end (reached at 0.5 h). Then Clear at 20 °C (pin `Climate` mean 18 °C, no amplitudes, Clear offset +2), RH 0.50 (Clear preset), wind 3 m/s (Clear preset), sun factor 0. The drying rate is $0.05 \times (1 + 3/5) \times (1 + 20/20) \times (1 - 0.5) \times (1 + 0) = 0.08$ h⁻¹, constant, so wetness falls from 1.0 to 0.1 in $0.9/0.08 = $ **11.25 h**; the plan's ±10 % band is [10.125, 12.375] h; assert wetness > 0.1 at 10.0 h and < 0.1 at 12.5 h and 0.1 ± 0.005 at 11.25 h. Sun factor 0 requires the sun below the horizon throughout, so run at latitude 85° N on 2026-12-21 (polar night, `GeoLocation`); with the sun held at factor 1 the time would be 5.625 h. Table of $0.9/\text{rate}$ in hours, 20 °C, RH 0.5, rows wind 0 / 3 / 5 m/s, columns sun 0 / 0.5 / 1: 18.0, 12.0, 9.0 / 11.25, 7.5, 5.625 / 9.0, 6.0, 4.5 (computed). The GPU mean over exposed flat cells and the CPU `SurfaceWeathering::wetness` agree within 0.01 at every half hour (same ODE, Euler step ≤ 36 s).
2. **Rain-wetness limits.** 1 mm of rain (1 h at 1 mm/h, with $f_s$ = 0) gives wetness 0.5 + (drying in that hour); the rain that exactly fills the film is 2 mm: wetness 1.0 ± 1e-3 when drying is off. Rain on a cell with snow cover 1 adds 0.
3. **Film = free-water threshold.** `WetnessSettings::default().puddle_min_m == 1e-3 / wet_per_mm` (0.002 m) and `dry_rate_h == 0.1 / (1/wet_per_mm)` (0.05) to 1e-9. 4 mm/h rain with no evaporation on a flat cell yields 2.0 mm of `water` at the same simulated time wetness reaches 1.0 (0.5 h ± one step).
4. **Puddle mask.** $p(d_w)$ = 0, 0.156, 0.5, 1 at 2, 6.5, 11, 20 mm (±1e-3), 0 below 2 mm.
5. **Shelter.** Wind 7 m/s along +x, Rain 4 mm/h ($\bar v_f$ = 4.39): ground at z = 0 under a roof box (half extents 5, 0.5, 5; centre y = 5): sheltered for x from 2.17 to 13.77 m, exposed for x < 2.17 (including most of the ground under the roof) and x > 13.77 (±0.1 m, slab-test CPU mirror and GPU mask). Wetness after 1 h of Rain at 7 m/s: 0 inside the band, 1.0 outside it; after 1 h at zero wind: 0 for $|x| \le 5$.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Shelter is a ray along the precipitation direction, not a vertical test.** Plan: "sheltered cells (blockers) get no rain", "0 under blockers". Source: Lagarde 2b ("a depth map taken from the rain direction"), consistency with M2 particles that fall slanted with the wind. With zero wind it reduces to the plan's rule; with 7 m/s wind and a 5 m roof the sheltered band is displaced 7.2–8.8 m downwind and no longer covers the upwind half of the roof's footprint, so the `weathered_town` example (plan visual check "none under the blocker roof") must run with wind near 0. Adds the `shelter` buffer and a mask kernel that runs only on change.
2. **Porosity from roughness.** The plan's formula uses `porosity` without saying where it comes from; `WeatheredExtension` only has `exposure`. Adopted: Lagarde 3b's gloss-based estimate, $\phi = \mathrm{saturate}((r - 0.5)/0.4)(1 - \text{metallic})$ (mapping of his gloss to Bevy roughness is [INFERENCE]).
3. **Puddle F₀ via Bevy reflectance 0.354.** The plan says "puddles: roughness 0.02". Source: Lagarde 2b (water F0 0.02, IOR 1.33) and `pbr_functions.wesl:279` (`F0 = 0.16·reflectance²`). Puddle normals are the world up, not the terrain normal.
4. **Ripples are procedural, not a tiled normal texture, with Lagarde's layer constants.** Reason: no texture binding or mip loss (Lagarde: "mipmaps make the ripples vanish"); ring grid 0.4 m and radius 0.2 m are this note's choice.
5. **Rain streaks are subtle and drive local wetness.** Source: Lagarde 2b caution that streaming water on walls "is the exception not the standard"; amplitude 0.6 and the noise scales are [INFERENCE].
6. **`ElementsGlobals` stays a shared storage buffer (flora precedent), with its uniform alternative and the 3-slot budget recorded.** Plan: "binds the shared `ElementsGlobals` storage buffer". The terrain note asked for a uniform; a uniform field of an `AsBindGroup` is per material, so a shared live value needs the storage handle. Decision left at implementation if the terrain material hits the 3-slot ceiling.
7. **Drying and wetting keep the plan's numbers; the shared `evaporation_mm_h` carries the factors.** No numeric change. Recorded because hydrology evaporation has no sun term and wetness has $(1 + \sigma_\odot)$: the gap is deliberate (plan 3.2 vs 4.1) and flagged, not resolved.
8. **Kernel rate ≤ 20 Hz.** Not in the plan; cost control.
9. **`ElementsGlobals` is five `vec4<f32>` (80 B), not four.** The cloud shadow map is sampled along the light, so the shader needs the light direction (`[4].xyz`) and the camera's mean transmittance (`[0].w`, the spare slot); the 64 B layout of the note had room for neither. `GroundExtension` carries them in its own uniform. The layout is `surface_cover::ElementsGlobals`, `weathered::ElementsGlobals::pack` and the test `the_globals_pack_as_the_shader_struct` keep Rust and WESL in step.
10. **Rain streaks are drenched rivulets on top of the film, saturate at 4 mm/h, and use a narrower noise window.** The note's `w_e = max(w_e, 0.6 f s m_v)` is invisible the moment the film is full: rain at 4 mm/h saturates wetness in 0.5 h (Validation 3), and `max(1, ·)` is 1 (seen in the first `weathered_town` rain shot, where a soaked wall showed no streaks). Adopted: the streak adds to the film, `w_e = min(w + 0.4 · min(1, f / 0.2) · s · m_v, 1.4)`, and `weather_surface` lets the albedo term run to 1.4 (×0.3, the 0.2–0.3 of Lagarde's empirical wet albedo in Model adopted 2, so a rivulet is "drenched" in his classes), while roughness saturates at the film's 0.1. (a) Amplitude 0.4 and a saturation at `f` = 0.2 replace 0.6 f: `f` = `min(1, R/20)` is a particle-count fraction and a wall streams fully at the 4 mm/h of the Rain preset, which gives `f` = 0.2 and would make the note's streak 0.12, not visible. (b) The noise is `n = 0.5 + 0.5 perlin3` and the window is `smoothstep(0.55, 0.8)`, not `(0.55, 1)`: `s` = 1 needs a Perlin value of 1, which the noise practically never reaches, so the note's window left rivulets at `s` of 0.3 or less, and with it the first measurement on a dry wall at `f` = 1 found 0 of 5600 pixels darker than the plain wall by even 2 levels (3 % contrast at best). With `(0.55, 0.8)` 395 of 5600 are darker by 4 levels or more in the sum of the channels (7 %), and 10 % of the samples of `rain_streaks_run_down_walls_only_while_raining` have `s` > 0.05. (c) The streak phase wraps at 8192 s, not 8 s: `perlin3` hashes its lattice modulo 4096 (`noise.wesl`) and the noise drifts 0.5 lattice units per second, so 8192 s is exactly one lattice period and the wrap is invisible (an 8 s wrap would jump). The numbers 0.4, 0.2, 0.55 and 0.8 are [INFERENCE] tuned by that measurement; the 1.4 cap is Lagarde's 0.2–0.3.
11. **Ripples: one ring per layer cell, a layer of weight 0 is absent, and the 0.35 is a bound.** Per layer the lattice cell is 0.4 m (shifted by (0.37, 0.23) · layer), its ring is centred within ±0.25 cells of the cell centre (a ring of radius 0.2 m never leaves its cell by more than 0.05 m), and the ring profile is Lagarde's `ComputeRipple` with the ring distance in place of his texture channel. Lagarde's `DropFactor = saturate(0.2 + 0.8 w − frac)` still draws drops at `w` = 0, so a layer whose weight is 0 returns nothing (Validation 7 says layers 2 to 4 are 0 at `f` = 0.2). The note's "maximum normal perturbation 0.35 ± 1e-3" cannot be attained: 0.35 is the multiplier, `DropFactor · x · sin(·)` reaches at most about 0.95 of it (analytically 0.944 at `frac` = 1/18, `x` = 1), measured 0.3267 over 4 layers, 20 phases and a 120 × 120 grid (`ripples_follow_lagardes_layers`); the test asserts ≤ 0.35 and > 0.15.
12. **Ripple and streak phases run on the virtual clock (`Time<Virtual>`), not game time.** The note says "t" without a clock. At a time scale of 300 game time would turn 0.625 s ripples into 2 ms flicker; the virtual clock pauses with the game and is in f64 on the CPU.
13. **Snow also takes `metallic` to 0.** The note mixes colour and roughness only; snow on a metal roof is snow, and a 1.0 metallic under a 0.8 albedo would render black. `reflectance` and the normal are unchanged.
14. **Scope of the shader against `pbr.wesl`.** `WeatheredExtension` has a forward and a deferred fragment (alpha discard, clustered decals, unlit materials and the visibility-range dither are kept) but not order-independent transparency, meshlet meshes or forward decals; the depth/normal prepass stays Bevy's, as the note says. A 3-slot fragment budget is untouched: the extension uses 1 storage buffer, and its cloud shadow is a texture/sampler pair at 102/103.
15. **The shared library is `src/surface_cover.wesl`, loaded by `ElementsGpuCorePlugin`**, so the import is `bevy_elements::surface_cover` as the notes say (a file under `weathered/` would be `bevy_elements::weathered::…`). `SNOW_ALBEDO` and `SNOW_ROUGHNESS` are public Rust constants so a consumer's own material can match.

Kept at the plan's values although sources differ: `wet_per_mm` 0.5 and `dry_rate_h` 0.05 (self-consistent as a 2 mm film, and `evaporation_mm_h` is within 0.5–1.5× of FAO-56 at the tested point); albedo multiplier 0.5 (between the film-physics 0.50–0.79 and Lagarde's 0.2–0.3, equal to flora's 0.55); roughness 0.1; puddle depths 0.002–0.02 m. Lagarde's advice that drying should take about a minute is a game-time statement; here drying is on game time (11.25 h at 20 °C, RH 0.5, 3 m/s, night) and is a few minutes of real time at the usual time scales of 120–600.
