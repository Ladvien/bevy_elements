## Model adopted

**State, per cell (SI).** $S = B_r + B_s$ the solid surface (`bedrock` + `sediment`, terrain.md), $d$ the water depth above it (`water`, m), $\mathbf f = (f^L, f^R, f^T, f^B)$ outflow fluxes (`flux`, m³/s; $T$ = row − 1, $B$ = row + 1, $L$ = column − 1, $R$ = column + 1; world +X = column, +Z = row), $\mathbf v = (u, v)$ the depth-averaged velocity (`velocity`, m/s), $s$ (`suspended`, erosion.md). Cell edge $l = $ extent/resolution (2 m for the 2048 m default at 1024²).

**Substep scheduler.** Each frame, in `ElementsSystems::Simulation`: $a \mathrel{+}= \Delta t_{real}\cdot\text{time\_scale}$ ($\Delta t_{real}$ from `Res<Time<Real>>`, independent of `WorldClock`, `Time<Virtual>` and pausing); $n = \min\big(N_{max},\ \max(0,\ \lfloor a/\Delta t + \tfrac12\rfloor)\big)$; $a \mathrel{-}= n\,\Delta t$; $a \leftarrow \min(a,\ N_{max}\Delta t)$. The remainder is *signed* in $[-\Delta t/2, \Delta t/2)$ so the long-run rate is exact; the last line caps the backlog at one maximal frame so a slow frame rate makes the simulation run slow instead of spiralling. Without the carry, rounding alone would give 60 substeps per second at 60 fps for a 50 per second target. *(Measured with the scheduler above, 10 s runs: 60 fps → 50.0 substeps/s, 144 fps → 50.0, 30 fps → 50.0, 15 fps → 50.0; time_scale 10 at 60 fps with $N_{max}$ = 8 → 480/s (9.6 simulated s per real s, capped), with $N_{max}$ = 2 → 120/s.)* The simulated clock is $\sum n\,\Delta t$.

**Water sources.** For every substep $d_1 = \max(0,\ d + \Delta t\,\rho_{cell} + q_{rain,cell} + q_{evap})$ is *recomputed identically* in both substep kernels (so it is never stored):

- *Rain.* $\text{precipitation\_mm\_h}\,/\,3.6\times10^{6}$ m/s, all phases, delivered per cell with the share that falls as rain: $(1 - f_s(T_{cell}))\cdot\text{mask}$, $T_{cell} = T + \text{LAPSE\_RATE\_C\_PER\_M}\,(S - \text{reference\_elevation\_m})$ and $f_s$ the 1-at-−1 °C, 0-at-2 °C ramp of `Weather::snow_fraction` (`weather.rs:159-163`), exposed as the WGSL fn `snow_fraction_at(t_c)` shared with the snow kernel (agreed with the snow-cover note: both sides split the same total, so ground above the freezing level gets snow and no rain, and nothing is counted twice). `mask` is 0 under a `PrecipitationBlocker` (the box test of `src/precipitation`: a cell is shaded if its centre column meets a blocker box above the surface) and 1 elsewhere, stored in `source_map.y` and rebuilt only when blockers move. Uniform rain over the whole terrain with the weather's rate; spatially varying rain is not modelled.
- *`WaterSource`, `WaterSink`.* `#[derive(Component)] #[require(Transform)] struct WaterSource { rate_m3_s: f32, radius_m: f32 }` (and `WaterSink`); position from `GlobalTransform::translation()` XZ; at most 64 of each are uploaded (the rest are ignored with one `warn!`, in `Entity` order, so the choice is deterministic). Each covers the cells whose centres lie within `radius_m`, but at least the cell containing the centre. The CPU counts the covered cells $N_i$ with the same f32 expression the shader uses and uploads $\rho_i = \pm\text{rate}/(N_i\,l^2)$ (m/s of depth, + for sources); a one-cell rim mismatch changes the delivered rate by $1/N_i$. The per-cell sum over all sources is written once into `source_map.x` by the `source_map_build` kernel (below), so no substep loops over sources. A sink removes at most the water present: $\max(0, \cdot)$ in $d_1$ clamps it, so a sink's *delivered* rate is $\le$ its rate; this and an open boundary are the only places volume leaves.
- *Snowmelt (M4).* The snow kernel writes metres of melt water straight into `water` (read-write, dispatched before the first substep of the frame). It must apply melt in quanta ≥ 5×10⁻⁶ m per cell, for the reason given next.
- *Quantised delivery.* An f32 depth of 0.5–1 m cannot hold a rain increment of 10⁻⁸ m: ulp(0.5) = 6.0×10⁻⁸, ulp(1) = 1.2×10⁻⁷, ulp(4) = 4.8×10⁻⁷. *(Measured: 1000 substeps of `d += rain·Δt` on d = 0.5 m: 4 mm/h and 1 mm/h gain exactly 0 (ideal 2.2×10⁻⁵ and 5.6×10⁻⁶ m); 10 mm/h gains 0 for d ≥ 1 m; 25 mm/h, the Thunderstorm preset, gains 0 for d = 4 m.)* So rain on a lake or a flooded valley would silently vanish. The CPU keeps $P$ (f64, m): $P \mathrel{+}= \text{rate}\cdot\Delta t_{sim}$ per substep and, on the *first substep of a frame*, if $P \ge 5\times10^{-6}$ m it uploads the quantum $q = P$ (applied to every wet-or-dry cell as $q\cdot\text{mask}\cdot(1 - f_s)$ in both kernels of that substep) and sets $P = 0$; otherwise $q = 0$. Delivered rain is exact in f64 over time and the f32 error is at most half an ulp per *quantum*, not per substep: a 5×10⁻⁶ m quantum onto 0.5–2 m of water has a relative error of 0.14 % and 4.6 % at 8 m, 14 % at 20 m *(measured, 1000 applications)*. At 20 mm/h a quantum arrives every 0.9 s (45 substeps). Two uniform buffers with identical layout, one with the quantum and one with zero, give two bind groups so substeps 1…n−1 of a frame pass no rain. The same accumulator mechanism carries evaporation (below).

**Pipe model (Mei 2007), with the pipe area taken from the local depth.** Per pipe from cell $i$ to a neighbour $j$ ($\eta = S + d_1$):

$$ h_f = \min\!\Big(\max\big(\max(\eta_i,\eta_j) - \max(S_i,S_j),\ 0\big),\ h_{cap}\Big),\qquad h_{cap} = \frac{0.16\,l^2}{g\,\Delta t^2} $$

$$ f^k \leftarrow \max\!\Big(0,\ \frac{f^k + \Delta t\,g\,h_f\,(\eta_i - \eta_j)}{1 + \Delta t\,g\,n^2\,\bar v\,/\,\max(h_f,\ d_{wet})^{4/3}}\Big)\ \ (f^k = 0\text{ if } h_f = 0),\qquad \bar v = \tfrac12(|\mathbf v_i| + |\mathbf v_j|) $$

with $g$ = 9.81, $n$ = `manning_n` = 0.035 s m⁻¹ᐟ³, $d_{wet} = 10^{-4}\,l$. Mei's Eq. 2 is the same with the constant $A\,g/l$ replaced by $g\,h_f$, i.e. *a pipe of cross-section $A = l\,h_f$*, which is the plan's "A = l" with the missing depth restored (the plan's $A = l$ is $h_f = 1$ m numerically). The numerator is the LInA update of Cozzolino Eq. 19 (with $\lambda = 1$) written for the flux $f = l\,q$; the denominator is Manning friction coupled through $|\mathbf v|$ of the two cells (Cozzolino: coupled friction preferred; the cell speeds are already stored, so coupling costs two extra reads). Then Mei's scaling and update:

$$ K = \min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{d_1\,l^2}{\Delta t\sum_k f^k}\Big),\qquad f^k \leftarrow K f^k,\qquad d_2 = \max\!\Big(0,\ d_1 + \frac{\Delta t}{l^2}\big(\textstyle\sum_{in} f - \sum_k f^k\big)\Big) $$

$$ u = \frac{\tfrac12\big(f^R_{x-1} - f^L_x + f^R_x - f^L_{x+1}\big)}{l\,\bar d},\quad v = \frac{\tfrac12\big(f^B_{y-1} - f^T_y + f^B_y - f^T_{y+1}\big)}{l\,\bar d},\quad \bar d = \tfrac12(d_1 + d_2) $$

with $\mathbf v = 0$ where $\bar d < d_{wet}$ and $\mathbf v \leftarrow \mathbf v\,\min(1,\ 0.5\,l/(\Delta t\,|\mathbf v|))$ (Chentanez §2.1.4–§2.1.5: $\epsilon = 10^{-4}\Delta x$, $\alpha = 0.5$). The $\max(0, d_2)$ only absorbs f32 rounding (it never fires in the emulation: minimum depth 0 over 2000 substeps) and the K factor guarantees $\sum_k f^k\Delta t \le d_1 l^2$, so no collect-back pass is needed (Mei §3.2.1).

*Why not the plan's constant $A = l$.* In 1-D linearised form the model is $\eta_{tt} = (A g/l)\,\eta_{xx}$: the wave speed is $c = \sqrt{A g/l}$, **independent of the water depth**. With the plan's $A = l$, $c = \sqrt g = 3.13$ m/s at every depth. *(Measured, Gaussian pulse σ = 12 cells on flat water, plan model: 2.88, 2.91, 2.91, 2.86 m/s at depths 0.05, 0.5, 2, 10 m, against shallow-water $\sqrt{g d}$ = 0.70, 2.21, 4.43, 9.90; with $A = l\,h_f$: 0.65, 1.97, 4.01, 8.84, i.e. 0.89–0.93 of $\sqrt{g d}$, the shortfall being numerical dispersion of the 24-cell pulse.)* Worse, the acceleration is depth-independent, so a thin film is accelerated as hard as a deep river: with no friction a 1 mm sheet leaves its cell every step and the speed saturates at $l/\Delta t$ = 100 m/s. *(Measured: the plan model on a 2 % V-valley with 50 m³/s: max speed 100 m/s throughout; on the 1 m-deep column in a 6 m paraboloid bowl, 22.9 m/s at 0.28 m depth.)* Erosion capacity is $\propto |\mathbf v|$ (erosion.md), so the plan model would erode on a speed that is two orders of magnitude off; and the `flood` expectation fails: the plan model's depth at a marker rises to a bore of 1.4–2.4 m and then **falls back to 0.11–0.13 m** *(measured: first > 0.5 m at 6, 12, 24, 48 s for markers 20, 40, 80, 160 m downstream; running-max minus current up to 1.3, 1.4, 1.7, 2.3 m)* because frictionless water keeps accelerating, spreads thin and fast, and the steady depth is $Q/(W v)$ with $v$ at its cap. Manning's formula for the same V-valley (side slope 1:3.3, bed slope 2 %, $n = 0.035$) gives a steady depth of 1.97 m and 3.9 m/s. With $A = l\,h_f$ and friction *(measured, same valley, 256²)*: steady depth 1.45 m at all four markers (first > 0.5 m at 7, 14, 26, 50 s), never below 0.69 m after the bore, speed at the front ≈ 3 m/s. Terminal speed of a sheet follows Manning, $v = h^{2/3}S^{1/2}/n$: 0.09 m/s for 1 mm on a 10 % slope, 0.46 m/s for 5 mm on 30 %.

*Why the pipe model, not Chentanez & Müller or Kass & Miller.*

| | virtual pipes (this note, Mei) | Chentanez & Müller 2010 | Kass & Miller 1990 |
|---|---|---|---|
| volume | exact by construction: every flux subtracted from one cell is added to its neighbour; K keeps $d \ge 0$ | exact in the height integration (upwind $\bar h$), lost in coupling and overshoot reduction (§3) | not exact: needs a connected-region projection each step |
| per-cell work | 2 gather dispatches, 4 neighbours, no atomics | MacCormack velocity advection with fallback, depth upwind, PML layers, overshoot reduction, particles | tridiagonal solve per row then per column: serial along a row, needs cyclic reduction ($\log_2 1024 = 10$ passes × 2 directions) |
| time step | explicit, $\Delta t \le l/(2\sqrt{g h})$ (derived below), safe with the cap | explicit with clamps (α, β), 16.7 ms | implicit, unconditionally stable, step = frame time |
| wet/dry | native (K, $h_f = 0$ blocks flow) | reflective-face rule $\epsilon$, depth clamps | $h_i < b_i$ repaired by projection |
| momentum / vortices | no advective momentum; fluxes persist (local inertia) — "vortices … are not present" (Chentanez §1.1) | full SWE with advection | linearised about constant depth, no convective term |
| velocity field for erosion | from the fluxes (Mei Eq. 8–9) | explicit | only from $\partial h/\partial t$ [INFERENCE] |
| cost per 1024² step | ≈ 150 B/cell (below) | ≥ 4 kernels incl. an advection with 4 gathers per component; reported 0.75 ms HF at a small grid on a GTX 480 | 20+ dispatches |

The pipe model is kept: it is the only candidate that is exactly volume-conserving, gather-only (no atomics, no reductions) and already provides the velocity erosion needs, at two dispatches per substep. What it lacks (vortices, accurate momentum at bends, foam from breaking) is out of scope for a heightfield flood. Depth-dependent pipe area plus Manning friction turn it into the local-inertia scheme of de Almeida & Bates, whose accuracy for flood inundation Bates et al. 2010 report; that is the one change to the plan's flow model.

**Boundaries.** *Closed* (Mei): the four outward pipes of an edge cell are 0. *Open* (default): an edge cell's outward pipes see a ghost neighbour with $S_j = S_i$ and $d_j = 0$, so $\Delta\eta = d_1$, $h_f = d_1$: water leaves as a free overfall at the edge cell's own bed level, at a rate set by its depth, and nothing enters from outside. Sediment leaves with it (erosion.md). The open edge is not a perfectly absorbing layer (Chentanez §2.1.4 uses 10-cell PMLs for open ocean); at 2 m cells a valley outlet needs only the free overfall, and a wave reflected from a high rim is physical.

**Evaporation (kept as in the plan).** $E\ [\mathrm{mm/h}] = 0.1\,(1 + u_{10}/5)\,(1 + \max(T, 0)/20)\,(1 - RH)$ with $u_{10}$ = `Weather::wind_speed_ms`, $T$ = `temperature_c`, $RH$ = `relative_humidity` (0–1); one shared function `evaporation_mm_h(wind_ms, temperature_c, relative_humidity)` (public, `src/hydrology/model.rs`, also called by wetness in M4). Check against the FAO-56 Penman–Monteith reference ET (daily means, the equation recalled above with $R_n$ the day-mean net radiation, $G$ = 0, $P$ = 101.3 kPa) *(computed here)*:

A temperature-only check with Oudin's formula (KarstMod Eq. 3, $R_e$ = 41/25/10 MJ m⁻² d⁻¹ for a mid-latitude summer, spring, winter day, defaults $K_1$ = 100, $K_2$ = 5 [INFERENCE]): 5.02, 1.73, 0.20 mm/day against 4.32, 1.92, 0.86 for the plan. No scenario is off by an order of magnitude except fog (0.04 mm/day, which is simply "no evaporation in fog"); the formula is kept. It has **no sun term**: the plan's formula and the PM comparison both average over day and night, and the wetness code (`SurfaceWeathering`) that multiplies by `1 + sun_factor` must not be applied on top of it by hydrology. Delivery uses the same quantum accumulator as rain: $E\,\Delta t$ = 0.18 mm/h × 0.02 s = 10⁻⁹ m per substep is far below the ulp of any depth above 10 mm, so the CPU accumulates and applies $\min(d, q_{evap})$ in $d_2$ (only to wet cells) once $\ge 5\times10^{-6}$ m (every ≈ 100 s at 0.18 mm/h). Skipped when `HydrologySettings::evaporation` is false.

**Stability (derived, then checked).** Linearise about depth $h$: $\eta_t = (f_{i-1/2} - f_{i+1/2})/l^2$, $f_t = g\,h\,(\eta_i - \eta_j)$. Then $\eta_{tt} = (g h/l^2)\,\delta^2\eta$ with $c^2 = g h$; the 2-D five-point Laplacian has $|\delta^2| \le 8$ (checkerboard), so $\omega_{max}^2 = 8 g h/l^2$, and the semi-implicit (symplectic) update is stable for $\omega\,\Delta t \le 2$. That gives $\Delta t \le l/\sqrt{2 g h}$ *for signed fluxes*. Mei's pipes are one-way ($f \ge 0$ per direction): across a sign reversal the pair $(f^{i\to j}, f^{j\to i})$ changes by $2\,\Delta t\,g\,h\,|\Delta\eta|$, doubling the stiffness, so $\omega_{max}^2 = 16\,g h/l^2$ and

$$ \Delta t \ \le\ \frac{l}{2\sqrt{g\,h}}\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad h \ \le\ \Big(\frac{l}{2\Delta t}\Big)^2\frac{1}{g}. $$

*(Measured on a 64² closed flat tank with random 0.1 % noise, 300 steps: the signed-flux linear system is stable at Δt = 0.44 s and explodes at 0.46 (predicted $l/\sqrt{2g}$ = 0.4515 for $l$ = 2 m); the one-way clamped model with constant $A = l$ is stable at 0.30 and unstable from 0.32–0.34 (predicted $l/(2\sqrt g)$ = 0.319); with $A = l\,h_f$: $h$ = 1 m stable up to 0.319 and unstable at 0.351; $h$ = 10 m stable up to 0.101, unstable at 0.111 (predicted 0.319 and 0.101).)* The plan's hint $\Delta t \le l/\sqrt{g\,d_{max}}$ is the *physical* CFL for the wave speed; the stability limit above is 2× tighter because of the one-way pipes. The depth cap $h_{cap} = 0.16\,l^2/(g\Delta t^2)$ makes the model stable at *any* $\Delta t$ and $l$: it pins $c\,\Delta t/l \le 0.4$, i.e. $\omega\,\Delta t \le 1.6 < 2$ (a margin of 0.8), by letting water deeper than $h_{cap}$ propagate waves at the capped speed (Chentanez §2.1.5 does the same with $\beta$ = 2). So `step_s` needs no clamp and the plan's 0.02 is kept.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low (256²) | 8 m | 1.28, 0.40, 0.18 s | 4077 m | 2610 m | 200 m/s |
| Medium (512²) | 4 m | 0.64, 0.20, 0.09 s | 1019 m | 652 m | 100 m/s |
| High (1024²) | 2 m | 0.32, 0.10, 0.045 s | 255 m | 163 m | 50 m/s |
| Ultra (2048²) | 1 m | 0.16, 0.050, 0.023 s | 64 m | 41 m | 25 m/s |

At Ultra with 0.02 s the stable depth is 64 m: a 100 m lake would exceed it, which is why the cap exists (its waves then travel at 20 m/s instead of 31 m/s, with no visible consequence). Mei's CFL remark, $\Delta t\,|\mathbf v| \le l$, is what the speed cap enforces with a factor 2: $0.5\,l/\Delta t$ = 50 m/s at High. The cap is active in practice only at wet fronts and the open edge (*measured:* maximum speed over a 120 s flood run 50.0 m/s at some front cell, 19.3 m/s at the end of the run; Manning sheet flow stays below 2 m/s).

**Conservation analysis.** Water volume $V = l^2\sum d$ changes only by rain, sources, sinks, evaporation and the open-edge outflow: $V(t) = V_0 + \int(\text{rain} + \text{sources} - \text{sinks}_{delivered} - \text{evap} - \text{edge})\,dt$. Inside the loop the pair of kernels is a matched pair: the flux leaving $i$ through pipe $k$ is the same f32 value that $j$ adds, so the sum over cells of $\Delta d$ is zero up to the rounding of the per-cell sum. The only sources of drift are f32 rounding and the $\max(0,\cdot)$ clamp, which can only *add* water at the rounding level. *(Measured, 256², $l$ = 2 m, closed, no rain, a 1 m column of radius 8 cells (208 cells, $V_0$ = 832 m³) in a paraboloid bowl $b = 6\,\mathrm{m}\,(r/128)^2$: relative drift of $V$ after 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 substeps = −1.5×10⁻⁷, −1.8×10⁻⁷, −1.8×10⁻⁷, −2.0×10⁻⁷ for the adopted model; −2.2×10⁻⁷ with $A = l\,h_f$ and no friction; −3.5×10⁻⁸ for the plan's constant-$A$ model; the minimum depth is exactly 0 in every run, i.e. the clamp created no water.)* The plan's 0.1 % is met by a factor of 5000. With `Open`, the edge outflow cannot be separated from rounding on the GPU without a ninth storage buffer, so the budget is closed on the CPU: lost volume = initial + delivered − evaporated − current (`HydrologyStats::lost_m3`).

**Readback to `EnvironmentSnapshot` (`water_depth`, `water_velocity`).** Every `ElementsReadback::interval` (250 ms) two requests through `Readbacks::request(key, handle, decode)` (`src/gpu/readback.rs`): `"water_depth"` of the `water` buffer (4 MiB at 1024²; at 2048² a 2× mean `downsample` dispatch into a scratch buffer first, as the plan prescribes for fields wider than 1024²) and `"water_velocity"` of a half-resolution `array<vec2<f32>>` (512² × 8 B = 2 MiB at 1024²) written by a `downsample_velocity` kernel that averages each 2×2 block *weighted by depth*, $\bar{\mathbf v} = \sum d\,\mathbf v/\sum d$, and is 0 where $\sum d < 10^{-3}$ m. Together 6 MiB per 250 ms = 24 MiB/s (`ReadbackOnce::buffer`, `gpu_readback.rs:134`). `EnvironmentSnapshot` gains `water: Option<WaterSnapshot { origin: Vec2, cell_m: f32, size: UVec2, depth: Vec<f32>, vel_size: UVec2, velocity: Vec<Vec2> }>`. `Environment::water_depth(Vec2) -> Option<f32>` is a bilinear sample of `depth`, `None` before the first readback and outside the terrain extent, `Some(0.0)` on dry ground; `water_velocity(Vec2) -> Option<Vec2>` the bilinear sample of `velocity` with the same `None` rule. Both are one interval plus a frame or two old (`snapshot_age`). The state "live" is published only after the first real dispatch (`WindGpu::live` pattern), so a cold pipeline returns `None`, not zeros.

**Bake and settings.** `HydrologySettings { step_s: 0.02, time_scale: 1.0, boundary: Open, evaporation: true, manning_n: 0.035 }` (`init_resource`, `Reflect`; `manning_n` is the one new field, below). `ErodeTerrain` / `TerrainBaked` and the substep rule during a bake are in erosion.md; during a bake rain comes from the message through the same quantum accumulator. A small `HydrologyStats` resource (`substeps: u64`, the simulated seconds, and `lost_m3: f64` = delivered input − evaporation − current volume + initial volume, from the depth readback) lets tests and the `flood` example wait on simulated time and report how much water left through an `Open` boundary or a sink; it is filled by the same readback tick, so it is up to one interval old.

**3.4 Wiring.** (a) The wind solid mask binds the terrain surface $B_r + B_s$ (the `height` read of the wind `mask` kernel, which already has a slot for it, `wind.md`): *not* the water surface, so wind does not see a lake as a solid. (b) Precipitation collides with the terrain surface from the same buffer (a particle is removed when it falls below $S$); water depth does not stop rain. (c) `ElementsFloraPlugin` writes `FloraGround` from the height snapshot (terrain.md owns `terrain_height`). (d) Hydrology idles when there is no `Terrain`, and its kernels dispatch only when all of `flux`, `depth_erode`, `thermal_flux`, `thermal_apply`, `source_map_build`, `downsample`, `downsample_velocity` are `ready` (`src/gpu/kernel.rs`). (e) Weather rain is read from `Res<Weather>` and the `ElementsQuality` resource (`Option<Res<_>>` where it may be absent). (f) The `Environment` terrain and water methods are `terrain_height`, `water_depth`, `water_velocity`.

## GPU mapping

Domain: the terrain grid (`Grid2 { size, cell_m, origin }`), row-major, 2-D kernels `@workgroup_size(8, 8, 1)` (64 invocations; 1024² → 128×128 = 16 384 groups per dispatch, 2048² → 256×256, both ≤ 65 535 per dimension, `limits.rs:456`). All buffers `storage_buffer(...)` of `src/gpu/grid.rs` (`STORAGE | COPY_SRC | COPY_DST`, `RENDER_WORLD`).

Buffers (shared contract with terrain.md / water-rendering.md; M4 uses `bedrock`, `sediment`, `water`): `bedrock` f32, `sediment` f32 (terrain owns), `water` f32, `flux` `vec4<f32>` (L, R, T, B), `velocity` `vec2<f32>`, `suspended` f32, `transport` `vec2<f32>` ($(\sin\alpha, s/d_1)$ written by `flux`), `source_map` `vec2<f32>` ($(\rho_{cell}\ \mathrm{m/s}, \text{rain mask})$), `tflux_a`, `tflux_b` `vec4<f32>`, plus scratch for the readback downsample. At 1024²: 88 MiB in all (56 MiB layers + 32 MiB thermal), 352 MiB at 2048² with the largest buffer `flux` at 64 MiB (< 128 MiB binding, `limits.rs:441`; < 256 MiB buffer, `limits.rs:443`).

Kernels and storage-buffer count per stage (limit **8**, `limits.rs:434`; no storage textures, limit 4, `:435`; the uniforms are 1–2 small structs):

| kernel | when | storage buffers | per-cell bytes (r + w) | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `source_map_build` | when sources, sinks or blockers change (≤ 1 per frame) | 1 (`source_map` w) | 8 w | loops over ≤ 128 source entries and ≤ 256 blocker boxes in uniforms (≈ 2 KiB + 20 KiB, under the 64 KiB uniform limit); ≈ 400 M evaluations at 1024², **0.2–0.3 ms when it runs** [INFERENCE, not measured], so a moving source costs that every frame |
| `flux` | every substep | 8 (`bedrock`, `sediment`, `water`, `source_map`, `flux` rw, `velocity` ro, `suspended` ro, `transport` w) | 48 + 24 = 72 | pipe update, K, $\sin\alpha$, $s/d_1$; in-place on `flux` is race-free (a pipe reads only its own cell's old flux and the neighbours' *water* and *surface*) |
| `depth_erode` | every substep | 8 (`flux` ro, `transport` ro, `water` rw, `source_map` ro, `velocity` w, `suspended` rw, `bedrock` rw, `sediment` rw) | 48 + 24 = 72 | depth, velocity, sediment gather, erosion, evaporation quantum; neighbours' `flux`/`transport` are read-only this dispatch, everything else own cell |
| `thermal_flux`, `thermal_apply` | every 4th substep | 4 each | 40, 48 → 22 amortised | erosion.md |
| `downsample`, `downsample_velocity` | on readback ticks | 2 each | — | 2× mean of `water` (> 1024²); depth-weighted 2×2 mean of `velocity` |

Both substep kernels use all 8 allowed buffers: if a device reports only the 4 of `downlevel_defaults` they cannot be built and the plan's contingency (split into two dispatches) applies: `flux` at its `transport` write, `depth_erode` at the erosion block. Per substep the order is `flux` → `depth_erode` → (`thermal_flux` → `thermal_apply` on every 4th); each is its own `dispatch_workgroups` inside one compute pass per substep, so the storage-buffer visibility between dispatches is the pass's usage-scope barrier. Pipelines compile asynchronously: kernels not `ready` skip the frame and nothing is read back as live until the first real dispatch.

**Cost estimate for the bench (plan: hydrology + erosion at 1024² ≤ 0.6 ms at 8 substeps).** The kernels do about 150–250 flops per cell and move 166 B per cell per substep (`flux` 72 + `depth_erode` 72 + 22 amortised thermal); at 936 GB/s (RTX 3090) the arithmetic (≈ 1 M cells × 200 flop = 0.2 GFLOP, ≈ 6 µs at 35 TFLOP/s) is negligible and the kernels are bandwidth-bound. Perfect halo reuse is assumed (each array read and written once per kernel):

| resolution | per substep (peak / 80 % of peak) | 2 substeps | 4 | 8 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 256² | 12 / 15 µs (dispatch floor 14 µs each) | 0.03 ms | 0.06 | 0.12 | 0.23 |
| 512² | 47 / 58 µs | 0.09 | 0.19 | 0.37–0.47 | 0.74–0.93 |
| 1024² | 186 / 233 µs | 0.37–0.47 | 0.74–0.93 | **1.49–1.86** | 2.98–3.72 |
| 2048² | 744 / 930 µs | 1.5–1.9 | 3.0–3.7 | 6.0–7.4 | 11.9–14.9 |

So **1024² at 8 substeps is predicted to cost 1.5–1.9 ms, 2.5–3× the plan's 0.6 ms**; 512² at 8 substeps (0.37–0.47 ms) or 1024² at ≤ 3 substeps fit. The wind pass sets the dispatch floor: ≈ 14 µs per dispatch, so 8 substeps × 2.5 dispatches = 0.28 ms even at tiny grids (`wind.md` Deviation 1). Cross-check with the paper: Mei's 6.65 ms for 1024² on an 8800 GTX (86.4 GB/s, seven passes) scales by bandwidth to 0.61 ms per iteration on a 3090 (548 B/cell, 3.3× the 166 B here), i.e. 4.9 ms for 8 substeps; Jákó's 15.47 ms (HD4870, ≈ 115 GB/s, hydraulic + thermal in one iteration) scales to 1.9 ms per iteration. The scaling is crude [INFERENCE] and both papers predate fused kernels; they agree on the order of magnitude and on the budget being unreachable at 1024²/8. The plan's contingency therefore applies and is recorded as expected: the High setting lowers to the Medium values (512² sim at 8 substeps = 0.37–0.47 ms) unless one optimisation pass (skipping dry 8×8 tiles through a per-tile active flag: a `flood` scene is mostly dry and would cut the cost proportionally, but rain over the whole terrain wets every tile) reaches 0.6 ms at 1024². The measured number goes in the README `Performance` table either way. Timing must follow the AGENTS note: log `nvidia-smi` clocks in the same shell and report the minimum over the diagnostic history.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Pipe cross-section $A = l\,h_f$ (local depth), not the constant $A = l$.** Evidence: the constant-$A$ model's wave speed is $\sqrt{A g/l}$ = 3.13 m/s at every depth (measured 2.9 m/s at 0.05–10 m), thin sheets run at the 100 m/s cap, and the `flood` depth falls from a 1.4–2.4 m bore to 0.12 m *(measured)*; with $h_f$ the wave speed is 0.89–0.93 of $\sqrt{g d}$ and the steady depth is 1.45 m. Source: Mei Eq. 2 (A free), Bates et al. 2010 and Cozzolino 2020 Eq. 19–20 ($h_f = \max(\eta_i, \eta_j) - \max(z_i, z_j)$).
2. **Manning friction, new field `HydrologySettings::manning_n` = 0.035.** Source: Cozzolino 2020 Eq. 19 and $n_M = 0.03$; coupled form through the cell speeds because decoupled friction is 14 % weak on average and anisotropic (Cozzolino §2.1).
3. **Depth cap $h_{cap}$, velocity clamp $0.5\,l/\Delta t$ and wet threshold $10^{-4}\,l$.** Source: Chentanez §2.1.4–§2.1.5; the stability limit $\Delta t \le l/(2\sqrt{g h})$ derived above and verified.
4. **Rain, evaporation and melt are delivered in CPU-accumulated quanta ≥ 5×10⁻⁶ m,** not as a per-substep increment: the f32 depth absorbs increments below half an ulp (measured: 4 mm/h and 1 mm/h gain nothing on d ≥ 0.5 m, 25 mm/h gains nothing at 4 m).
5. **Rain is split per cell with the same snow fraction as the snow kernel** (`snow_fraction_at(T_cell)`, lapse rate 6.5 K/km), instead of the plan's global `precipitation·(1 − snow_fraction)`; agreed with the snow-cover note to avoid counting water twice on high ground.
6. **Open boundary = free overfall at the edge cell's bed level** (ghost cell with $d$ = 0, $S$ = own $S$); the plan only says "edge cells drain their outflow out of the domain".
7. **Substep scheduler is specified as signed-remainder rounding with a one-frame backlog cap,** a precision of the plan's "clamp(round(…)); the remainder carries over", verified to give exactly 50 substeps per second at any frame rate.
8. **Water sources are pre-rasterised into `source_map` by the CPU-counted cell footprint**, so the substep never loops over sources (a per-cell loop of 64 sources × 8 substeps would cost ≈ 0.1–0.3 ms at 1024² [INFERENCE]).
9. **The `flood` and bench expectations are restated** (items 6 and 11 above): "rising monotonically" becomes "never below 0.5 m after first crossing" on valleys ≤ 2 %; the 0.6 ms bench is expected to miss at 1024² × 8 substeps, so the plan's contingency (High → Medium values) is expected to trigger.
10. **Not changed:** the evaporation formula (no order-of-magnitude error except fog, FAO-56 PM comparison), `step_s` 0.02 (no clamp needed), $g$, the four-neighbour pipes (Mei: "the four von Neumann neighbors can produce satisfactory results"), the 64-source cap, the readback sizes.
11. **A bake's `rain_mm_h` is liquid.** erosion.md says "nothing else about the hydrology changes", but with the per-cell split above a bake under a cold climate would put no rain on the high ground and a bake in a snowy preset would do nothing, which is not what a message named `rain_mm_h` asks. During a bake the rain is delivered whole (`misc.z` = 1 skips `snow_fraction_at`); the blocker mask still applies.
12. **Open edge friction uses the edge cell's own speed for the ghost** ($|\mathbf v_j| = |\mathbf v_i|$ in $\bar v$); the note fixed $h_f$ and $\eta$ of the ghost but not its speed. Not measured separately.
13. **Kernel files and bind-group layouts.** The kernels are four WESL files, not one `hydrology.wgsl`: `substep.wesl` (`flux_step`, `depth_erode`), `thermal.wesl`, `source_map.wesl`, `readback.wesl`. The entry points of a file share one layout (all eight storage bindings of the substep kernels `read_write`, as every dispatch only reads the buffers it does not write by convention, since two entry points of one module cannot declare one binding with two access modes). `source_map_build` reads the sources and the blockers from read-only storage buffers (`array<vec4<f32>>`, `array<Blocker>`: the precipitation kernel's box test) rather than from uniforms, so the shader has no fixed limit; the CPU caps are 64 + 64 sources and 256 blockers (the nearest to the camera, as precipitation).
14. **The thermal kernels bind `transport` as a scratch for (sediment moved, bedrock moved)**, five storage buffers each instead of four (+16 B per cell per pass, 1 % of the traffic). `thermal_apply` cannot recompute a cell's own two amounts without reading its neighbours' heights, which it is writing in the same dispatch.
15. **Scheduling handshake with the render world.** The main world schedules substeps only while the render world reports every pipeline compiled and every buffer present (`HydrologyGpu::ready`), counts the substeps the render world recorded (`executed`) and the ones it was asked for and could not run (`dropped`); `HydrologyStats::substeps` is `executed`, and a bake ends when `executed + dropped` reaches its target (so `TerrainBaked` follows the last recorded substep by one to three frames, 3753 frames for 3750 at 8 substeps and 7503 for 7500 at 4). The bake length is `ceil(duration/step − 10⁻³)` substeps, because `step_s` is an `f32` (600 s / 0.02 s = 30 000.0007).
16. **The water readback is two fields of `EnvironmentSnapshot`** (`water_depth`, `water_velocity`, each its own grid), not one `water: Option<WaterSnapshot>`: the two requests complete at different times and a reader must not see one without the other's grid. The readback kernels also run every frame until the first readback is requested, so the first `Some` comes with the first readback tick.
17. **`HydrologyStats::lost_m3` is an estimate**: the volume at the first depth readback plus the nominal input since (rain over the whole extent with the reference elevation's phase, sources and sinks at their rates, evaporation over the cells wet at the previous readback) minus the volume now. It does not know the per-cell snow split or the blockers' shade, and a sink is counted at its rate whatever it removed. The note asked for the quantity but not for its error.
18. **With erosion on, a flood carves its bare bedrock.** Not a change to the model; a measurement that the note's flood numbers need `ErosionSettings::enabled = false`: the same flood with default `ErosionSettings` (capacity 0.6 s, bedrock erodes after the sediment layer, which is empty on a `Terrain::flat`/heightmap terrain) carves the valley floor by **up to 7.94 m in 120 s**, delays the front at 100 m from 36 s to 63 s and leaves the marker pulsing 0.2–1.2 m (a pit at the source and a stepped bed); nothing is raised in the bedrock layer (deposits land in `sediment`). The `flood` example and the flood test disable erosion; `README.md` says so.
19. **Wiring (3.4).** Items (a) wind solid mask, (b) precipitation collision and (c) `FloraGround` are the terrain note's (`terrain.md`, "Readback and wiring") and are not in this change; (d) idle without a terrain and dispatch only when every kernel is ready, (e) `Res<Weather>`/`ElementsQuality`, and (f) `Environment::water_depth`/`water_velocity` are here. The terrain's `TerrainDispatch` set is re-exported crate-wide so the hydrology pass runs `.before` it, and the main world sets `TerrainGpu::dirty` on every frame that runs a substep.
20. **Bench contingency applied: `High` takes `Medium`'s values.** 1024² × 8 substeps measures 1.860 ms (Result *Bench* above), 3.1× the 0.6 ms budget, and one optimisation pass has nothing left to find (0.2325 ms per substep is 80 % of the card's bandwidth with the layers read once per kernel). Per the plan's contingency `ElementsQuality::terrain_max_resolution` becomes 256 / 512 / **512** / 2048 and `hydrology_substeps` 2 / 4 / **4** / 16: High measures **0.258 ms** (512² × 4), inside the budget with room for the thermal and readback kernels; Ultra keeps 2048² × 16 (14.75 ms). A 600 s bake at High is 7500 frames (125 s at 60 fps) instead of 3750. The cells are 4 m at the default 2 km extent; the stability table's High row is now the Medium row (cap 652 m).

Items marked [INFERENCE]: the FAO-56 and Oudin equation forms and constants (recalled), natural-channel $n$ range, the 2.5–3× scaling of Mei's and Jákó's timings, the `source_map_build` cost, the Chentanez grid size per timing row, and the claim that Kass & Miller's velocity comes only from $\partial h/\partial t$.
