## Model adopted

**One idea: the snow map is a thickness over a column top, and the volume field is a union of the user's field with a slab that exists only over the column top.** Nothing about snow changes in the voxel path: accumulation, melt, drift, shed and the Festenberg diffusion run on the terrain grid exactly as `snow-cover.md` describes; the voxel code only supplies the terrain's surface heights (column tops) and consumes the snow depth read back.

### Components and resources (crate-internal wiring, public surface in bold)

- **`VoxelGround { resolution: UVec2 }`**, a `Component` placed on the `VoxelVolume` entity. `resolution` is the terrain grid in cells (clamped and rounded by `resolve_resolution`: power of two, 32 to `terrain_max_resolution`). Recommended: `extent / layout.cell_size()` rounded to a power of two (64 m / 0.5 m = 128), so a terrain cell is a voxel cell wide.
- **`VoxelGroundSystems`** (public `SystemSet`, ordered `.before(IsomeshSystems)` and `.before(ElementsSystems::Simulation)`): user edit systems that call `replace_field` or insert `NeedsRemesh` order `.before(VoxelGroundSystems)`. The plan names only `.before(IsomeshSystems)`; this set is the minimum extra surface that lets the ground see an edit in the frame it is made (see "What happens when the user carves the top away").
- **`SnowyField`** (public, `isomesh::Sdf<Scalar = f32>` and therefore `VolumeField`): `{ base: Arc<dyn VolumeField>, cell_size: f32, top: Arc<[f32]>, top_grid: TerrainGrid, snow: Arc<[f32]>, snow_grid: TerrainGrid }`. Public so tests and consumers can build it; `VoxelGround` is the only constructor that wires it.
- Crate-internal `VoxelGroundState` (component on the volume entity, not public): the user's current `base` `Arc`, the installed `Arc<dyn VolumeField>` (to detect a user `replace_field`), the top grid (`Vec<f32>`, the terrain's `resolution`), the held snow grid, the chunk extent, the terrain entity, flags (`warned_erosion`, `built`), and counters `swaps`, `columns_rescanned` (used by tests).

### The field

For a world point `p`, with `c = layout.cell_size()`, `t(x, z)` the column top and `s(x, z)` the held snow depth (both bilinear in the column grid through `TerrainGrid::sample`, the same tap rule as `grid_taps`, edge clamped):

```
sample(p) = min( base(p),  max( p.y − (t + s),  (t − c) − p.y ) )
```

- `max(p.y − (t + s), (t − c) − p.y)` is the signed "distance" to the slab `t − c < y < t + s`: negative strictly inside it. Slab top is the snow surface, slab bottom is one cell **below** the column top so the slab overlaps the solid it sits on and the union has no gap or sliver at the join (Marching Cubes cannot resolve a gap thinner than a cell).
- `min` is the union (negative inside), so the base is never carved: where the base is solid it stays solid. The gradient is the default central difference (`sdf.rs:79-92`), six samples; no override (an analytic gradient would also need the bilinear derivative and the `max` branch; not worth it, see Parameters, cost).
- Columns with no solid anywhere in the volume have no top: `t` is stored as `y_min − 2c` (a plane two cells under the volume, no sample ever sits inside the slab) and `s` is forced to 0 in the held grid for them. For the terrain they appear as a flat floor at `y_min`.
- Edges: columns outside the chunk extent clamp to the nearest edge column (the `TerrainGrid::sample` rule).
- **The unit test the plan asks for:** `SnowyField` over `base = Sphere { center: [0, 0, 0], radius: r }`, `top ≡ r` over an 8 × 8 grid of 1 m cells covering `x, z ∈ [−4, 4]`, snow ≡ 0.2, `cell_size = 0.5`. At `[0, r + 0.1, 0]`: slab `= max(r + 0.1 − (r + 0.2), (r − 0.5) − (r + 0.1)) = max(−0.1, −0.6) = −0.1`, base `= +0.1`, `min = −0.1 < 0`. At `[0, r + 0.3, 0]`: slab `= max(0.1, −0.8) = +0.1`, base `+0.3`, `min = +0.1 > 0`. (A constant `top` is deliberate: with the true sphere heights sampled at the four cell centres around the origin the bilinear value at `x = z = 0` is `√(r² − 0.5) < r`, and the assertion at `r + 0.1` would be about the interpolation, not the slab; that is the second test below.)

### Column-top extraction

The top of column `(x, z)` is the **highest zero crossing of `base` along the vertical**, searched downward from the volume's top plane `y_hi` (the maximum `world_of_sample(base_sample(id) + [n; 3]).y` over the volume's chunk entities) to its bottom `y_lo`, i.e. the first `f ≥ 0` to `f < 0` transition from above (zero is outside, `sdf.rs:10-16`). This is the surface a snowflake falling vertically from the sky reaches first; it means a cave under a hillside is skipped, a roof over a tunnel is the top, and a shaft open to the sky has its floor as the top (snow falls down the shaft; shelter is not modelled at the column level, `Limitations`).

- **Bracketing.** Sample `base` at `y_k = y_hi − k · c`, `k = 0, 1, …` (step = the voxel cell size, the plan's "sampled at `cell_size`"), stop at the first `k` with `f(y_k) ≥ 0` and `f(y_{k+1}) < 0`. A solid layer thinner than `c` that falls between two samples is missed; it is also below what Marching Cubes at `c` resolves (an isomesh chunk samples the lattice at `c` and a feature that no lattice point sees leaves no triangles), but the column scan is at the terrain's `(x, z)` centres and the mesh lattice has its own, so a feature visible to one and not the other can exist at the margin; accepted, `[INFERENCE]` that it affects only slivers under `c`.
- **Refinement.** 8 bisection steps on the bracket: the error is `c / 2⁸ = 1.95 mm` at `c = 0.5 m`. (A secant step converges faster on a smooth SDF; bisection is chosen because the base is not a distance and can have `|∇f| ≪ 1`, where regula falsi stalls. The slab is at least `0.25 m` thick whenever it exists, so 2 mm is two orders below any visible effect.)
- **Cost per column** = `(y_hi − t) / c + 1` scan samples + 8 refinement samples, the scan stops at the first crossing, so it is `O(height above the top)`, not `O(volume height)`.
- **Example numbers** (`voxel_snow`: 8 × 8 chunk columns of 16 cells at `c = 0.5` m = 64 m × 64 m = 128 × 128 terrain columns of 0.5 m, three chunk layers `y ∈ [−8, 16)` = 48 cells, hill crest at `y ≈ +6`): scan from 16 m down to 6 m = 20 samples + 1 + 8 = 29 per crest column, about 30 on average, at worst 48 + 8 = 56 where the hill is lowest. Whole volume: `128² × 30 = 491 520` evaluations (worst `128² × 56 = 917 504`). At 100-150 ns per gyroid-plus-`smooth_min` evaluation `[INFERENCE: not measured, six trig calls in f32; see Validation 9 to measure]` the initial scan is 49-74 ms (worst 92-138 ms), once, on the main thread at start-up. One chunk column (16 × 16 = 256 columns) is 7 680 evaluations, 0.8-1.2 ms `[INFERENCE]`. The cost scales with the field: a base that is a table lookup is 10× cheaper.
- **Monotonicity guard (assertable):** the refined `t` satisfies `|base(x, t, z)| ≤ |∇base| · c / 2⁸`; the test uses the sphere (`|∇| = 1`): `|t(0, 0) − r| ≤ 2e-3` for `r = 2`, `c = 0.5`, volume `y ∈ [−4, 4]`, and the call count is at most `⌈(4 − 2)/0.5⌉ + 1 + 8 = 13` per column (a counting `Sdf` wrapper checks it).

### The terrain side

`VoxelGround` creates a crate-internal `Terrain` simulation domain: spawn a **separate** entity (not a child of the volume: the volume entity's transform is ignored by isomesh and a child would inherit it) with `Terrain { source: TerrainSource::Heightmap { image, height_range_m: 0.0..1.0 }, extent_m, resolution, render: TerrainRender::External }` (`Terrain::external()`, `mod.rs:215`) and a `Transform` of `(cx, 0.0, cz)` (so `y0 = 0` and a stored height is a world height, `model.rs:37`), where `(cx, cz)` and `extent_m` come from the XZ bounding box of the volume's chunk entities (`world_of_sample(base_sample(min))` to `world_of_sample(base_sample(max) + [n; 3])`; `mark_changed_chunks` uses the same box rule, `plugin.rs:395-410`). The heightmap source is an `Image` of format `R32Float`, `resolution.x × resolution.y` texels holding the top heights **in metres**: with `height_range_m = 0.0..1.0` the import is `mix(0, 1, v) = v` (`heightmap.wesl:72`) and at texel ratio 1 the taps are exact (`axis_tap`, `heightmap.wesl:35-45`: `g = d`, `f = 0`), so the stored bedrock equals the scanned top to the last bit (the terrain's own `resolve_resolution` may clamp `resolution`; the image is built at the **resolved** size, which `ensure_terrain` computes from `terrain.extent_m` and `terrain.resolution`; the build system calls `resolve_resolution(extent, resolution, quality.terrain_max_resolution())` itself). Column `(i, j)` is sampled at world `(origin.x + (i + 0.5) · cell, origin.z + (j + 0.5) · cell)`, the cell centre, as the terrain grid defines it (`model.rs:27-28`). `sediment` is 0 after the import (`heightmap.wesl:73`).

- The terrain does not exist before the first scan finishes; `VoxelGround` spawns it in the same frame the image is ready. The snow maps (`GroundCoverPlugin`) and hydrology then run on the terrain unchanged: rain, puddles, flow over the **top** surface, snow accumulation, melt, drift, shed, shelter.
- **Erosion is disabled for voxel ground.** Erosion would rewrite `bedrock`/`sediment` on the GPU while the volume field (the truth) does not change, so the terrain and the mesh would disagree. The hydrology uniform build reads `erosion.enabled` at `hydrology/mod.rs:698` and `:718`; under a `VoxelGround` both read `false` and a one-shot `warn!` ("`ErosionSettings` is ignored for voxel ground") is logged the first time `ErosionSettings::enabled` is `true` (a `Local<bool>` or `VoxelGroundState::warned_erosion`). The README says so. Water still flows; only dissolve, deposit and slump are off.
- **Edits to the tops** rewrite `bedrock` only: replace `Assets<ShaderBuffer>[state.bedrock]` data with the full top grid (`res² × 4` B, 64 KiB at 128², 1 MiB at 512²) and set `TerrainGpu::dirty` (`gpu_tests.rs:559-579` pattern). Because erosion is off, nothing on the GPU writes `bedrock` after import, so the CPU copy is authoritative and writing it whole is safe. (The same pattern for the **snow** layer would wipe the GPU-evolved state with a stale CPU mirror, which is why snow is never written from the CPU, "What happens when the user carves the top away".)
- `VoxelGround` and a user-spawned `Terrain` are mutually exclusive (one terrain at a time, `mod.rs:154-157`); a second one is warned and ignored by the existing code. `VoxelGround` documents it.

### Data flow from render readback to the SDF

Per frame, in `Update` (all CPU, no GPU work added by this milestone):

1. `ElementsSystems::Readback` (existing): `read_cover` completes a readback on the 250 ms tick, replacing `EnvironmentSnapshot::cover` with new `Arc<[f32]>` depth and wetness grids on the readback grid (`m4-cover` `cover/mod.rs:542-580`).
2. **`voxel_ground_snow`** `.after(ElementsSystems::Readback).in_set(VoxelGroundSystems)`: runs only when `Arc::ptr_eq(&cover.depth, &state.last_depth)` is false (a new readback). Resample the snapshot depth to the held grid if the grids differ (readback grid 2× coarser above 1024²: `TerrainGrid::sample` at each column centre; identical grids index directly). For every column compare the fresh depth `d` with the **held** depth `s`: if `|d − s| > 0.5 c` the column is *over threshold*. If none are, do nothing (no allocation, no swap). Otherwise build the new snow grid by **per-column sample-and-hold**: over-threshold columns take `d`, every other column keeps `s`, force `0` under no-top columns, wrap it in a new `Arc<[f32]>`, build a new `SnowyField { snow: new, top: current, .. }`, `volume.replace_field(new_field)`, and mark the chunks derived below (`commands.entity(e).try_insert(NeedsRemesh)`), the same call `mark_changed_chunks` makes internally (`plugin.rs:426`).
3. `IsomeshSystems` (`spawn_meshing_tasks`, `apply_finished_meshes`): the marked chunks spawn tasks holding `Arc::clone(&volume.field)` (`plugin.rs:457`), i.e. the new field, because step 2 ran first in the same frame; mesh tasks already in flight finish against the old field (`replace_field` doc, `:220-224`).
4. `Update`, after `IsomeshSystems`: the consumer's `attach` system (the `game_showcase.rs:399-421` pattern) gives new `ChunkMesh`es a `Mesh3d` and the ground material.

What crosses from the render readback to the SDF is one `Arc<[f32]>` (the readback's `depth`, per-cell snow depth in metres on the terrain grid), compared and copied on the CPU. The `top` grid never crosses: it originates on the CPU. No GPU buffer is written for snow.

**Why sample-and-hold, not "the whole new snapshot".** The plan swaps "the field" when any column changed by more than `0.5 c`. If the swap installed the whole new snapshot, every column's depth would update, but only chunks around over-threshold columns would be remeshed; neighbouring chunks that were not remeshed would keep meshes made with the old depths while later remeshes of *their* neighbours sampled the new ones, so the two sides of a shared plane would disagree by up to `0.5 c` (a seam crack: Marching Cubes chunks only close when both sides sample identical field values at the shared plane, `plugin.rs:117-119`, `chunk.rs:219-253`). With hold, the installed field is exactly what each unmarked chunk was meshed from, so every seam stays closed, and the error between the installed snow and the true snow is bounded by `0.5 c` at every moment (Parameters).

### How dirty chunks are derived from changed columns

Not `mark_changed_chunks`; see the next section for why. For each changed column `(i, j)` with old held depth `s₀`, new `s₁`, top `t`, and top-change handling below:

- **XZ footprint**: a sample at world `(x, z)` reads column `i` iff `x ∈ (cx_i − cell, cx_i + cell)`, `cx_i = origin.x + (i + 0.5) cell` (bilinear support of one centre is ±1 cell). Widen by one voxel cell `c` each side: a chunk samples the plane one beyond the cells it owns (`sample_shape`, `chunk.rs:183`; `chunk_of` is half-open, `:259-262`), so the neighbour chunk that shares the plane reads the same column. Box `x ∈ [cx_i − cell − c, cx_i + cell + c]`, same for `z`.
- **Y interval**: the slab surface moved between `t + s₀` and `t + s₁`. Mesh geometry changes in cells whose corners straddle either; those lie in `y ∈ [t + min(s₀, s₁) − c, t + max(s₀, s₁) + c]` (one cell halo). Where `s₀` or `s₁` is 0 the slab bottom face `t − c` coincides with solid (see the carve case), so no further extent is needed.
- **Chunk set**: the axis-aligned box `[x₀, x₁] × [y₀, y₁] × [z₀, z₁]` maps to `layout.chunk_of(corner_min) ..= layout.chunk_of(corner_max)` per axis. With `cell = c = 0.5` and `n = 16` the horizontal box is `2 cell + 2 c = 2 m` wide against an 8 m chunk (1 or 2 chunks per axis) and the Y span is `|s₁ − s₀| + 2 c`, 1.25 to 1.5 m for the validation steps (1 or 2 layers). A column interior to a chunk marks 1 chunk; a corner column marks up to `2 × 2 × 2 = 8`. Union over all changed columns into a `HashSet<ChunkId>`; insert `NeedsRemesh` on each id whose entity exists (look up by `VoxelChunk.id`: the system holds a `HashMap<ChunkId, Entity>` of the volume's chunk entities built the way `mark_changed_chunks` does, `plugin.rs:393-410`, refreshed when a `VoxelChunk` is added or removed).
- **Cost**: `O(changed columns × ≤ 8)` hash inserts; no field evaluation at all.

### Why not `mark_changed_chunks` per swap

`mark_changed_chunks` compares `before` and `after` over the cell bounding box of **every** chunk entity of the volume (`plugin.rs:371-375, :395-410`) and `mark_edit` allocates and fills `(box + 1)³` samples (`dirty.rs:355-389`). `voxel_snow`: 128 × 128 × 48 cells → `129 × 129 × 49 = 815 409` corner samples, each evaluated through both the old and the new `SnowyField` (the old wraps the gyroid): `1 630 818` evaluations, `[INFERENCE]` 0.16-0.25 s at 100-150 ns, on the main thread, inside the system that calls it. At the readback cadence (4 Hz at most) that is 65-100 % of a core, and it scales with the volume (a 256² × 96 volume is 4 × 2 = 8× more). It is also redundant: the region where the slab can change is known exactly from the columns (above), `O(columns)`, no evaluation. So `mark_changed_chunks` is **not** used for snow swaps (Deviation 4). It stays the user's tool for their own edits (which is what it is for: their brush region is small, the cost is their choice).

### What happens when the user carves the top away

A carve is a user edit; there are two things to settle, the tops and the snow that sat on them.

1. **Detection.** The user's edit calls `volume.replace_field(new_base)` (or mutates their own field) and `mark_changed_chunks`/inserts `NeedsRemesh` on the affected chunks, in a system ordered `.before(VoxelGroundSystems)`. Because the edited `base` is the user's, `volume.field` after `replace_field` is the **bare new base**, i.e. it has lost the slab wrapper. `voxel_ground_edits` (`.in_set(VoxelGroundSystems)`) runs in the same frame, before `IsomeshSystems` removes `NeedsRemesh`:
   - If `volume.field` is not the `Arc` the ground installed (`Arc::ptr_eq` against `VoxelGroundState::installed`, compared as `Arc<dyn VolumeField>` data pointers), adopt it as the new `base`.
   - Collect the `(cx, cz)` of chunks that carry `Added<NeedsRemesh>` (the `Added` filter sees every insertion since the system last ran; the system runs every frame and before the removal in `spawn_meshing_tasks`, so no insertion is missed provided the user's marking system is ordered `.before(VoxelGroundSystems)`: an insertion made *after* the ground system in the same frame is also removed by `spawn_meshing_tasks` in that frame, and would be unseen next frame; this ordering is the contract). The frame the ground was built skips this pass (the initial scan already covered every chunk; every freshly spawned chunk has `NeedsRemesh`).
   - **Rescan the full vertical column of every footprint**, not only the marked layers: a carve in chunk layer 2 changes the top of columns whose old top was in layer 1. The rescan region is the union of the marked chunks' XZ footprints, 256 columns per chunk column at `n = 16` and 0.5 m terrain cells.
2. **The new top.** The rescan finds the new highest crossing: after carving a pit through the crest it is the pit floor (if the pit is open to the sky) or the rock above a covered cavity (if the carve opened a cave whose roof remains), exactly as "highest zero crossing" says; if the whole column was removed, `t = y_min − 2c` and the held snow for it is forced to 0. The new tops are written into `bedrock` (full buffer replace plus `dirty`), the `top` `Arc` is rebuilt, and a new `SnowyField` wraps the adopted base with the new `top` and the **held snow unchanged** (the old depth `s`).
3. **Chunks to remesh, same frame.** The user's marked chunks are already in the queue. In addition, every column whose top changed by more than `1e-3` m marks the chunks of the Y interval `[min(t₀, t₁) − c − c, max(t₀ + s, t₁ + s) + c]` (the old slab must disappear from where it was, the new one appear where it is; both lie in layers the user's mark may not include: the pit floor's layer is below the carved layer). Footprint and halo rule as above. Because this runs before `spawn_meshing_tasks`, every marked chunk (the user's and these) is meshed once, against the **new** field, in this frame: no chunk is meshed with stale tops and then again with fresh ones.
4. **The snow that was on the carved surface.** The held and the simulated snow depth are per column, defined as a depth above whatever the top is. After a carve the slab therefore *follows the top down*: the pit floor receives the depth the crest had, until snowfall, melt, drift and the 250 ms readback sort it out (the snow map is not told the top changed; its own shed-by-slope rule applies to the new height gradient, which the terrain readback gives it, because the bedrock buffer changed). This is a visible but bounded artefact: it is wrong only for a column whose top moved by a lot and carries thick snow, and it heals at the snow's own timescale. **Clearing the snow of carved columns would need a GPU-side write** (zero `snow` in those cells), and the CPU→`ShaderBuffer` replace pattern cannot do it: the main-world asset is a stale mirror (zeros or the initial state), replacing the buffer would erase the snow everywhere. A clear needs a small kernel in the `m4-cover` code (a mask buffer of columns to zero, consumed by `snow_source`), which is a change to that branch's contract and not part of 5.2; recorded as a limitation (Deviation 6) and not worked around with a fallback.
5. **Raising the top** (the user builds on the crest): the rescan finds the higher top; the same flow applies, the snow depth is carried over onto the new top. Buried snow does not melt or vanish; same limitation.

`voxel_ground_edits` costs, per chunk column marked, 256 columns × (scan to the top + 8) evaluations (Column-top extraction): about 1 ms at the example's numbers `[INFERENCE]`, synchronous. A brush that marks `k` chunk columns pays `k` ms in the frame that edits; the user's own `mark_changed_chunks` over a brush-sized region is comparable. A paint stroke across a 64 m volume (8 chunk columns) is about 8 ms, which exceeds a 60 Hz frame budget; to keep the cost off one frame, `VoxelGround` rescans at most `rescan_budget` chunk columns per frame `[decision: 4]` and carries the rest to the next frame; **the chunks it has not rescanned must not be meshed with stale tops**, which is only possible if their `NeedsRemesh` is withheld, and the plugin gives no way to withhold it (it removes the marker when it spawns the task, `plugin.rs:463-479`). So there is **no per-frame cap**: a large edit stalls the frame that makes it by its rescan cost, and the note says so (Limitations). `[INFERENCE: the stall bound is the cost above; measure in Validation 9]`.

### How tunnels stay snow-free

The slab exists only in `t − c < y < t + s` over each column. Take any point `p` inside a tunnel below the roof: `p.y` is at least the roof thickness below the top, and the tunnel is by definition below the highest crossing, `p.y < t`. Where `p.y ≤ t − c` the slab term `(t − c) − p.y ≥ 0`, so `max(…) ≥ 0`: the slab never contributes a negative value at any point more than `c` below the column top, and `sample(p) = min(base, slab)` is negative iff `base < 0` (the slab is `≥ 0`). **The sign, and so the mesh, is the base's, exactly, at every point `≥ c` below the top; no snow is ever generated in a cave under a roof thicker than `c`.** Magnitude can differ where `slab < base+` (that is only within `|base|` of the plane `t − c`), which can move a root along an edge by a fraction of a cell, not create or remove a surface; the MC vertex position there is a linear interpolation between the two corner values (`marching_cubes`, "first-order accurate either way", `fields/mod.rs:1020-1025`).

Two consequences to state, because they set the visible limits:

- A roof **thinner than `c`** is thickened up to `c` where the slab bottom `t − c` reaches the cave: the slab fills `t − c < y ≤ t` regardless of what the base is there. Fine detail below one voxel is already below what the mesher resolves; nothing is added above the roof beyond `s`.
- A hill **overhang** (a ledge of rock below the highest crossing with open air above it and below it) does not receive snow: the column's top is the highest crossing only. This is the heightfield limit of the snow *map* (one value per column); the plan's slab "over the column top only" accepts it.

The **material** must agree with the geometry, or tunnel walls are shaded white. The `m4-cover` `GroundExtension` shades by XZ alone (`ground.wesl:51-61, 79-91`); applied to the voxel chunks it would paint a tunnel's walls and floor white wherever the map has snow above. The ground shader needs a **column gate**: a `GroundParams` field `column_gate_m` (default `−1.0e30`, meaning no gate, so existing external grounds are unchanged); the fragment multiplies the snow cover input by `step(top(xz) − column_gate_m, world_position.y)`, `top` from `terrain_cells.x + terrain y0` (the surface height the pack holds, which for voxel ground is exactly our `bedrock`). `VoxelGround` sets `column_gate_m = c` on the material the example uses (`voxel_snow`); the user's own material for a real game must do the same, which is documented. The same gate applies to the wetness/puddle input (water on the heightfield is on the top, not in tunnels). This is a change to `ground.wesl` and `GroundParams` after `m4-cover` merges (Deviation 8): the unit contract is "no snow shading for a fragment more than `c` below the column top".

### Swap threshold and the remesh rate

The plan's rule is `|Δ depth| > 0.5 c` in any column since the last swap, checked at each readback. Adopted as stated, with the per-column hold above. Arithmetic:

- The check runs only at a readback, which is at most every 250 ms (4 Hz), so swaps are at most 4 per second whatever the snow does.
- A column is over threshold only after its depth moved by more than `0.5 c = 0.25 m`. At snowfall `P` mm/h of water equivalent and density `ρ` kg/m³, depth grows `P · 1000 / ρ` mm/h; fresh snow (`ρ_new = 100`): `0.01 P` m/h. Real seconds per `0.25 m`, at game-clock scale `k`: `0.25 / (0.01 P) · 3600 / k`.

| P (mm/h w.e.) | m/h depth | k = 1 | k = 600 | k = 3600 | k = 86 400 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 (validation, forced Snow) | 0.015 | 16.7 h | 100 s | 16.7 s | 0.69 s |
| 5 | 0.05 | 5 h | 30 s | 5 s | 0.21 s (→ 0.25 s cap) |
| 15 | 0.15 | 1.67 h | 10 s | 1.67 s | 0.07 s (→ 0.25 s cap) |

  (computed; densification, which lowers the rate by about a factor 3 over days, is ignored: the numbers are an upper bound on rate. With the validation sequence, 1.5 mm/h and settling, the layered integral gives depth 0.1331 m at 10 h, 0.2423 m at 20 h, 0.4219 m at 40 h, 0.5724 m at 60 h, and crossings of 0.25, 0.50, 0.75 m at 20.7, 50.0 and 86.8 h; computed, matches the bulk kernel of `snow-cover.md` at 10 h to 0.04 %.)
- Swaps happen only for columns that moved by more than the threshold, so a uniform snowfall swaps **all columns together** (every column crosses at about the same time) and remeshes every surface chunk at once. For `voxel_snow`: 64 chunk columns, the hill crosses 1-2 chunk layers, say 80 surface chunks (`[INFERENCE]`, depends on the hill). Cost per chunk (Marching Cubes, `[INFERENCE]` 17³ = 4 913 samples at 100-150 ns = 0.5-0.74 ms; 4 096 cells at about 10 ns = 0.04 ms, `README.md:67`; about 400 vertices × 6 gradient samples at 100-150 ns = 0.24-0.36 ms): about 0.8-1.2 ms of worker time. A full swap is 64-96 ms of CPU, spread over `2 × available_parallelism` tasks (48 in flight on 24 cores): about 4 ms of wall time on the workstation; on the main thread the meshes are applied under `MeshBudget` (4 ms per frame, at least one mesh per frame), so the queue drains in `⌈80 / applied per frame⌉` frames; if applying a mesh costs 0.1 ms `[INFERENCE]` that is 40 per frame, two frames.
- **The rate bound.** Worst-case remesh load = `n_dirty × t_chunk × min(4, r / (0.5 c))` per second, with `r` the fastest column depth rate in m/s (`r / (0.5 c)` swaps per second, capped at the 4 Hz readback). At the 4 Hz cap (reached only at the extreme time scales of the last column of the table): `80 × 1.2 ms × 4 = 0.38` core-seconds per second, 1.6 % of a 24-core machine and 38 % of the single thread wasm has. At `P = 15` mm/h the swap interval is 10 s at k = 600 (`80 × 1.2 ms / 10 s ≈ 1 %` of one core) and 1.67 s at k = 3600 (`≈ 6 %` of one core, 0.25 % of 24 cores); at `P = 1.5` mm/h, k = 3600 it is 16.7 s (`≈ 0.6 %` of one core). Verdict: **the `0.5 c` threshold bounds the rate acceptably on desktop at every time scale up to 86 400× (the cap is 1.6 % of the machine); on wasm it is acceptable up to about 600× and above that the remesh work (all on the one thread, below) is the limiter and a larger threshold or a smaller `MeshBudget::max_in_flight` is the knob.** The threshold also quantises what the player sees: below `0.25 m` of snow the *geometry* does not change; the *shading* (map-driven, smoothstep to full cover at 5 cm) does, through `GroundExtension`, so a dusting reads as white rock until the slab appears. That is the intended level of detail: below half a voxel the mesher's linear interpolation of the root is already the finest geometry it can show, and 5 cm of snow is a shading effect.
- **Hysteresis against flicker.** Hold keeps a column at its installed depth until it moves by more than `0.5 c` again, so melting and snowing around the threshold does not toggle remeshing every readback; a column that has just swapped needs a further `0.5 c` of change, not a 1 µm one, to swap again. (This is the plan's "since the last swap" read per column.)

### Remesh on a swap, and meshing constraints from isomesh

- Meshing is asynchronous on the task pool, never in a system; the budget bounds only the apply step. A full-field swap is therefore not a frame stall on native: the field is a new `Arc` and no task blocks (`plugin.rs:441-461`). The one synchronous piece is the user-side (`mark_changed_chunks`, avoided) and the rescan (above).
- A chunk whose `NeedsRemesh` is set while its previous task still runs is re-queued after it finishes (`Without<MeshingTask>`, `:305-306`): a second swap 250 ms later cannot be lost, and a stale result can briefly overwrite a fresher mesh only if the older task finishes after the newer is applied, which `spawn_meshing_tasks` prevents by not spawning a second task for a chunk until the first is removed.
- Choose `Extractor::MarchingCubes` (closed chunk seams, `plugin.rs:163-169`); the dual methods leave seam gaps at the snow surface.
- A power-of-two `cell_size` (0.5) is required (M-32, `chunk.rs:231-253`); `VoxelGround` `warn!`s once if `layout.cell_size()` is not a power of two (the terrain, in contrast, only needs a power-of-two *cell count*).
- `SnowyField` is `Send + Sync`: its `Arc<[f32]>` grids are immutable and shared by every task; the new field is a new allocation of two `Arc`s plus the wrapper, nothing is mutated after construction.

### wasm

- `bevy_tasks` on wasm32 has no multi-thread pool (`lib.rs:21-31`, single-threaded task pool `:188-200`, web `spawn_local`): **isomesh meshing runs on the main thread** and the `MeshBudget` limits only the apply step, not the extraction. The remesh load computed above lands entirely in the frame; at `80 × 1.2 ms = 96 ms` for a full swap that is six dropped frames each time it happens `[INFERENCE: wasm speed 1.5-2× slower than native not accounted for]`. The column rescan is also synchronous, so it adds to the same thread.
- Mitigations in the design, not fallbacks: the threshold (`0.5 c`) and the readback cadence (250 ms) cap swaps; `MeshBudget::max_in_flight` is the only throttle isomesh provides, and the example sets it low on wasm; `voxel_snow` is documented as native-first; the `feature = "isomesh"` build must still pass `cargo build --lib --target wasm32-unknown-unknown --features isomesh` (the gate in `AGENTS.md` builds default features only) and needs `bevy_platform`'s `web` feature as documented in `bevy_isomesh/Cargo.toml:57-128`. WebGPU limits of the cover maps are unchanged (the voxel path adds no GPU pass and no storage buffer).
- The `Arc<[f32]>` grids and `HashSet<ChunkId>` bookkeeping have no platform constraint. `Instant` is isomesh's own and is via `bevy_platform` (`plugin.rs:56`).

## GPU mapping

There is no new GPU work in this milestone. "GPU mapping" here is the CPU and ECS mapping, per the task: which systems run in which schedule, and what crosses from render readback to the SDF.

**Plugin.** `ElementsPlugins` adds `VoxelGroundPlugin` when feature `isomesh` is on (the dependency list gains the optional `bevy_isomesh = "0.0.11"` and `isomesh = "0.0.11"`; feature `isomesh = ["dep:bevy_isomesh", "dep:isomesh"]`; `tests/leaf.rs:15` `ALLOWED_DEPS` gains `bevy_isomesh` and `isomesh`; nothing from them is re-exported except `SnowyField` and `VoxelGround`). `VoxelGroundSystems` is configured `.before(IsomeshSystems)` in the plugin (`app.configure_sets(Update, VoxelGroundSystems.before(IsomeshSystems))`). Within a frame the order is: user edit systems → `build` → `edits` → `snow` → `IsomeshSystems` (`spawn_meshing_tasks`, `apply_finished_meshes`) → the consumer's attach system. `ElementsSystems::Readback` (which fills `EnvironmentSnapshot`) is before `snow` and, as a set, is independent of `IsomeshSystems`; the snow system is `.after(ElementsSystems::Readback)` and in `VoxelGroundSystems`, so Bevy resolves it as Readback → snow → Isomesh. `[INFERENCE: no set cycles, since neither Readback nor Simulation is ordered against IsomeshSystems or VoxelGroundSystems in either direction besides the two edges named; verify with the Bevy schedule graph in the first implementation test]`.

**What crosses from the render readback to the SDF.** (1) The readback's `Arc<[f32]>` depth (metres, row-major on the readback grid, `CoverSnapshot::depth`) → compared against the held grid on the CPU → a new `Arc<[f32]>` held grid in `SnowyField`. (2) Column tops: CPU-born from `base`, written into `bedrock` (CPU → GPU, 64 KiB at 128²) at edit time; the terrain pack and heightfield readback then publish them as the surface. (3) Nothing else.

**Memory.** Top grid and held snow grid: `res² × 4` B each (64 KiB at 128², 1 MiB at 512², 16 MiB at 2048²); the `R32Float` `Image` is released after the terrain imports it (the `Terrain` holds a handle to it; drop it from `VoxelGroundState` after `is_live`). A swap allocates one `res² × 4` B grid.

**Readback grid versus terrain grid.** At 2048² (Ultra) the readback is 2× coarser than the simulation grid (`m4-cover` `readback_downsample`); `SnowyField` holds the top at the simulation grid and the snow at the readback grid as two `TerrainGrid`s and samples each with its own `TerrainGrid::sample`. Reading a 1024 readback of a 2048 simulation costs nothing extra; the swap threshold is evaluated on the readback grid.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Pin `bevy_isomesh` and `isomesh` at 0.0.11, not "the published 0.20 version".** The only published `bevy_isomesh` is 0.0.10, which has neither `IsomeshSystems`, `replace_field` nor `mark_changed_chunks` (PR #8, unreleased on 2026-10-13). Source: `cargo search bevy_isomesh` shows 0.0.10; `isomesh_apis` `bevy_isomesh/Cargo.toml:4` is 0.0.10 and `CHANGELOG.md` "Unreleased" carries the 0.20 port. 5.2 starts after 0.0.11 is published (`AGENTS.md`, "Plays well with the family" 4).
2. **The plan's signatures are corrected to the real ones**: `IsomeshSystems` is a `SystemSet` around `(spawn_meshing_tasks, apply_finished_meshes)` (`plugin.rs:308-317`); `replace_field(&mut self, impl VolumeField) -> Arc<dyn VolumeField>` (`:225`); `mark_changed_chunks(commands, volume_entity, &VoxelVolume, &Query<(Entity, &VoxelChunk)>, &dyn VolumeField, &dyn VolumeField) -> isomesh::Result<EditReport>` (`:384`). The plan's `SnowyField` is `isomesh::Sdf<Scalar = f32>` and the `VolumeField` blanket impl (`:74-76`) makes it a field; no extra trait is implemented.
3. **Per-column sample-and-hold instead of swapping the whole snapshot.** Plan: swap when depth changed by more than `0.5 c` "since the last swap". Adopted: the threshold is per column against the *installed* value, and a swap installs fresh depth only for over-threshold columns. Source: Marching Cubes chunks close only when both sides of a plane sample identical field values (`plugin.rs:117-119`; `chunk.rs:219-253`); a whole-snapshot swap that remeshes only chunks around over-threshold columns leaves seams that disagree by up to `0.5 c`. The error bound `0.5 c` on installed versus true depth then holds at every moment.
4. **Dirty chunks are derived from the changed columns, not by `mark_changed_chunks`.** Plan: "the dirty chunks are marked with `mark_changed_chunks`". `mark_changed_chunks` samples `before` and `after` over the whole volume's chunk bounding box (`plugin.rs:371-375`, `dirty.rs:355-389`): `1.63 M` evaluations `[INFERENCE: 0.16-0.25 s]` per swap in the example, on the main thread, at up to 4 Hz. The column rule marks the same chunks (a superset by at most the halo, by construction: it marks any chunk whose lattice samples the changed columns could affect) without evaluating the field. `mark_changed_chunks` remains correct for user edits.
5. **`VoxelGroundSystems` is a new public set**, ordered before `IsomeshSystems`, so that user edit systems have something to order against. Plan: the plugin orders itself `.before(IsomeshSystems)`; that alone leaves a user edit made after the plugin's system (same frame) unseen, because `spawn_meshing_tasks` removes `NeedsRemesh` before the next frame's `Added` query runs.
6. **Carving does not clear the snow that was on the carved surface.** Plan: "recomputed for columns in chunks that the user's edits mark `NeedsRemesh`" (tops only). Adopted: tops are recomputed and the slab follows the new top with the old depth; clearing snow per carved column needs a GPU kernel the `m4-cover` branch does not have (the CPU cannot write one column of a GPU-evolved buffer; replacing the buffer erases the whole snow map). If a clear is wanted it is a change to the `m4-cover` contract (a column mask consumed by `snow_source`), after that branch lands.
7. **No Festenberg `g₁(r) = r(2 − r)` profile on the slab edge.** `snow-cover.md` proposes it for depth below `0.25 Δx`. With hold at `0.5 c`, a column never carries a slab thinner than `0.5 c` (below the threshold it keeps its previous value, which is 0 for a column that has never swapped), so the profile would never be evaluated, and where it would be, the bilinear interpolation of adjacent columns already gives the soft margin one terrain cell wide that the 2-iteration diffusion in the map prepares. Revisit if a visual shot shows a hard lip at snow patch edges.
8. **The ground shader needs a column gate** (`column_gate_m`, Validation 8 "tunnels stay dark"). Not in the plan; the map-driven `GroundExtension` shades by XZ only (`m4-cover` `ground.wesl:51-61, 79-91`), which would whiten tunnel walls under a snowy top. This is a change to `GroundParams` and `ground.wesl` after `m4-cover` merges; if `m4-cover` changes that file first, coordinate through Main.
9. **No per-frame cap on column rescans.** Plan silent. A cap would require withholding `NeedsRemesh`, which `bevy_isomesh` does not allow (`plugin.rs:463-479`). A large edit stalls its frame by the rescan; see Limitations.
10. **`VoxelGround` also needs `GroundCoverPlugin` for snow** (the plan says "snow, wetness and hydrology then run on the domain unchanged"; without the cover plugin there is no snow and the ground is only a hydrology and wind domain).
11. **The scan's evaluation count is `⌊(y_hi − t)/c⌋ + 2 + 8`, not `⌈(y_hi − t)/c⌉ + 1 + 8`.** Validation 4 counts 13 for the sphere column; the scan needs the sample at or above the surface and the one below it, so `y = 4, 3.5, 3, 2.5, 2, 1.5` is six samples and the column costs 14 (`column_top_extraction` asserts at most `6 + 8`). The refinement returns the midpoint of the last bracket, so its error is `c / 2⁹` = 1 mm, not `c / 2⁸`.
12. **`VoxelGroundSystems` is ordered `.after(ElementsSystems::Readback).before(IsomeshSystems)`, not `.before(ElementsSystems::Simulation)`.** The note asks for both edges at once for the set that holds `voxel_ground_snow`, which must follow `Readback`; the chain `Simulation → Readback` makes that a cycle (`Readback → snow → Simulation → Readback`). The set follows `Readback` (the first scan, the edits and the swap all run there) and the erosion guard, which has to precede hydrology, is a separate system `.before(ElementsSystems::Simulation)`. A terrain spawned by the first scan is built by `ensure_terrain` in the next frame.
13. **The plugin is `VoxelPlugin`**, not `VoxelGroundPlugin`; the public items are `VoxelPlugin`, `VoxelGround`, `VoxelGroundSystems` and `SnowyField`. It is added by `ElementsPlugins` last, after the plugins it needs.
14. **The column gate is the material's own and its off value is tested, not stepped.** `GroundExtension::with_column_gate_m(metres)` stores `column_gate_m` in `GroundParams::gate.x` (default `−1e30`, no gate) and `update_materials` keeps it per material while it writes the shared fields. The shader shows a fragment when `p.y ≥ top − gate`, where `top` is the packed surface height plus `y0`; with the note's `step(top − column_gate_m, y)` the default `−1e30` would gate everything, so the default is tested explicitly (`gate.x < −1e29`). The gate multiplies the snow and wetness map and the puddle depth, and with it the bed's water absorption. `VoxelGround` cannot set it (the material is the user's); `voxel_snow` does.
15. **The edit pass reads `Changed<NeedsRemesh>` and skips its own marks.** A chunk marked while its task runs, or while `max_in_flight` holds it back, keeps `NeedsRemesh` for several frames; `Added` does not fire for a mark on a chunk that already has one, `Changed` does. The ground's own marks (the swap marks about a hundred chunks) would otherwise be read as user edits and make the next frames rescan their columns; they are remembered for one frame and skipped.
16. **`bedrock` is rewritten once the terrain is live, not at the edit.** The heightmap import runs once, from the image, and would overwrite an earlier write; `tops_dirty` holds the edit until `TerrainState::is_live`. The image is the heights of the first scan.
17. **A column that is solid at the volume's top plane has its top there**, the note's "first `f ≥ 0 → f < 0`" has no crossing for it and the volume has no chunk above it.
18. **Cleanup the note does not describe.** Removing `VoxelGround` restores the user's field and remeshes every chunk of the volume; a `resolution` or quality level that resolves to another grid size tears the ground down and the next frame builds it again; the terrain entity carries a marker (`VoxelGroundTerrain`) and is despawned with its volume; another `Terrain` entity, or a second `VoxelGround`, leaves the volume without a ground and logs one warning.
19. **The erosion lock is a resource.** Hydrology reads `Option<Res<ErosionLocked>>` and `erosion_enabled(&ErosionSettings, locked)` for the uniform's `flags.y` and `gpu.thermal` (the note's helper took `has_voxel_ground`); the guard inserts and removes the resource with the component and logs one warning. Validation 11's `warn!` count is asserted on the decision (`should_warn_erosion`) rather than on the log.
20. **`swaps == 2` holds only where every column accumulates alike.** A column that sheds (slope over 35°) or sits at the foot of a cliff, or in a shaft open to the sky, accumulates at its own rate and so crosses the swap threshold at its own readback: the field then swaps at every readback until each has crossed. Measured: the example's island flank (slopes up to 69°) 158 swaps in 60 game hours, the same hill with tunnel portals open to the sky 104. The per-column hold and the column rule keep each of those swaps small (tens to hundreds of columns, their chunks only). Validation 8 uses the hill without the flank and with roofed tunnels, where the swaps are the two the note predicts.
21. **Validation 9 is measured in tests, not in `bench_elements`.** The rows (a)-(d) are CPU times and `bench_elements` reads GPU pass spans, so they are the ignored release test `voxel_cost` (`cargo test --release --features isomesh --lib -- --ignored --nocapture voxel_cost`), which asserts the note's targets; (e), the frame time through the swaps, is recorded by the GPU test. Validation 11's `flags.y == 0` is asserted through `HydrologyGpu::thermal` (which takes the same `erosion_enabled` value) and the unit tests of `erosion_enabled`; `HydroParams` has private fields.

**Limitations (kept, not deviations).** (a) Heightfield limit: ledges below the highest crossing and cave floors under roofs get no snow; a shaft open to the sky gets snow on its floor (no per-column shelter model). (b) The scan misses solid layers thinner than `c`. (c) Carving or building leaves old snow depth on the new top (Deviation 6). (d) wasm meshes on the main thread (above). (e) A roof thinner than `c` is thickened to `c` where the slab bottom meets it. (f) The mesh sees only the *held* snow, within `0.5 c` of the true value, and thin snow (< `0.5 c`) is shading only. (g) Large edits stall their frame by the synchronous rescan. (h) The initial scan stalls the first frame (`[INFERENCE]` 50-140 ms) and on a streamed volume whose chunk set changes the terrain extent would have to change; the extent is frozen at first build from the chunk set present then, later chunks outside it get no snow, with a one-time `warn!`. (i) Chunk entities must have identity transforms and no parent offset (field space is world space, as in `game_showcase.rs:392-396`); the volume entity's own `Transform` is ignored by isomesh and by this feature.
