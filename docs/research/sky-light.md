# Sky light and reflections under cloud: the clouds in the environment map

Every PBR surface in Bevy reads the sky through one cube: the camera's environment map, whose prefiltered mips give the specular reflection and whose irradiance convolution gives the diffuse sky light (Karis 2013's split sum, which Bevy's `GeneratedEnvironmentMapLight` implements). Until now the crate's cube was Bevy's atmosphere cube: the clear sky, lit by the sun's `DirectionalLight`, which under cloud carries only the beam `T̄`. The clouds were a screen composite, and their light a uniform `AmbientLight` of `0.25 (1 − T̄)` of the clear-sky illuminance (`celestial.md` Deviation 18, `broken-cloud-light.md`). So:

- Water, puddles and wet ground under cumulus mirror a uniform blue sky with no cloud in it (iteration 25 baseline `pond_pc_16.png`: the sky shows a field of cumulus, the pond a flat blue).
- Under overcast the water is brighter than the sky it reflects: rows of the `waves` pond near the horizon read `(230, 238, 240)` under a sky of `(226, 226, 226)` (`pond_overcast_16.png`). Bevy's ambient light has a specular term (`EnvBRDFApprox(F0, roughness, NdotV)` times the ambient colour), and the crate boosts the ambient by `1 / 0.452` so its *diffuse* term lights a horizontal surface with `π L` (`BEVY_AMBIENT_DIFFUSE_SCALE`); near grazing, where water's Fresnel factor approaches 1, the boosted specular term reflects 2.2 × the sky's radiance. The cyan cast is the clear-sky cube beneath it.
- The diffuse sky light under cloud is uniform over normals (AGENTS.md: "it cannot shade tops brighter than sides"), where an overcast sky is two to three times brighter overhead than at the horizon.

The feature: march the clouds into a cube from the followed camera, compose it with Bevy's atmosphere cube exactly as the screen composite composes the sky pixels, and hand that cube to Bevy's environment-map generator in place of the clear one. Reflections then show the clouds, the gaps and the bright cloud around the sun; the diffuse sky light comes from the same dome, graded and directional; the uniform cloud ambient is retired on that camera.

## Model adopted

**1. The cloud cube.** A cube of the size of the camera's atmosphere cube (128² per face by default) holds, per texel direction `d` from the clouds' camera point (`CloudParams::camera`, the followed view), the clouds marched exactly as the screen march marches a sky pixel: premultiplied scattered luminance `L_c` and transmittance `T_c`, with the far veil beyond the march range, in luminance (unexposed). Only directions above the planet (that reach the layer) are marched.

**2. Aerial perspective along a cube direction.** The screen march takes the air between the camera and the cloud from Bevy's aerial-view LUT, which is indexed by screen position. A cube direction uses the transmittance LUT for the air's transmittance `T_ap(d, D)` to the cloud's mean depth `D` (as the screen march already does) and, for the in-scatter, the atmosphere cube itself: the sky radiance `L_atm(d)` is the in-scatter of the whole path to the top of the atmosphere, of which the part in front of the cloud is taken as `(1 − T_ap(D)) / (1 − T_ap(∞))` (in-scatter proportional to the extinction along the path), plus the air lit by the cloud-scattered diffuse light `(1 − T_ap) · L_air` as in the screen march (`clouds.md` Deviation 25).

**3. Composition, sky directions.** `L = L_atm · T_c · g + L_c`, with `g` the composite's sky gain (`celestial::sky_gain`, ramped in above a cloud transmittance of 0.02 to 0.2), then the weather's air over the path to the layer `L ← L · t + H (1 − t)`, `t = exp(−σ_air · s)`, `H` the camera's fog colour in luminance (its display-referred `DistanceFog::color` divided by the camera's exposure). This is `composite.wesl`'s sky branch, moved into one function both call.

**4. Composition, ground directions** (rays that meet the planet before the layer): Bevy's planet ground times the sky gain, `L = L_atm · g`. Bevy lights its albedo-0.3 ground with the `DirectionalLight`s, whose sun carries only the beam under cloud; `g` restores it to the dome's total `0.25 + 0.75 T̄`, so the ground bounce follows the light that actually reaches the ground. No cloud, no fog: the ground that lights a surface from below is the ground beside it.

**5. Hand-over.** The composed cube replaces `GeneratedEnvironmentMapLight::environment_map` on the followed camera, so Bevy's generator filters it into the specular mips and the irradiance map. The atmosphere cube itself stays the clouds' own skylight source (the march's `sky_irradiance` must see the clear sky above the layer, not the clouds). The camera's `AmbientLight` no longer carries the cloud-scattered light on that camera: the dome's cloud luminance in the cube is that light, and Bevy's environment diffuse is Lambert-correct. A camera without the crate's atmosphere, without an environment map, or not followed keeps the uniform model ambient.

**6. Amortisation.** Each frame marches one texel of every `k × k` block of every face (`k = size / edge`, `edge` by quality) and copies the rest; a marched texel blends into its history with weight `α`, or replaces it when the history is invalid (first frames, a camera jump). The composition runs over every texel every frame, so the sky and the sun move smoothly between marches.

**7. Bring-up.** The cube is handed to Bevy only after the render world has composed it on `k²` frames (every texel marched once), and the ambient is retired on the same frame (AGENTS.md: a pass that dispatches every frame needs a bring-up handshake). Until then, and on any frame the cube is not live, the camera has the clear cube and the model ambient, as before.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cube size | the camera's `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight::size` (Bevy default 128²) | Bevy default; UE's default capture is 128² |
| Marched share per frame, `edge` per face | Low 32, Medium 64, High 64, Ultra 128 (so `k` = 4, 2, 2, 1 at 128²) | UE 9-frame time slicing; Schneider & Vos 2015 |
| History weight `α` | 0.25 (1 on an invalid history) | choice: the march's per-texel jitter averaged over ~7 marches; the lag (High: a texel is marched every 4 frames, half-life ≈ 10 frames ≈ 0.16 s) is far under the clouds' motion (≈ one texel in 6 s at 5 km and 10 m/s) |
| Sky gain ramp | `smoothstep(0.02, 0.2, T_c)` | `composite.wesl` (`clouds.md` Deviation 28) |
| March steps, light samples | the quality's (`CloudQuality`) | as the screen march |
| Format | `Rgba16Float`, luminance in cd/m² | Lagarde & de Rousiers §4.9.1.1; Bevy's atmosphere cube |

## GPU mapping

- One compute kernel, `cloud_sky_probe`, a second entry point in `march.wesl` so the screen march and the cube share one ray march. The per-ray integration is lifted out of `cloud_march` into a function that returns unexposed luminance, transmittance and the mean depth; `cloud_march` multiplies by `view.exposure` at its store, as before. The cube entry binds no `View`, no depth and no aerial LUT; its bindings are the march's at their indices (uniform, noise, weather map, transmittance LUT, samplers, the atmosphere cube) plus three of its own: the previous cloud state (`texture_2d_array`, `textureLoad`), the next cloud state and the composed cube (`texture_storage_2d_array<rgba16float, write>`, two storage textures in the stage).
- Dispatch: `size/8 × size/8 × 6` workgroups of 8×8. Each thread: its slot (`frame mod k²` within its block) marches and blends, else copies; every thread composes its texel with the atmosphere cube sampled at the texel's direction and writes the composed cube. The cloud states ping-pong by frame parity.
- Scheduling: in `Core3d`, in `MainPass` before the opaque pass, after `cloud_resolve`; Bevy's `atmosphere_environment` (after the prepass, before `MainPass`) has written the atmosphere cube by then. Bevy filters the composed cube at the start of the next frame: one frame of latency.
- Main world: a `SkyProbe` component on the followed camera records the atmosphere cube's handle, owns the composed and state images (`TEXTURE_BINDING | STORAGE_BINDING | COPY_SRC`, cube view), and swaps `GeneratedEnvironmentMapLight::environment_map` once the render world's frame count (an `Arc<AtomicU32>`) reaches `k²`. A camera that stops being followed gets its atmosphere cube back.
- WebGPU: two write-only storage textures in the stage; `Rgba16Float` storage is core WebGPU; the cube is sampled through a cube view and written through a 2D-array view of the same texture.
- Cost at 128² and High: 6 · 128² / 4 = 24 576 threads, of which about half march (the lower hemisphere composes only), against the screen march's 960 × 540 = 518 400 at 1080p: about 2.5 % of its rays.

## What great looks like

- A pond or a puddle under fair-weather cumulus mirrors white cloud and blue gaps, rippled by the waves, the clouds sliding with the wind; under the bright cloud near the sun the reflection glows, never a sun disc behind cloud.
- Under overcast, water is a grey mirror a little darker than the sky (Fresnel), darker still looking down, and has no blue tint; wet streets and roofs show soft grey highlights, no sun glint.
- A white box under overcast is lit from above: its top brighter than its sides, its sides brighter than its underside; soft, shadowless, as an overcast day looks.
- Clear sky: nothing changes.
- No cube seams, no texel shimmer in mirror reflections, no popping as texels are re-marched, no black or blue frame at start-up.

## Validation targets

1. **Clear air, no cloud** (cover 0): the composed cube equals the atmosphere cube within 1 % in every texel (gain 1, `T_c = 1`, no air).
2. **Overcast at noon**: the cube's horizontal irradiance `E = ∫ L cos θ dω` over the upper hemisphere within ±35 % of what the model gave the diffuse sky light (`T̄ · E_sky + 0.25 (1 − T̄) · E_clear`); the zenith-to-horizon luminance ratio (zenith against 5–10° of elevation, averaged over azimuth) between 1.5 and 3.5 (Kähler's 2:1 and 3:1, CIE type 1 3:1); `L_Z / E` between 0.30 and 0.45 (Lambertian 0.318, Kähler 0.382–0.409).
3. **Partly cloudy**: the upper hemisphere carries the clouds: a share of its texels with `T_c < 0.5` within 0.15 of the sky's cover above 10° elevation, and the cube's luminance varies over the dome where the clear cube is smooth.
4. **Specular** (`waves`, Overcast, 16:30): the water is darker than the sky at every row; no cyan cast (`r ≈ g ≈ b` within 8 DN in the reflection of a grey sky).
5. **Diffuse** (`weathered_town`, Overcast): the top of the white box at least 1.3 times brighter than its sun-facing side, where a uniform ambient gave them equal sky light.
6. **Reflections of cumulus** (`waves`, PartlyCloudy, 16:30): white cloud shapes and blue gaps visible in the pond at 1×, matching the sky above them in a 4× crop.
7. **Temporal** (`waves`, PartlyCloudy, 120 frames): no visible popping or shimmer in the reflection; the frame-to-frame difference of the water away from the bodies no larger than with the clear cube.
8. **Seams**: no visible cube-face edge in a mirror reflection (4× crops at the face boundaries).
9. **Cost**: the probe kernel at most 0.1 ms at High (minimum over the diagnostic history, RTX 3090).
10. **Scope**: a camera with `ElementsCamera::atmosphere` off or `ambient` off keeps its ambient untouched.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **The cloud ambient is retired on the probe camera** (`celestial::update_ambient`). Its job, the cloud-scattered sky light, is done by the cube; keeping both would count the light twice and keep the ambient's boosted specular (the overcast-water blowout). The exposure and `illuminance_at` keep the calibrated model.
2. **The lower hemisphere is Bevy's planet ground, restored by the sky gain**, not the screen composite's ground haze (which floors its distance at the 32 km march range for pixels past a finite terrain). A lit surface's lower hemisphere is the ground beside it; UE offers a solid lower hemisphere for the same reason.
3. **Aerial perspective along a cube direction** is the atmosphere cube's in-scatter scaled by the extinction fraction (Model 2), not Bevy's aerial-view LUT, which is defined only over the screen.
4. **No reprojection of the cube history**: the cube is fixed to the world's axes and the clouds are kilometres from the camera; a camera jump resets the history.
5. **The sun disc is not in the cube** (Bevy's atmosphere cube has none); the sun's specular highlight stays the `DirectionalLight`'s, which carries the beam.
6. **Correction (iteration 24's regression)**: Bevy reads an `Atmosphere`'s `GlobalTransform` as the planet's centre and moves a default one `inner_radius` below the origin when the component is added; the crate's spawned atmosphere had `Transform::from_scale(u)`, and transform propagation then wrote the identity back, putting the planet's centre at the world origin (the sky of the `waves` example under Clear became a beige dome of radial streaks). The crate's atmosphere `Transform` is now `(0, −R·u, 0)` with scale `u` (`plugin::planet_transform`), at spawn and when `ElementsUnits` changes; `plugin::tests::the_crate_atmosphere_centres_the_planet_below_the_origin`. Also recorded in `units.md`.
