# Hydraulic and thermal erosion — research note

Plan 3.2, erosion part. The water part (virtual-pipe flow, boundaries, evaporation, readback) is `docs/research/hydrology.md`; the two notes share one substep and one set of buffers, and the kernel table lives in hydrology.md. Numbers marked *(measured)* come from a throw-away float32 NumPy emulation of the kernels written for this note (grid 128² or 256², cell 2 m, Δt = 0.02 s, the substep order below). It is not committed; it is the oracle the first GPU kernels are compared against, and every figure should be re-derived by the real tests listed under `## Validation targets`.

## Model adopted

**Layers and units (m).** $B_r$ bedrock, $B_s$ sediment (loose soil and sand), $S = B_r + B_s$ the surface the water flows over, $s$ the suspended sediment as a height-equivalent (volume of solids per cell area, m), $d$ the water depth above $S$ (hydrology.md). Total solid inventory $M = \sum (B_r + B_s + s)\,l^2$ is the conserved quantity.

**Order inside one substep.** The plan lists sources, flux, depth/velocity, erosion, transport, evaporation. The kernels fuse this into two dispatches per substep (hydrology.md `flux`, `depth_erode`), so the cycle is rotated by one half-step: *flux and sediment ratio → depth, velocity, transport of the previous suspended load, erosion/deposition with the new velocity*. Every transfer between two stores is written once from one amount, so the rotation does not change what is conserved. Only the first and last substep differ from the plan's order (the first transports an empty field, the last ends with erosion and no transport).

**Capacity** (Mei Eq. 10 with the minimum tilt):

$$ C = K_c\,\max(\sin\alpha,\ s_{min})\,|\mathbf v|,\qquad \sin\alpha = \frac{|\nabla S|}{\sqrt{1 + |\nabla S|^2}},\qquad \nabla S = \Big(\frac{S_R - S_L}{2l},\ \frac{S_B - S_T}{2l}\Big) $$

with $K_c = 0.6$ and $s_{min} = 0.05$ (≈ 2.87°). $|\mathbf v|$ is the depth-averaged speed from hydrology.md (clamped to $0.5\,l/\Delta t$, with $v = 0$ in cells with $d \le 10^{-4}\,l$). The slope is read from the surface *before* the step's erosion; the flux kernel computes $\sin\alpha$ because it reads the neighbour heights anyway, and writes it for the erosion block, which must not read neighbour heights that other invocations of the same dispatch are modifying. **Units:** $K_c$ is a time (s): $C$ is a height-equivalent in m and $|\mathbf v|$ is in m/s. $C$ is not a concentration: nothing bounds $s/d$.

**Dissolve and deposit** (Mei Eq. 11–12 / Jákó Eq. 12–13, per cell, only where the water is deep enough to flow; $k_s = \min(1,\ K_s\,\text{strength}\,\Delta t/0.02)$, $k_d = \min(1,\ K_d\,\text{strength}\,\Delta t/0.02)$, so $K_s$, $K_d$ are per-step fractions at the default 20 ms step and are rates per second ($1.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ each) independent of `step_s`, as in Jákó Eq. 12–13 which carry $\Delta t$):

$$ a = k_s\,(C - s)\ \text{if } C > s:\quad \Delta B_s = -\min(a, B_s),\ \Delta B_r = -(a - \min(a, B_s)),\ \Delta s = +a $$

$$ a = k_d\,(s - C)\ \text{if } C \le s:\quad \Delta B_s = +a,\ \Delta s = -a $$

Dissolved material comes from the sediment layer first, then from bedrock (Jain 2024 §3: sediment-first apportioning; Galin §4.1.1: removed bedrock becomes silt, here at the deposit). Deposits always land in the sediment layer. Where $d \le 10^{-4}\,l$ all of $s$ settles into $B_s$ at once (a dry cell has $v = 0$ and no transport; Mei/Jákó leave this case open, and without it an evaporated cell would hold sediment forever). The transferred amount is the amount *actually applied after f32 rounding*: $B_s' = \mathrm{fl}(B_s - a)$, then $a_{eff} = B_s - B_s'$ is what $s$ gains (and symmetrically for deposits). Without this the transfer leaks whenever $a$ is below half an ulp of the layer (ulp(20 m) = 1.9×10⁻⁶ m, ulp(1 m) = 1.2×10⁻⁷ m, so a 3×10⁻⁸ m deposit onto a metre of sediment would vanish from $s$ without reaching $B_s$); the retained remainder is retried next substep.

**Suspended sediment transport** (flux form, replaces the plan's semi-Lagrangian step; the measurements are under *Conservation analysis*). The flux kernel stores the ratio $c_i = s_i / d1_i$ (with $d1$ = depth after sources, before the flux update; $0$ if $d1 < 10^{-4} l$). The depth kernel then moves sediment with exactly the water that moves, through the K-scaled pipe fluxes $f^{k}_{i}$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) the same step produced:

$$ s_i' = s_i + \frac{\Delta t}{l^2}\Big(\sum_{j \to i} c_j f_{j\to i} - c_i \sum_k f^k_i\Big) $$

Because the K factor guarantees $\sum_k f^k_i\,\Delta t \le d1_i\,l^2$, the sediment leaving cell $i$ is at most $c_i\,d1_i = s_i$: $s' \ge 0$ without a clamp, unconditionally stable for any $\Delta t$ the pipes accept, and every unit leaving one cell enters another (Krištof's donor–acceptor property). With an open boundary the pipes to the outside carry sediment out of the domain; with a closed boundary nothing leaves. The numerical diffusion of this first-order upwind scheme is $D = \tfrac12 |v|\,l\,(1 - |v|\Delta t/l)$, the same expression as for bilinear semi-Lagrangian advection at the small Courant number here ($|v|\Delta t/l \ll 1$: 1 m/s gives $D \approx 1\ \mathrm{m^2/s}$ at $l = 2$ m for both), so the plan's visual character is kept.

**Thermal erosion** (every 4th substep, a separate two-dispatch gather; Jákó §2 pipes, Musgrave §4.2 magnitudes). For each cell $i$ and each of the eight neighbours $k$ at distance $d_k \in \{l, l\sqrt2\}$ with surface drop $h_k = S_i - S_k$:

$$ e_k = \max(0,\ h_k - d_k \tan\theta_t),\qquad e^b_k = \max(0,\ h_k - d_k \tan(\theta_t + 10°)),\qquad E = \max_k e_k,\ E^b = \max_k e^b_k $$

$$ D = \tfrac12\,r_t\,E,\quad m_s = \min(D,\ B_s),\quad m_b = \min\big(D - m_s,\ \tfrac12\,r_t\,E^b\big),\quad q_k = m_s\,\frac{e_k}{\sum_j e_j} + m_b\,\frac{e^b_k}{\sum_j e^b_j} $$

with $\theta_t$ = `talus_deg` (35°) and $r_t = \min(1,\ \text{thermal\_rate}\cdot\text{strength})$, here a *per-pass fraction* (0.15). Pass 1 (`thermal_flux`) writes the eight $q_k$ per cell into two `vec4` buffers; pass 2 (`thermal_apply`) does $B_s \mathrel{-}= m_s$, $B_r \mathrel{-}= m_b$, and $B_s \mathrel{+}= \sum q$ from the eight neighbours that point at the cell. Moved bedrock becomes sediment at the receiver (Galin §4.1.1). Every $q$ is added once and subtracted once, so mass is conserved to rounding *(measured: relative drift −2.3×10⁻⁷ over 1000 passes)*. $E/2$ is Jákó's stated maximum transfer (above it the scheme oscillates); using the excess $E$ over the talus rather than the whole drop $H$ is Musgrave's rule ($c_t(\Delta a - T)$) and makes the scheme converge to the talus from above instead of ringing around it, which Jákó's $H$-proportional rule does at an amplitude of about $r_t H/2$ (≈ 0.05 $l$ per pass at 35°, i.e. ±2.5° of slope at $r_t = 0.15$). The bedrock rule (a bedrock cell loses material only where a neighbour slope exceeds talus + 10° = 45°, and only after the sediment above it is exhausted) has no source; 45° is the top of Galin's 30–45° range for earth forms. A cone made of *bedrock* therefore relaxes to 45° and stops *(measured: footprint p99 central-difference slope 46.4° after 1000 passes)*; the 60° cone of `gpu_thermal_respects_talus` must be made of sediment.

**Bake.** `ErodeTerrain { duration_s, rain_mm_h }` (a `Message`) starts a bake; `TerrainBaked { duration_s }` is written when the simulated time since the start reaches `duration_s`. During a bake: the substep count per frame is the quality maximum (2/4/8/16 for Low…Ultra), weather rain is ignored and the uniform `rain_mm_h` is used in its place (the quantised delivery in hydrology.md), and nothing else about the hydrology changes (boundary, evaporation and friction settings still apply). A second `ErodeTerrain` received during a bake is queued and starts when the first one completes [INFERENCE: the plan is silent]. Real time per bake: $T/(N_{max}\,\Delta t)$ frames, e.g. 600 s at High = 600/(8×0.02) = 3750 frames = 62.5 s at 60 fps (Low: 15 000 frames = 250 s; Ultra 1875 frames = 31 s).

**Conservation analysis.** $M$ changes only through (i) f32 rounding in each store, (ii) sediment leaving through an open boundary, (iii) nothing else: dissolve, deposit, dry settling, transport and thermal moves are all matched pairs (compensated for rounding as above). The one transport scheme the plan names, semi-Lagrangian advection $s'(\mathbf x) = s(\mathbf x - \mathbf v\Delta t)$, is *not* a matched pair: each cell reads its upstream neighbours with bilinear weights, and the weights placed on one source cell sum to 1 only where the velocity field is divergence-free, which the depth-averaged flow is not (water piles up and drains). The same 5000 or 30 000 substeps on the same terrain (128², cell 2 m, 0.5 m of sediment over a 40 m relief, closed boundary, 20 mm/h, Manning friction as in hydrology.md), with the same erosion kernel, differ only in the transport:

| transport | total $M$ drift, $\Delta M/M_0$ (5000 substeps) | $\Delta M$ / dissolved mass | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| bilinear semi-Lagrangian (plan) | −6.9×10⁻⁵ | −8.3 % (−10 % of the peak suspended mass); at 30 000 substeps −1.7×10⁻³ and −36 % | 4 gathers of $s$ + $\mathbf v$; ping-pong of $s$ |
| semi-Lagrangian + global rescale to the pre-advection sum | −3.0×10⁻⁹ | 0 | two global reductions per substep (workgroup partial sums, a second 1-workgroup dispatch, a scalar read by the next kernel) |
| flux form through the pipe fluxes (adopted) | +5.1×10⁻¹⁰ (+7.3×10⁻¹⁰ at 30 000 substeps) | 0 | reads the neighbours' $c_j f_{j\to i}$; no ping-pong |

The plan's target — bedrock + sediment + suspended within 0.5 % of the start — **can** be met by plain semi-Lagrangian advection at 5000 substeps *(measured: 0.0069 %)*, with a factor of 70 to spare, but only because $M_0$ is dominated by the absolute elevation of the terrain (mean 19 m × 16 384 cells = 3.1×10⁵ m·cells) while the suspended load is a 10⁻³ fraction of it: the test as worded cannot see the loss of 8.3 % of the moved material, nor would it see the loss of *all* suspended sediment. And the error does not stay small: over the 30 000 substeps of the 600 s bake semi-Lagrangian reaches −0.17 % of $M_0$ (−36 % of the dissolved mass; the lost sediment is re-dissolved, so the maximum erosion is 7.1 m against 1.0 m with flux form), 3× from the plan's 0.5 % and growing faster than linearly. The note therefore (a) adopts the matched-pair flux form, which holds the total to float rounding, and (b) makes the target datum-free (`## Validation targets`).

**Dimensional check of the plan's constants.** With $K_c = 0.6\ \mathrm{s}$, a hillside of slope 0.3 and a flow of 1 m/s the capacity is $C = 0.18$ m of solids per cell area. Sheet flow on rain at 20 mm/h is a few millimetres deep, so the model carries $s/d$ up to 88–250 *(measured: maximum at the end of the 30 000-substep run with the adopted transport 88; 249 with semi-Lagrangian)*. That is a bookkeeping amount, not a concentration, and the same property is in Mei's and Jákó's models (neither bounds $s$ by $d$). It is mass-neutral and harmless to the solver, so it is kept; consumers must not read $s/d$ as a volume fraction. Water rendering must map `suspended` through a saturating function (e.g. $1 - e^{-s/0.02}$), not through $s/d$. Jákó's two corrections that would bound it are not adopted (see Deviations).

**What the bake actually removes.** With the plan's constants, 600 s of rain on a 40 m-relief hill (slopes up to ≈ 0.3), sediment layer 0.5 m, flux-form transport *(measured)*:

| rain | mean \|Δz\| | p99 \|Δz\| | max erosion | max deposit | bedrock lowered (max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 mm/h (the plan's `erosion_bake`) | 0.124 m | 0.622 m | 1.00 m | 1.64 m | 0.50 m (the whole sediment layer on the steepest cells) |
| 200 mm/h | 0.604 m | 3.05 m | 2.24 m | 4.46 m | 3.95 m |

Total water at 20 mm/h and 600 s is 3.3 mm. Transport is capacity-limited ($s \approx C$ almost everywhere), so `strength` ($K_s$, $K_d$ × strength, each capped at 1 per step) can speed up reaching $C$ by at most $1/0.03 = 33\times$ and does not raise $C$; the amount moved scales with the water flux and with $K_c$. At 20 mm/h the 0.5 m sediment layer is exhausted on the steepest cells within 600 s (bedrock lowered 0.50 m, equal to the layer); at 200 mm/h bedrock is lowered by 3.95 m. Scaling these to the 900 m `mountains` preset is [INFERENCE]; the first screenshot of `erosion_bake` decides whether rain or $K_c$ must be raised.

## GPU mapping

Fused into the two substep kernels of hydrology.md (`flux`, `depth_erode`) plus two thermal kernels, all `@workgroup_size(8, 8, 1)` over the terrain grid (1024²: 128×128 = 16 384 workgroups, ≤ 65 535 per dimension), all in `hydrology.wgsl`.

| kernel | storage buffers (limit 8 per stage) | per-cell bytes (read + write) | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `flux` | 8: `bedrock`, `sediment`, `water`, `source_map`, `flux` rw, `velocity` ro, `suspended` ro, `transport` w | 48 + 24 = 72 | writes $\sin\alpha$ and $c = s/d1$ as `vec2` into `transport`; reads neighbours' surface and water only |
| `depth_erode` | 8: `flux` ro, `transport` ro, `water` rw, `source_map` ro, `velocity` w, `suspended` rw, `bedrock` rw, `sediment` rw | 48 + 24 = 72 | gathers neighbours' `flux` and `transport` (read-only this dispatch); every other access is its own cell, so no invocation reads what another writes |
| `thermal_flux` (every 4th substep) | 4: `bedrock` ro, `sediment` ro, `tflux_a` w, `tflux_b` w | 8 + 32 = 40 | `tflux_a` = the four axis neighbours, `tflux_b` the four diagonals, `vec4<f32>` each |
| `thermal_apply` | 4: `bedrock` rw, `sediment` rw, `tflux_a` ro, `tflux_b` ro | 40 + 8 = 48 | each cell gathers the $q$ aimed at it; own-cell writes only |

Both substep kernels are at the 8-buffer limit and use no storage textures (the limit is 4); a device that reports only the downlevel 4 (`wgpu-types-30.0.1/src/limits.rs:557-575` per `docs/research/bevy-0.20-render.md`) cannot run them, and the plan's contingency (split into two dispatches) applies: `flux` splits at the `transport` write, `depth_erode` at the erosion block, which adds one dispatch and one 8-byte round trip per cell. Buffer sizes at 1024²: `bedrock`, `sediment`, `water`, `suspended` 4 MiB each; `flux` 16 MiB; `velocity` and `transport` and `source_map` 8 MiB each; thermal 2×16 MiB; 88 MiB in all (2048²: 352 MiB, the largest single buffer `flux` at 64 MiB against `max_storage_buffer_binding_size` 128 MiB and `max_buffer_size` 256 MiB, `limits.rs:441,443`).

Per substep 144 B/cell plus 88/4 = 22 B/cell of amortised thermal traffic = 166 B/cell. At 1024² (1 048 576 cells) that is 174 MB per substep, 186 µs at the 3090's 936 GB/s peak, 8 substeps = 1.49 ms (1.86 ms at 80 % of peak). See hydrology.md for the budget statement against the plan's 0.6 ms.

Dispatch order per substep: `flux`, `depth_erode`, and `thermal_flux`, `thermal_apply` after `depth_erode` on every 4th substep. Pipeline compile is asynchronous: the domain dispatches only when all four kernels are `ready` (`src/gpu/kernel.rs`, `ready`, `queue_kernel`).

## Validation targets

All numbers *(measured)* are from the float32 emulation described at the top; they are the expected GPU values, not CPU unit tests of the shader. GPU tests are `#[ignore = "needs a GPU"]` in `tests/gpu_hydrology.rs`.

1. **`gpu_erosion_conserves_mass`** (plan: closed boundary, 5000 substeps of `ErodeTerrain` at 20 mm/h, evaporation off, total within 0.5 %). Setup: a deterministic 256² heightfield (smooth, 40 m relief, 2 m cells) with 0.5 m of sediment, `HydrologySettings { boundary: Closed, evaporation: false, .. }`; read back `bedrock`, `sediment`, `suspended` (256 KiB each) and sum in f64. Assert (a) $|M_{end}/M_0 - 1| \le 5\times10^{-3}$ (the plan; expected ≈ 10⁻⁹ with the adopted transport, and 6.9×10⁻⁵ even with semi-Lagrangian); (b) the datum-free form $|M_{end} - M_0| \le 10^{-3}\cdot \tfrac12\sum |\Delta (B_r + B_s)|\,l^2$, i.e. under 0.1 % of the moved mass (expected ≈ 10⁻⁷; plain semi-Lagrangian would give 0.083, which is how this assertion would catch a regression to it); (c) $\min s \ge 0$, $\min B_s \ge 0$, $d \ge 0$ everywhere; (d) $\max_i s_i > 0$ at some readback during the run (otherwise the test conserves a null field). Why both: (a) is the plan's number and is insensitive; (b) is the assertion that has teeth.
2. **`gpu_thermal_respects_talus`** (plan: 60° cone, 4000 substeps, p99 slope ≤ talus + 2°). **The plan's setup is not enough to pass.** Use a **sediment** cone on flat bedrock: 256² cells, $l$ = 2 m, apex at the centre, base radius 12 cells (24 m), slope tan 60°, height 41.6 m, `ErosionSettings { enabled: true, .. }`, no rain, no sources (so hydraulic erosion is inert), `thermal_rate` 0.15. Metric: the steepest of the eight neighbour slopes, $\arctan(\max_k h_k/d_k)$ on $B_r + B_s$ (the quantity the kernel enforces), p99 over the cells whose surface is more than 1 cm above the floor (the cone and its apron). After 4000 substeps (= 1000 thermal passes) *(measured)*: footprint p99 = 35.0°, max 35.7° (≤ 37° with 1.3° to spare); before: 59.9°+; drift of the solid total −2.3×10⁻⁷. Assert p99 ≤ 37° and max ≤ 37.5°, and total solid within 10⁻⁵. Reported, not asserted: the central-difference slope of the same field (what the terrain shader's normal uses) is 37.15° (p99 on the cone), because an 8-neighbour-stable cone reads 2.15° steeper through central differences; the plan's "+2°" is therefore a margin for this metric difference, and a test that used central differences would fail by 0.15°. Sensitivity *(measured, passes = 1000, rate 0.15, footprint p99 central-difference / steepest)*: base radius 8 cells 37.2/—, 12 cells 37.15/35.0, 14 cells 37.2/35.1, 16 cells 38.05/37.7, 20 cells 40.3/40.2, 24 cells 43.6/43.6 (3000 passes: 37.2/36.6). Convergence time scales with the square of the radius: **the plan's own sizes cannot be met above ~14 cells at $r_t$ = 0.15**. With a p99 taken over the *whole* domain (the plan's wording) a cone under 1 % of the 65 536 cells (radius ≤ 14 cells) hardly registers: p99 reads 0.0° for radius 8 cells, so the footprint form above is required. A bedrock cone of the same shape ends at 46.4° (central) after 1000 passes and is *expected* to: use it as a second case asserting p99 ∈ [44°, 47°] (the 45° bedrock rule). With Jákó's per-second reading of $K_t$ ($r_t$ = 0.15×0.08 = 0.012 per pass) a 16-cell cone is at 47.4° after 4000 passes (*measured*), which is why the per-pass reading is adopted.
3. **Per-pass thermal CPU oracle** (unit test on the transfer rule, no GPU): for a 1-D slope of 40° (two cells, sediment only, $l$ = 1), $E = h - \tan 35°\,l$ = 0.1388, $D = 0.5\cdot0.15\cdot0.1388 = 0.01041$ m moved per pass; the mass left equals the mass gained (±10⁻⁷); bedrock exposed under a 46° slope moves $m_b = 0.5\cdot0.15\cdot(1.0355 - 1.0)\,l$ = 0.00266 $l$; at 44° it does not move.
4. **Capacity and dissolve/deposit (CPU oracle):** $\sin\alpha$ for gradient (0.3, 0): 0.2873; $C$ for $|v|$ = 1 m/s: 0.6×0.2873 = 0.1724 m; below the minimum tilt (gradient 0.02): $C = 0.6\cdot0.05\cdot|v|$; a cell with $s = 0$ and $C = 0.1724$ dissolves $0.03\cdot0.1724$ = 5.17×10⁻³ m per substep from sediment, with bedrock untouched while $B_s \ge 5.17\times10^{-3}$; a cell with $s = 0.2$, $C = 0$ deposits 6×10⁻³ m. With `step_s` = 0.04 the same cell moves 0.06 (not 0.03) of the difference.
5. **Compensated transfer (CPU):** $B_s = 1.0$ f32, deposit $a = 3\times10^{-8}$: $B_s$ unchanged, $s$ unchanged (nothing leaks); the naive form would lose 3×10⁻⁸ per substep.
6. **Bake accounting:** `ErodeTerrain { duration_s: 600.0, rain_mm_h: 20.0 }` at High issues exactly 3750 frames at 8 substeps (±1 frame) before `TerrainBaked { duration_s: 600.0 }`; the simulated clock advanced $n\cdot0.02$ s per frame. Weather rain during the bake is not applied (a Thunderstorm preset's 25 mm/h changes no cell: compare two runs, one with a forced Thunderstorm, within f32 noise).
7. **Visual (`erosion_bake`, 3.5):** the table under *What the bake actually removes* is the expectation: 20 mm/h × 600 s changes the surface of a 40 m-relief hill by a p99 of 0.62 m and exhausts the 0.5 m sediment layer on the steepest cells (bedrock lowered 0.50 m); 200 mm/h gives 3.05 m. The check is that the `sediment` layer thins on slopes and thickens at the foot (compare `sediment` before and after: negative on slopes > 0.1, positive where the surface flattens) and that bedrock is exposed only on the steepest cells.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **Transport of suspended sediment is flux-form (donor–acceptor), not semi-Lagrangian.** Evidence: Krištof 2009 §4.3 (donor–acceptor conserves mass to floating point, unlike semi-Lagrangian); measured: plain semi-Lagrangian loses 8.3 % of the dissolved mass in 5000 substeps and 36 % in the 30 000-substep (600 s) bake, where it also changes the result (maximum erosion 7.1 m against 1.0 m for flux form, because lost sediment is re-dissolved); flux form holds the total to 10⁻¹⁰ of $M_0$. The plan's 0.5 % total is met by plain semi-Lagrangian at 5000 substeps (0.007 %) but it already reaches 0.17 % at 30 000 and grows faster than linearly, so this is a correction of the model, not only of a test. The global-rescale correction was measured too (3×10⁻⁹) and rejected: two grid-wide reductions per substep plus a scalar dependency for a fix that spreads the error over the whole field.
2. **$K_s$, $K_d$ are per-step fractions at $\Delta t = 0.02$ s and are scaled linearly by `step_s`/0.02** (rates of 1.5 s⁻¹). Source: Jákó Eq. 12–13 carry $\Delta t$. At the default step this is the plan's number exactly.
3. **`thermal_rate` is a per-pass fraction of the largest stable transfer, $r_t E/2$ (Musgrave $c_t$, Jákó's $H/2$ maximum), with $E$ the excess over the talus**, not Jákó's per-second $K_t$ with the full drop $H$. The numerical 0.15 is unchanged. Source: Jákó Eq. 17 (the Δt and $H/2$), Musgrave §4.2. With per-second semantics the plan's cone test cannot pass (47.4° after 4000 passes, measured).
4. **Test geometry of `gpu_thermal_respects_talus` is fixed** (sediment cone, base radius 12 cells at 256² and 2 m cells, steepest-neighbour slope, p99 over the footprint, plus a max bound). The plan states "60° slopes" and "p99" only; the original statement is not satisfiable for large cones (24 cells: 43.6°) and is vacuous for small ones (p99 over the whole domain reads 0.0° below 1 % coverage).
5. **`gpu_erosion_conserves_mass` gains the datum-free assertion (b)** next to the plan's 0.5 % total, which is dominated by the terrain's absolute elevation and cannot detect loss of the transported material.
6. **Layer transfers use the amount actually applied after f32 rounding** (compensated). Not required by anything observed: the emulation drifts 10⁻¹⁰ with and without it at these amplitudes; it is a derived guard (ulp(20 m) = 1.9×10⁻⁶ m, so a deposit or dissolution below ~10⁻⁶ m on a bedrock cell would otherwise create or destroy mass) [INFERENCE].
7. **Dry cells settle all suspended sediment into the sediment layer.** Not in Mei/Jákó; needed for conservation under evaporation.
8. **`erosion_bake` example parameters are not changed by this note.** At the plan's `rain_mm_h: 20.0` the 600 s bake changes a 40 m-relief hill by a p99 of 0.62 m (mean 0.12 m) *(measured)*: visible as gullies at the scale of the example only if the 900 m `mountains` slopes give a comparable response, which is [INFERENCE] and must be judged on the first screenshot; the knobs that scale the result are the rain rate and `capacity`, not `strength` (table under *What the bake actually removes*).
9. **Not adopted, with the reason:** (a) Jákó's depth ramp $l_{max}(d)$ (Eq. 10): as printed (page 3 image) it is 0 at $d \le 0$ and 1 at $d \ge K_{dmax}$, i.e. it *increases* with depth, which contradicts the paragraph around it ("the erosion will occur only in shallower areas"); with either direction it adds a parameter ($K_{dmax}$, 10 in his unit system, no SI value) with no source; deep still water already deposits here because $C \propto |v| \to 0$. (b) Jákó's 12c/13c (adding the dissolved amount to the water height): it makes $d$ a mixture depth, which with $s/d$ up to 250 would make the water volume mostly sediment; the ripple problem it solves (soil removal lowering a lake surface) is expected to be small here because the surface step per substep ($\le k_s C$ ≈ 5 mm at 1 m/s on a 0.3 slope) is far below slope-driven head differences ($0.3\,l$ = 0.6 m); to be confirmed by a lake in the `flood` example. (c) Jákó's hardness field $R$ and softening (Eq. 14), and a separate bedrock hardness: the plan has one `capacity` and one `dissolve` for both layers, so exposed bedrock erodes at the sediment rate (measured: bedrock lowered 3.95 m at 200 mm/h); a per-layer hardness is the natural next parameter and has no source value. (d) Krištof's shear-stress law: needs $\tau$ from a depth-resolved flow.
10. **A bake's rain is liquid** (hydrology.md Deviation 11): the bake's `rain_mm_h` skips the per-cell snow split, so a bake is not a no-op in a cold climate. The blocker mask still applies.
11. **The thermal kernels use `transport` as scratch for the two amounts a cell moves** (hydrology.md Deviation 14): five storage buffers each, not the four of the GPU mapping table.
12. **"Dry" is the depth after the step** ($d_2 \le 10^{-4}\,l$): the dry-settle test of the dissolve/deposit block uses the depth the cell ends the substep with, since the block runs after the depth update; the transport's $c = s/d_1$ uses the depth before, as written.
13. **The suspended load is clamped at 0 after transport** (`max(0, ·)`). The note says the K factor makes $s' \ge 0$ without a clamp, which holds in exact arithmetic; in f32 $c_i\,\sum f\,\Delta t / l^2$ can exceed $s_i$ by an ulp. The clamp can create at most that ulp per cell and substep; the mass test above (3×10⁻⁷ of the moved mass) is the bound on it.
14. **A bake at `High` is 7500 frames, not 3750**: `High` takes `Medium`'s 4 substeps a frame and 512² (hydrology.md Deviation 20), so 600 s is 125 s at 60 fps. Ultra: 1875 frames as the note says.

Items marked [INFERENCE] above: that 45° (talus + 10°) is a sensible bedrock threshold (no source; consistent with Galin's range), the queueing of a second bake, the extrapolation from the 40 m-relief hill to the 900 m `mountains` preset, and anything about Julien & Simons 1985.
