## Model adopted

**One idea.** Snow is a per-cell pair (depth, water equivalent) on the terrain grid, advanced on game time. Depth follows from SWE and a bulk density that relaxes from fresh to settled; melt is a degree-day law on the lapse-rate temperature of the cell; wind moves SWE by the divergence of a threshold-cubic flux (Exner continuity, the same form as the dune model in `dust.md`); a short Laplacian smoothing gives snow its soft edges (Festenberg & Gumhold). The global point model `SurfaceWeathering` is this model evaluated on one flat exposed cell at the reference elevation, so the two agree by construction.

**State per cell** (`snow: array<vec4<f32>>`, 16 B): x depth $d$ [m], y SWE $S$ [m of water], z pending melt $m_p$ [m], w unused. Bulk density is $\rho = 1000\,S/d$ kg/m³ (water 1000 kg/m³). The plan's single `snow: f32` cannot carry the density that densification needs (Deviation 1). The renderer reads depth and SWE through `cover_cells`.

**Local temperature and phase.** $T_{loc} = T - \Gamma\,(z - z_{ref})/1000$ with $T$ = `Weather.temperature_c`, $\Gamma$ = `LAPSE_RATE_C_PER_M` × 1000 = 6.5 K/km (`climate/model.rs`), $z_{ref}$ = `Climate::reference_elevation_m`, $z$ the ground surface (`terrain_cells.x`). Phase fraction by the existing ramp, evaluated at $T_{loc}$: $f_s = \mathrm{snow\_fraction\_at}(T_{loc}) = \mathrm{clamp}((2 - T_{loc})/3, 0, 1)$ (`weather.rs:160-162`), exported as one shared WGSL function `snow_fraction_at(t_c: f32) -> f32` that hydrology imports too, so both sides use one definition. Precipitation arrives as `Weather.precipitation_mm_h` (all phases) and is split per cell, so the rain–snow line follows elevation.

**Accumulation.** Per step $\Delta t$ [h of game time], with $E$ = exposure $= (1 - \mathrm{shed}(\text{slope}))\cdot s_{snow}$, $s_{snow}$ = the snow shelter mask (0 under a `PrecipitationBlocker`, `wetness.md`):

$$\Delta S = 10^{-3}\,P\,f_s\,E\,\Delta t,\qquad \Delta d = \Delta S\cdot\frac{1000}{\rho_{new}},\qquad \mathrm{shed}(\alpha) = \mathrm{smoothstep}(\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \alpha)$$

with $P$ in mm/h, $\rho_{new}$ = `new_snow_density` and $(\alpha_0, \alpha_1)$ = `shed_slope_deg` (35°, 55°). The slope $\alpha = \arctan\lvert\nabla z\rvert$ is unpacked from `terrain_cells.w`. After adding, the bulk density is the volume-weighted mix, $\rho = 1000\,(S + \Delta S)/(d + \Delta d)$. This is the same bookkeeping as `weathering.rs:57-62`. Fearing's observation that the steepest surfaces are "swept of what little snow they accumulate" and that avalanching from them is "negligible compared to accumulations on nearby stable sites" is the justification for dropping shed snow instead of moving it (it is not conserved; the water balance counts melt, not shed).

**Densification.** Exponential relaxation of the bulk density toward `settled_density`, never loosening: $\rho \leftarrow \rho + \max(\rho_s - \rho, 0)(1 - e^{-\Delta t/\tau})$, $\tau$ = `settle_days` × 24 h, then $d = 1000\,S/\rho$. After 1, 3 and 10 days a fresh 100 kg/m³ layer is 156.7, 226.4 and 292.9 kg/m³ (computed from the formula). Step-size independence: the bulk relaxation over 10 h of 1.5 mm/h snowfall gives depth 0.13306, 0.13281 and 0.13155 m for steps of 1 min, 10 min and 1 h, and 0.13304 m for 10 s steps (computed); the exact layered integral $\int_0^{10\,h} 1.5\,\mathrm{mm/h}\cdot1000/\rho(a)\,da$ with $\rho(a) = 300 - 200e^{-a/72h}$ is 0.13301 m. The two agree to 0.04 %, so the bulk form used by `SurfaceWeathering` is a faithful single-state version of per-layer settling.

**Melt.** Hock Eq. (2) with $T_0$ = 0 °C: $M = \min(S,\ \mathrm{DDF}\cdot 10^{-3}\max(T_{loc}, 0)\,\Delta t_d)$ [m w.e.], $\Delta t_d$ in days. $S \leftarrow S - M$, density unchanged so $d = 1000\,S/\rho$ (ripening is not modelled), $m_p \leftarrow m_p + M$. `degree_day_factor` 4 mm w.e./°C/day sits in the middle of Hock's Table 1 snow column. Hock warns that daily means are biased near 0 °C and that "a high time resolution for temperature integration is desirable": the kernel integrates the instantaneous $T_{loc}$ over short steps, never a daily mean. Melt water leaves the snow only through $m_p$.

**Release to hydrology (conservation).** When $m_p \ge 5\cdot10^{-6}$ m, or when $S = 0$ (flush the remainder), the kernel moves $m_p$ into `water[i]` (read–write binding) and zeroes it. Total water added equals total melt exactly, apart from the sub-quantum remainder that is flushed when the cell empties. The quantum exists because an f32 `water` cell at depth 0.4 m has ulp 3·10⁻⁸ m and a 60 fps step of 5 mm/h melt is 2.3·10⁻⁸ m, which would otherwise vanish (hydrology note's finding). Rain falling on snow is not intercepted: hydrology adds rain to `water` as in plan 3.2 (the plan has no liquid-water-holding capacity or refreezing; the corpus routines have both; Limitation 1 below).

**Drift.** One 10 m reference wind. Surface wind $u_s$ is the terrain-modified wind of `dust.md` (speed-up and the 15° lee shadow), shared as one WGSL function. Potential flux of SWE through a cell, per unit width:

$$q_s = \frac{k_d}{\rho_w}\,\big(\max(u_s - u_t, 0)\big)^3\quad[\mathrm{m^2/s\ w.e.}],\qquad u_t = \texttt{drift\_threshold\_ms}$$

Component fluxes $F = q_s\,\hat u$ cross cell faces by donor-cell upwind. Per step, cell $i$ sends $o_x = \lvert F_x\rvert\,\Delta t/\Delta$ of SWE through its downwind x-face and $o_z = \lvert F_z\rvert\,\Delta t/\Delta$ through its downwind z-face; if $o_x + o_z > S_i$ both are scaled by $S_i/(o_x + o_z)$ (supply limit: the cell cannot export more than it holds). Depth leaves with the donor's density, $d_{out} = o\,d_i/S_i$. The receiving cells gain exactly what the donors lose, so drift conserves SWE and depth exactly. Deposition is the divergence: where the potential flux declines downwind, because the wind slows over the lee or in a shadow, more arrives than leaves and the pack grows, which is Exner continuity, $\partial S/\partial t = -\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf F$ (Kok et al. 2012 Eq. 3.4 and 3.7 form; Festenberg & Gumhold §7: wind "enters Equation (2) as a varying snowfall rate"). On uniform terrain under uniform wind there is no divergence and the pack does not change: edges use a zero-gradient ghost cell (neighbour outside = edge cell's own $F$), so a flat test stays flat. The plan says snow is "deposited where wind convergence is negative (lee)"; with $\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf u<0$ meaning compression that sign is ambiguous, and the flux-divergence statement above is the unambiguous physical form (Deviation 3).

Coefficient $k_d$: the plan gives only $\propto (u-u_t)^3$. Pomeroy & Gray: saltation alone is linear in $u_*$ and a minority of the total at high wind, so the cubic is a proxy for saltation plus suspension. Calibrate $k_d$ so that the flux equals Pomeroy & Gray's saltation rate at $u_{10}$ = 10 m/s: with intrinsic roughness 0.3 mm, $\ln(10/3\cdot10^{-4}) = 10.41$, $u_* = 0.4\,u_{10}/10.41$ = 0.384 m/s, and $u_t$ = 5 m/s maps to $u_{*t}$ = 0.192 m/s (inside the fresh-snow range 0.07–0.25); $Q_{salt} = 0.68\cdot1.2\cdot0.192/(9.81\cdot0.384)\cdot(0.384^2 - 0.192^2) = 4.6\cdot10^{-3}$ kg/m/s, so $k_d = 4.6\cdot10^{-3}/5^3 = 3.7\cdot10^{-5}$ kg s²/m⁴. Check at $u_{10}$ = 18 m/s (Blizzard): the cubic gives 0.081 kg/m/s; the same formula's saltation is $u_* = 0.69$, $Q_{salt}$ = 0.0102 kg/m/s, and "8 % at $u_*$ = 0.7" implies a total of about 0.13 kg/m/s, so the cubic is 0.64× of that total at 18 m/s and equals the saltation rate at 10 m/s, where saltation is most of the total. [INFERENCE for the recalled $Q_{salt}$ form and the roughness.] In SWE units, dividing by $\rho_w$: $q_s$ = 4.6·10⁻⁶ m²/s w.e. at 10 m/s and 8.1·10⁻⁵ at 18 m/s. At 10 m/s that is 4.6·10⁻⁶ × 3600 / 2 m = 8 mm w.e. per hour exchanged across a 2 m cell where the flux changes by its full value over one cell, compared with 1.5 mm/h of snowfall: drift redistributes strongly wherever wind speed changes, as in a real blow.

**Diffusion (Festenberg & Gumhold).** After drift, `snow_diffuse_iterations` = 2 passes of an explicit 4-neighbour Laplacian on both channels $(d, S)$ with $\lambda = D\Delta t/\Delta x^2 = 0.125$: $h' = h + \lambda(h_N + h_S + h_E + h_W - 4h)$, no-flux edges. This is their Eq. (2) with the smallest kernel, which their §6 uses for the growth animation. Applying one linear operator to $d$ and $S$ conserves both and keeps the density a mass-weighted average. One iteration has variance $2\lambda$ cells² per axis, two give $4\lambda = 0.5$ cell², $\sigma$ = 0.71 cell: a soft edge, no visible blur at 2 m cells. Plan: "a 2-iteration diffusion (Festenberg)". The temperature dependence of $D$ is not used (their §4 and §7 contradict each other).

**Coverage and shading** (shared WGSL import `bevy_elements::surface_cover`, used by `TerrainExtension`, `GroundExtension` and `WeatheredExtension`). Terrain and ground, from the map: $c = \mathrm{smoothstep}(0, 0.05, d)$ (5 cm full cover, the value `SurfaceWeathering` uses, `weathering.rs:25,74-75`; $c$ = 0.156, 0.5, 1 at $d$ = 1.25, 2.5, 5 cm). Base colour $\leftarrow \mathrm{mix}(\text{base}, \text{SNOW\_ALBEDO}, c)$ with `SNOW_ALBEDO` = (0.80, 0.83, 0.88) copied from flora `common.wesl:388`, `perceptual_roughness` $\leftarrow \mathrm{mix}(r, 0.9, c)$ (Deviation 4: plan 0.6, flora 0.9). Terrain vertex displacement adds $d$ to the surface height, so the vertex stage reads `cover_cells` too (it must also be displaced in the prepass vertex shader, `bevy-0.20-render.md` "MaterialExtension").

Any mesh (`WeatheredMaterial`), from the point model: the flora curve with `snow` = `SurfaceWeathering.snow_cover`, cover $= \mathrm{snow}\cdot\mathrm{smoothstep}(0.85 - 0.6\,\mathrm{snow},\ 1.05 - 0.6\,\mathrm{snow},\ n_y)\cdot\mathrm{exposure}$ ($n_y$ the geometric world normal, not the normal-mapped one). Values (computed): `snow` 0.5 gives cover 0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.422, 0.078, 0 at $n_y$ = 1.0, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.6, 0.5; `snow` 1 gives full cover for $n_y \ge 0.45$ (a 63° face); `snow` 0 gives none. Terrain shedding (35–55°, $n_y$ 0.82–0.57) is geometric and stricter than the flora curve at full snow; an up-facing roof (0°) is white in both, a 50° gable face keeps 0.16 in the terrain model and full cover in the flora curve at full snow. That mismatch is deliberate in the plan (trees and buildings share one curve) and is recorded as a known difference, not changed.

**Readback and `SurfaceWeathering`.** `Environment::snow_depth(Vec2)` samples the readback of `cover_cells.x`. `SurfaceWeathering` (plan 4.1) becomes the mean of the map within 50 m of the camera; the CPU point model stays as the fallback before the first readback and when no `Terrain` exists. A unit test compares the two on flat exposed terrain (Validation 7).

**M5 reuse (voxel snow slab).** `SnowyField` needs only the depth grid: `cover_cells.x` from the readback snapshot as `Arc<[f32]>`, cell size equal to the terrain grid's, snow thickness in metres above the column top. Festenberg & Gumhold's boundary factor $g_1(r) = r(2 - r)$ (a quarter circle that makes the height vanish at the snow boundary without disturbing the interior slope) is the profile to use on the slab edge when depth falls below $0.25\,\Delta x$; the diffusion above already produces the soft margin it needs. The swap rule (depth changes by more than $0.5\,\Delta x$ in any column) is checked against `snow.x` at each readback. `voxel-snow.md` takes it from here.

**Limitations.** (1) No liquid-water holding or refreezing (CWH, CFR in the corpus routines; CemaNeige's cold content $T_{sn}$): melt leaves the pack the moment it is produced, so meltwater arrives earlier and in a sharper pulse than in real catchments; the plan's validation asks for water = SWE, which this satisfies. (2) Density does not ripen with melt. (3) Shed snow is lost, not re-deposited. (4) Blockers beyond the 256 uploaded around the camera shelter nothing for cells far from the camera. (5) Rain falling on a pack neither melts it nor wets the ground beneath. (6) The drift threshold is one value; Li & Pomeroy found 7.7 m/s for dry and 9.9 m/s for wet snow on average.

## GPU mapping

Kernels (WESL, workgroup 8×8×1 = 64 invocations, limit 256, `queue_kernel` and `ready` from `src/gpu/kernel.rs:30,59`, dispatch pattern as `fog/gpu.rs`, `precipitation/gpu.rs`). They run in the render-graph schedule before `camera_driver`, after hydrology's substeps, and only when all are `ready`. Per snow step, in order:

| kernel | storage buffers (binding: access) | other | writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `snow_source` (accumulate, densify, melt, release) | `snow` rw, `terrain_cells` ro, `water` rw, `shelter` ro (vec2: rain, snow exposure) | uniform `SnowParams` (Δt, P, T, Γ, z_ref, settings) | `snow`, `water` |
| `snow_drift_flux` (skipped when max speed-up × wind < $u_t$) | `snow` ro, `terrain_cells` ro, `drift` rw (vec4: SWE out x/z, depth out x/z) | uniform | `drift` |
| `snow_drift_apply` | `snow` rw, `drift` ro | uniform | `snow` |
| `snow_diffuse` ×2 | iteration 1: `snow` ro → `drift` rw; iteration 2: `drift` ro → `snow` rw | uniform | ping-pong |
| `cover_pack` | `snow` ro, `wetness` ro, `cover_cells` rw | uniform | `cover_cells` |

The drift apply pass reads only the neighbours' `drift` entries and its own `snow`, so in-place update is race-free; the diffusion passes alternate buffers for the same reason. At most 4 storage buffers per kernel, far below the 8-per-stage limit (`bevy-0.20-render.md:799`; 4 is also within `downlevel_defaults`). No storage textures. Memory at 1024² (computed): `snow` 16 MiB, `drift` 16 MiB, `cover_cells` 16 MiB, `shelter` 8 MiB; 2048² is four times that. `snow`, `drift`, `cover_cells` are created in the main world as `Handle<ShaderBuffer>` with `RENDER_WORLD` usage (`src/gpu/grid.rs:13`), read back through `ReadbackOnce`/`Readback` on the 250 ms cadence (`src/gpu/readback.rs`), one outstanding readback per field; `cover_cells` is downsampled 2× above 1024².

Per-pass cost at 1024², RTX 3090 (936 GB/s peak, taking 50 % as achievable) [INFERENCE, not measured; to be recorded by `bench_elements` as `elements_snow_*`]: source 64 B/cell → 0.14 ms; drift flux 48 B (plus a 5-tap horizon march that stays in cache) → 0.11 ms; drift apply 80 B → 0.18 ms; two diffusion passes 64 B each → 0.28 ms; pack 36 B → 0.08 ms. About 0.8 ms per step with drift, 0.5 ms without; at the 20 Hz cap that is at most 0.27 ms per 60 Hz frame on average. The plan has no M4 budget; the target recorded here is ≤ 0.3 ms mean per frame at High. At 256² the whole chain is about 0.05 ms per step.

Materials (`TerrainExtension`, `GroundExtension`, `WeatheredExtension`): extension storage bindings start at slot 100 and share one bind group with `StandardMaterial` (`bevy-0.20-render.md:281`), default visibility is vertex+fragment (`bevy_render_macros-0.20.0/src/as_bind_group.rs:1821`), so `cover_cells` for terrain is `#[storage(N, read_only, visibility(vertex, fragment))]` (the vertex stage reads snow depth for displacement). Fragment-stage Bevy storage buffers with an atmosphere camera are 5: `mesh` at group 2 binding 0 (`mesh_bindings.rs:46-49` overrides it to VERTEX_FRAGMENT; `metadata`, binding 9, is vertex-only, `:261-273`), the three clustered-light buffers at group 0 bindings 8–10 (`mesh_view_bindings.wesl:28-32`, `CLUSTERED_FORWARD_STORAGE_BUFFER_COUNT = 3` at `pbr/cluster/mod.rs:39`, storage when the device limit ≥ 3, `render_device.rs:298-306`) and the atmosphere buffer at binding 33 (`mesh_view_bindings.rs:463-465`). That leaves 3 of 8: `terrain_cells`, `cover_cells` and one shared globals buffer (`wetness.md`). Vertex stage: `mesh`, `metadata` and the visibility-range buffer (binding 14, storage at limit ≥ 4 on the Rust side) = 3 of 8 (an upper bound; unused bindings are not counted by wgpu). On a downlevel device (limit 4) the fragment stage has no room for any extension storage buffer; terrain on such devices is outside this milestone, as in the plan's wasm contingency (WebGPU's default 8 holds). Colour and roughness edits in an extension's fragment need no prepass override, but the deferred G-buffer does (`pbr/extended_material.rs:294-299`; the example overrides it at `examples/shader/extended_material.rs:105-107`), and a vertex displacement needs `prepass_vertex_shader` with the same displacement.

## Deviations from this plan

1. **State is `snow: array<vec4<f32>>` (depth, SWE, pending melt), not `snow: f32`.** Source: densification toward a settled density needs a per-cell density, and Hock's remark about resolution plus the hydrology note's f32-ulp finding require a pending-melt channel. Reason: with depth alone the density cannot be tracked; Festenberg & Gumhold's and the plan's uses of depth are unchanged (it is channel x).
2. **Melt is released to `water` in quanta of ≥ 5·10⁻⁶ m per cell (flushed when the cell empties), not added every step.** Source: hydrology note (agreed by message), f32 ulp arithmetic above. The totals are unchanged.
3. **Drift deposition is the divergence of the potential flux, with a calibrated coefficient and a supply limit.** The plan says flux "∝ (u − u_t)³ … deposited where wind convergence is negative (lee)". Adopted: $q_s = (k_d/\rho_w)(u_s - u_t)_+^3$, $k_d$ = 3.7·10⁻⁵ kg s²/m⁴ (Pomeroy & Gray 1990 magnitudes), donor-cell fluxes limited by the cell's SWE, zero-gradient edges, deposition wherever the flux declines downwind (Kok et al. 2012 Eq. 3.4/3.7). Reason: the plan's sign convention is ambiguous, the coefficient is missing, and a flux scheme conserves mass by construction. The exponent 3 is kept although Pomeroy & Gray find saltation linear in $u_*$: they also find saltation is only 8 % of the total at $u_{10} \approx$ 15 m/s, and the cubic reproduces their saltation rate at 10 m/s and the total at 18 m/s within 1.6×.
4. **Snow roughness 0.9, not 0.6.** Source: `bevy_flora` `common.wesl:424` mixes roughness toward 0.9; the plan wants trees and buildings to match and names only the albedo and the curve. Reason: a 0.6 ground next to 0.9 trees would read as two different snows. Revert to 0.6 if a gloss look is wanted; it is one constant in `surface_cover`.
5. **Snow fraction and lapse rate per cell.** The plan applies `precipitation·snow_fraction` with the reference-elevation fraction and also uses a lapse rate only for melt. Adopted: $f_s(T_{loc})$ per cell (the same ramp), through the shared WGSL `snow_fraction_at`. Source: ISO 2533 lapse via `climate-weather.md`; consistency with melt. Agreed with the hydrology note: it takes total precipitation and applies `rain·mask·(1 − f_s(T_cell))` with `T_cell = Weather.temperature_c + LAPSE_RATE_C_PER_M·(surface_m − Climate.reference_elevation_m)`, so rain and snow shares sum to the precipitation in every cell.
6. **Melt threshold $T_0$ = 0 °C stated explicitly** (plan writes `max(T_local, 0)`). Source: Hock 2003 Eq. (1)/(2); no numeric change.
7. **Step cap 600 s and ≤ 20 Hz kernel rate**, not in the plan: cost control, state is slow in game time.

8. **The diffusion weight is a rate in game time: λ = 0.125 · min(Δt / 600 s, 1) per pass.** Source: Festenberg & Gumhold Eq. (2) is $\partial h/\partial t = D\nabla^2 h + \sigma$, a rate; the note's fixed λ per kernel step would make the smoothing depend on the frame rate and the 20 Hz cap (a hole under a roof closed by 13 cells in an hour at 60 steps a second in the first GPU run). λ = 0.125 is exact at the longest step (600 s), the case Validation 5 specifies, and shorter steps scale linearly (`diffusion_is_a_rate_in_game_time`: 600 s of game time smooths the same in one step or in sixty). A cell under a `PrecipitationBlocker` is a wall of the diffusion, so a sheltered cell stays bare and the snow beside it keeps its edge.
9. **Drift edges are closed, not zero-gradient.** Nothing enters from outside the grid and nothing leaves through the downwind edge (the donor's export is zero there), so SWE and depth are conserved exactly (Validation 4c asks for 1e-5 relative). A zero-gradient ghost inflow at the upwind edge was tried first and creates snow whenever the edge cell holds any (+0.3 % over 1 000 steps from a Gaussian tail 4.4σ from the edge). Consequence: in uniform wind on a uniform pack the wind erodes the upwind column and piles snow against the downwind one (Validation 4a is asserted away from the ten edge columns; the below-threshold case is unaffected).
10. **The surface wind is a local function of `snow.wesl`, not a shared import.** `dust.md` names one shared WGSL function with snow drift; it does not exist on this branch's base (the aeolian kernels are on `m4-dust`), so `snow.wesl` carries the same formulas (Venturi on height above the 50 m ring mean, k_W = 5·10⁻³ m⁻¹, clamp 0.5–2; 10°–15° shadow over taps of one cell out to 10 m). To be unified when both branches are merged; the two must agree because the drift and the dunes see one wind.
11. **The shelter mask is the slanted ray of `wetness.md`, not the vertical test of `source_map.wesl`.** Snow and wetness read `shelter` (rain and snow rays); hydrology's own rain mask (`source_map.y`) stays vertical, so under a wind-slanted roof shadow the rain that wets the ground is masked by the ray but the rain that fills the water layer by the vertical column. Hydrology's mask is not changed here.
12. **Snow state, wetness and the shelter mask are owned by `GroundCoverPlugin`, not by `TerrainState`.** `terrain.md` Deviation 16 has the terrain allocate every hydrology layer; the cover layers (`snow`, `drift`, `wetness`, `shelter`, `cover_cells`, a readback buffer) are created by `CoverState` for the active terrain, so the terrain plugin does not change and a game that drops the plugin pays nothing. The render pack `cover_cells` is still a flat `array<vec4<f32>>` bound at slot 102 of `TerrainExtension` and `GroundExtension`.
13. **Kernel scheduling: the clock's game time since the last step, in steps of at most 600 s, at most 20 Hz (`SnowSettings::max_rate_hz`, 0 = every frame).** A frame integrates all the game time since its last step in `ceil(Δt / 600 s)` equal steps (at most 144; a longer jump is cut, as `weathering.rs` clamps to 24 h). The weather is held constant over the frame. The wetness update runs once per frame with the whole frame's Δt.

Kept at the plan's values although the sources differ or are silent (no deviation): settle time 3 days (no source gives a time constant); drift threshold 5 m/s (observed range 4–11 m/s, mean 7.7 dry); shed range 35–55°; new/settled density 100/300 kg/m³ (within Judson & Doesken and Sturm et al.); DDF 4 (Table 1 median region); 2-iteration diffusion.
