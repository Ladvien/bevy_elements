## Model adopted

**Pool and geometry.** Water is drawn by a second CDLOD pool that reuses the terrain's selection (terrain.md): the same levels, ranges, morph and `MeshTag::new(level)`, one `Aabb` per patch from the readback bounds `(h_min, water_surface_max)` padded by 4 m (a flood front rises faster than the 2 m terrain pad allows: 50 m³/s into a 4 m² cell is a depth change measured in metres per second at the source cell, so the pad is larger and the cost of a stale AABB is an early cull of a patch at the frustum edge, never a crack). A patch whose `water_depth_max` < 0.003 m is `Visibility::Hidden` (no water to draw). Vertex height is `y0 + surface + water_depth + 1 mm` from `terrain_cells.x + .z` (bilinear, `grid_taps`): the 1 mm lift replaces Jákó's −Δy offset trick: both pools displace from the same buffer, so at a dry cell the water vertex coincides with the terrain vertex and the lift plus the fragment discard keep the two from z-fighting (water is discarded where depth < 3 mm, so the 1 mm separation is only ever visible where water is at least 3 mm deep). The water pool exists for `TerrainRender::External` too (terrain.md Deviation 6).

**Two draws per patch, because one blend cannot do chromatic absorption.** The plan's design is one `AlphaMode::Blend` pass with Beer–Lambert from the depth prepass. Blend writes `out = a·S + (1 − a)·B` (`BlendState::ALPHA_BLENDING`, `$R/bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/render/mesh.rs:3531`): the background `B` is scaled by one scalar for all three channels, so the plan's per-channel `σ = (0.45, 0.06, 0.03)` could only enter through a single scalar `σ̄` (luminance-weighted mean 0.141 m⁻¹) and the water could never change the *hue* of what is under it. Quantified on the plan's σ: the exact transmittance ratio red/blue at 2 m is `exp(−(0.45 − 0.03)·2) = 0.432`, and 0.657 at 1 m, 0.122 at 5 m; a scalar model gives 1.000 at every depth. Per-channel filtering of the destination needs either dual-source blending (`DUAL_SOURCE_BLENDING = WEBGPU_FEATURE_DUAL_SOURCE_BLENDING`, `wgpu-types-30.0.1/src/features.rs:82,1767`: an optional WebGPU feature, not guaranteed on a device, so using it would be a second code path) or two passes. Bevy ships both blend states needed:

- `AlphaMode::Multiply` (`mesh.rs:3543-3557`): `out = dst·src.rgb + (1 − src.a)·dst` with the shader premultiplying `src.rgb *= src.a` (`pbr_functions.wesl:956-970`); writing `src = (X, a)` gives `out = dst·lerp(1, X, a)`: a per-channel filter with an alpha-controlled edge.
- `AlphaMode::Add` (shares the `BLEND_PREMULTIPLIED_ALPHA` pipeline, `mesh.rs:3535-3542`, `BlendState::PREMULTIPLIED_ALPHA_BLENDING`; the shader writes `(rgb·a, 0)` for the Add flag, `pbr_functions.wesl:926-947`): `out = dst + a·S`, pure addition.

Both write no depth (`depth_write_enabled = false`, `mesh.rs:3534, 3542, 3557`) and both are drawn in the sorted transparent phase (`material.rs:1749-1757`). So one extension type `WaterExtension`, two material instances: **`absorb`** (base `StandardMaterial.alpha_mode = Multiply`) and **`surface`** (base `alpha_mode = Add`); the shader branches on the `BLEND_MULTIPLY` shader def Bevy sets for the first (`mesh.rs:3553-3554`). `MaterialExtension::alpha_mode()` is a static function (`extended_material.rs:45`) and `ExtendedMaterial::alpha_mode(&self)` returns the extension's value when `Some`, else the base's per instance (`:242-247`); the extension returns `None`. The radiance model that the pair implements, with `F` the Fresnel reflectance at the wave normal, `T` the per-channel transmittance of the water column, `S_refl` the reflected radiance, `L_body` the in-scattered body radiance:

$$ L = F\,L_{refl} + (1-F)\,\big[\,T\,L_{bed} + L_{body}\,(1-T_s)\big],\qquad \text{absorb: } \mathrm{dst} \leftarrow \mathrm{dst}\cdot\big[(1-F)\,T\,(1-f_{foam})\big],\quad \text{surface: } \mathrm{dst} \mathrel{+}= F\,L_{refl} + (1-F)\,L_{body} + f_{foam}\,L_{foam} $$

(edge alpha `a_edge` mixes each pass toward the identity). Foam occludes the bed by zeroing the absorb factor and adds its own lit colour. The order within one patch is absorb first, surface second; both items share a sort key: `Transparent3d` sorts ascending by `rangefinder.distance(mesh_center) + depth_bias` where `distance` is the view-space z, which "increases towards the camera" (back-to-front, `core_3d/mod.rs:425-437, 449-496`, `render_phase/rangefinder.rs:18-24`), with `mesh_center` the transformed centre of the *mesh asset's* AABB, i.e. the patch centre (`material.rs:1325-1340`). The surface material therefore carries `StandardMaterial::depth_bias = 1.0` (documented as "render closer … affects render ordering", `pbr_material.rs:686-697`), which adds 1 m to its sort z and draws it after its patch's absorb pass; neighbouring patches do not overlap, so their relative order is irrelevant. The raster depth bias `bias as i32` (`pbr_material.rs:1407-1408`) of 1 unit is harmless. Sorted-phase items batch only when adjacent with the same key, so the interleaving makes one draw per pass per patch: ≤ 2 × the water-bearing patches (≲ 100 worst case at High, typically 20–60), against ≈ 10 for the opaque ground.

**Rejected alternatives (evidence).** (a) *Screen-space reflections*: `ScreenSpaceReflections` is "currently only supported with deferred rendering" and requires `DepthPrepass` + `DeferredPrepass` (`ssr/mod.rs:54, 78-80`); Bevy's own water example is an opaque `ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, Water>` on a deferred `Msaa::Off` camera (`$R/bevy-0.20.0/examples/3d/ssr.rs:50-100, 380-445`, base roughness 0.09). A blended water pass is not written to the G-buffer, and the crate does not force deferred rendering, so reflections come from the `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight` that `ElementsCamera` already carries (render.md §Atmosphere) plus the sun's specular highlight from the standard lighting loop. (b) *`ScreenSpaceTransmission`* (refraction by copying the main texture): in 0.20 it is an opt-in camera component (`transmission/mod.rs:59-98`, migration guide `transmission_opt_in.md`), its pass runs `.after(main_opaque_pass_3d).before(main_transparent_pass_3d)` (`:49-55`) where the atmosphere's sky pass also sits (render.md §Where systems go: no ordering between the two), a material's `thickness`/`attenuation_distance` are material scalars, not per pixel, and `reads_view_transmission_texture` comes from the *base* material (`extended_material.rs:257-259`), so it would couple the water to a camera setting and change the phase of the water; the two-pass pair gives per-channel absorption exactly and needs no camera component. Refraction displacement is omitted, and that has a cost at grazing angles: the lateral shift of the bed point is `d(tan θ_v − tan θ_r)` (computed), 0.35 m at d = 2 m and θ_v = 30°, 1.75 m at 60° (about one 2 m sim cell), 0 at nadir. Re-sampling the bed through the refracted ray would need the transmission texture rejected in (b); the omission is a known visual error of shallow-angle views over deep water, judged acceptable for floods a metre or two deep [INFERENCE: not measured].

**Water thickness from the depth prepass (verified).** `AlphaMode::Blend`, `Premultiplied`, `Add` and `Multiply` meshes are not queued in the prepass (`prepass/mod.rs:1143-1151`), so the depth prepass texture holds the opaque scene only (terrain, rocks, buildings) — exactly "what is under the water". Binding: with a `DepthPrepass` camera the forward view layout gains `depth_prepass_texture` at group 0 binding 20 (`render/mesh_view_bindings.wesl:73-74`, not multisampled; `MeshPipelineViewLayoutKey::DEPTH_PREPASS` comes from `ViewPrepassTextures.depth`, `mesh_view_bindings.rs:204-209`; the shader def `DEPTH_PREPASS` from the view key, `mesh.rs:422, 3582-3583`), and `bevy_pbr::prepass::utils::prepass_depth(frag_coord, sample_index)` is a `textureLoad` of it (`prepass/utils.wesl:3-9`). `ElementsCamera` inserts `DepthPrepass` and `Msaa::Off` (AGENTS.md traps), so the texture is the non-multisampled one. A view without `DepthPrepass` has no such binding: `WaterExtension::specialize` (signature `extended_material.rs:123`) returns `Err(SpecializedMeshPipelineError)` when `MaterialExtensionKey::mesh_key` lacks `MeshPipelineKey::DEPTH_PREPASS`, so water is simply not drawn for such a view and the pipeline cache logs it [INFERENCE: the error path was not run; the alternative is a silent second code path, which the crate rules forbid]. Reverse-Z conversion: `view_z = depth_ndc_to_view_z(ndc_depth)` = `−near / ndc_depth` for a perspective view (`view_transformations.wesl:169-171`, `near = clip_from_view[3][2]`, `perspective_camera_near`, `:163-165`), negative in front of the camera. With the fragment's own view-space z `z_f` (from its world position) and the scene's `z_s`, both on the same camera ray of unit view direction `r̂`:

$$ t_{ray} = \frac{z_f - z_s}{-\hat r_z}\quad[\mathrm{m}],\qquad t_{eff} = t_{ray}\,\frac{\cos\theta_v}{\cos\theta_r},\quad \cos\theta_r = \sqrt{1 - \frac{1-\cos^2\theta_v}{n^2}},\ n = 1.33 $$

`t_ray` is the thickness along the straight view ray, exact for the geometry the prepass sees; `t_eff` replaces it by the path along the *refracted* ray under a flat surface (Snell; exact for a horizontal bed, an approximation for walls), which is shorter: the ratio `cos θ_v / cos θ_r` is 0.935 at 30°, 0.659 at 60°, 0.377 at 75° (computed). `θ_v` is the angle between the view ray and the wave normal. The ratio is a correction the plan does not contain; without it grazing views over-absorb by up to 2.6×. Per-channel and turbidity extinction use `t_eff`; foam uses `t_ray`.

**Absorption coefficients checked against measurement.** The plan's `σ = (0.45, 0.06, 0.03) m⁻¹` against the two spectra (m⁻¹; Pope & Fry 1997 from the OMLC file ×100; Hale & Querry 1973 likewise):

| λ nm | 450 | 465 | 500 | 550 | 600 | 620 | 650 | 680 | 700 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pope & Fry | 0.0092 | 0.0101 | 0.0204 | 0.0565 | 0.2224 | 0.2755 | 0.3400 | 0.4650 | 0.6240 |
| Hale & Querry | 0.0280 | 0.0260 | 0.0250 | 0.0450 | 0.2300 | 0.2700 | 0.3200 | 0.4520 | 0.6000 |

The plan's numbers are samples of these curves: red 0.45 at ≈ 680 nm (0.465 / 0.452), green 0.06 at ≈ 552 nm (Pope & Fry 0.0593 at 552.5 nm), blue 0.03 at ≈ 450 nm of the *older* Hale & Querry data (0.028); Pope & Fry's point is precisely that the blue absorption of pure water is 3× lower (0.0092 at 450 nm; minimum 0.0044 at 418 nm, which this file reproduces: 0.0000442 cm⁻¹ at 417.5 nm). The RGB-equivalent coefficients for display primaries are different from single wavelengths: computed here by weighting `exp(−a(λ)d)` with the CIE 1931 matching functions (Wyman–Sloan–Shirley multi-lobe fit, [INFERENCE: the fit coefficients are from memory]), a 6504 K blackbody as D65 stand-in, the XYZ → linear-sRGB matrix, normalised to white at `d = 0`, spectrum cut at 700 nm, and `σ_eff = −ln T / d`:

| depth d | Pope & Fry σ_eff (R, G, B) | Hale & Querry σ_eff (R, G, B) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 m | 0.293, 0.048, ≈ 0.000 | 0.298, 0.044, 0.024 |
| 1 m | 0.294, 0.049, 0.000 | 0.302, 0.044, 0.024 |
| 2 m | 0.299, 0.049, 0.001 | 0.309, 0.044, 0.024 |
| 5 m | 0.325, 0.051, 0.002 | 0.357, 0.045, 0.025 |

(values for `d ≥ 10 m` are invalid in the red channel: the sRGB red matching function has a negative lobe and the table ends at 700 nm). So for pure water the sRGB-effective absorption is `(0.29–0.33, 0.049–0.051, ≈ 0.00–0.002)`; the plan's red is 1.4–1.5× that and its blue 15× (Pope & Fry) or 1.2× (Hale & Querry). Natural freshwater is not pure: dissolved organic matter and chlorophyll add absorption that rises toward blue (Bricaud et al., not read), which is the plausible reason to keep a blue floor of 0.03; the plan's values therefore describe slightly tinted natural water and are kept as the default (no source here fixes a better number for "a river"), exposed as `WaterSettings::absorption` (default `(0.45, 0.06, 0.03)`), with the pure-water RGB-equivalent `(0.30, 0.05, 0.004)` recorded as the lower bound a test asserts the default exceeds channel for channel. For reference the plan's transmittances: `T(0.5 m) = (0.799, 0.970, 0.985)`, `T(1) = (0.638, 0.942, 0.970)`, `T(2) = (0.407, 0.887, 0.942)`, `T(5) = (0.105, 0.741, 0.861)`; pure water per Pope & Fry would give `T(2) = (0.55, 0.906, 0.999)`.

**Surface: Fresnel and reflection.** F₀ of water is 0.02 (RTR4 Table 9.1). Bevy computes `F₀ = 0.16·reflectance²` (`pbr_functions.wesl:279`), so `reflectance = √(0.02/0.16) = 0.3536` instead of the default 0.5 (which is F₀ = 0.04). Schlick at the view angle: F = 0.020 (0°), 0.0506 (60°), 0.398 (80°) (computed). Perceptual roughness: Bevy clamps it to ≥ 0.089 in the lighting code (`pbr_lighting.wesl:548-550`, "According to Filament design 0.089 is recommended for mobile"), so the plan's base roughness 0.04 is unreachable; the base is set to 0.089 (Bevy's own SSR water example uses 0.09), and the ripple normals supply the visible roughness. `metallic = 0`, `base_color` is set per pixel (below), `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight` provides the sky reflection, the sun's `DirectionalLight` the glint with its shadows.

**Surface: the pixel recipe (`water.wesl`, `fragment`).**

1. *Depth and edge.* `d_px` = bilinear `terrain_cells.z` (four loads). Discard when `d_px < 0.003 m`; `a_edge = smoothstep(0.003, 0.03, d_px)` (plan).
2. *Thickness* `t_ray`, `t_eff` (above).
3. *Flow-mapped normal.* `v = ` bilinear `flow_cells.xy`, multiplied by `smoothstep(0.003, 0.03, d_px)` (Mei's velocity `Δflux / (d̄ l)` is noisy at tiny depth); speed `|v|`. Vlachos's two layers: with period `T = 1.5 s`, tile `L = 8 m`, distortion limit `D = 1/3` of a tile, flow offset per unit phase `o = clamp_len(v·T/L, D)` (a vector in tile units, the speed saturates at `v_max = D·L/T = 1.78 m/s`), spatial phase noise `ψ(x, z) = 0.5 + 0.5·perlin(x/37 m, z/37 m, 0.5)`, phases `p_i = fract(t/T + ψ + 0.5·i)` and weights `w_i = 1 − |2 p_i − 1|` (`i = 0, 1`; `w₀ + w₁ = 1` identically, `w_i = 0` at the restart of its own phase, so the jump is invisible), coordinates `uv_i = (x, z)/L − o·p_i + δ_i` with `δ₀ = 0`, `δ₁ = (0.37, 0.59)` (Vlachos's offset against repetition). Normal `n_t = normalize(Σ w_i·(n_i.xy·s, 1))` with the strength `s = s_wind·lerp(0.25, 1, saturate(|v|/1.5))` (Vlachos: strength scaled by flow speed; the 0.25 floor keeps wind ripples on still water, the 1.5 m/s reference is the foam threshold). The wave-slope base `s_wind = √(0.003 + 0.00512·U₁₀)` is the Cox & Munk mean-square slope of a clean sea surface [INFERENCE: from memory, not read]: 0.153 at the climate's mean 4 m/s, 0.233 at 10 m/s. `T` is below Chentanez's τ ≤ 3 s; `D = 1/3` is Vlachos's "first 1/3 of uv space"; `L`, `δ₁`, `0.25`, `37 m` are design values [INFERENCE]. The saturation speed 1.78 m/s sits just above the foam threshold 1.5 m/s on purpose: where the flow outruns the texture's advection foam takes over.
4. *Absorb pass output:* `X = (1 − F)·T(t_eff)·(1 − f_foam)`, `T = exp(−σ·t_eff)` per channel, `src = vec4(X·a_edge, a_edge)`; no fog call (a transmittance must not be fogged), written directly rather than through `main_pass_post_lighting_processing`.
5. *Surface pass output:* `pbr_input_from_standard_material` is built with `base_color = body·α_b·(1 − f_foam) + foam_albedo·f_foam`, `reflectance = 0.3536`, `perceptual_roughness = lerp(0.089, 0.8, f_foam)`, `metallic = 0`, normal `n_t` (transformed to world with the flat water normal `(0, 1, 0)` as the frame); `apply_pbr_lighting` returns the diffuse of the body colour plus the specular `F·L_refl` (for a dielectric specular is independent of `base_color`, so the body colour scales only the diffuse term); `main_pass_post_lighting_processing` adds Bevy's distance fog for the premultiplied-additive mode; the colour is scaled by the cloud-shadow ratio `ρ` of terrain.md and `a_edge` (`PREMULTIPLY_ALPHA` makes Bevy write `(rgb·a, 0)`). The body: `body = lerp(deep_water, silt_tint, τ_t)`, `α_b = 1 − exp(−σ_s·t_eff)`, with `σ_s = κ·c`, `c` = `flow_cells.z` (turbidity as a volume fraction, `suspended/max(water, 10⁻³)`), `κ = 6 m⁻¹`, `τ_t = σ_s / (σ_s + σ_clear)`, `σ_clear = 0.05 m⁻¹` (a nominal scattering of clear water that makes the body colour present at all), `silt_tint = (0.45, 0.38, 0.25)` (the plan; read as sRGB-encoded like the terrain palette, linear (0.17, 0.12, 0.05), muddy water reflects about 0.1–0.2 [INFERENCE]), `deep_water = (0.000, 0.005, 0.025)` linear. `κ` is calibrated, not measured: Mei's equilibrium suspended load is the transport capacity `C = K_c·sin α·|v|` (`erosion.md`), e.g. `0.6·0.1·2 = 0.12 m` in a flood channel at 1 m depth, so `c = 0.12` and `κ = −ln 0.5 / (1 m · 0.12) = 5.8 ≈ 6 m⁻¹` makes a 1 m column of the plan's flood half opaque; re-fit at implementation against the `flood` example. `deep_water` is the Gordon-type diffuse reflectance `R ≈ 0.33·b_b/a` with the plan's absorption and pure-water backscatter `b_b ≈ (0.0003, 0.0008, 0.0022) m⁻¹` at (650, 550, 450) nm: `(0.0002, 0.0044, 0.024)`, rounded [INFERENCE: both the relation and the backscatter values are from memory]; without a body term deep water would absorb to black (T at 30 m is `(10⁻⁶, 0.17, 0.41)` and the bed disappears), which no lake does.
6. *Foam.* `f_v = smoothstep(1.35, 1.65, |v|)` (plan: |v| > 1.5 m/s, ramp ±10 %), `f_t = 1 − smoothstep(0.02, 0.08, t_ray)` (plan: thickness < 0.05 m, ramp centred there; this is the shoreline and the contact with any object in the water, including rocks and posts), `f_foam = max(f_v, f_t)·m_foam` with the patchiness `m_foam = smoothstep(0.30, 0.65, a_foam(uv_0))` where `a_foam` is the normal map's `a` channel (a tileable fbm, advected by the same flow phases); `f_t` ignores `m_foam` below 0.03 m so the waterline is a continuous strip. Foam albedo 0.9, roughness 0.8 [INFERENCE]. Cross-check of the speed threshold: `|v| = 1.5 m/s` is Froude number 1 (`v/√(g d)`) at `d = v²/g = 0.23 m`, so in the shallow supercritical flow at a flood's edge the rule fires at the physically meaningful regime, and in water deeper than 0.23 m it asks for more than critical speed [derived; the open-channel white-water thresholds are not read].
7. *Cloud shadow:* `ρ` as in terrain.md (the sun glint and the diffuse body are both scaled by the cloud transmittance at the pixel).

**The bed under the water (terrain side).** The absorb pass applies the *view* leg of the attenuation. The *sun* leg (light going down to the bed before it is reflected up) belongs to the terrain/ground fragment, which already knows `d` (`terrain_cells.z`): `lit *= exp(−σ·d / μ_s')` with `μ_s' = √(1 − (1 − μ_s²)/n²)`, `μ_s` the sine of the sun's elevation; at 60° elevation `μ_s' = 0.927` (computed). The same factor is applied to the sky-ambient part (a diffuse leg of about the same length), [INFERENCE]. Together a bed 2 m under water viewed from straight above with the sun at 60° elevation, with the plan's σ and F = 0.02, has radiance ratio `(0.98)·T_view·T_sun = (0.151, 0.764, 0.865)` of its dry value (computed; Validation 11). The terrain shader reads `water_depth` from the same `terrain_cells`, so there is no second buffer; the helper `bed_attenuation(d, μ_s, σ)` lives in a shared WESL module `bevy_elements::water` imported by the terrain and ground extensions.

**Generated normal map (`water_normals`, once).** `512²`, `rgba8unorm`, a write-only storage texture (WebGPU: ≤ 4 per stage, write-only, `rgba8unorm` allowed, render.md §WebGPU limits), 10 mip levels, repeat addressing, linear filtering, `anisotropy_clamp = 16`. Height `h(u, v) = Σ_{o=0}^{4} 0.7^o · perlin3_periodic((u, v)·8·2^o, 0.5 + o, period = 8·2^o)` with `u, v` the texel coordinate over 512 (so octave `o` has wavelength `64 / 2^o` texels = 1 m … 6 cm at `L = 8 m`; the finest, 4 texels, is at the Nyquist limit); the periodic noise tiles exactly (period a power of two ≤ 4096, `src/noise.wesl:97-116`, `NOISE_PERIOD` 4096). The normal is the central difference of `h` at ±1 texel with wrap, scaled by one constant so the rms slope of mip 0 is 0.15 (the Cox & Munk value at 4 m/s; the constant is measured once at implementation), encoded `n·0.5 + 0.5` in RGB, with the tileable foam fbm in `a`. A mip level `m` is not a box-filtered copy: it is the same kernel dispatched with octaves of wavelength below `2^{m+1}` texels omitted (a band-limited analytic mip: no downsample pass and no energy bleeding across the tile seam), one dispatch per level through a per-mip storage view (`texture.create_view(&TextureViewDescriptor { base_mip_level: m, mip_level_count: Some(1), .. })` in the render world, [INFERENCE: not run]); this removes shimmer at distance while the 16× anisotropy handles grazing angles. With slope gain 0.7 per octave the rms slope of mip `m` is `0.15·√(Σ_{kept o} 0.49^o / Σ_{all o} 0.49^o)`: 1.0 / 0.953 (mip 3, octaves 3 and 4 dropped) of mip 0 (computed, Validation 7).

**Pass ordering relative to the rest.** Both water materials draw in the main transparent pass (`Core3dSystems::MainPass`) before the cloud composite (`clouds.md`: composite `.after(main_transparent_pass_3d)`), the precipitation overlay (`precipitation.md`) and Bevy's volumetric fog (`.after(MainPass)`), so clouds and rain are drawn over the water and the ground fog attenuates it like the terrain. The water does not enter the prepass or the shadow maps (`enable_prepass()` and `enable_shadows()` return false; the prepass skips blended meshes anyway, and a 1 m-deep film casts no useful shadow).

## GPU mapping

- **Materials.** `WaterExtension` (not bindless: `combined_bind_group.rs:17-24`) with `#[uniform(100)] params: WaterParams` (σ, tint, deep colour, κ, L, T, D, foam constants, `GridParams`, sun-leg data), `#[storage(101, read_only, visibility(vertex, fragment))] cells: Handle<ShaderBuffer>` (`terrain_cells`), `#[storage(102, read_only, visibility(fragment))] flow: Handle<ShaderBuffer>` (`flow_cells`), `#[texture(103)] #[sampler(104)] normals: Handle<Image>`, `#[texture(105)] #[sampler(106)] cloud_shadow: Option<Handle<Image>>`. `vertex_shader()` is the terrain's CDLOD function with the water height; `fragment_shader()` is `water.wesl`; `enable_prepass()`, `enable_shadows()` false; `alpha_mode()` `None`; `specialize()` rejects views without `DEPTH_PREPASS`. Two assets: `absorb` (base alpha Multiply, `depth_bias` 0) and `surface` (base alpha Add, `depth_bias` 1, `reflectance` 0.3536, `perceptual_roughness` 0.089). `MaterialPlugin::<ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, WaterExtension>>::default()` once.
- **Entities.** Water pool: capacity 192 entities × 2 (absorb and surface share the patch transform, `MeshTag` and `Aabb`; two entities per patch because a `MeshMaterial3d` holds one material), the shared 33² mesh. Draw calls ≤ 2 per water-bearing patch (sorted phase; terrain.md Batching).
- **Per-fragment cost** (estimate, [INFERENCE]; the plan has no water budget): 8 vec4 loads (bilinear `terrain_cells` and `flow_cells`), 2 anisotropic normal-map samples, 1 prepass-depth load, a 3D noise for `ψ`, Bevy's PBR (sun + 4 cascades + environment map) with a black base colour: ≈ 0.15–0.25 ms for a full-screen water view at 1080p on the RTX 3090, ≈ 0.02 ms for the absorb pass, so 0.05–0.1 ms in a flood scene where water covers a quarter of the screen. Vertex: ≤ 135 k vertices × 2 passes × 4 loads, < 0.05 ms. Bench: print the transparent-phase span with the water on and off.
- **`water_normals`**: once at spawn, 10 dispatches (512², 256², … 1), 8 × 8 workgroups, one write-only `rgba8unorm` storage texture view per dispatch (1 of the 4 allowed), uniform `{ level, max_octave, slope_scale }`; ≈ 5 M `perlin3_periodic` evaluations for level 0 (5 octaves × 4 neighbours × 262 144 texels), ≈ 0.1 ms once [INFERENCE]. No storage buffers.
- **Limits.** Fragment stage: 2 storage buffers + Bevy's 5 = 7 of 8; 0 storage textures; textures: normal map, cloud shadow, prepass depth, the standard material's own (the base `StandardMaterial` binds none unless set) — well below the 16-sampled-textures-per-stage default. Vertex stage: Bevy's `mesh`, `metadata`, `visibility_ranges` + `cells` = 4 of 8. No `read_write` storage texture, no `r32float` filtering.
- **Where the CPU touches it**: the pool update shares the terrain's selection; the `Aabb` and visibility of the water slots are rewritten from `leaf_bounds.zw` at the 4 Hz readback; a `Changed<Aabb>` re-extracts only that instance (`render/mesh.rs:1965`).

## Deviations from this plan

1. **`AlphaMode::Blend` → an `AlphaMode::Multiply` absorb pass plus an `AlphaMode::Add` surface pass (one `WaterExtension`, two material instances).** The plan: one Blend pass with per-channel Beer–Lambert. A single blend scales the background by one scalar (`ALPHA_BLENDING`, `mesh.rs:3528-3532`), so per-channel absorption is impossible; dual-source blending is an optional wgpu feature (`features.rs:82,1767`). Evidence: the red/blue transmittance ratio of the plan's σ is 0.43 at 2 m, the scalar approximation gives 1.0. Source: Bevy files cited, arithmetic.
2. **Perceptual roughness 0.089, not 0.04.** Bevy clamps below 0.089 (`pbr_lighting.wesl:548-550`); the plan's value would silently render as 0.089. Source: Bevy.
3. **`reflectance = 0.3536` (F₀ = 0.02)**, the plan gives only the roughness. Source: RTR4 Table 9.1; `pbr_functions.wesl:279`.
4. **Thickness is corrected for refraction (`× cos θ_v / cos θ_r`)** and uses the prepass; the plan says "thickness from the depth prepass". Refinement, Snell's law (derivation), up to 2.6× at grazing angles.
5. **Water vertex lift +1 mm** with an explicit depth discard/fade, instead of Jákó's −Δy trick (Jákó 2011 §3); the plan specifies the discard and fade, not the geometry offset.
6. **Added terms not in the plan:** a deep-water body colour (without it deep water is black), the sun-leg absorption applied by the terrain shader, `σ_clear`. Derivations in Model; all [INFERENCE] values flagged.
7. **Normal map details:** 5 periodic octaves, 8 m tile, band-limited analytic mips (the plan fixes only 512² `rgba8unorm` tileable).
8. **Absorption defaults kept** (`(0.45, 0.06, 0.03)`) although they are not the pure-water values (red ×1.4–1.5, blue ×3 to ×15): natural water is not pure; the pure-water bound is recorded and tested (Validation 2). No source here supports a different single "river" value.
9. **The silt tint is read as sRGB-encoded** (same convention as the terrain palette). [INFERENCE]
10. **Water draws in `TerrainRender::External`** (terrain.md Deviation 6).
11. **The normal map's octave gain is a *slope* gain of 0.7, so the height amplitude ratio per octave is 0.35.** The note says "slope gain 0.7 per octave" and computes the mip shares with `0.49^o` (the squared slope gain), but the height formula as written (`Σ 0.7^o · perlin(…·2^o)`) has a slope that grows by `0.7·2 = 1.4` per octave and is dominated by the Nyquist octave (measured: the share of mip 3 against mip 0 came out 0.52, not 0.953). The kernel's height amplitude is `0.35^o`, which makes the slope of octave `o` `0.7^o` of the first and gives the note's shares. The slope gain constant `K` that gives mip 0 the rms slope 0.15 is **4.7** (the CPU mirror, `normal_gain_gives_the_cox_munk_slope`; the 8-bit GPU readback gives 0.1506).
12. **The foam fbm in the map's alpha is stretched ×2.5 about 0.5 and the patchiness ramp is `smoothstep(0.45, 0.75)`, not `(0.30, 0.65)`.** Measured on the GPU readback, the periodic fbm is narrow: percentiles p10/p25/p50/p75/p90 of alpha were 0.412/0.447/0.494/0.541/0.584, so the note's ramp put foam on about 75 % of every fast reach (the first `flood` screenshot was a white band). After the stretch the percentiles are 0.275/0.369/0.490/0.608/0.710 (mean 0.490) and the ramp selects about the upper third. Source: measured here; Vlachos/Chentanez fix no number.
13. **The body scattering is `σ_s = σ_clear + κ c` and the silt weight `τ_t = κ c / σ_s`.** The note's `σ_s = κ c` with `τ_t = σ_s / (σ_s + σ_clear)` leaves clear water (`c = 0`) with no body colour at all, contradicting its own description of `σ_clear` ("makes the body colour present at all"). Same two parameters, same limits.
14. **`WaterExtension::specialize` does not reject a view without `DEPTH_PREPASS`.** `SpecializedMeshPipelineError` has one variant (`MissingVertexAttribute`, `bevy_material-0.20.0/src/specialize.rs:50`), so there is no honest error to return. `prepass_depth` exists only under the `DEPTH_PREPASS` shader def, so on such a view the water shader fails to compile and the pipeline cache logs it; water is not drawn there.
15. **The sun leg takes the brightest directional light**, in both the terrain's and `GroundExtension`'s fragment (the first directional light can be the moon), reads σ and `n` from `WaterSettings` through a `vec4` in the terrain and ground uniforms, and is multiplied into the cloud-shadow factor. `GroundExtension` keeps its existing wet-darkening ramp (0.5 at 2 cm) in addition; the owned terrain has none yet (M4's wetness).
16. **The surface pass clears the material's fog flag** instead of letting `main_pass_post_lighting_processing` fog it: a fog blend on an additive pass pulls the added radiance toward the fog colour a second time, because the bed under the water was fogged by the ground pass. The tone mapping and premultiplication in that function still run.
17. **The ripple slope is `ripple_scale · s_wind / 0.15` relative to the map**, with `s_wind = √(0.003 + 0.00512 U₁₀)` (the note multiplies the decoded slope by `s_wind` directly, which would square the 0.15). `U₁₀` is `WindState::speed_10m`, quantised to 0.5 m/s so the bind group is not re-prepared every frame, or `WaterSettings::wind_speed_ms`.
18. **The 1-texel mip is flat, not "about the mean tile normal":** a mip keeps the octaves whose wavelength is at least two of its texels and none from mip 6 (octave 0 has 64 texels), so mips 6–9 are the flat normal `(0, 0, 1)` with a foam alpha of 0.5, which is what a box filter of a zero-mean tile gives.
19. **Test 12(c) is checked on the CPU** (the layer coordinates `uv − o·p` move along `+v`, the offset saturates at `D` at 1.78 m/s: Validation 6), not by cross-correlating two GPU frames; (a) and (b) are read from screenshots, tone-mapped (8-bit sRGB, converted to linear) rather than on the HDR target.
20. **Validation 11 is checked at two depths against a constant additive residual** of 0.007–0.013 linear radiance that rides on the wet image and is not in the model (see the results); it is neither the fog nor the surface pass. The note's plain per-channel ratio is asserted where it is not dominated by that residual (±15 % or 0.015 absolute).
